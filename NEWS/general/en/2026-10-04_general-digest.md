### 1. Merz arrives in Kyiv during air-raid alerts and announces more than €1.3 billion in aid (Ukraine)
**Summary:** German Chancellor Friedrich Merz reached Kyiv by train on Sunday to meet President Volodymyr Zelenskyy as air-raid sirens sounded repeatedly. The Associated Press reported that Merz announced more than €1.3 billion in new aid: about €1 billion in military support, including long-range strike weapons, combat drones, and satellite technology so Ukraine can also target Russian launch sites, and about €350 million before winter for generators, substations, and grid repairs. Zelenskyy said the two sides signed 15 security documents worth more than €8.5 billion, including joint production of interceptors for jet-powered drones; he said Russia launched 900 such drones this week and Ukraine intercepted about half, and that Washington has proposed a technical-level trilateral meeting by the end of October, possibly in the United Arab Emirates, which Kyiv told US negotiators on Saturday it would join while it waits for Moscow. Kyiv Mayor Vitali Klitschko said a Russian attack again damaged the Northern Bridge over the Dnipro on Sunday and wounded two people; the AP said strikes since Thursday have fully or partly closed three of the capital's six road bridges. Russia's Defense Ministry said Sunday it would intensify strikes on military-industrial sites and related infrastructure in Kyiv and elsewhere, and officials in Sumy said an aerial attack there killed one person and wounded 16.

**Summary:** The Star reported Sunday, citing the South China Morning Post, that US Trade Representative Jamieson Greer said Thursday on the sidelines of the G20 trade ministers' meeting in Milwaukee that tariff cuts under the US-China Board of Trade have no implementation timeline yet. The sides agreed to extend the Busan trade truce from its original November 10 expiry to January 10, 2027. Greer also said the meeting failed to reach consensus on forced labor and excess capacity; in July the United States imposed tariffs of 10 to 12.5 percent on 60 economies, including China, Japan, South Korea, India, and European Union members, after a trade investigation into forced-labor allegations.

### 8. Commerce ministry: the G20 trade meeting produced a joint statement only on food (Trade)
**Summary:** A Chinese commerce ministry spokesperson said Sunday that G20 trade ministers, meeting in Milwaukee, Wisconsin, on September 30 and October 1, discussed the US chair's topics of opposing the weaponization of food, excess capacity, forced labor, and adjusting the most-favored-nation principle, and that only the food topic produced a joint statement. The spokesperson said China and many other emerging and developing members argued that food importers and exporters should jointly keep supply chains stable, while each side retains the right to act for domestic public-policy goals and food security, and that those points were reflected in the statement. Excess capacity and forced labor were contentious from the start, most members disagreed, and no outcome document was reached; Beijing noted that the US chair's statement said Washington and two other G20 countries issued a forced-labor statement outside the G20 framework. China and the great majority of members called most-favored-nation treatment a cornerstone of the multilateral trading system, and the chair did not table an outcome text on that topic. The spokesperson said China is willing to work toward practical trade results at the coming Miami summit.

### 11. Flooding in Catalonia kills two people and leaves one missing (Disaster)
