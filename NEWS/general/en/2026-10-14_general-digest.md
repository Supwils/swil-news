### 1. Russian strikes cut power to about 600,000; Starlink speed limits disrupt Ukraine's front line (Ukraine)
**Summary:** The BBC reported Wednesday that President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said at least 600,000 people were left without electricity after overnight missile and drone strikes, with critical energy sites hit in Kyiv, the capital region, and Kharkiv, despite President Donald Trump's announcement of an "energy ceasefire" on Sunday. Ukraine's air force said Russia launched 39 ballistic and cruise missiles and 128 drones, of which four missiles and 115 drones were shot down. Local officials said three people were killed in Kyiv, and at least six were killed later Wednesday in a drone strike on a warehouse in Lutsk. Zelenskyy adviser Serhiy Beskrestnov said SpaceX overnight capped Starlink terminals at 160 km/h without telling Kyiv, disrupting missions; defense ministry sources told the BBC that terminals lose connection above that speed, complicating interceptor drones, mid-range strikes, logistics, and reconnaissance. SpaceX did not comment. CBS, citing multiple sources, reported that the United States may have intentionally withheld early warnings of Russian strikes this week and had blamed a detection failure on "bad weather," an explanation Ukrainian officials rejected. Those sources said the warning system appeared to be working again for a barrage around 3 a.m. Wednesday. Zelenskyy arrived in Britain to meet Prime Minister Andy Burnham and, on X, asked the United States for a harsh response to what he called a demonstrative Russian slight. Russia's defense ministry said it shot down 902 Ukrainian drones overnight.

An Iranian official told Reuters that Tehran began using different attack tactics about 10 days ago, in part to influence the US midterms. US Central Command said it helped Gulf partners ship more than 19 million barrels on Monday and rejected the Guards' claim to control the strait.

Integrated-circuit exports rose 99.1%, and exports of electric vehicles, lithium batteries, and wind turbines and parts rose 72.3%, 36.4%, and 22.4%. Private firms traded 22.73 trillion yuan, up 19.1%, or 56.9% of the total. Trade with Belt and Road countries was 20.37 trillion yuan, up 17.1%, or 51% of the total.

### 10. Chinese state buyers take 8 to 10 US soybean cargoes, the first such deals since the Trump-Xi summit (agriculture)
**Summary:** Reuters reported Wednesday, citing three trade sources, that Chinese state buyers purchased 8 to 10 Panamax cargoes of US soybeans this week, the first such deals since the September summit between Presidents Donald Trump and Xi Jinping in Washington. Customs data showed September soybean imports of 11.88 million tons, down 7.7% from a year earlier. Mysteel said Sinograin on Tuesday sold 40.5% of 518,000 tons of imported soybeans offered at auction.
