### 2. Russia Bombs Zaporizhzhia Aid Hub: At Least 31 Killed; Glide Bombs in Focus (Ukraine)
**Summary:** Ukrainian local authorities said Russian forces struck a school used for weekly humanitarian aid distribution in Komyshuvakha, Zaporizhzhia region, on November 2 with multiple aerial glide bombs, also hitting a shelter; by the evening of November 3 at least 31 civilians were dead, five injured, and about 48 missing, with rescue work continuing. Victims included police and emergency workers. President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said the attack came soon after the Oleshky humanitarian ceasefire ended and that Russia knew residents gathered there every Monday. The UN Human Rights Monitoring Mission in Ukraine noted that the distribution time had been announced on social media and highlighted the devastating impact of glide bombs in populated areas.

## III. China Policy and Economy

### 7. PBOC Conducts 1 Trillion Yuan SFISF Operation: 31 Firms Bid; Largest Single Round Yet (Economy)
**Summary:** On November 3 the People’s Bank of China conducted a securities, funds and insurers swap facility (SFISF) operation of 100 billion yuan via fee bidding; 31 institutions participated at a winning fee of 10 basis points. A day earlier, the PBOC, NFRA and CSRC held a symposium pledging timely operations, more participants, lower fees and optimized collateral; stock repurchase/refinance loans were made a standing facility with the rate cut from 1.25% to 1%. ### 8. PBOC Raises FX Reserve Requirement Ratio to 6%: First Hike in Nearly Five Years (FX)
**Summary:** The People’s Bank of China announced on November 3 that, effective November 15, the foreign-exchange reserve requirement ratio for financial institutions would rise by 2 percentage points from 4% to 6%, to strengthen FX liquidity management. It is the first hike since December 2021 (the last move was a cut to 4% in September 2023). Analysts said freezing more bank FX liquidity and curbing FX lending should help balance supply and demand and ease yuan appreciation pressure; the yuan briefly moved sharply against the dollar after the announcement.
