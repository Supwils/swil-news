### 4. A magnitude-7.7 earthquake injures 27 in Panama; the canal stays open (Disaster)
**Summary:** AFP reported Saturday that the US Geological Survey put a shallow quake at 12:56 p.m. local time Friday about 10 kilometers from Pitaloza Arriba in Herrera province, revising an initial magnitude of 8.0 down to 7.7. A tsunami warning for the eastern Pacific was later lifted. Panama's National Civil Protection System said 27 people suffered minor injuries and that no deaths had been reported. President Jose Raul Mulino said some roads had completely collapsed and called it a major disaster. The health minister said the town of Tonosi in Los Santos province was cut off by land. The Panama Canal Authority said shipping was unaffected and operating as normal. The capital's international airport closed temporarily, the education ministry suspended classes nationwide, and the local geosciences institute recorded more than 80 aftershocks, one above magnitude 6.0.

## II. U.S. Politics and the Economy

### 5. Washington licenses Russian diesel transactions through April 2027; Kyiv calls it a weak decision by strong partners (Energy)
**Summary:** Al Jazeera reported Saturday that Trump said Friday, after a call with Putin, that Russia would immediately supply more than 300,000 tonnes of diesel to the US and global markets, another 500,000 tonnes in November, 1 million tonnes after that, and a further 3 million tonnes depending on the condition of Russian refineries. The Treasury's Office of Foreign Assets Control issued General License No. 135 the same day, authorizing transactions otherwise prohibited by the Russia and Ukraine sanctions rules that relate to the sale, delivery, offloading, or importation, including into the United States, of Russian-origin diesel through 12:01 a.m. eastern daylight time on April 7, 2027. The license does not authorize debits to accounts of the Central Bank of the Russian Federation, the National Wealth Fund, or the Ministry of Finance at US financial institutions. Al Jazeera, citing the American Automobile Association, said the US national average diesel price is $6.20 a gallon after a September record of $6.52. President Volodymyr Zelenskyy called it a weak decision by strong partners and said the deal plays into Russia's hands by allowing it to kill more people and wage war longer. Neil Atkinson, former head of the International Energy Agency oil markets division, told Al Jazeera the volumes would not move prices much because the United States uses about 3.7 million barrels of diesel a day. Windward, citing Vortexa, said Russian diesel and gasoil exports fell to 190,000 barrels a day in the third quarter from 850,000 in the first.
