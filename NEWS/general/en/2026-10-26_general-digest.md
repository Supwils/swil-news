### 1. Lula wins Brazil's runoff with 51.79 percent; Flavio Bolsonaro rejects the result (Politics)

**Summary:** President Luiz Inacio Lula da Silva took 51.79 percent of valid votes in Sunday's runoff and led by about 4.4 million votes, the first president since Brazil's return to civilian rule in 1985 to win after trailing in the first round, and the first elected to a fourth term. Flavio Bolsonaro refused to recognize the result and demanded annulment because Supreme Court Justice Cristiano Zanin extended voting nationwide by two hours; the BBC reported that both campaigns had asked for more time, and Supreme Electoral Court president Kassio Nunes Marques, appointed by Jair Bolsonaro, confirmed the victory and said the extension did not change the outcome. CNBC said the real fell 9 percent to 5.4255 per dollar, and the Sao Paulo exchange opened 15 percent lower, triggering a brief suspension before a partial recovery. On Monday, President Trump told reporters, "I didn't take sides, but it looked like another rigged election," without providing evidence; the new term starts on January 5 and runs until 2030, and Lula turns 81 on Tuesday.

### 2. Vucic concedes Serbia's parliamentary election as the student list heads for a majority (Politics)

**Summary:** Former Serbian president Aleksandar Vucic conceded in Belgrade on Sunday night that his Progressive Party coalition had lost the snap parliamentary election. On preliminary figures he said the Student List would take 135 to 140 seats in the 250-seat parliament and his party about 105. The Guardian quoted him as saying a stratified sample already showed that "we have lost these elections." CBC, citing Reuters, reported turnout near 70 percent, the highest since the breakup of Yugoslavia in 1992. The list is led by cardiologist Ilija Srdanovic and includes professors, actors, and farmers; who becomes prime minister is still unclear. The snap vote followed nearly two years of anti-corruption protests after a train-station canopy collapse in Novi Sad killed 16 people in 2024.

### 9. The central bank creates a bond-market facility for large nonbank redemptions (Economy)

**Summary:** On October 26 the People's Bank of China issued open-market operations announcement No. 12 of 2026, creating the Bond Market NBFI Swap Facility (BNSF) to support bond-market stability. If asset managers face group liquidity stress and the bond market comes under systemic pressure, systemically important bank wealth-management subsidiaries and public-fund managers, or their parent banks and securities parents, may borrow central-bank bills, finance themselves through market repos, buy eligible bonds or shares of their own redeemed public products, and pledge assets to the central bank. Xinhua said Governor Pan Gongsheng recently told the opening of the 2026 Financial Street Forum that the central bank would work with the National Financial Regulatory Administration and the China Securities Regulatory Commission on a liquidity tool for large nonbank redemptions, in order to build a bond-market stability mechanism.
