### 1. The United States sanctions the International Criminal Court as an institution, hours after a former judge wins the Nobel Peace Prize (International justice)
**Summary:** The BBC and CBS News reported Friday that the Trump administration imposed new sanctions on the International Criminal Court, extending penalties from 17 already sanctioned judges and officials to the court itself in an effort to make it financially unable to operate. Secretary of State Marco Rubio said the measures were meant to "defend US sovereignty" and that "either the ICC will end its threats, or we will end the ICC." He also said he would dismantle the court "brick by brick" if necessary. Assets under US jurisdiction are blocked, and US companies and individuals are generally barred from doing business with the court. The Treasury is allowing member states about 180 days, a six-month window, to keep certain operations going while they negotiate what Washington calls a solution. The sanctions text does not require withdrawal of arrest warrants for Israeli officials before the measures can be lifted. The court called the move "an assault on the rule of law" and said it would not be deterred. UN Secretary-General Antonio Guterres called it a serious blow to international criminal justice. The United Kingdom, Canada, Denmark, Germany, France, Italy, Japan, and the Netherlands reaffirmed their "continued and unwavering support." Hours earlier, the Norwegian Nobel Committee awarded the 2026 Peace Prize to Navanethem "Navi" Pillay, an ICC judge from 2003 to 2008. Benjamin Netanyahu's office criticized the award.

### 2. Tanker strikes in Hormuz hit a wartime high as Brent falls to $102.94 in early trade (Energy)
**Summary:** CNBC reported Friday, citing the International Maritime Organization, that 11 tankers were attacked while transiting the Strait of Hormuz in the week ended October 4, the most strikes on commercial ships since the war began in late February, and that five more tankers were hit this week. Guard-linked media said attacks would not be confined to the strait. A separate CNBC report the same day said front-month Brent was last seen Friday morning 1.3 percent lower at $102.94 a barrel and West Texas Intermediate 1 percent lower at $90.51. Tehran said it was reviewing a US proposal and would reply within days.

### 6. A federal judge vacates the Justice Department's nationwide voter-roll policy (Elections)
**Summary:** Democracy Docket reported Friday that US District Judge Sparkle Sooknanan vacated what the Justice Department internally called its "Voter List Maintenance Policy," finding that the department exceeded its statutory authority and violated federal law. The record described in the ruling says the department demanded complete, unredacted voter rolls from 49 states and Washington, D.C., including names, birth dates, addresses, driver's license numbers, and Social Security numbers. At least 18 states turned files over. Others refused, prompting a wave of lawsuits. The department shared data from cooperating states with the Department of Homeland Security, which ran it through the SAVE citizenship database, and arranged for Homeland Security Investigations to receive the files. Participating states agreed that if voters were flagged as potentially ineligible they would "clean" their rolls within 45 days and resubmit them. The judge wrote that the policy has already removed US citizens from the rolls in their home states and risks disenfranchising many more. She set aside the nationwide policy under the Administrative Procedure Act rather than limiting relief to the plaintiffs. She noted that at least 25 federal district courts had already dismissed separate voter-roll enforcement cases.

### 10. China and the EU record 16 agreed outcomes, with channels left open on rare earths and hybrids (Trade)
**Summary:** Taiwan's Central News Agency reported Friday that China's Ministry of Commerce published a joint statement after Commerce Minister Wang Wentao and EU trade and economic-security commissioner Maros Sefcovic held the second session of their trade and investment consultation mechanism in Beijing on October 8 and 9. The outcome list has 16 items in four areas: trade and investment balance, export controls, intellectual property, and World Trade Organization reform. The two sides will keep exploring, under WTO rules, the possibility of lowering some tariffs, and they reached an understanding on hybrid-vehicle trade consistent with those rules, while continuing price-undertaking and review procedures in the electric-vehicle anti-subsidy case. On export controls, China said it is willing to keep using a "green channel" to facilitate licenses for rare earths and permanent magnets shipped to Europe, and both sides agreed to notify each other before listing items. The next session is set for March 2027, with a ministerial video call in January before that. CNA, citing the Financial Times, also reported that the EU side had proposed cutting China's share of the EU hybrid market from more than one-third to about 15 percent or facing extra tariffs, and that Beijing rejected it. That quota is not in the joint statement.
