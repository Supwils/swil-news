## I. Global Headlines

### 1. Zelenskyy says five ballistic missiles hit Kyiv with no partner warning; Kharkiv reports 4 dead and 41 wounded (Ukraine)
**Summary:** The Associated Press reported Tuesday that President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said Ukraine received no early warning from partners about five Russian ballistic missiles that hit civilian infrastructure in Kyiv. He called the barrage "particularly vile" and wrote that early warning of ballistic missiles is what prevents people from being killed. A senior Ukrainian official, speaking anonymously, said the lack of warning was described as a "failure," suggesting weather or similar circumstances rather than a deliberate cutoff. At the White House, President Donald Trump told reporters the United States has not stopped sharing intelligence with Ukraine. Ukraine's air force said it had no prior surveillance warning before six ballistic missiles were fired at Kyiv late Monday and that the causes are being investigated. Foreign Minister Andrii Sybiha said in Warsaw that Kyiv continues to receive US intelligence. Zelenskyy said Russia had used more than 100 weapons since Monday evening, that a residential building in Kharkiv was destroyed, killing four people, and that 41 people were wounded in the city in one evening. One person was killed in the Fastiv district of Kyiv region. He also said Ukraine had not struck Russian refineries or other energy sites in the two days since Trump announced a purported energy ceasefire. France 24 reported that Tuesday was the first day since that announcement with no Russian strikes on Ukrainian energy targets, though it was unclear whether the omission was deliberate. One missile landed just over a kilometer from a plant that heats much of Lviv. The air force said 88 of 108 drones were shot down and damage was recorded at 17 locations. Russia's Defense Ministry said it struck military-industrial sites, logistics, rail and port infrastructure, and vessels carrying military cargo, and that it shot down 604 Ukrainian drones. Moscow Mayor Sergei Sobyanin said 417 drones targeted the Moscow region and 65 were intercepted as they approached the capital.

### 2. Drones hit two merchant ships off Bulgaria, sinking one and leaving 11 missing (Black Sea)
**Summary:** Al Jazeera reported Tuesday that Bulgarian Prime Minister Rumen Radev said two ships were hit by drones off Bulgaria's Black Sea coast and one sank. The Ukrainian-owned Chelsea 3, sailing under a Maltese flag, was hit by three drones and damaged about 18 nautical miles off Bulgaria at about 1:30 a.m. Tuesday. All 15 crew members, seven Ukrainians and eight Azerbaijanis, were rescued. Five received medical care and none was seriously hurt. President Iliana Iotova identified the sunken ship as the Guinea-Bissau-flagged cargo ship HS Dignity, which went down off the village of Krapets near the Romanian border. Bulgarian officials said five crew members were rescued and 11 remain missing, with the search continuing. Radev called the incident "absolutely unacceptable," said there was no clear evidence of who carried it out, but that "the outcome benefits" Russia, and that the Russian ambassador would be summoned. European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen said the Black Sea has become another front of Russia's war in Ukraine. After speaking with Radev, NATO Secretary General Mark Rutte said the alliance has already strengthened its eastern-flank posture and will adjust deterrence and defense as needed. Last week, drones hit two ships in the same area and sank one; all 10 crew members from that ship remain unaccounted for.

## II. US Politics

### 4. Twelve senators from both parties urge Trump to rescind the Russian diesel deal and the Treasury waiver (Energy and sanctions)
**Summary:** CBS reported Tuesday that twelve senators wrote to President Trump urging him to withdraw from his diesel deal with Vladimir Putin and to reverse a Treasury move that temporarily waives sanctions on Russia's energy sector so Russian diesel can reach the global market. The letter says the deal "damages American security interests and is a betrayal of the people of Ukraine" and that "this deal is a gift to our adversary and a betrayal of our Allies; it should be rescinded." Signatories include Republicans Thom Tillis, Susan Collins, Alan Armstrong, Lisa Murkowski, Mitch McConnell, and John Cornyn, and Democrats Richard Blumenthal, Jeanne Shaheen, Dick Durbin, Sheldon Whitehouse, Jack Reed, and Chris Coons. The senators acknowledged an urgent need to lower energy costs, but said Russian diesel on global markets would offer "limited to no relief" and a "negligible impact on fuel prices," while Kremlin revenue would finance a war of attrition and advance security cooperation with China, Iran, and North Korea. They noted that a sanctions law Congress passed last month, and that Trump signed, requires economic pressure on Russia and the chief enablers of its war. The letter released by Blumenthal's office names Treasury General License No. 135 as the waiver to rescind. CBS said Trump announced last week that Moscow would immediately release 300,000 tons of diesel, followed by millions more in the coming months.

## V. Global Economy and Markets

### 9. The IMF lifts this year's global growth forecast to 3.1% and says resilience is nearing its limits (Bangkok meetings)
**Summary:** CNA reported from Bangkok on Tuesday that the IMF World Economic Outlook sees global growth at 3.1% in 2026, up from 3.0% in July and below 3.5% in 2025, and at 3.4% in 2027, unchanged from July. Chief economist Silvana Tenreyro said, "The global economy has been resilient, but that resilience is reaching its limits," and that policy has to move from coping to rebuilding fiscal space and supply-side resilience. Deputy research director Petya Koeva Brooks said growth this year would have fallen to 2.5% without reserve drawdowns, alternative suppliers, and lower oil demand, and that those buffers are fading. The forecast assumes the Strait of Hormuz reopens in March 2027 and shipping broadly returns to pre-war conditions by October 2027, with oil averaging about $96 a barrel in 2026 and about $87 in 2027. Global headline inflation is projected at 4.7% in 2026 and 4.1% in 2027. US growth is 2.3% in both 2026 and 2027, unchanged this year and 0.1 point higher next year versus July. Euro-area growth this year was raised to 1.1%. Saudi Arabia is now forecast to contract 3.3% this year, against 1.7% growth in the July forecast. Iran, Iraq, Kuwait, Qatar, and the UAE are forecast to contract 7.6%, 16%, 16.7%, 15.6%, and 3.9% in 2026. India is forecast at 7.0% in both years. Just 32 economies now account for 95% of global growth, down from 54 in April. In an adverse case with oil at $130 a barrel through the first quarter of 2027, growth would be 0.2 point lower in 2026 and 0.5 point lower in 2027, and 2027 inflation would rise another 1.1 points to 5.2%. El Pais reported that the IMF sees US headline inflation at 3.6% this year, 0.2 point above the April forecast, easing to 2.8% in 2027, with core inflation returning to the Federal Reserve's 2% target only in mid-2028.
