### 2. COP31 talks in Antalya stuck after 36+ hours of overtime over fossil fuels

**Summary:** Negotiations at COP31 in Antalya, Turkey, have run more than 36 hours past the deadline without consensus on a final text. The EU and Pacific island states reject a draft that barely mentions cutting fossil-fuel demand, while the Africa Group wants more adaptation finance that the EU says should first use existing pledges. EU Climate Commissioner Wopke Hoekstra said there would be "no deal" without the climate action the bloc needs; teams have moved Sunday departure flights to Monday. If consensus fails, the summit could end with no agreement and reconvene next year — only the second such outcome since COP meetings began in 1995.

## II. US Politics and Policy

### 5. Trump executive order: federal contractors must clear congressional inquiries with the White House

**Summary:** Trump signed Executive Order 14438 on Friday requiring all federal contractors and subcontractors to notify the White House and contracting agencies of any congressional request about their communications or work with the executive branch, and to follow instructions to withhold material the administration deems protected by executive privilege; noncompliance can mean contract termination, suspension or debarment. The move comes as Democrats prepare tougher oversight after the midterms; incoming House Oversight Chair Robert Garcia has already told Trump family members and top officials to preserve documents, and White House counsel previously required agencies to get explicit permission before employees testify before Congress.

### 9. UK-EU summit seals three "reset" deals: SPS, ETS linking and youth mobility

**Summary:** Britain and the EU announced three post-summit arrangements: a Common Sanitary and Phytosanitary Area aimed at removing most agri-food, animal and plant checks from March 1, 2028; linking their emissions trading systems, targeted to operate from January 1, 2028 with mutual CBAM exemptions; and a Youth Experience Scheme offering about 100,000 places each way annually for people aged 18–31 to live up to three years for work, study or travel, with first travel expected no later than January 2030.
