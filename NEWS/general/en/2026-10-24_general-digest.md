## III. China

### 8. The Baqen earthquake response is raised to Level III, and Beijing allocates 50 million yuan in relief funds (Disaster)

**Summary:** Xinhua reported Saturday that the death and injury toll from the magnitude-7.0 earthquake in Baqen, Nagqu, Tibet, remained 16 dead and 76 injured, with 339 homes collapsed or damaged and 20 livestock sheds damaged. The central government has allocated 50 million yuan in emergency relief funds, earmarked another 30 million yuan for recovery, and raised the national earthquake emergency response from Level IV to Level III. On top of 5,000 relief items already sent, the Ministry of Emergency Management allocated another 5,000 items, including tents, coats, and folding beds, and coordinated 43,000 further items such as mobile toilets, heating stoves, food, and fuel. The zone sits at an average elevation of nearly 4,800 meters. Telecommunications base stations in Baqen that the quake had knocked out were all restored as of 10:30 a.m. Friday. As of 10 a.m. Friday, 12,489 people had been relocated, and three rounds of door-to-door checks had found no one missing as of 6 p.m. Friday.

## IV. U.S.-China Relations

### 10. China's commerce ministry opposes the new U.S. outbound-investment rule and reserves countermeasures (U.S.-China)

**Summary:** A Ministry of Commerce spokesperson, answering questions on Saturday, responded to the updated outbound-investment rule the U.S. Treasury published on October 23 and put out for public comment. The spokesperson said the proposal discriminatorily widens and tightens investment restrictions aimed at China, stretches the idea of national security, breaks important consensus reached by the two presidents, disrupts normal business between Chinese and U.S. companies, and damages the international economic order. China said it was seriously concerned and firmly opposed, would watch what follows, and reserved the right to take corresponding measures. It urged the United States to stop politicizing and weaponizing trade. Reuters reported that the U.S. proposal would add biotechnology to restricted sectors and expand coverage of high-performance computing and hypersonic systems. The existing program already restricts some U.S. investments involving China in semiconductors, quantum technology, and artificial intelligence. The Treasury has said the measures are not aimed only at China and are meant to stop U.S. capital from supporting adversaries' development of dual-use technologies that could threaten U.S. national security.
