### 1. US forces complete their withdrawal from Iraq as the Pentagon ends Operation Inherent Resolve (Middle East)
**Summary:** The last US troops, mainly from an air base in Erbil in the Kurdish region, left Iraq on Wednesday, ending the largest US war of the century, which began in 2003, Reuters reported. The pullout was agreed in 2024 under President Joe Biden and carried out by Donald Trump's administration while it wages war on Iran. The Pentagon said the United States and coalition partners formally concluded Operation Inherent Resolve and that Washington will keep providing targeted training and intelligence to Iraqi partners. Prime Minister Ali Faleh al-Zaidi said the end of the coalition mission does not end the fight against terrorism, but moves Iraq into a phase in which it takes full responsibility for its security. Trump wrote on Truth Social that the United States leaves Iraq with a "wonderful" prime minister who will hopefully handle things very well. Reuters noted that more than 4,500 US troops died in Iraq and that the Iraq Body Count project says more than 200,000 Iraqi civilians were killed. Of the 18 US service members listed as killed since Trump launched Operation Epic Fury with Israel in February, seven died in Iraq.

## II. U.S. Politics

### 5. The Government Accountability Office rules an about $810 million pocket rescission unconstitutional (Budget)
**Summary:** The nonpartisan Government Accountability Office wrote to Vice President JD Vance and senior members of Congress on Tuesday that the Trump administration's unilateral cancellation of roughly $810 million in appropriations exceeded its constitutional authority, the Deseret News reported via KSL. The cuts used a pocket rescission: a cancellation proposed so close to the fiscal year, which ends on October 1, that Congress cannot answer in time. The office said the legislative history of the Impoundment Control Act, Supreme Court precedent, and the constitutional framework require Congress to approve any spending cut the administration proposes. Senate Appropriations Chair Susan Collins, a Republican, said the Office of Management and Budget intentionally withheld the money for months to cancel bipartisan appropriations already signed into law, without reporting the impoundment and by usurping Congress's power of the purse. OMB Director Russ Vought defended the move on social media, saying the GAO had changed a 1970s view that the tool was available to the president. The administration used a similar package last year, the first by a president in nearly 50 years.

**Links:**

- [KSL / Deseret News — Federal watchdog: Trump canceling $810M in government funding was unconstitutional](https://www.ksl.com/article/news/politics/federal-watchdog-trump-canceling-810m-in-government-funding-was-unconstitutional/51630257)

**Commentary:** The fight is no longer only about this money, but about whether a president can let enacted appropriations die in the last days of the fiscal year.

## III. China Policy and Economy

### 8. The central bank cuts the PSL rate and adds relending quotas (Monetary policy)
**Summary:** The People's Bank of China decided to adjust several monetary tools, People's Daily reported on September 30. The one-year pledged supplementary lending rate was cut by 0.25 percentage point, from 1.75 percent to 1.5 percent, and support was extended to six networks: water, new power grids, computing, next-generation communications, urban underground pipes, and logistics. The relending quota for technology innovation and equipment upgrading rose by 200 billion yuan, from 1.2 trillion to 1.4 trillion yuan, and the supported share of each loan was raised from 60 percent to 100 percent. The rural and small-business relending quota rose by 500 billion yuan, including 300 billion yuan for private firms. After the increase, rural and small-business relending plus rediscounts stand at 4.85 trillion yuan, up from 4.35 trillion, and the private-firm quota rises from 1 trillion to 1.3 trillion yuan. The dispatch is datelined Beijing, September 29.

### 9. The six big banks start interest subsidies on qualifying first-home loans from October 1 (Property)
**Summary:** Industrial and Commercial Bank of China, Agricultural Bank of China, Bank of China, China Construction Bank, Bank of Communications, and Postal Savings Bank of China said on September 30 that they will carry out the finance ministry, central bank, and financial regulator notice on home-loan interest subsidies, Caijin [2026] No. 95, Economic Information Daily reported. From October 1, qualifying newly issued commercial mortgages get a subsidy automatically deducted from each month's interest, with no separate application. Loans must buy a first home of no more than 120 square meters priced at no more than 1.5 million yuan, and may not refinance an existing loan. Subsidized housing loans and housing-provident-fund loans are excluded. The fiscal subsidy is 1 percentage point a year for up to five years, capped at 1 million yuan per household, for a provisional one year, with the central and local governments sharing the cost 90 percent and 10 percent and with no overall cap. At the second-quarter 2026 national average new mortgage rate of 3.06 percent, the borrower would pay about 2.06 percent. On a 1 million yuan loan, interest saved can approach 50,000 yuan. CICC estimates that about 50 percent of new homes and 70 percent of existing homes in lower-tier cities qualify, against about 2 percent of new homes in the highest-tier cities.

### 11. China extends two investigations into US trade barriers until December 27 (Trade)
**Summary:** China's Ministry of Commerce extended two investigations that had been due to end on September 27 until December 27, citing the complexity of the cases, state broadcaster CCTV reported on September 30, according to The Straits Times.
