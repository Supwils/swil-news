## I. Global News

### 1. Witkoff meets Putin as the U.S. citizen Stephen Hubbard is freed and Moscow agrees in principle to mid-November talks (Ukraine)

**Summary:** U.S. envoy Steve Witkoff met Russian President Vladimir Putin on Wednesday at the Constantine Palace in St. Petersburg, with presidential aide Yury Ushakov and Russian Direct Investment Fund chief Kirill Dmitriev present. Russia released Stephen Hubbard, a 74-year-old Michigan teacher whom the State Department had formally designated as wrongfully detained: he was held by Russian forces in eastern Ukraine in 2022 and, after a closed 2024 trial, sentenced to nearly seven years on mercenary charges. Putin said Hubbard was already at Pulkovo Airport, and President Trump said no swap was required, while also saying the release would not have happened if the White House had not stopped new Russia sanctions from taking effect last week. After about three hours, Ushakov said Putin told Witkoff that Russia is ready in principle to resume U.S.–Russia–Ukraine talks around November 10 on a post-conflict security system, possibly in Abu Dhabi.

## II. U.S. Politics

### 6. Supreme Court declines to revive federal prison limits on care for transgender inmates (Courts)

**Summary:** In a brief unsigned order on Wednesday, the Supreme Court refused to pause an August 26 injunction by Senior U.S. District Judge Royce Lamberth, so the Federal Bureau of Prisons still cannot enforce a February 2026 policy that would deny transgender inmates hormone therapy and "social accommodations" such as makeup, wigs, and padding. Justices Samuel Alito and Clarence Thomas said they would have granted the administration's request, without explanation. The Bureau said the 2026 policy was adopted independently of the executive order President Trump signed on the first day of his second term. The inmates told the Court that withdrawing care would change their bodies and raise the risk of worsening dysphoria, depression, anxiety, self-harm, and suicidality; a divided D.C. Circuit panel had already refused to freeze Lamberth's order.

**Summary:** Zuo Gang, a deputy director-general at the National Development and Reform Commission, said gross domestic product rose 4.6% year on year in the first three quarters and 4.5% in the third, 0.2 percentage point faster than in the second. ### 13. UK inflation rises to 3.5% as public-sector net debt tops £3 trillion (Economy)

**Summary:** The Office for National Statistics said Wednesday that the Consumer Prices Index rose 3.5% in the year to September, up from 3.1% in August, with core inflation at 2.8% and services inflation at 3.7%. Transport, particularly motor fuels, made the largest upward contribution; restaurants and hotels were the largest offset. Public-sector net borrowing was £24.5 billion in September, £1.8 billion more than a year earlier and £1.9 billion above the Office for Budget Responsibility forecast, while debt interest was £10.9 billion, up £1.6 billion. Net debt was provisionally £3,005.0 billion, the first reading above £3 trillion, equal to 94.0% of GDP; borrowing in the financial year to September was £97.8 billion, still £4.4 billion below a year earlier but £6.0 billion above the OBR forecast.
