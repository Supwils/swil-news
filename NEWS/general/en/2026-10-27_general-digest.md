### 1. Israel votes with four-decade-high turnout as Netanyahu faces Eisenkot (Politics)
**Summary:** Israel held its first general election since the Gaza war began in 2023, with about 7.33 million eligible voters choosing a 120-seat Knesset; 61 seats are needed to form a government. The Central Elections Committee said turnout reached 74.3% by 8 p.m. local time—the highest at that hour since 1988, and about eight points above the 66.3% recorded at the same point in 2022. Most polling stations were set to close at 10 p.m. Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu’s Likud faced former IDF chief Gadi Eisenkot’s Yashar party; Likud’s request to extend voting hours was rejected. AP and The Times of Israel reported Netanyahu claiming rivals were trying to “steal” the election, while Eisenkot’s camp accused him of laying groundwork to contest the result.
