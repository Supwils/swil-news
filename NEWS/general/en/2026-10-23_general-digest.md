### 1. A magnitude-7.0 quake in Baqen, Nagqu, Tibet, has killed 16 people, and more than 12,000 have been relocated (Disaster)

**Summary:** Xinhua reported that a magnitude-7.0 earthquake struck Baqen County in Nagqu, Tibet, at about 10:33 p.m. on October 22. Xi Jinping ordered all-out search and rescue and treatment of the injured, and stressed that the high, cold disaster zone needs aftershock and weather monitoring, prevention of secondary disasters, supplies, shelter, and repairs to infrastructure; Li Qiang told the emergency and earthquake agencies to strengthen monitoring with other departments. The Nagqu government information office said Friday afternoon that, as of 3 p.m. on the 23rd, the quake had killed 16 people and injured 76, collapsed or damaged 339 homes, and damaged 20 livestock sheds. More than 5,000 rescuers from the regional, city, and county levels were deployed, 12,489 people had been relocated, 18 shelter sites were up in the worst-hit townships of Maru and Gangqie, and three rounds of door-to-door checks found no one still missing.

## IV. U.S.-China Relations

### 12. The U.S. Treasury proposes to add biotechnology to outbound investment limits, and Beijing says stop the restrictions (U.S.-China)

**Summary:** Axios reported Friday that the Treasury Department on Thursday night proposed rules to implement the Comprehensive Outbound Investment National Security Act, which Congress passed last year, placing biotechnology alongside semiconductors, artificial intelligence, and quantum computing and requiring notification of, or prohibiting, certain deals involving countries of concern including China, Cuba, Iran, and North Korea. The Treasury website puts the comment deadline at December 7. The proposed prohibited set includes small molecules, vaccines, blood and blood components, cell and gene therapies, monoclonal antibodies, and other drugs and their delivery systems, plus brain-computer interfaces and biomanufacturing equipment; covered transactions include equity acquisitions, certain debt financing, and joint ventures, with civil penalties and possible forced divestment. Foreign Ministry spokesperson Mao Ning said Friday that stretching national security into discriminatory limits on specific countries serves no one's interest, that the United States should follow market principles and stop disrupting normal business cooperation, and that China will watch the process and take necessary measures.

**Links:**
