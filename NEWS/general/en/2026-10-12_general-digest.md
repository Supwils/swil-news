### 2. The EU adopts its largest-ever Russia listings, aimed at mid-level arms suppliers (Sanctions)
**Summary:** The Council of the European Union said on October 12 that it had added 1,569 individuals and entities over Russia's escalating campaign and its deliberate strikes on civilian infrastructure, including 826 Russian companies and 743 people who serve as their chief executives. The Council called it the largest single set of listings the EU has ever agreed and said the Ukraine territorial-integrity sanctions list now totals close to 4,600. The package targets mid-level suppliers at the core of Russia's military industry, with annual turnover of at least 20 billion euros and more than 250,000 employees, providing components for missiles, drones, and aircraft. Those listed face an asset freeze and a ban on funds, and the individuals also face a travel ban. High Representative Kaja Kallas said more than half of the listings address the production chain for the ballistic missiles Russia uses against Ukrainian civilians, and that this is not the time to ease pressure on Moscow.

## IV. China's Policy and Economy

### 7. The Communist Party Central Committee and the State Council issue guidelines on new quality productive forces (Economy)
**Summary:** Xinhua reported on October 12 that the Communist Party Central Committee and the State Council issued guidelines on developing new quality productive forces, setting out 19 measures across five areas: scientific and technological innovation, the fusion of that innovation with industry, innovation in the mode of development, institutional reform, and talent policy. The document treats a sharp rise in total factor productivity as the core marker, calls for a full "AI Plus" drive, upgrading of traditional industries, growth of emerging industries, forward planning for future industries, and the global allocation of advanced factors of production. It also says development must establish the new before discarding the old, must not drift from the real economy into bubbles, and must not hollow out industries or chase novelty for its own sake. It bars officials from misusing policy in the name of new quality productive forces and says serious losses from herd investment will be investigated.
