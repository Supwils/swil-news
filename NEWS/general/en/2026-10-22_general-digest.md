## III. China Policy

### 8. China publishes new outbound-investment rules effective December 1 (Economy)

**Summary:** Xinhua reported Thursday that the National Development and Reform Commission published revised administrative measures for outbound investment, effective December 1, 2026, replacing the 2017 measures for overseas investment by enterprises. An NDRC official said China's outbound investment flows rose from $158.3 billion in 2017 to $213.6 billion in 2025, and the stock from $1.8 trillion to $3.4 trillion. The new rules restate that investors include domestic enterprises, other organizations, and resident individuals, and they keep channels such as the qualified domestic institutional investor program, Stock Connect, and Wealth Management Connect. Against demands by some countries for technology, data, or the disposal of equity and assets, the rules tighten reporting of major adverse events and add an annual information report. They also refine reporting of overseas reinvestment and require advance reports for a small number of large projects that bear on diplomatic relations.

### 11. The U.N. says a 1.5°C overshoot can no longer be avoided (Climate)

**Summary:** The U.N. Environment Programme on Wednesday published the Emissions Gap Report 2026, titled The Point of Return. It says global warming is set to exceed 1.5°C above pre-industrial levels. Its lowest-possible overshoot pathway limits the mid-century peak to 1.7–1.8°C and returns temperatures to or below 1.5°C by 2100 with about a 50 percent chance. Relative to 2025, that requires emissions cuts of 18 percent by 2030, 45 percent by 2035, and 69 percent by 2040. Full delivery of current national climate plans would cut emissions only 5–8 percent by 2030 and 11–15 percent by 2035. Continuing current policies could produce 2.8°C of warming this century; delivering all national plans would limit it to 2.6°C, and adding net-zero targets to 2.1°C. Those outlooks are worse than last year because the United States left the Paris Agreement and historical emissions were revised. Returning to 1.5°C would also require removing and permanently storing about 320 gigatonnes of CO2 this century. Secretary-General António Guterres said overshoot is not surrender.

**Links:**

- [UNEP — New UNEP report lays out emission cuts needed for return to 1.5°C](https://www.unep.org/en/news-and-stories/press-release/new-unep-report-lays-out-emission-cuts-needed-return-15degc)
- [UN News — Global warming will breach 1.5°C – but deep, fast cuts can still bring it back](https://news.un.org/en/story/2026/10/1168634)

**Commentary:** The report recasts 1.5°C as a question of how high and how long the overshoot runs, and whether temperatures can be brought back before 2100.
