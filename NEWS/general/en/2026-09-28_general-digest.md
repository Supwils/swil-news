### 3. Category 3 Hurricane Polo nears Baja California, with landfall expected the same day (Disaster)
**Summary:** Residents of Mexico's Baja California Sur boarded up homes on Monday as Hurricane Polo threatened high winds, floods, and mudslides, the Associated Press reported. The US National Hurricane Center said Polo was a Category 3 storm Monday morning with top sustained winds of 115 mph (185 kph). It was forecast to reach Baja California Sur later in the day near major-hurricane strength and to make landfall on the Mexican mainland late Monday night or early Tuesday as a hurricane, with life-threatening winds and flash floods expected soon and heavy rain possible in the US Southwest later in the week. Laura Velazquez, national coordinator of Mexico's civil protection agency, said landfall would come later in the afternoon and urged people in risky homes to shelter. Travel in the landfall area was to be suspended and ports closed. In Puerto San Carlos, a fishing town of about 5,700 people, waves were already flooding the coast, and some families planned to go to shelters in Ciudad Constitucion, about 37 miles away. The Mexican navy gathered personnel and aid in Guaymas and Mazatlan and deployed three vessels that could supply coastal communities cut off by the storm. Hurricane Nolo, with maximum sustained winds near 155 mph, was moving away from Hawaii's Big Island, but more than 10,000 customers on Maui were still without power Monday morning. President Trump had approved an emergency declaration, and more than 120 National Guard members were activated out of roughly 400 statewide.

### 5. White House moves to cancel $810 million Congress already approved, and appropriators call it illegal (Budget)
**Summary:** The Trump administration announced Friday night that it would cancel $810 million already appropriated for health, education, and other programs, days before the fiscal year ends, Roll Call reported. The pocket rescission withholds money until it can lapse, leaving Congress little time to respond. The House is already out and is not scheduled to return until after the November midterms. Senate Appropriations Chair Susan Collins, Republican of Maine, said the White House budget office "intentionally withheld these funds for months" to carry out what she called an unlawful cancellation of bipartisan appropriations signed into law. House Appropriations ranking Democrat Rosa DeLauro said the freeze itself is illegal and that the administration has been unlawfully impounding funds all year. The largest piece, $567 million, scales back an account that helps fund the Department of Health and Human Services' Office of Refugee Resettlement. Other cuts include $25 million for an Education Department program for migrant students, $70 million in grants and fellowships for foreign students and faculty, $28 million in health research the administration called wasteful or duplicative, and $56 million in Department of Housing and Urban Development housing counseling. After the midterms, Congress faces a December 11 deadline to fund the rest of fiscal 2027. The administration used the same maneuver last year to cancel about $4 billion in foreign aid; the Supreme Court ultimately ruled it had the right to cancel that money.

## IV. US-China Relations

### 9. China and the US publish roughly $30 billion tariff lists each; Beijing says more than 90 percent would lose the extra duties (Trade)
**Summary:** China's Ministry of Commerce on September 28 published details of the US-China Board of Trade and a reciprocal "$30 billion for $30 billion" tariff framework, Xinhua reported. The two presidents agreed in Beijing in May to create an intergovernmental trade council. An eighth round of talks in New York and Washington from September 20 to 23 reached consensus on the council's working procedures, the framework document, and product lists. Each side would cut tariffs on about $30 billion of imports, measured on 2024 bilateral trade, with more than 90 percent of the products having the full reciprocal extra tariffs removed and receiving most-favored-nation treatment. The US list covers toys, home appliances, baby products, kitchen and bath goods, and holiday gifts from China. China's list covers US agricultural products, personal-care goods, medical devices, and coal. Cuts would take effect at the same time after each side completes domestic legal procedures. The two sides also agreed to an agriculture working group, with a first meeting planned before the end of 2026. Euronews, citing the White House, said the lists are recommendations "with a view toward" reciprocal reductions, covering 1,619 categories of US products and 77 categories of Chinese goods, and that neither side has set the size or the start date of the cuts. Trade Representative Jamieson Greer said the two countries have recommended about $30 billion of non-sensitive trade each way for more favorable treatment and that the step would improve market access for about 30 percent of US exports to China. Euronews also reported that Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent confirmed the trade truce now runs to January 10, that Beijing's list includes a commitment to import at least 10 million metric tons of US coal in each of 2027 and 2028, and that soybeans appear only as seed for planting. Al Jazeera, citing the US Trade Representative, said two-way trade totaled $495 billion in 2025, down 25 percent from the year before.

## VI. Other Regions

### 12. Poland will loosen rules for shooting down objects coming from Russia, allowing fire on radar identification (Europe)
**Summary:** Poland's defense ministry will within days change the rules under which aircraft and helicopters engage objects entering from the direction of Russia, Deputy Defense Minister Cezary Tomczyk said in Deblin on September 28, Polish broadcaster RMF24 reported. Under current peacetime procedure, a pilot must fly close enough to identify a target visually before firing. The new procedure replaces that with so-called positive identification, so a radar reading is enough for the pilot or the operational commander to authorize a shot. Tomczyk said such options have usually been reserved for war or martial law, but Poland now faces the daily possibility of drones entering its territory. The decision was made by General Ireneusz Nowak, commander of the armed forces' operational command.
