**Summary:** Al Jazeera reported Tuesday, citing maritime trackers, that Gulf oil exports have recovered even as attacks on ships increase: Kpler estimated that Gulf oil flows excluding Iran were back above 81 percent of pre-war levels in September, and that Middle East crude exports exceeded pre-war levels on 14 days that month. Saudi Aramco chief executive Amin Nasser said in London on Monday that nearly three billion barrels of supply had been lost since the conflict began at the end of February, and that rebuilding inventories while meeting demand could take as long as two years.

### 5. Kenya confirms its first imported Ebola case after the patient dies in Nairobi (Public health)
**Summary:** The BBC reported Tuesday that Health Minister Aden Duale confirmed Kenya's first Ebola case: a Kenyan citizen who had lived in the Democratic Republic of Congo for seven years, fell ill about a month earlier, and was treated in several Congolese hospitals. He flew into Nairobi on Saturday, passed routine airport screening, and was taken to hospital by a relative; tests identified Bundibugyo Ebola. He died at 23:30 on Monday and was buried on Tuesday under outbreak protocols. Authorities have identified at least 28 contacts and are tracing 23 passengers and four crew from the same flight. A senior health ministry official said medication may have masked his symptoms and allowed him through airport screening. The World Health Organization has recorded 8,544 cases and 4,114 deaths in the current outbreak, the largest ever documented in Congo. Medecins Sans Frontieres said Monday that the virus was spreading "like a megafire," and that North Kivu has accounted for nearly 40 percent of newly confirmed cases since the spread intensified in early September. There is no approved vaccine or treatment for this species.

### 13. Spain's official gazette publishes the decree dissolving parliament, with the election set for November 29 (Europe)
**Summary:** Europa Press reported Tuesday that Spain's official gazette published Royal Decree 806/2026, dissolving the Congress and Senate elected on July 23, 2023, and calling elections to both chambers for Sunday, November 29, 2026. ### 14. ECB's Lane says energy prices are above the baseline and long-term yields will enter the rate decision (Central bank)
**Summary:** The European Central Bank published on Tuesday the full interview that Executive Board member Philip Lane gave to Italy's Ansa news agency on October 1. Lane said energy prices are higher than the ECB's baseline, but that strong second-round effects have not been seen so far, so it is too simple to say the economy is in the adverse scenario or the baseline. He called artificial intelligence one of the biggest global issues: U.S. AI investment is leading firms to raise a lot of long-term debt, which is one reason long-term yields are rising. An increase driven more by external than by European factors slows the European economy and, on its own, reduces inflation, and that will sit alongside the inflation and risk analysis when the policy rate is set. He said the main driver of rate decisions so far has been the inflation effect of the energy shock. A U.S.-Iran memorandum of understanding over the summer brought energy prices down and improved sentiment; the new question is a fresh rise in prices and uncertainty about how long the conflict lasts. The interview was conducted on October 1. In it, Lane said Italy's inflation reading the previous day was 4.1 percent, and that fiscal support for people on low incomes should be as targeted as possible.

**Links:**

- [ECB — Interview with Philip R. Lane, Ansa](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/press/inter/date/2026/html/ecb.in261006~bc94400297.en.html)

**Commentary:** Lane rests the case for higher rates on the energy shock, while conceding that the rise in U.S. long-term yields itself slows Europe, which leaves the next meeting with two arguments that pull in opposite directions.
