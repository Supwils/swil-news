### 1. High-speed train crash near Toledo, Spain, kills at least 68 (Breaking/Europe)
**Summary:** Around 9 a.m. local time on Oct. 28, on the Madrid–Seville high-speed line near Urda in Toledo province, a Renfe AVE train rear-ended a stopped Iryo train, derailing at least two carriages. Castilla-La Mancha authorities said the death toll rose to at least 68, with about 80 injured—roughly 50 seriously and 15 critically. Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez declared three days of national mourning (Oct. 29–31). Transport Minister Óscar Puente said a communications/signaling failure preceded an “inexplicable” control-centre decision to let traffic resume. It is Spain’s deadliest rail disaster since 2013 and the second high-speed collision on the same corridor in nine months.

### 4. Federal Reserve holds rates at 3.75%–4%; two officials prefer a hike (Economy)
**Summary:** On Oct. 28 the FOMC voted 10–2 to keep the federal-funds target range at 3.75% to 4%; Governor Lisa Cook and Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack dissented in favor of a quarter-point increase. Chair Kevin Warsh said politics and the Nov. 3 midterms played no role and foreshadowed fewer rate-decision meetings in 2027. US inflation rose to about 3.7% in September, driven largely by gasoline, diesel and crude costs.
