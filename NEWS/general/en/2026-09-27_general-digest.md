## III. China Policy and Economy

### 7. PBOC third-quarter meeting keeps a moderately loose stance and stresses countercyclical support (Monetary policy)
**Summary:** People's Daily Overseas Edition reported on September 27, with a Beijing dateline of September 26, that the People's Bank of China monetary policy committee recently held its third-quarter 2026 meeting. The committee said China should continue a moderately loose monetary policy, strengthen countercyclical adjustment, use both the aggregate and structural roles of policy tools, coordinate with fiscal policy, and support stable growth and a reasonable recovery in prices. It called for combining new and existing policies, keeping liquidity ample, and aligning growth in social financing and money supply with targets for growth and the price level. It also urged lower intermediary financing fees, attention to long-term yields from a macroprudential perspective, a market-set exchange rate, and steps to prevent herd behavior so the renminbi stays basically stable at a reasonable equilibrium. Securities Times noted that the previous quarter's wording called for stronger countercyclical and cross-cyclical adjustment, while this meeting stresses countercyclical adjustment, and that a specific call to "safeguard capital-market stability" did not reappear even as the committee again pledged to keep financial markets stable.

## V. Other Regions

### 9. Swiss voters reject stricter neutrality, with about 70 percent and every canton voting no (Europe)
**Summary:** Swiss voters on Sunday rejected the Neutrality Initiative backed by the far-right Swiss People's Party. The Guardian, citing final Federal Statistical Office results, reported that every canton and about 70 percent of voters opposed writing "perpetual and armed neutrality" into the constitution; passage would have limited NATO cooperation and banned sanctions on warring states except UN sanctions. The federal government and most parties opposed the measure, arguing Switzerland would no longer be a reliable security partner. Foreign Minister Ignazio Cassis said the result "is not a vote against neutrality" and that Switzerland was neutral yesterday, is neutral today and will remain neutral; the Guardian, citing Bloomberg, said backer Christoph Blocher spent 2 million Swiss francs, and the People's Party accepted the defeat with no current plan for a follow-up.
