## I. Chips & Critical Materials

### 1. Broader Hit From New U.S. Export Controls as TSMC and Samsung Pause Some China Shipments (Semiconductors)

**Summary:** Nikkei Asia reported on Nov 20 that tighter U.S. export controls that took effect Thursday are disrupting the global semiconductor supply chain more widely than expected, extending beyond advanced AI chips to everyday mobile and consumer applications and affecting designers beyond China. Multiple outlets citing the Financial Times said TSMC and Samsung Electronics recently told some Chinese customers that shipments would pause while they seek Commerce Department licenses. Reuters reported that TSMC is telling affected customers to halt orders at 16nm and below without a license—generally limiting them to 28nm/40nm if chips do not use HBM or advanced packaging—and is tightening checks on overseas subsidiaries and intermediaries. The prior packaging-and-testing workaround has also been closed.

- [TrendForce — TSMC, Samsung Reportedly Pause China Consumer Chip Shipments Under New U.S. Rules](https://www.trendforce.com/news/2026/11/20/news-tsmc-samsung-reportedly-pause-china-consumer-chip-shipments-under-new-u-s-rules-16nm-orders-affected)

**Commentary:** Controls have moved from banning top AI chips to foundry compliance and consumer-chip licensing, so short-term disruption risk hits Chinese design and smartphone supply chains first.

### 2. Nvidia Locks in Substrate and Advanced-Packaging Capacity With Long-Term Deals (Semiconductors)

**Summary:** On Nov 17 Nvidia announced expanded commitments with Kinsus, Samsung Electro-Mechanics, SPIL, Amkor, Foxconn and TSMC. Kinsus plans to raise Nvidia-dedicated advanced ABF substrate capacity at its Youth campus in Taoyuan to more than 3.5 times current levels, with production targeted for the second half of 2027 and full added capacity in Q3 2028, backed by more than $1 billion in Nvidia investment. SPIL, under a multiyear multibillion-dollar agreement, plans to expand Taiwan advanced-packaging capacity dedicated to Nvidia by mid-2029. Supply Chain Dive followed up on Nov 20, noting interim CEO Colette Kress told investors demand continues to outpace supply.

### 3. Global Electronics Association Warns of PCB Material Shortages and Longer Lead Times (PCB)

**Summary:** On Nov 18 the Global Electronics Association issued an industry alert that PCB supply-chain shortages are worsening, with automotive, industrial, medical, defense and telecom customers facing longer lead times and scarce supply. Association data show bookings up about 56.1% year on year through September while shipments rose only about 13.3%, and high-layer boards run 16–20 weeks or more. AI-server boards moving to 40–60+ layers draw far more glass fabric, resin, copper foil and laminate than typical boards; the association expects the shortage to last at least through the first half of 2027, with meaningful relief unlikely before mid-2028. Gasgoo cited comments from a China-based automotive supplier executive that automotive PCB lead times have stretched from about 2–3 weeks to roughly 20 weeks on average, with the bottleneck in upstream materials such as CCL rather than board fabs.

**Links:**

- [Global Electronics Association — Growing Shortages Across the PCB Supply Chain](https://www.electronics.org/news-release/global-electronics-association-growing-shortages-across-pcb-supply-chain-threaten-next)
- [Gasgoo — Why are PCBs short too?](https://auto.gasgoo.com/news/202611/11I70477503C108.shtml)

**Commentary:** The binding constraint is upstream materials and specialized looms, so adding board capacity alone will not stop AI from siphoning supply from automotive and industrial boards.

### 4. Indonesia Drought Cuts Nickel Refining and Pressures Battery Materials (Batteries)

**Summary:** Drought in Indonesia is forcing HPAL nickel refiners to cut output. Discovery Alert reports Nickel Industries' flagship ENC plant is running at about 30% of nameplate capacity, down from about 50%, with a base case for recovery around the December 2026 wet season; CRU cut its 2026 MHP forecast to about 370,000 tonnes of contained nickel from 400,000. Indonesia supplied about 66.6% of global mined nickel in 2025, and Chinese firms hold roughly 75% of Indonesian refining capacity, so Chinese battery makers sit directly downstream. LME nickel has fallen from a 2026 peak near $20,000/t to about $15,000, as surplus stocks still absorb the supply shock.

**Links:**

- [SCMP — Indonesia’s severe drought threatens to disrupt China’s vast battery industry](https://www.scmp.com/economy/china-economy/article/3373055/indonesias-severe-drought-threatens-disrupt-chinas-vast-battery-industry)
- [Discovery Alert — Indonesia Nickel Production Cuts Deepen as Drought Bites](https://discoveryalert.com/news/indonesia-nickel-production-cuts-enc-drought-november-2026/)

**Commentary:** This is a classic climate shock on a highly concentrated chain: inventories and LFP offer near-term buffers, but Indonesia–China nickel redundancy remains thin.

## II. Capacity & Migration

### 5. GlobalFoundries Breaks Ground on Malta Fab Under Up-to-$21B U.S.–Japan Plan (Capacity)

**Summary:** On Nov 13 GlobalFoundries broke ground in Malta, New York, on the first new 300mm fab under a U.S.–Japan initiative. Japan provides investment while GF contributes technology and operations under a framework of up to $21 billion; the first phase is about $10 billion, with roughly $570 million in New York incentives, and is expected online in 2029, adding about 900,000 300mm wafers of annual capacity for automotive, industrial and connected-infrastructure chips. The two-fab initiative is expected to create about 27,000 construction and ecosystem jobs and more than 1,000 high-skilled manufacturing jobs.

**Links:**

- [GlobalFoundries — Breaks ground on first new semiconductor fab under U.S.-Japan initiative](https://gf.com/news-and-events/news/globalfoundries-breaks-ground-on-first-new-semiconductor-fab-under-landmark-us-japan-initiative/)

**Commentary:** The U.S. is adding trusted mature-node capacity in parallel with AI leading-edge buildouts, not as a substitute for them.

### 7. Tariffs Alone Are Not Bringing Complex Supply Chains Back to the U.S. (Migration)

**Summary:** A Nov 19 Harvard Business Review article argues that tariffs are meant to bring manufacturing home, but reshoring complex auto and safety-critical supply chains is neither economically viable nor practical end to end. It cites a U.S. maker of safety-critical truck components that sought a domestic casting source for its Mexican plant: more than 100 U.S. foundries were contacted, only six or seven quoted, and all were at least 50% above the Chinese supplier's roughly $200 price, with the highest at $1,000. Separately, the Reshoring Initiative recorded about 247,000 announced reshoring-plus-FDI manufacturing jobs in 2025 and, based on first-half 2026 announcements, projects about 309,000 for 2026.

**Links:**

- [HBR — Why Tariffs Aren’t Bringing Factories Back to the U.S.](https://hbr.org/2026/11/why-tariffs-arent-bringing-factories-back-to-the-u-s)
- [Manufacturing Dive — Reshoring and FDI added 247K jobs in 2025](https://www.manufacturingdive.com/news/reshoring-foreign-direct-investment-247k-jobs-2025-report-reshoring-initiative/873070/)

**Commentary:** Announced reshoring jobs keep rising, but midstream casting and tooling ecosystems still decide what can actually move; tariffs alone cannot relocate an entire auto chain.

## III. Policy, Geopolitics & Logistics

### 8. Strike Risk Rises as LA/Long Beach Watchmen Contract Expires Overnight (Logistics)

**Summary:** The Pacific Maritime Association says the contract extension covering about 250 ILWU Local 26 port watchmen expires at 12:01 a.m. Pacific Time on Nov 21, and the union has indicated it will not agree to another extension; talks were set to resume on Nov 20. The watchmen are only about 2% of the ILWU workforce at the ports but work at terminals handling roughly 85% of Long Beach cargo and 33% of Los Angeles cargo; if they strike, thousands of other dockworkers are expected to honor picket lines. The Los Angeles Times reported on Nov 20 that a stoppage would disrupt cargo during the holiday shipping peak and a record volume year.

### 9. U.S. Truckload Spot Rates Stay Elevated as October Container Imports Hit a Record (Logistics)

**Summary:** RXO's mid-November Curve update showed Q3 2026 truckload spot rates excluding fuel rose 37.9% year on year—the highest since 2021 and the tenth consecutive quarter of annual gains—while Q4 rates so far remain inflationary year on year but softer sequentially. Carrier exits driven by regulatory enforcement and higher costs are the main support for pricing. Descartes data cited by FreightWaves put October U.S. container imports at about 2.67 million TEU, a monthly record and up roughly 16% year on year, with China-origin volumes near 988,000 TEU, up about 22.9%. Average transit delay across 10 gateways rose to about 6.3 days.
