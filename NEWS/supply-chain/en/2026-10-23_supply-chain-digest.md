## I. Chips and Equipment

### 1. Samsung reportedly orders more 2nm tools for Taylor Fab 1, with a 20,000-wafer target (chips)
**Summary:** Wccftech and DIGITIMES reported on October 23, citing South Korea's ZDNet, that Samsung Electronics has ordered tools for its first Taylor, Texas fab capable of supporting about 6,000 2-nanometer wafers a month, filling the remaining space and described as the last stretch of core equipment spending. The reports say that order builds on earlier procurement aimed at about 10,000 wafers a month and, once a pilot line is counted, sources believe the fab could reach its design maximum of 20,000 2-nanometer wafers a month in the second half of 2027. Wccftech also wrote that Taylor Fab 1 is planned to start operating by the end of this year and will use extreme-ultraviolet lithography for 2-nanometer chips. The accounts cite unnamed industry sources, and Samsung has not publicly confirmed the order.

### 2. Intel's CTO says leading-edge output can probably only double (equipment)
**Summary:** The Next Web reported on October 23 that Intel chief technology officer Pushkar Ranade told Dwarkesh Patel's podcast the world does not have enough tools for Intel to make 10 times as many leading-edge wafers, and that doubling is about the limit. He said wafer output cannot rise faster unless equipment makers and their own suppliers produce more; extreme-ultraviolet lithography is the biggest bottleneck, but most tools are short. Once the 18A process is set, he said, about 80 percent of those tools can be reused for the next node, 14A. Tools ordered today take about 18 months to arrive, he added, and a new fab on top of that reaches the end of the decade; had Intel known a year ago what it knows now, it would have bought more equipment. He said the supply crunch is worse for memory than for compute, and that selling the flash business to SK hynix was, in hindsight, probably not the smartest time.

**Links:**

- [The Next Web — Intel CTO tells Dwarkesh Patel the world lacks tools to 10x chip output](https://thenextweb.com/news/intel-cto-ranade-dwarkesh-chip-tools-2x-not-10x)

**Commentary:** "Not 10 times, but probably 2 times" and the 18-month tool lead time are his comments on a podcast, not a capital-spending release. His line that Intel has already caught TSMC on technology is his characterization, and the write-up does not include an independent yield comparison.

## II. Batteries and Critical Minerals

### 5. Korean cathode makers switch lines toward LFP, with industry estimates up to about 330,000 tonnes (batteries)
**Summary:** MoneyToday reported on October 23, citing battery-industry sources, that POSCO Future M has converted one of four ternary-cathode sintering lines at its Pohang plant to lithium iron phosphate, plans to finish the other three by May next year, and aims to lift LFP to about one-third of cathode output between 2030 and 2035. Industry estimates of conversion efficiency were revised up: about 45,000 tonnes of ternary equipment at Pohang could yield up to 100,000 tonnes of LFP, and about 55,000 tonnes at Gwangyang up to 110,000 tonnes, taking the two sites from a prior estimate of about 150,000 tonnes to a maximum of 210,000 tonnes. L&F Plus plans to expand from about 30,000 tonnes a year to 60,000 tonnes in the first half of next year, and is considering as much as 120,000 tonnes. IBK Investment & Securities estimates potential ESS LFP cathode demand from Korea's three cell makers rising from about 230,000 tonnes in 2027 to 350,000 tonnes in 2030, while the two cathode makers' potential capacity combined is put at a maximum of about 330,000 tonnes. The report says domestic LFP cathode still prices about 30 to 40 percent above Chinese material.

**Links:**

- [MoneyToday — China-made LFP cathode material substitution begins](https://www.mt.co.kr/en/industry/2026/10/23/2026102115110818530)

**Commentary:** The 210,000-tonne and 330,000-tonne figures are industry estimates of what converted lines could support, not production volumes the two companies confirmed that day. The 30 to 40 percent price gap is also the market level as reported.

### 7. BMI: China's rare-earth exports to Japan fell 14.8 percent in nine months, and yttrium supply has collapsed (rare earths)
**Summary:** Mining Weekly reported on October 23 that BMI said China's rare-earth shipments to Japan fell 14.8 percent year on year in the first nine months of this year, with September alone down 8.4 percent. Yttrium, used as a thermal-barrier coating on turbine blades and as a protective coating and insulator in semiconductors, has seen shipments to Japan "effectively collapse," BMI said. China's overall rare-earth exports fell 9.6 percent year on year to 43,700 tonnes over the same nine months, even as September shipments rose 6.7 percent. BMI said the second wave of rare-earth export controls was initially suspended until November, and that the trade truce after Xi Jinping's September 23-25 visit to the United States runs to January 10, 2027, but Beijing has yet to publish an official notice extending the November rare-earth deadline. BMI also described the US Project Vault stockpile, with up to $10 billion in Export-Import Bank financing and close to $2 billion in private capital; Glencore and Mercuria joined VaultCo in September with combined commitments of $1 billion.

**Links:**

- [Mining Weekly — Mounting Chinese rare earth pressures force Japan to strengthen partnership with Western partners, BMI reports](https://www.miningweekly.com/article/mounting-chinese-rare-earth-pressures-force-japan-to-strengthen-partnership-with-western-partners-bmi-reports-2026-10-23)

**Commentary:** The 14.8 percent drop and the 43,700 tonnes are BMI's reading of exports over the first nine months. The yttrium "collapse" is not given a tonnage in this report, and no official notice has confirmed that the January 10 truce also extends the rare-earth controls.
