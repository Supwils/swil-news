## I. Chips & Critical Materials

### 1. Fujifilm and Tata Electronics sign a materials MoU: phased Dholera plant at about 800 crore rupees (chip materials)
**Summary:** On **September 19**, Fujifilm and Tata Electronics announced a memorandum of understanding, from Mumbai and Tokyo, to develop and supply advanced semiconductor materials for Tata’s fab under construction in Dholera, Gujarat, and to help Indian chemical makers with technology, quality, and process know-how for high-purity chemicals. Fujifilm will invest about **800** crore Indian rupees (about **$83 million**) in phases in a materials plant in the Dholera Special Investment Region, covering photoresists, CMP slurries, thin-film materials, and high-purity process chemicals from front-end through back-end. The public text does not give a start-up date or annual capacity for the materials plant.

### 3. Equipment-part lead times double; some Japanese core parts take up to 40 months (equipment)
**Summary:** The Electronic Times reported on **September 17**, citing industry accounts from the **16th**, that lead times for critical semiconductor-equipment components have more than doubled. Deposition-equipment parts stretched from about **four** months after order to as long as **10** months. A laser-process equipment maker is waiting up to about **40** months for core Japanese components, and paying a premium has not moved the date. Packaging-equipment parts sourced in South Korea still take about **50%** longer, from roughly **four** months to **six** months or more. The paper said equipment deliveries to chipmakers have also doubled, and that Samsung’s Pyeongtaek build and SK hynix’s Yongin build could be affected; Japanese suppliers remain cautious on capacity, and firms are avoiding Chinese parts amid the U.S.–China trade conflict.

**Links:**

- [Electronic Times — Semiconductor equipment component delivery time doubles, up to 40 months](https://en.etnews.com/20260917200002)

**Commentary:** The next gate on AI capacity is not the wafer headline but deposition, laser, and packaging parts—when lead times are measured in years, the building arrives before the tool.

### 4. Three years after gallium and germanium curbs, prices are still about 9–10 times 2023 levels (critical minerals)
**Summary:** Reuters reported on **September 18** that three years after China restricted gallium and germanium exports in **2023**, Western prices remain about **9 to 10** times 2023 levels and replacement supply has not caught up. Project Blue estimates China still accounted for about **98.9%** of primary gallium and **68.6%** of germanium in **2025**. S&P Global expects ex-China gallium capacity of only about **20** tons by the end of **2026**, a gap of about **678** tons, and non-Chinese germanium metal output of about **31** tons, about **177** tons short. Even if eight announced projects lift ex-China gallium supply to about **386** tons by **2030**, Reuters’ calculation on S&P data still leaves about **65%** of ex-China demand looking to China; ex-China germanium refining capacity of about **126** tons would cover only about **48%** of projected ex-China demand.

**Links:**

- [Kitco / Reuters — Niche metals test West’s resilience to Chinese export curbs](http://www.kitco.com/news/off-the-wire/2026-09-18/niche-metals-test-wests-resilience-chinese-export-curbs)

**Commentary:** The project list is longer than it was three years ago, but the tonnage still does not close the gap—chip, infrared, and clean-energy risk remains a licensing and inventory problem, not a second source.

### 5. Austria’s Plansee expands non-Chinese tungsten supply as shipments to Japan are cut (tungsten)
**Summary:** The Japan Times on **September 19** carried a Bloomberg report that Plansee Group, based in Reutte, Austria, is building one of the more substantial tungsten operations outside China. Tungsten is used in carbide tools that cut auto parts, in heat-resistant semiconductor manufacturing, and—at nearly twice the density of lead—in armor-piercing ammunition. The report says that over the past two years China has restricted exports, drawn in more raw material, and most recently cut key shipments to Japan, sending buyers scrambling and costs higher. The visible text does not give an investment figure or added capacity for this expansion.

**Links:**

- [The Japan Times — How Austria can loosen China’s grip over a key critical mineral](https://www.japantimes.co.jp/business/2026/09/19/austria-china-key-critical-mineral/)
- [Bloomberg — Austria's Plansee is challenging China's grip on tungsten](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2026-09-18/austria-s-plansee-is-challenging-china-s-critical-minerals-grip-on-tungsten)

**Commentary:** Tungsten does not headline like rare earths, but cutting tools, chip heat parts, and defense share it—when shipments to Japan tighten, European processing is the testable alternative, not another mine announcement.

### 7. CATL licenses battery-pack technology to Egypt’s BME: 1 GWh at first, cells still bought in (batteries)
**Summary:** CATL said on **September 16** that it signed a cooperation agreement with Egyptian battery maker BME, a joint venture of commercial-vehicle maker MCV and auto-solutions firm Auto D that will fully fund the plant. BME plans to buy cells from CATL, while CATL licenses pack technology and provides equipment and training. Initial capacity is **1 GWh** a year for heavy commercial vehicles; the long-term plan is **5 GWh**, adding passenger-vehicle packs and solar and wind storage. Caixin reported on **September 18** that this is CATL’s first battery-pack technology-licensing project. The official text does not give investment or a production date.

**Links:**

### 8. Hansae Mobility expands in Michigan by more than $93.2 million, pulling Korean machining onshore (autos)
**Summary:** The State of Michigan announced on **September 15** that Hansae Mobility USA, the U.S. unit of the South Korean auto-parts maker, will build an about **400,000**-square-foot plant in Chesterfield Township, Macomb County, with a capital commitment of **$93,214,535** (more than **$93.2 million**), **102** new jobs, and retention of **171** existing jobs. **Links:**

- [Michigan Governor — Hansae Mobility to add 102 jobs in Chesterfield Township](https://www.michigan.gov/whitmer/news/press-releases/2026/09/15/whitmer-announces-102-jobs-coming-to-chesterfield-township-by-hansae-mobility)

**Commentary:** Here reshoring is not an automaker slogan but moving driveline machining out of Korean suppliers and into a U.S. plant—small in scale, clear in cutting a cross-border process step.
