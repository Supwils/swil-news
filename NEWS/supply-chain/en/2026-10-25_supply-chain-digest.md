## I. Capacity Siting and Local Manufacturing

### 1. Audi publishes plans for its first U.S. plant in Georgia, at about $4 billion (vehicles)
**Summary:** On October 25 Audi said it plans to build its first U.S. factory in Georgia, invest about $4 billion, and create approximately 4,500 jobs. The plant is designed for up to 200,000 vehicles a year. Construction is scheduled to begin in 2027, production in the early 2030s, at the Middle Georgia Mega Site. CNBC reported the same day that President Donald Trump announced the project on Saturday at a rally in Georgia. The company told CNBC the plant will build the Q7, Q8, and Q9, and that roughly half of output will be exported outside the United States. Chief executive Gernot Döllner said at a Sunday roundtable that local production is meant to grow U.S. sales and reduce exposure to import tariffs. CNBC said the tariff on vehicles from Germany is now 15 percent, down from 25 percent, and that U.S. tariffs cost Audi 1.2 billion euros ($1.4 billion) last year because every vehicle it sells in the United States is imported from Europe or Mexico.

- [CNBC — Audi defends plans for a $4 billion Georgia plant amid VW restructuring, tariffs](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/25/audi-georgia-us-plant-tariffs-volkswagen.html)

### 2. Chery considers manufacturing in Canada, with no site or investment figure (vehicles)
**Summary:** Bloomberg, via Supply Chain Brain on October 25, reported that Chery Automobile is considering local manufacturing in Canada, building on a sales launch planned for next year. Executive vice president Guibing Zhang told an interview at headquarters in Wuhu that Canada is worth a factory even without the U.S. market. Options under discussion include taking idle capacity at existing plants and working with partners. He did not name the partners. The article said that after an arrangement with Beijing, Canada allows annual low-tariff imports of 49,000 Chinese new-energy vehicles, with the quota rising each year. Industry Minister Melanie Joly has previously required a locally sourced supply chain, Canadian labor standards, and software secure enough to address national-security concerns, and has ruled out knockdown kits that would be built in China and only assembled in Canada. Chery has a joint-venture plant in Spain, has taken over a Nissan factory in South Africa, and is exploring production at Nissan's Sunderland plant in the United Kingdom.

### 3. ADNOC puts more than AED 60 billion of local manufacturing opportunities on the table (equipment)
**Summary:** The Emirates News Agency reported on October 25 that ADNOC, at the second Make it with ADNOC Forum, unveiled more than AED 60 billion in local manufacturing opportunities through 2030. The package covers more than 200 essential products, many of them currently imported, plus a portfolio of critical spare parts. The National the same day converted that package to $16.3 billion. The agency said a redesigned supplier registration and prequalification process targets up to an 80 percent cut in the time needed to become ready to compete, halves the number of required documents, and removes prequalification for 60 percent of service categories. The Local+ list is expanding to more than 100 UAE manufacturers, from 70 at its launch in May 2026. From the program's start in 2018 through the end of 2025, ADNOC said it had redirected more than AED 307 billion into the UAE economy and supported localization of more than 130 products and 95 UAE factories.

## II. Chips, Solar Technology, and Factory Equipment

### 4. Commerce signs letters of intent for up to $389 million on power chips and quartz (chips)
**Summary:** On October 22 the U.S. Department of Commerce said it had signed letters of intent to provide up to $389 million in CHIPS and Science Act research awards for power semiconductors and materials used in the grid, data centers, and fusion. Helion would receive up to $175 million, Commonwealth Fusion Systems up to $85 million, Vertical Semiconductor up to $73 million, and Sibelco up to $56 million. Reuters said Helion's award would be accompanied by $50 million in new private investment, and that Sibelco would use the funding for next-generation leaching technologies and a demonstration facility in North Carolina. Commerce said final awards are conditioned on a minority, non-controlling, non-voting equity stake in each company. The demonstration target for Vertical, in the department's release, is what it called the world's first 2.5 MW super-intelligence data-center rack.

**Links:**

- [NIST — Commerce announces letters of intent for $389 million of R&D awards](https://www.nist.gov/news-events/news/2026/10/department-commerce-announces-letters-intent-4-companies-389-million-rd)

**Commentary:** The $389 million is the combined ceiling of four letters of intent, not money already disbursed. The 2.5 MW figure is a demonstration target.

### 5. The FCC gives Intel a one-year waiver for in-plant automated guided vehicles (equipment)
**Summary:** Reuters reported on October 23 that the Federal Communications Commission on Thursday granted Intel a one-year waiver so the company can keep making software, firmware, and hardware changes to certain automated guided vehicles in its U.S. chip plants that could be classified as advanced robots. The stated purpose is to avoid disruptions in domestic semiconductor manufacturing services for Intel's customers. Intel told the commission there is a lack of proven U.S. alternatives that meet its operating requirements, and that replacing the vehicles would take years and could disrupt production. The vehicles move materials inside the plants and have been in operation for more than two decades, the company said. An FCC official said the waiver allows minor changes to existing models and does not involve swapping U.S.-made components for foreign-made ones. Reuters also wrote that in July the commission barred imports of new Chinese robots and power inverters.

**Links:**

- [Reuters via Investing.com — FCC grants Intel waiver to use existing foreign-made robotic devices](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/fcc-grants-intel-waiver-for-foreignmade-robotic-devices-4967773)

**Commentary:** The one-year waiver covers the existing vehicles named in the petition and the changes described there. It is not a general import license for foreign robots.

### 6. China's solar-technology catalogue is still out for comment as Indian module makers worry about upstream tools (solar)
**Summary:** On October 16 China's Ministry of Commerce said it and the Ministry of Science and Technology had issued Announcement No. 48 of 2026, seeking public comment on revisions to the catalogue of technologies prohibited or restricted from export, and applying interim pre-export registration until a formal revision takes effect. The draft would add 40 technologies across artificial intelligence, biomedicine, and photovoltaics: 14 proposed for prohibition and 26 for restriction. The photovoltaic portion is 12 technologies in polysilicon, wafer, and cell manufacturing. The spokesperson's examples included perovskite and tandem-cell module production technology, proposed for prohibition, and polysilicon engineering technology with single-line capacity of at least 30,000 tonnes a year, proposed for restriction. The spokesperson said products made with the controlled technologies are outside the scope of the controls. On October 25 The Hindu BusinessLine quoted Solex Energy chairman Chetan Shah as saying that, as of June 2026, India had about 262 GW of module capacity and about 37 GW of cell capacity, with ingot-wafer capacity estimated at 2 GW and polysilicon at nil. He estimated that China controls 75 to 90 percent of the global solar supply chain, and asked that the ALMM-II timeline be extended by at least 12 months.

**Links:**

## III. Logistics and Energy-Project Supply Chains

### 7. ADNOC lines up 2027 tenders for sour gas and pipelines (energy projects)
**Summary:** The National reported on October 25 that ADNOC plans to tender offshore sour-gas developments, an export gas pipeline, and enhanced-oil-recovery projects in 2027. Adel Al Yafei, senior vice president for group commercial operations and execution, told suppliers on Sunday that the company has awarded 10 major engineering, procurement, and construction projects so far this year, plus more than 8,000 requisitions, contracts, and purchase requests involving more than 1,500 suppliers. ADNOC is seeking to raise oil production capacity to 5 million barrels a day by 2027, from 4.85 million. For 2028 it outlined oilfields, gas storage, and infrastructure without disclosing values. The newspaper said the UAE is expanding Habshan-Fujairah crude-pipeline infrastructure to reduce reliance on the Strait of Hormuz. The same article said ADNOC aims to cut tender cycle times by 40 percent, and that from November 1 bids will be weighted 70 percent to commercial terms and 30 percent to in-country value.

### 9. MSC adds a $200-per-container surcharge from the East Mediterranean to North Europe on November 1 (shipping)
**Summary:** In a customer advisory dated October 22, MSC said it will introduce an Emergency Operations Surcharge on cargo from Greece, Türkiye, the Black Sea, and non-Italian Adriatic ports to Northwest Continental Europe, the United Kingdom, and the ScanBaltic region. The charge takes effect on November 1, 2026, by bill-of-lading date, until further notice. It is $200, 200 euros, or 200 pounds per container, depending on the trade and payment arrangements. MSC said the surcharge reflects extra operating costs in the present market and is meant to support a direct service to Northern Europe. At the same time, the main-service rotation becomes Le Havre, Antwerp, Felixstowe, Vigo, Sines, Piraeus, Gemlik, Tekirdag, Aliaga, then Sines and Le Havre.

**Links:**

**Commentary:** The $200 charge starts on November 1 by bill-of-lading date. The advisory does not give a volume of containers affected.
