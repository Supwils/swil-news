## I. Chips & Critical Materials

### 1. Panasonic Hsinchu Hukou fire: ~4% of global high-end CCL supply at risk; Taiwan makers watch order shifts (Laminates)
**Summary:** TrendForce reported on Nov 5, citing Economic Daily News and Commercial Times, that an explosion and fire at Panasonic Industrial Devices Materials Taiwan’s (PIDMTW) Hukou plant in Hsinchu early on Nov 4 has raised alarms for ultra-low-loss copper-clad laminates (CCLs) used in AI servers. Supply-chain sources put Panasonic’s MEGTRON series at roughly 20% of the global high-end CCL market, with Hukou about 20% of that series’ worldwide capacity—about 4% of global high-end supply; the site mainly makes M6 while developing M7/M8. CNA reported five injured and two initially missing. Analysts say cleanroom and equipment precision could push recovery—repairs, tool replacement, and customer requalification—out for months. With NVIDIA and U.S. CSPs already stretching CCL lead times, Elite Material, TUC, ITEQ, and Ventec are seen as possible alternate sources, though their own capacity is tight.

### 2. Nikkei: China’s tungsten export curbs lift AI PCB micro-drill prices, squeeze carbide bit supply (Critical metals)
**Summary:** Nikkei Asia reported on Nov 5 that China’s export controls on critical metals are disrupting tungsten-carbide micro drill bits used to drill PCBs and chip substrates for AI, sending tool prices higher. Secondary coverage notes licenses have been required since February 2025 for ammonium paratungstate, tungsten oxides, and tungsten carbide; after tighter rules early in 2026, Japan’s tungsten imports from China in April were roughly half the 2025 monthly average. AI-server boards often rise from about 12–16 layers to about 24–40, and harder substrates cut bit life from roughly 1,000 holes to about 200–300; standard bits in China were about 1.20 yuan in May versus about 0.95 a year earlier, with some premium models up more than about 35%. MetalMiner put Rotterdam APT near $278/kg in October versus about $74/kg ex-works in China. USGS figures cited elsewhere put China’s 2025 tungsten output at about 79% of world production.

These are secondary summaries—buyers should verify against primary supplier guidance.

### 6. Gartner: Energy availability is becoming a structural constraint on supply-chain growth; laggards may face 20%–30% higher costs (Energy)
**Summary:** In a Nov 5 press release, Gartner said rising electricity demand plus grid capacity and reliability challenges mean energy availability is increasingly constraining supply-chain growth—raising operating costs, delaying expansions, and slowing electrification. It predicts that by 2029 most manufacturers without advanced energy-management capabilities will face about 20%–30% higher costs from price and supply volatility, network congestion, and capacity constraints. Analyst Marta Muñoz said the assumption that energy is always available is getting harder to sustain, affecting site choice, expansion, and automation/electrification plans; CSCOs should treat energy as core to network design and resilience.

### 7. S&P Global: October world factory output still strong, but shortages push selling-price inflation toward a four-year high range (Manufacturing pulse)
**Summary:** S&P Global’s Nov 5 Daily Update, citing PMI surveys, said worldwide manufacturing production in October continued to expand at one of the strongest rates in five years, yet supply-chain bottlenecks and capacity constraints remained widespread. ## III. Policy, Critical Minerals & Logistics/Trade

### 8. UK parliamentary inquiry: “Dangerous” China reliance on critical minerals; calls for a finance facility to diversify (Policy)
**Summary:** The Guardian reported on Nov 5 that the UK House of Commons business select committee found Britain “dangerously” dependent on China for critical raw materials needed for defense, renewables, and AI, urging a centralized policy and a “critical minerals finance facility” using grants, equity, and debt to fund mines and diversification. Chair Liam Byrne said Britain cannot rearm, build AI, or deliver net zero without securing those minerals. Hearings highlighted processing bottlenecks, with China dominant in refining seven mineral categories; lithium demand could rise about 45-fold by 2030 and rare earths for permanent magnets about 16-fold. A U.S.–China pause on broader rare-earth export curbs extended into January 2027 leaves the UK exposed to bilateral diplomacy. Government officials said about £400m is already aimed at domestic production and recycling, with 2035 goals of meeting about 10% of demand domestically, about 20% via recycling, and no more than about 60% of any critical mineral from a single country.

**Links:**

- [The Guardian — UK found to be ‘dangerously’ reliant on China for critical minerals](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/nov/05/uk-dangerously-reliant-on-china-critical-minerals-military-ai-renewable-energy)
