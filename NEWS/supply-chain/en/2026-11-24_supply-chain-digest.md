## I. Chips & Critical Materials

### 1. Analog IC Price Pressure Extends into 2027: ST Eyes New Hikes, 80% of NXP Portfolio Past 16-Week Leads (Chips)
**Summary:** TrendForce reported on Nov 24 that analog IC pricing pressure is expected to persist into 2027 as AI and industrial demand recover while consumer electronics remain weak, with longer lead times and fiercer competition for foundry and OSAT capacity. STMicroelectronics is reportedly preparing another round of selected-product price increases from Jan 1, 2027, after three adjustment notices in 2026, citing higher raw-material, logistics, labor and manufacturing costs. Microchip’s CEO expects major foundries and packaging/test providers to raise prices again in January 2027; the company plans to absorb the incremental cost for now after an August hike. NXP said book-to-bill remains well above one across end markets and regions, with backlog stretching further into 2027 and about 80% of products now beyond 16-week lead times, versus roughly 25% a year earlier. Executives warn that capacity is being diverted toward higher-margin AI products, leaving mature processes used in auto and industrial undersupplied.

### 2. China Extends Suspension of Gallium, Germanium and Antimony Curbs on U.S. Shipments to Jan 27, 2027 (Critical Materials)
**Summary:** China’s Commerce Ministry announced on Nov 24 that it will extend the suspension of tighter U.S.-bound dual-use controls under Announcement No. 46 of 2024 through Jan 27, 2027, with the new suspension window running from Nov 28; the prior pause had been due to expire Nov 27. The measures generally restrict export licences for dual-use items related to gallium, germanium, antimony and superhard materials bound for the United States, and impose stricter end-user and end-use reviews on certain graphite dual-use shipments. Licensing requirements remain in force, and exports of controlled dual-use goods to U.S. military users or for military use stay banned. The United States relies almost entirely on imports for gallium, with integrated circuits accounting for about 73% of U.S. consumption; separate rare-earth control suspensions follow different calendars.

**Links:**

- [China Daily — China extends US dual-use export control suspension to 2027](https://www.chinadaily.com.cn/a/202611/24/WS6b0569ade4b03ebf8519e6c2.html)

**Commentary:** Critical-mineral export policy remains a tunable lever—two more months of relief, but licences and the military ban mean the chain is paused, not unlocked.

## II. Batteries & Critical Materials

### 3. DRC Suspends Cobalt Exports from Nov 30; Chinese Cobalt Futures Hit Three-Month High (Batteries)
**Summary:** Reuters/Mining Weekly reported on Nov 24 that the Democratic Republic of Congo’s strategic minerals regulator ARECOMS will suspend all cobalt exports from Nov 30 until further notice, covering industrial and artisanal output and including previously authorized shipments that have not left by the deadline. The authority said the step responds to sustained price weakness since mid-year, rising inventories and speculative trading, and plans to review the measure after about four months while adjusting quotas. Cobalt contracts for November, December and May on the Wuxi Stainless Steel Exchange hit daily limits; the December contract jumped about 10.97% to 316 yuan ($47.42) per kilogram, the highest since Aug 18. DRC Ministry of Mines data show about 61,441 metric tons of contained cobalt exported in January–September 2026.

### 4. Sila Signs Multi-Year Silicon-Carbon Anode Offtake with Panasonic Energy, Potential Sales Above $1 Billion (Batteries)
**Summary:** Sila announced on Nov 18 a multi-year commercial offtake agreement with Panasonic Energy Corporation of North America with potential sales exceeding $1 billion over time. Sila will supply Titan Silicon Si/C anode material from its Moses Lake, Washington, plant to Panasonic’s U.S. cell sites. The deal follows a roughly $1.4 billion loan commitment from the U.S. Department of Defense’s Office of Strategic Capital and is a key commercial milestone tied to financing readiness, with about 500 additional manufacturing and construction jobs expected at Moses Lake as volumes scale. MIT Technology Review reported on Nov 19 that the agreement could help Sila expand production at least tenfold versus current levels; Panasonic has committed to tens of thousands of tons, though total volume and price were not disclosed.

## III. Capacity & Migration

### 5. GlobalFoundries Breaks Ground on $10 Billion New York Fab; First Phase of U.S.–Japan Push Targets 2029 (Capacity)
**Summary:** GlobalFoundries broke ground on Nov 13 on a new 300mm fab in Malta, Saratoga County, New York—the first facility under a U.S.–Japan semiconductor initiative, with an initial investment of about $10 billion backed by roughly $570 million in New York incentives and expected online in 2029. The broader two-fab program could reach about $21 billion. When fully operational, the first fab is expected to add about 900,000 300mm wafers of annual U.S. capacity for foundational chips serving automotive, industrial and connected infrastructure. State officials framed the project as part of a roughly $12.3 billion multi-phase expansion at Luther Forest that would create more than 1,100 high-skilled manufacturing jobs.

### 6. “South-east Asia Plus One” Becomes the New Norm as Firms Diversify Inside the Region (Migration)
**Summary:** At the South-east Asia Growth Forum on Nov 18, Accenture’s Asia-Pacific supply-chain lead told The Business Times that the manufacturing narrative has shifted from “China plus one” to “South-east Asia plus one,” with extra sites often still inside the region amid geopolitics and tariffs. China’s share of U.S. imported manufactured goods has fallen from more than 20% to under 10% in four years, and about three in four U.S. manufacturers are moving supply bases out of China into the rest of Asia, with Southeast Asia the largest recipient. Maersk’s regional sales head said apparel is shifting south from Vietnam to Indonesia, while some solar makers hit by U.S. tariffs have moved from China to Malaysia, Indonesia and now the Philippines. Accenture warned that many firms diversify only on paper: two-thirds of electronics manufacturers in the region were hit by single-source disruption in the past 18 months, with global revenue lost to supply-chain disruption estimated at about $1.9 trillion a year.

**Links:**

- [The Business Times — ‘South-east Asia plus one’ becomes new norm as disruption redraws supply chains](https://www.businesstimes.com.sg/international/asean/bt-south-east-asia-growth-forum/south-east-asia-plus-one-becomes-new-norm-disruption-redraws-supply-chains)

**Commentary:** Migration has evolved from leaving China to redistributing inside ASEAN; without multi-sourcing of inputs, “plus one” merely relocates concentration risk.

### 8. BAE Opens Fully Automated 155mm Shell-Filling Plant at Glascoed, Targeting Roughly 16x Capacity (Capacity)
**Summary:** BAE Systems announced on Nov 20 the opening of a new explosive-filling facility at Glascoed, South Wales—described as the UK’s first fully automated large-calibre ammunition factory—as part of a £180 million investment aimed at raising 155mm shell capacity about sixteen-fold versus a 2023 baseline. The melt-cast line will be run by robotic systems and autonomous guided vehicles with limited staffing to support longer production hours; scaled output begins after live trials, without a disclosed ramp timetable or annual volume target. Shell bodies continue to be made at BAE’s Washington plant in northeast England and shipped to Glascoed for filling.

### 10. Trans-Pacific Spot Rates Still Near 4x Year Ago as Port Drayage and Inland Backlogs Persist (Logistics)
**Summary:** Supply Chain Management Review and FreightWaves, citing Xeneta, said Asia–U.S. East Coast spot rates averaged about $10,901 per FEU and West Coast about $8,203 for the week ending Nov 19—down roughly 1.9% and 1.7% week on week as capacity rose, yet still near four times year-ago levels. October U.S. container imports reached about 2.67 million TEU, but longer dwell, constrained appointments and inaccessible cargo limited drayage productivity at major gateways. FreightWaves also noted lingering West Coast rail/intermodal backlogs and a roughly 28% drop in average daily Red Sea container transits from a late-October peak, with Far East–Mediterranean trades most exposed. Elevated rates plus tariff uncertainty are pushing shippers to bake automation, inventory buffers and routing contingencies into 2027 plans.
