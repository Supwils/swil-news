## I. Chips & Critical Materials

### 1. Samsung breaks ground in Asan-Onyang: ₩46 trillion HBM advanced-packaging hub, start brought forward to June 2028 (HBM)
**Summary:** MoneyToday and the Korea JoongAng Daily reported on Nov 12 that Samsung Electronics held a Chungcheong mega-project groundbreaking at its Onyang campus in Asan, South Chungcheong Province, attended by President Lee Jae-myung and Samsung Electronics Executive Chairman Lee Jae-yong. Samsung plans to invest about ₩46 trillion at Onyang in sequence (roughly ₩6 trillion for plant construction and ₩40 trillion for advanced equipment), building five HBM lines and expanding the site from about 270,000 to about 420,000 square meters; the new three-story plant will have roughly 31,000 square meters of cleanroom, with about ₩1.3 trillion in construction spend for this phase. DS Division Vice Chairman Chun Young-hyun said the five lines are targeted to start in June 2028, about a year earlier than planned; Samsung has already settled 2027 HBM volumes but still faces customer requests for more, and expects shortages to last into 2028. Yole Group forecasts the high-performance advanced-packaging market to grow about 27% a year from 2025 to 2031 and exceed $51 billion by 2031.

### 2. Foxconn’s record quarter comes with a warning: chips, memory and key parts may stay tight into next year (EMS)
**Summary:** Reuters and others reported on Nov 12 that Hon Hai (Foxconn) posted third-quarter net profit of about NT$77.5 billion ($2.35 billion), up about 34% year on year and above an LSEG consensus near NT$69.6 billion; quarterly revenue was about NT$3.02 trillion, a record. Foxconn’s own release likewise flagged key-component shortages as an industry-wide challenge and raised the outlook for all four major product segments.

### 3. Nexperia China claims full-chain localisation: 12-inch platform covers all categories; some lead times cut to 4–6 weeks (Discretes)
**Summary:** The South China Morning Post reported on Nov 12 that Nexperia China CEO John Chang told the 23rd China International Semiconductor Expo in Beijing that the unit’s domestic 12-inch wafer platform now covers all of its product categories, working with Chinese foundries while using its own plants and local contractors for packaging and testing. “We have completed the localisation of the entire chain, from product definition and development to manufacturing,” Chang said. Delivery times for some components have fallen to four to six weeks from 12 to 16 weeks previously. The claim comes more than a year after the Dutch parent stopped direct shipments of European wafers to China, as the control and supply rift between the two sides deepens—framed as a milestone in building an independent China supply chain.

### 4. Yttrium’s Europe–China price gap hits ~125x: April licensing still bites; the “pause” does not cover today’s tightest rare earth (Rare earths)
**Summary:** OilPrice and Mining.com, citing Argus and Bloomberg, said yttrium oxide is priced at about $1.15 million a ton in Europe versus about $9,144 in China—a roughly 125-fold gap. China’s yttrium oxide exports are running near two-fifths of their 2023–2024 average, with almost none going to Japan; customs data show about 4,000 kg shipped to Japan in September versus about 51,000 kg a year earlier. Beijing extended the pause on its October 2025 expanded controls to Jan 10, 2027, but the April 2025 licensing regime on seven medium and heavy rare earths—including samarium, gadolinium, terbium, dysprosium, lutetium, scandium and yttrium—was never part of that pause. A Japanese industry ministry official described a stalled global export flow and a supply-demand crunch; Lynas is not due to start yttrium output in Malaysia until 2028, so non-Chinese relief remains distant.

## II. Critical Minerals & Batteries

### 5. Copper futures fall while China pays record premiums: enough metal globally, just not where buyers need it (Copper)
**Summary:** LME Insight reported on Nov 12 that LME three-month copper fell about 2.7% in the week to Nov 6—the steepest weekly drop since March—while Shanghai spot premiums over the SHFE November contract briefly hit a record 2,615 yuan a ton after roughly tripling in two weeks, and the Yangshan import premium reached a record $230 a ton by Nov 9. SHFE-monitored inventories fell more than 24% in a week to about 33,848 tons, the lowest in almost three years; U.S. warehouses held roughly 85% of global exchange copper stocks (COMEX plus U.S. LME), and more than 70% of LME copper stocks alone sat in American warehouses. China’s refined copper output was estimated down about 3.03% month on month in October, with cathode imports down about 16.34% year on year in the first nine months. By Nov 10 Shanghai spot premiums had eased to about 1,700 yuan a ton, though the Yangshan warrant premium remained at its record—underscoring that the issue is location and access, not aggregate tonnage.

### 6. BMW locks in Gen6 regional cells: Samsung SDI in Europe, LG Energy Solution in North America from the late 2020s (Batteries)
**Summary:** BMW Group announced around Nov 9–11 that it has added cell partners for its sixth-generation (Gen6) high-voltage batteries: Samsung SDI will supply from established European plants, and LG Energy Solution from established U.S. plants, with deliveries expected from the late 2020s; volumes, values and specific factories were not disclosed. Europe-side cell sourcing will then include CATL, EVE Energy and Samsung SDI, while LG Energy Solution joins the U.S. cell base. BMW stressed a “local for local” approach, with Gen6 pack assembly at five new plants—Irlbach-Straßkirchen (Germany), Debrecen (Hungary), Shenyang, Woodruff (U.S.) and San Luis Potosí (Mexico)—kept close to vehicle plants to shorten logistics and raise supply security.

## IV. Inventories, Logistics & Trade

### 8. GEP Volatility Index: North American and European manufacturers accelerate stockpiling; transport costs at a four-month high (Inventories)
**Summary:** The GEP Global Supply Chain Volatility Index with S&P Global, released Nov 11–12, showed manufacturers in North America and Europe sharply raising precautionary stockpiling in October even as global purchasing growth slowed. Drawn from a monthly survey of about 27,000 businesses, North American safety-stock indicators doubled versus September and were the strongest in five months, while European inventory buffers rose rapidly; Asia’s index climbed from 1.49 to 1.67, the greatest pressure in four months. Material shortages stayed above the long-run average for an eighth month, though they were the least pronounced since the Middle East conflict erupted in March; oil, electronic and electrical components, and metals remained widely short. Rising oil prices pushed the transportation cost index to a four-month high. GEP consulting VP John Piatek warned that extra buying supports output now but ties up working capital, and that purchasing could slow once buffers are built—creating a drag on manufacturing and the wider economy in 2027.

Employers’ group UNO called the EU logistics impact “limited and localised” but said Algeciras and Valencia were most exposed; APMT said terminals were operating normally again.
