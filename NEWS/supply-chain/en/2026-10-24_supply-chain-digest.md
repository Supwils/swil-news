## I. Chips and Advanced Packaging

### 1. NVIDIA locks in substrate, packaging, and test capacity, with a $1.1 billion test prepayment (chips)
**Summary:** On October 21 at GTC Berlin, NVIDIA published capacity arrangements with suppliers. In the release, Jensen Huang said demand is extraordinary and capacity is the constraint. Substrate agreements with IBIDEN, Samsung Electro-Mechanics, and Unimicron are meant, in aggregate, to raise related substrate capacity to 2.5 times the current level over the next three years. Amkor plans to increase interposer-wafer advanced-packaging capacity by more than 300 percent by the end of 2028. King Yuan Electronics will build new test facilities and equipment in the United States, backed by a $1.1 billion capacity-reservation advance from NVIDIA, with high-volume production targeted for April 2027. NVIDIA expects its total chip-test capacity to double from 2026 to 2027. SPIL, part of ASE, is targeting a 15-fold increase in optical-engine capacity for co-packaged optics by mid-2027, and aims to double that again by the end of 2028.

**Links:**

- [NVIDIA Newsroom — NVIDIA and supply chain partners expand capacity to accelerate AI infrastructure buildout](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-and-supply-chain-partners-expand-capacity-to-accelerate-ai-infrastructure-buildout)

**Commentary:** The 2.5-times and 15-times figures, and the $1.1 billion, come from the October 21 company release. They are agreement targets and a prepayment, not output already running.

### 2. Samsung and SK hynix upgrade NAND plants in China, with Xi'an aimed at 286 layers (chips)
**Summary:** Reuters reported on October 21 that Samsung Electronics and SK hynix are upgrading factories in China to make NAND with more than 200 layers, according to four people familiar with the plans, as they respond to AI demand and competition from Yangtze Memory Technologies. Samsung confirmed to Reuters that upgrades are underway in close consultation with Washington. SK hynix said it is installing equipment at its Dalian plant, also in consultation with the U.S. government. Neither company disclosed the production plans Reuters reported for the first time. One person said Samsung's internal project "Monami" is converting the Xi'an plant from about 200-layer output to 286-layer V9 NAND, with targets of 5,000 wafers a month in December this year and 40,000 wafers a month in December 2027. Samsung said the upgrade complies with U.S. export rules, is meant to meet demand from global customers including those in the United States, and does not expand capacity or add a new fab.

**Commentary:** The 5,000-wafer and 40,000-wafer monthly figures come from one person. Reuters wrote that it could not determine how far U.S. authorities have gone in licensing the equipment the upgrades need.

### 3. TSMC asks suppliers about rare-earth-free motors; the talks are still early (equipment)
**Summary:** Reuters reported on October 22 that TSMC is looking for alternatives to rare-earth parts in factory equipment, three people familiar with the matter said, so that Beijing's export controls do not disrupt plants that make advanced chips. Three industry executives briefed by the company said TSMC has asked suppliers about using rare-earth-free motors in factory-automation equipment. One of them said the push is driven by concern about supply disruptions from China. Rare-earth magnets are commonly used in motors that move parts inside that equipment. Reuters said the discussions remain at an early stage, and two of the people said there has not been a broad switch to rare-earth-free equipment. TSMC declined to comment.

**Links:**

- [Reuters via Yahoo Finance — TSMC seeks factory-equipment alternatives to thwart China's rare earths grip](https://ca.finance.yahoo.com/news/exclusive-tsmc-seeks-factory-equipment-090944535.html)

**Commentary:** What can be checked is a supplier inquiry and the direction toward rare-earth-free motors. The reports do not give a replacement share, a contract, or a count of tools already changed.

## II. Rare Earths and Critical Materials

### 4. ITRI plans a rare-earth demonstration plant aimed at 500 tonnes a year by the end of 2030 (rare earths)
**Summary:** CNA reported on October 24 that Industrial Technology Research Institute president Chang Pei-zen said ITRI plans a rare-earth demonstration plant that could meet about half of Taiwan's demand by the end of 2030. The project is still on the drawing board. The output target is 500 metric tons a year, construction is targeted for completion by the end of 2028, and process tuning would follow before the plant reaches planned output. ITRI intends to put the plant in an industrial park so it can later be transferred to a private company. Funding would need to be budgeted by the National Science and Technology Council, and commercial terms, including possible government price guarantees, are still under discussion. Chang said the existing experimental line produces about 5,000 kilograms a year, and the new plant's target is about one hundred times that.

**Links:**

- [Focus Taiwan — ITRI plans rare-earth plant to meet half of Taiwan's demand by 2030](https://focustaiwan.tw/business/202610240006)

**Commentary:** The 500 tonnes and the "half of demand" line are targets from the president's interview. The plant is still being planned, and the budget and any price guarantee are not set.

### 5. Malaysia clears a first export of unprocessed rare earths; the applicant and the volume are undisclosed (rare earths)
**Summary:** The Straits Times reported on October 22, citing Bloomberg, that Malaysia's Ministry of Natural Resources and Environmental Sustainability told Bloomberg the Department of Mineral and Geoscience had approved one export of unprocessed rare earths for research and development, the first under a new framework. The ministry did not name the applicant or give shipment details. The framework, released earlier this month, conditionally allows unprocessed material to move for research and development or for processing, easing a moratorium in place since 2024. The ministry said the guidelines are open to eligible applicants, including foreign companies, and that the priority is the quality of investment, technology transfer, and long-term value rather than the number of approvals. A ministry guideline dated October 1 caps a single research-and-development export application at 100 metric tons. That cap is not the published weight of the shipment that was approved.

**Links:**

**Commentary:** What can be checked is that one research-and-development export was approved. The applicant, the material, and the tonnage were not published. The 100 metric tons is the ceiling written into the guideline for a research application.

### 6. GM cancels a long-term U.S. rare-earth magnet contract, and the two sides are in arbitration (magnets)
**Summary:** Reuters reported on October 22 that General Motors cancelled a long-term supply agreement with a U.S. factory making rare-earth permanent magnets, and that the two sides are in arbitration, according to regulatory filings and a person familiar with the matter. Germany's Vacuumschmelze signed a deal with GM in 2023 to supply permanent magnets for electric vehicles from a new plant in South Carolina. Energy Fuels agreed in June to buy VAC for $1.9 billion. In a Securities and Exchange Commission filing last week, Energy Fuels said a customer of the plant terminated its supply agreement on July 1 and that VAC began arbitration several days later. The filing did not name the customer. GM said in an SEC filing this week that a supplier of a component for EV traction motors is seeking damages after GM terminated a strategic sourcing agreement. The person familiar with the matter confirmed that the cancelled contract was with VAC's U.S. unit, e-VAC Magnetics. Both companies declined to comment on the arbitration, citing confidentiality.

**Links:**

- [Reuters via Investing.com — GM cancels deal with new US rare-earth magnet plant, documents say](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/exclusivegm-cancels-deal-with-new-us-rareearth-magnet-plant-documents-say-4967695)

**Commentary:** The termination date is July 1; Reuters reported it this week. Capacity of just under 2,000 tonnes a year, with about 90 percent planned for GM, is what VAC's chief executive told Reuters last September, not a new figure in the arbitration filing.

### 7. Thailand's Board of Investment says advanced-materials filings total about $690 million (materials)
**Summary:** Thailand's Board of Investment said on October 24, through Access Newswire, that makers of capacitors, magnets, battery materials, and related inputs have filed 35 advanced-materials projects worth about $690 million (23.5 billion baht) since 2023, through the first half of 2026. The dollar figure uses the Bank of Thailand's average selling rate of about 33.76 baht per dollar. The release also says one high-end server can use up to 30,000 multilayer ceramic capacitors, and that Murata Electronics holds more than 35 percent of the global market for that part.

## III. Production Stoppages, Tariffs, and Logistics

### 8. Boeing's 777 freighter line has been down for nearly two months over a sole-source part (aerospace)
**Summary:** Reuters reported on October 23 that Boeing's dispute with Florida supplier Vaero has halted production of the 777 freighter for nearly two months and disrupted the 767, including freighters and the U.S. military's KC-46 tanker. Boeing sued in August, alleging that Vaero stopped delivering trailing-edge rib kits and stalled work at the Everett, Washington, wing facility. The complaint called the part indispensable, named Vaero as the sole supplier, and said the interruption was costing as much as $6.5 million a day. The companies settled in late September, but three Boeing workers said 777 freighter production has not resumed. One of them said some parts have since arrived for the 767 line. Two people said Boeing is trying to bring the rib kit in-house. Boeing declined to comment. Vaero did not respond to requests for comment.

**Links:**

- [Reuters via Investing.com — Boeing supplier dispute has halted 777 freighter production for two months](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/exclusiveboeing-supplier-dispute-has-halted-777-freighter-production-for-two-months-sources-say-4970513)
- [Reuters via U.S. News — Boeing supplier dispute has halted 777 freighter production for two months](https://money.usnews.com/investing/news/articles/2026-10-23/exclusive-boeing-supplier-dispute-has-halted-777-freighter-production-for-two-months-sources-say)

**Commentary:** The $6.5 million a day is what the August complaint said, not a loss the company published on the day of the report. The 21 Boeing 777 freighters delivered so far this year are the company's published delivery count. No restart date was given.

### 9. The White House retunes the 50 percent extra duty list on Canada, effective October 30 (tariffs)
**Summary:** The White House published a fact sheet on October 23 saying President Trump signed a proclamation under Section 338 of the Tariff Act of 1930, further adjusting duties on certain Canadian products that were announced on July 20 and modified on September 8. Plywood sheets, some paper products, certain machinery and refrigeration equipment, and certain packing crates and boxes come off the 50 percent additional-duty list. Products going on include certain valves, carpets, waste products, and others. The duties apply whether or not a good originates under the U.S.-Mexico-Canada Agreement, and they stack on top of Section 232 tariffs under the Trade Expansion Act of 1962. The additions and removals take effect on October 30, 2026.

**Links:**

- [The White House — Fact sheet on further modifying Canadian products subject to Section 338 tariffs](https://www.whitehouse.gov/fact-sheets/2026/10/fact-sheet-president-donald-j-trump-further-modifies-canadian-products-subject-to-tariffs-under-section-338/)

**Commentary:** What can be checked is the list change and the October 30 effective date. The rate remains the 50 percent set in the earlier proclamation. This fact sheet does not give the trade value of the goods moved on or off the list.

### 10. USTR proposes extending the pause on China-linked ship port fees through January 9, 2027 (logistics)
**Summary:** gCaptain reported on October 23 that the Office of the U.S. Trade Representative has proposed extending the suspension of port fees on China-linked vessels by another two months, through January 9, 2027. The current pause runs through November 9. Under the proposed text, the measures would stay suspended from 12:00 a.m. Eastern Time on November 10, 2026, through 11:59 p.m. Eastern Time on January 9, 2027. During that window, fees under Annexes I, II, and III of the original Section 301 action would not accrue, nor would the related duties on ship-to-shore cranes and cargo-handling equipment. Comments are due by 5:00 p.m. Eastern Time on October 30, under docket USTR-2026-0694. gCaptain said the extension is not final, and the notice does not say whether China will extend its own fees on U.S.-linked ships.

**Links:**

The target is 500 advanced intelligent factories by 2028, and robot density of 600 robots per 10,000 employees.
