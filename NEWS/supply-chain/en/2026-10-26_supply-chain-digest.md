## I. Tariffs and Maritime Policy

### 1. The White House revises the 50 percent Section 338 tariff list on Canadian goods, effective October 30 (tariffs)
**Summary:** A White House fact sheet dated October 23 said the president signed a proclamation under Section 338 of the Tariff Act of 1930 that again changes the scope of additional duties on certain Canadian products, a measure first announced on July 20 and modified on September 8. Products removed include plywood sheets, some paper products, certain machinery and refrigeration equipment, and certain packing crates and boxes; products added include certain valves, carpets, waste products, and others. The duties apply whether or not a good originates under the United States-Mexico-Canada Agreement, they stack on Section 232 duties, and the additions and removals take effect on October 30, 2026. The fact sheet's figure of about $20 billion describes retaliatory tariffs Canada imposed this summer on U.S. exports.

### 2. USTR proposes extending the China maritime Section 301 suspension through January 9, 2027 (shipping policy)
**Summary:** The Federal Register for October 26 carried a U.S. Trade Representative notice proposing, so talks with China can continue, to suspend the Section 301 maritime, logistics, and shipbuilding action for two further months, through January 9, 2027. The current suspension runs from 12:01 a.m. Eastern Time on November 10, 2025, through 11:59 p.m. Eastern Time on November 9, 2026; the proposed window would run from 12:00 a.m. Eastern Time on November 10, 2026, through 11:59 p.m. Eastern Time on January 9, 2027. During that window, parties would not accrue the maritime-transport fees in Annexes I, II, and III of the April 23, 2025 notice, as modified on October 16, 2025, or the duties in Annex V.A. Comments are due by 5:00 p.m. Eastern Time on October 30 under docket USTR-2026-0694, and the notice is a request for comment.

**Links:**

- [Federal Register via GovInfo — Request for Comments on Further Suspension of Section 301 Action](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-10-26/html/2026-21755.htm)

## II. Batteries, Critical Components, and Factories

### 3. Intertek CEA sees U.S. storage DC-block prices falling 15 to 30 percent by 2030 (batteries)
**Summary:** pv magazine USA on October 26 cited Intertek CEA's third-quarter reports: U.S. energy-storage DC-block prices are projected to fall about 15 to 30 percent by 2030, while import markets could see declines of more than 40 percent. Lithium-ion shipments among major suppliers tracked in its programs were about 230 GWh in the second quarter of 2026, up 18 percent from the previous quarter, which the article ties largely to stocking ahead of China's elimination of the remaining battery VAT rebate on January 1, 2027; CATL accounted for more than 25 percent among the profiled companies, and the top 10 vendors, all based in China, held a combined share above 65 percent. The report says about 11 percent of announced 2026 U.S. capacity is at risk of slipping into 2027, citing LG Energy Solution adding 33 GWh across three factories and Samsung SDI planning an LFP line in Indiana; Korean cathode makers have announced about 23 GWh of LFP cathode capacity for 2026, which could meet nearly 40 percent of U.S. domestic demand if fully realized. Lithium prices fell about 15 percent on the quarter to near $20 per kilogram, and from January through July U.S. imports of rack-level lithium-ion backup batteries were 5,529 metric tons, up 131 percent year over year, while centralized UPS imports were 10,723 metric tons, up 77 percent.
