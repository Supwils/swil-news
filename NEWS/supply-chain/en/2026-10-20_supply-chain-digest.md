## I. Chips and Critical Materials

### 1. Japan's yttrium stocks could run out within months, and Tokyo asks Washington to press China (rare earths)
**Summary:** The Financial Times reported that Japanese officials and trading-company executives warn yttrium used in cars, jet engines, and chipmaking could run short before year-end, on a timeline of months rather than years. A Japanese government official said stockpiles are falling and efforts to use less rare earth have a limit. Yahoo Finance carried the report on October 20: Tokyo has appealed to the U.S. government and major American companies. Argus assessments put yttrium oxide at about $1,150 per kilogram outside China, roughly 179 times the price two years ago, against about $9 per kilogram inside China. A senior U.S. official told the Financial Times last month that Beijing was meeting roughly two-thirds of its rare-earth commitments to Washington while allowing Japan only enough for minimum needs. Washington has also told Beijing it will not export jet engines for the Comac C919 because yttrium shortages have constrained turbine production.

**Commentary:** "Within months" and the $1,150 versus $9 prices come from officials quoted in the Financial Times and from Argus assessments, not from an inventory table published that day by Japan's industry ministry.

### 4. Unimicron says some equipment lead times have stretched to 30 months, with a possible 20 percent materials gap in 2027 (substrates)
**Summary:** TrendForce reported on October 20, citing Wealth Magazine and the Economic Daily News, that Unimicron chairman S.C. Chien told the Taiwan Printed Circuit Association Show that day that lead times for some key equipment have stretched to 30 months and that critical materials could face a 20 percent supply shortfall in 2027. Low-CTE glass-fiber cloth, copper-clad laminates, and ABF are especially tight. Lead times have lengthened for more than 80 percent of equipment, and more than 20 percent of those items are delayed by more than double their original lead times. He projected that by 2028, CoWoS capacity would be 6.8 times the 2024 level and hyperscaler capital spending 6.3 times, while IC-substrate capacity would only about double and DRAM capacity would rise about 1.8 times. He added that those multiples do not by themselves mean a shortage, because the starting bases differ.

**Links:**

- [TrendForce — Unimicron Warns of IC Substrate Crunch: Up to 30-Month Equipment Lead Times, 20% Material Gap in 2027](https://www.trendforce.com/news/2026/10/20/news-unimicron-warns-of-ic-substrate-crunch-up-to-30-month-equipment-lead-times-20-material-gap-in-2027/)

**Commentary:** The 30-month lead time and the 20 percent gap are the chairman's assessment at a trade show, as carried by Wealth Magazine, not a formal shortage notice Unimicron issued that day.

## II. Capacity and Manufacturing Footprint

### 5. Apple commits up to $100 million over three years for U.S. titanium (materials)
**Summary:** On October 20, Apple said it will commit up to $100 million over the next three years to Perryman Company in Houston, Pennsylvania, for grade 5 titanium with at least 80 percent recycled content, including the enclosure of its first foldable phone, the iPhone Duo. Apple is also expanding work with Delaware-based Qnity Electronics, which will make films in the United States for the variable-aperture camera on the iPhone 18 Pro. Apple said nearly 100 components in the two phones come from American companies in more than a dozen states. TSMC's Arizona fab will produce more than 100 million chips for Apple in 2026, and Corning's Kentucky plant will make all cover glass for iPhones and Apple Watches sold worldwide by the end of the year. The arrangements sit inside the American Manufacturing Program launched last year, part of a $600 billion, four-year commitment.

**Links:**

- [Apple — Apple announces new American Manufacturing Program partners for iPhone Duo and iPhone 18 Pro](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2026/10/apple-announces-american-manufacturing-program-partners-for-latest-iphone-lineup/)
- [MarketScreener (Reuters) — Apple commits up to $100 million for US titanium in domestic manufacturing push](https://www.marketscreener.com/news/apple-commits-up-to-100-million-for-us-titanium-in-domestic-manufacturing-push-ce785cd8d88ff32c)

**Commentary:** The $100 million is a three-year purchasing ceiling. The titanium is melted, forged, and rolled in Houston, Pennsylvania. It is not a claim that the whole phone is assembled in the United States.

**Summary:** Reuters reported on October 20 that Cleveland-Cliffs posted its first quarterly profit in more than two years on Monday and raised its 2027 outlook. ### 7. China's industry ministry is drafting a next-generation smart-manufacturing policy, with more than 13,000 advanced factories already built (manufacturing)
**Summary:** Xinhua reported on October 20 that Wang Weiming, chief engineer of the Ministry of Industry and Information Technology, told a State Council Information Office briefing that day that China is drafting policies for next-generation smart manufacturing. The aim is to run artificial intelligence through the whole manufacturing chain and to upgrade equipment in tiers, from basic and advanced plants to leading factories. Support would cover smarter machine tools, robots, inspection and control equipment, industry-specific models, and industrial AI agents. Abroad, China would use mechanisms such as BRICS to help partners build smart factories. He said China has already built more than 13,000 advanced-level smart factories and more than 1,000 application scenarios across 20 industries, including steel, automobiles, and pharmaceuticals. By the end of September, smart computing power had reached 2,592 EFLOPS, up 146 percent from a year earlier.

**Links:**

**Commentary:** The 13,000 factories and 2,592 EFLOPS describe capacity already in place, as stated at the briefing. The next policy is still being drafted, and no funding figure was announced that day.

## III. Batteries and Ocean Freight

### 8. Chinese storage batteries pull back from the U.S. market, while Korean makers still trail in first-half share (batteries)
**Summary:** Aju Press reported on October 20 that Chinese energy-storage suppliers have largely withdrawn from U.S. operations this year as regulation, tariffs, and ownership scrutiny tightened, leaving more room for LG Energy Solution and Samsung SDI. SNE Research figures show that of batteries shipped to the North American storage market in the first half, CATL accounted for 38.8 percent and Hithium 17.2 percent, against 13.6 percent for LG Energy Solution and 6.1 percent for Samsung SDI. The Section 301 tariff on Chinese lithium-ion batteries for non-vehicle use rose in January from 7.5 percent to 25 percent. Executive Order 14421, issued on August 26, gives the Energy Department authority to restrict transactions in foreign grid equipment judged to pose national-security risks. Implementing rules are due within 120 days, in late December, and the order does not automatically require installed Chinese batteries to be removed. Hana Securities said China's lithium-battery exports rose 36 percent in the first three quarters, CATL's share of the EV-battery market outside China rose from 29 percent to 34 percent, and the combined overseas share of Korea's three makers fell from 38 percent to 27 percent.

**Links:**

- [Aju Press — Korean batteries charge ahead as China retreats from US storage](https://www.ajupress.com/view/20261020144845730)

**Commentary:** In first-half North American storage shipments, CATL and Hithium together still held about 56 percent. The opening Korean makers see is a gap created by U.S. rules, not a share they have already taken worldwide.

### 9. Gemini aims to put nearly all services back through Suez in the first quarter of 2027 (ocean freight)
**Summary:** FreightWaves reported on October 20 that Hapag-Lloyd chief executive Rolf Habben Jansen said the Gemini Cooperation of Maersk and Hapag-Lloyd expects to return nearly all of its services to Suez routings in the first quarter of 2027 if conditions do not change. The first sailing of a new China-to-western-Mediterranean loop is scheduled to leave Shanghai on November 22. Liner analyst Lars Jensen estimated that Suez routing across the market has returned to roughly 30 percent to 35 percent of normal. The Freightos Baltic Index put Asia-to-U.S. West Coast rates at $8,126 per FEU, with East Coast rates up 1 percent to $9,700. Asia-to-North Europe rates rose 6 percent last week to $3,442. Shorter voyages would release effective capacity in 2027, and excess supply is a risk if demand does not keep up. Security incidents in the Red Sea and the Strait of Hormuz are continuing.

**Links:**

- [FreightWaves — How new capacity is raising rates in chaotic ocean market](https://www.freightwaves.com/news/how-new-capacity-is-raising-rates-in-chaotic-ocean-market)

**Commentary:** The 30 percent to 35 percent figure is an analyst's estimate of how far the return has gone, and $8,126 is that week's spot rate. A return to Suez is not the same thing as loose capacity.
