## I. Chips and Storage Capacity

### 1. Tata's Jagiroad plant starts pilot chip production (chips)
**Summary:** India Today NE reported on Oct 3 that Assam Chief Minister Himanta Biswa Sarma said the first clean room at Tata Semiconductor Assembly and Test's facility in Jagiroad, Morigaon district, is ready and pilot chip production has begun. A second clean room is still under construction. He expects Prime Minister Narendra Modi to inaugurate the plant in December or January. The project involves about Rs 27,000 crore and is designed for up to 48 million chips a day, using wire bonding, flip chip, and integrated systems packaging for automotive, electric-vehicle, communications, and consumer-electronics customers. It was approved in February 2024 under the India Semiconductor Mission. The Union government has described it as India's first indigenous semiconductor assembly-and-test facility for domestic and global markets. Government estimates put combined direct and indirect employment potential at about 27,000 jobs. Sarma called it the world's largest semiconductor assembly-and-test facility.

### 2. King Yuan unit considers a $1.3 billion chip-test plant in Fort Worth (chips)
**Summary:** The Fort Worth Report wrote on the evening of Oct 2 that AeternZ Semiconductor Testing, a subsidiary of Taiwan's King Yuan Electronics, is considering a $1.3 billion, 23-acre plant in Fort Worth South Business Park. Tax-abatement documents call for at least $1 billion in real property and $300 million in equipment. The City Council is set to consider the abatement on Oct 27. The documents describe King Yuan as a supplier to Nvidia and other chipmakers, providing wafer probing, final test, and assembly. The report, citing Reuters, said the company announced a $1.4 billion U.S. investment plan in June. If Fort Worth is selected, the site would be the largest of several electronics plants already landing in the city. The same article notes that Wistron began work in July on two AI-supercomputer factories at AllianceTexas, investing $761 million and expecting more than 800 jobs, and that Foxlink opened its first North American plant in July with an expectation of 900 jobs.

### 3. Toshiba plans to roughly double Philippine hard-drive capacity by fiscal 2027 (storage)
**Summary:** Nikkei reported on Oct 2 that Toshiba plans to roughly double, by storage capacity, its production of hard-disk drives for AI data centers by fiscal 2027 from the fiscal 2025 level. The company will invest about 60 billion yen ($380 million) to expand its Philippine plants, its first major hard-drive investment in about five years. Toshiba, one of the three largest drive makers alongside Western Digital and Seagate, aims to lift its share by storage capacity from just over 10 percent to 30 percent over the medium term. The expansion includes lines for drives with up to about 40 percent more capacity per unit and supports mass production of 65-terabyte-class drives by 2030, with 100-terabyte-class products later. The report said data centers are deploying more high-capacity drives because they cost less than solid-state drives.

- [Yahoo Finance — Toshiba to double hard disk output capacity amid AI data centre demand](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/toshiba-double-hard-disk-output-023335775.html)

## II. Battery Manufacturing

### 4. American Lithium Energy opens an expanded battery plant in Vista (batteries)
**Summary:** The California Energy Commission said on Oct 2 that it attended the opening of American Lithium Energy's expanded factory in Vista. The site grew from 23,000 square feet to 82,000 square feet and makes lithium-ion batteries for aerospace and defense. Two grants totaling more than $13 million, from the Clean Transportation Program and the Electric Program Investment Charge, support expanded electric-vehicle cell production and a pilot line for high-capacity silicon-anode prismatic cells. Chief strategy officer William Hadala said the state money also helped raise another $40 million to $50 million from private and federal sources. The company employs 150 to 200 people in San Diego County. Its nano-silicon 18650 cells are already used in low-Earth-orbit satellites and hybrid aircraft.

**Links:**

- [California Energy Commission — California Energy Commission Celebrates American Lithium Energy Factory Opening](https://www.energy.ca.gov/news/2026-10/california-energy-commission-celebrates-american-lithium-energy-factory-opening)

**Commentary:** The expansion adds defense and aerospace cells, scaled from a pilot line, and is not a gigawatt-hour car-battery plant.

## III. Policy and Geopolitics

### 5. G20 splits over a statement on excess industrial capacity (policy)
**Summary:** CNBC reported on Oct 3 that the U.S. Trade Representative's office said on Friday a handful of G20 trade ministers rejected U.S. calls to curb excess industrial capacity and non-market policies, a result the U.S. presidency called severely disappointing. The United States chairs the G20 this year. A day after the Milwaukee trade meeting, only Mexico and Argentina had signed a U.S.-led statement on keeping goods made with forced labor out of supply chains. The administration has already imposed tariffs of 10 percent or 12.5 percent on goods from 59 countries and the European Union, citing weak enforcement of forced-labor bans. USTR is running a second Section 301 investigation into 16 partners that show signs of excess capacity, widely expected to lead to new duties in coming months. The statement did not name the objectors. China objected to similar language at a finance meeting in North Carolina about a month earlier. The G7 on Friday agreed to release some 100 million barrels of diesel reserves to try to bring down record U.S. diesel prices. Trade Representative Jamieson Greer said on Thursday he did not seek a joint statement on reforming most-favored-nation tariffs.

**Links:**

- [Nikkei Asia — Handful of G20 countries reject US stance on excess industrial capacity](https://asia.nikkei.com/politics/international-relations/handful-of-g20-countries-reject-us-stance-on-excess-industrial-capacity)

## IV. Logistics, Freight, and Plant Stoppages

### 6. Containers seized for an Islamabad blockade pile up at Karachi (logistics)
**Summary:** Dawn on Oct 3 carried a Reuters report that Pakistan's effort to keep supporters of jailed former prime minister Imran Khan out of the capital has left shipping containers piling up at Karachi, about 1,400 kilometers away. Truckers are refusing to drive north because they fear their vehicles will be seized and the containers used to barricade Islamabad. A march by Khan's PTI party, first set for Sept 27, is planned for Oct 4. Rana Mohammad Aslam, president of the Karachi Goods Carrier Association, said authorities began stopping vehicles around Sept 5. Police requisitioned more than 2,000 vehicles in September, many still loaded and held for days with little compensation. Routes to Islamabad, Rawalpindi, Jhelum, Peshawar, and Attock were affected, and some operators halted journeys beyond Gujranwala. Daily demurrage on one container can run to 30,000-40,000 rupees ($110 to $150). The statistics bureau said on Thursday that consumer prices rose 10.26 percent in September from a year earlier and the transport price index rose 27.43 percent.

### 7. JAL and Nippon Express start a weekly 747 freighter for semiconductor cargo (logistics)
**Summary:** Nippon Express Holdings said on Oct 1 that it and Japan Airlines will operate a Boeing 747 freighter on a Los Angeles-Narita-Taipei Taoyuan-Singapore-Narita-Los Angeles circuit. The first flight leaves Los Angeles on Oct 3, with one flight a week through 2026. Maximum payload is about 100 tons. The companies said demand for AI and semiconductor cargo is very strong among Tokyo, Taipei, and Singapore, while few airlines fly large freighters inside Asia. The service can carry oversized cargo such as server racks and data-center equipment. JAL cargo executive Yuichiro Kito said the partnership brings large-freighter service to the U.S. West Coast for the first time for JAL.

### 8. Toyota extends the shutdown of three Thai plants through Oct 10 (autos)
**Summary:** The Bangkok Post reported on Oct 2 that Toyota Motor Thailand said on Friday it would suspend production at three plants from Oct 5 to 10 because flooding has disrupted parts deliveries in Samut Prakan, Chachoengsao, and Chon Buri. The notice updates a Wednesday announcement tied to suppliers and to employees who could not get to work. The Samrong, Banpho, and Gateway stoppages can be shortened or lengthened depending on conditions and parts deliveries. Toyota said it is working with suppliers to restore deliveries, manage inventories, and adjust production and logistics plans, and that dealers will update customers if delivery dates change. A preliminary Federation of Thai Industries assessment expects damage from the past week's rain in the eastern and central regions to exceed 1 billion baht.
