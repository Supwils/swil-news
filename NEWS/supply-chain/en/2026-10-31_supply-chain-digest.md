## I. Chips and Critical Materials

### 1. Samsung and SK hynix: dense “sold out” HBM signals; memory tightness may run into 2028–2030 (memory)
**Summary:** At its October 29 earnings call, Samsung Electronics said AI infrastructure and personal AI-agent demand will keep memory supply tight through 2028, with 2027 HBM volumes already sold out at target prices and major customers still requesting more. Samsung said it has signed long-term agreements with 10 major customers and is negotiating with about 10 more, aiming for LTAs to cover 60%–70% of capacity by 2030. SK hynix said on October 28 that, combining secured LTAs with demand under active negotiation, DRAM and HBM volumes through 2029 are effectively sold out, with customer HBM requests more than double what it can supply, and shortages potentially lasting through 2030; LTAs with 14 major customers already lock in more than 50% of 2027–2028 capacity. An Aju Press roundup dated October 31 also noted TrendForce’s earlier view that 2027 HBM ASPs could more than double year over year.

- [Aju Press — Samsung and SK Hynix Declare Full Sales Amid AI-Driven Memory Shortages](https://www.ajupress.com/view/20261031060470702)

**Commentary:** AI is turning memory from a cyclical commodity into a multi-year hard constraint, concentrating allocation and pricing power in the hands of leading suppliers via LTAs.

### 2. TrendForce: AI inference tightens high-capacitance MLCC supply; shortages may last through 1H27 (passives)
**Summary:** TrendForce said on October 29 that the shift from AI training to inference and AI agents is lifting CPU, power-module, and DDR5 demand, which in turn sharply raises high-capacitance MLCC consumption on motherboards and memory modules. NVIDIA’s Vera Rubin platform is expected to ramp through 4Q26, and AMD has begun procuring specialized high-capacitance MLCCs for its MI450 platform; ODMs expect the high-end MLCC shortage to be most severe in 1Q27 and 2Q27. Consumer electronics remain soft, with major notebook ODMs expecting 4Q26 orders down about 10%–15% year over year. Murata has acknowledged that current capacity falls far short of demand and that even accelerated expansion is unlikely to add meaningful supply before 2028. TrendForce expects the high-end MLCC shortage to extend through the first half of 2027, with Japanese makers likely to follow Samsung Electro-Mechanics on price hikes in 1Q27 talks starting in November.

**Links:**

- [TrendForce — AI Inference Drives CPU and DDR5 Demand, High-Capacitance MLCC Shortages Through 1H27](https://www.trendforce.com/presscenter/news/20261029-13284.html)

**Commentary:** Passives are again an invisible AI hardware bottleneck—without enough MLCCs, server boards and optical modules cannot scale either.

### 3. Reuters: CXMT plans limited HBM3E output despite poor yields as China pushes AI memory self-reliance (memory)
**Summary:** Reuters reported on October 28 that ChangXin Memory Technologies (CXMT), China’s largest DRAM maker, plans to begin limited HBM3E production within about two months despite yields of only about 20%–30%, versus roughly 70% at SK hynix. CXMT has sent samples to several domestic AI chip designers and offered both a standard design and a yield-improved variant. The report said U.S. export curbs on certain advanced HBM products since December 2024 have left Chinese AI chipmakers short of advanced memory, pushing CXMT ahead despite high cost and scrap rates; in testing, performance was closer to prior-generation HBM3 than to SK hynix’s peer HBM3E.

## II. Manufacturing Pulse and Capacity Footprints

### 5. China’s official manufacturing PMI falls to 49.3 in October; high-tech and equipment still expand (activity)
**Summary:** China’s National Bureau of Statistics reported on October 31 that the official manufacturing PMI fell to 49.3, down 0.8 points from September and back below the expansion threshold after the prior month’s rebound. The production sub-index was 49.8 and new orders 49.4 as both supply and demand softened. NBS attributed the retreat partly to demand pulled forward ahead of the National Day holiday and a complex international environment; high-tech manufacturing PMI stayed at 51.3 and equipment manufacturing at 50.6, with railway/shipbuilding/aerospace and computer/communications/electronics production and orders at or above 53.0. Large-enterprise PMI was 50.2, while medium and small firms printed 48.4 and 47.5; input and output price indexes eased to 56.8 and 51.7. Xinhua and Reuters same-day coverage also highlighted softer export-order momentum.

### 6. Solidigm discusses about $45 billion U.S. NAND fab with Commerce, tying it to Dalian upgrade licenses (relocation)
**Summary:** Bloomberg reporting around October 27–28 said SK hynix’s U.S. subsidiary Solidigm is discussing an about $45 billion U.S. NAND flash plant with the Commerce Department, with total investment potentially approaching $100 billion over time; New York is among candidate sites and construction could begin as early as 2027. The company has linked the U.S. project to export licenses to ship more equipment to upgrade its existing Dalian, China plant, arguing higher Dalian output would help fund and accelerate the U.S. build. The backdrop is Washington’s August 2025 revocation of validated end-user status for Samsung and SK hynix China fabs, which forced annual licensing for U.S. tool shipments; Reuters had earlier reported SK hynix conditioning a new U.S. factory on more equipment-export approvals for China.

### 7. DOE unveils U.S. battery strategy and BattMan accelerator, targeting a full domestic chain by 2030 (batteries)
**Summary:** On October 28, DOE’s Office of Critical Minerals and Energy Innovation released a national battery strategy and launched the Battery Manufacturing Accelerator (BattMan), a consortium of national labs, industry, and academia aimed at cutting manufacturing costs, speeding lab-to-fab transitions, and strengthening critical-mineral supply chains with more abundant domestic elements. Goals include a fully integrated domestic battery supply chain by 2030 and U.S. leadership in battery technology and manufacturing by 2035. DOE projects U.S. battery demand across transport and storage rising from about 179 GWh in 2025 to about 707 GWh in 2035. Six pillars cover upstream/midstream investment, commercialization, process innovation, grid deployment, international coordination, and talent.

## III. Logistics, Tariffs, and Geopolitics

### 9. Dockworkers strike across 113 ports in 22 countries, disrupting Mediterranean hubs (logistics)
**Summary:** Reuters reported from Athens on October 30 that dockworkers struck at 113 ports in 22 countries to protest ports being used for arms shipments and seafarer casualties from the Gulf to the Black Sea, while also seeking pay rises, better conditions, and doctors aboard vessels crossing war zones. More than 25 unions backed the action, affecting hubs including Piraeus, Valencia, Le Havre, Algeciras, Genoa, Morocco’s Tangier Med, and India’s Nhava Sheva. Cargo work was disrupted in Piraeus and Greek island ferries were suspended as about 3,000 dockworkers and seafarers joined; Greek seafarers’ union PENEN cited 28 vessel attacks in the Gulf in the first 21 days of October and 570 confirmed Black Sea attacks this year with 40 seafarers dead. An activist group claimed the protest could halt about 52,000 containers and snarl about $2.8 billion of cargo; Reuters could not independently verify the impact figures.

### 10. LA–Long Beach watchmen strike deadline pushed to November 4 as rail backlogs stay elevated (logistics)
**Summary:** The Pacific Maritime Association and ILWU Local 26 agreed on October 30 to extend talks for about 250 terminal watchmen from November 1 to 5 p.m. Pacific on November 4 (Reuters also described an extension through November 5), with federal mediators still assisting. The watchmen handle gate security and critical-cargo guarding at terminals that move about 85% of Long Beach cargo and 33% of Los Angeles cargo; if other ILWU members honor picket lines, vessel, yard, and gate work at affected terminals could halt. Both ports set September volume records, while rail-bound import boxes remain heavily backlogged—Forwarder and related reports cited roughly 35,000-plus boxes waiting for rail around October 26.

### 11. Grimaldi warns it may exit U.S. trades if Section 301 port-fee pause is not extended; seeks permanent Annex III relief (tariffs)
**Summary:** Italian carrier Grimaldi filed comments on October 30—the USTR deadline—supporting a further suspension of Section 301 port fees targeting China’s maritime and shipbuilding practices through January 9, 2027, and asking for permanent relief from Annex III fees on foreign-built vehicle carriers; the current pause expires November 9. The company said U.S. customers fear it could redeploy ships out of American trades if the pause is not implemented. Over the past three years it reported nearly 985,000 tons of U.S. exports and 1.31 million tons of imports, with about 74.7% of import volume belonging to American automakers; Forwarder and related coverage also said about 86% of its U.S.-trade cargo is tied to U.S. carmakers. The Federal Register proposes a renewed suspension from November 10, 2026, through January 9, 2027.
