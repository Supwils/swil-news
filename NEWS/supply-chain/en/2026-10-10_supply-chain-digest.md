## I. Chips and Capacity

### 1. GlobalFoundries tops out the Dresden SPRINT expansion, aiming past one million wafers by the end of 2028 (chips)
**Summary:** On October 9, GlobalFoundries held a topping-out ceremony for its SPRINT expansion in Dresden, Germany, and said it plans to develop and manufacture FDX Fusion there, a next-generation fully depleted silicon-on-insulator platform aimed at physical AI. The company said the current construction phase of the 1.1 billion euro project is on schedule and on budget. The target is to raise Dresden's annual capacity to more than one million 300mm wafers by the end of 2028, which the company expects would make the site Europe's largest foundry. Existing capacity is up to about 950,000 wafers a year depending on product mix, with about 60,000 square meters of cleanroom and laboratory space and about 3,000 employees. GlobalFoundries has invested more than 10 billion euros in Dresden since 2009. Development of FDX Fusion starts only after the German federal government approves the IPCEI Advanced Semiconductor Technologies project. General contractor Exyte said the existing fab keeps running during the expansion.

**Commentary:** The topping-out confirms construction progress, not wafers already coming off a new line; the million-wafer figure is an end-2028 target, and FDX Fusion still depends on government approval.

### 3. Rare-earth oxide trade stays thin after the holiday, while magnet prices hold (rare earths)
**Summary:** A Shanghai Metals Market analysis on October 10 said inquiries and offers for rare-earth oxides were sparse during China's National Day holiday. Most separation plants kept production running while sales staff were off, and completed deals were rare. Downstream buyers had stocked up before the holiday, so near-term restocking need is limited. As some separation plants resume, oxide supply may rise modestly and put downward pressure on prices. Metal plants kept producing over the holiday with sales desks closed, and praseodymium-neodymium metal prices are expected to move in a narrow range in the first week back. NdFeB prices were broadly stable because praseodymium-neodymium metal prices held and trading staff were away. Most magnet plants scheduled maintenance, new orders paused, and only production against existing orders and shipments of finished goods continued. SMM expects magnet prices to stay stable in the first post-holiday week, with the market waiting for fourth-quarter year-end stocking from mid-October.

## III. Industrial Policy and Shipping

### 7. China's commerce ministry answers the excess-capacity statement signed by 14 economies and the United States (policy)
**Summary:** China News Service reported on October 10 that a Ministry of Commerce spokesperson said on the evening of October 9 that China opposes individual countries practicing protectionism in the name of so-called overcapacity, and that China will make a necessary response if economies later use that label as a pretext for measures that harm Chinese interests. The statement in question is the joint ministerial text the Office of the United States Trade Representative published on October 7, Eastern time. Argentina, Australia, Canada, the European Union, France, Germany, India, Italy, Japan, South Korea, Mexico, Poland, Turkiye, the United Kingdom, and the United States said they will examine the issue and take action in new sectoral platforms. The starting sectors named in the statement are autos and electric vehicles, batteries, chemicals, foundational semiconductors, and solar panels. China News Service also noted the ministry's July 28 position paper, which says large exports and a large surplus are not the same thing as overcapacity.

### 9. The National Retail Federation cuts its September import forecast and says the container peak has passed (logistics)
**Summary:** FreightWaves reported on October 9 that the National Retail Federation and Hackett Associates' Global Port Tracker now treats August as likely the busiest month of 2026, replacing September as the expected peak.
