## I. Chips and Critical Materials

### 1. Samsung says memory tightness may last through 2028 as next-year HBM is sold out and long-term contracts expand (chips)
**Summary:** Samsung Electronics reported on October 29 third-quarter operating profit of 107.4 trillion won, with semiconductors contributing about 106.5 trillion won—nearly all of group profit—after chip-division capex jumped about 62% this year to 76.8 trillion won. It said HBM supply for next year is already sold out, with key customers still seeking more, and expects AI infrastructure capital spending to hit a historic level in 2027 with demand far exceeding supply, keeping the memory market tight through 2028. Samsung has signed long-term supply contracts with 10 major customers and is negotiating with 10 more, aiming for such deals to cover 60%–70% of total capacity by 2030, including mobile and automotive buyers; its Taylor, Texas plant is set to begin mass production next year for Tesla chips.

### 2. TrendForce: AI inference drives high-capacitance MLCC shortages expected through 1H27 (components)
**Summary:** TrendForce said on October 29 that the shift from AI training to inference and agents is lifting CPU, power-module, and DDR5 demand, sharply raising high-capacitance MLCC needs on motherboards and memory modules. NVIDIA's Vera Rubin platform is expected to ramp month by month from 4Q26, and AMD is starting specialized high-capacitance MLCC procurement for MI450, so ODMs expect the high-end shortage to peak in 1Q27–2Q27 and persist through the first half of 2027. By contrast, major notebook ODMs expect 4Q26 orders down about 10%–15% year over year, and consumer mid/low-capacitance MLCC spot prices slipped about 5% in early October. Murata has said existing capacity falls far short and new capacity is unlikely before 2028; Japanese makers may follow Samsung Electro-Mechanics on 1Q27 price hikes.

**Links:**

- [TrendForce — High-Capacitance MLCC Shortages Expected to Persist Through 1H27](https://www.trendforce.com/presscenter/news/20261029-13284.html)

**Commentary:** The bottleneck has moved from HBM and substrates into passives, as AI capacity grabs rewrite consumer-electronics cost and availability.

### 3. AI capacity squeeze sparks PCB crunch: some lead times stretch to about 30 months as CCL costs soar since July (PCB)
**Summary:** Nikkei Asia reporting relayed by Tom's Hardware on October 29 said PC makers still struggle to secure enough printed circuit boards for 2027 planning even if PC shipments may fall again. One PCB maker said lead times have doubled for a significant share of products, with some orders taking as long as about 30 months; another said a new order placed now may not ship until late 2028. A Taiwanese PC maker said copper-clad laminate (CCL) costs have surged at least about 12-fold since July, and a leading CCL supplier said its order book is filling through 2030 despite plans to roughly triple capacity by 2028. The report also noted Nvidia's capacity partnerships with substrate makers including Ibiden, Samsung Electro-Mechanics, and Unimicron.

## II. Batteries and Capacity Reshoring

### 5. Samsung SDI books more than 60 GWh of ESS orders and wins its first European EV LFP project (batteries)
**Summary:** On its October 29 third-quarter call, Samsung SDI said ESS demand has secured more than 60 GWh of battery orders year to date—enough to run U.S. Starplus Energy and Ulsan lines at full capacity through 2028—and it aims to win a similar incremental volume by year-end. Prismatic LFP ESS cells began mass production this month at Starplus in Indiana, with cell shipments from mid-October and finished Battery Box deliveries planned from December; two converted lines are set for phased startup in the first half of next year with orders already filled. The company also won its first LFP battery project for European EVs, citing a Europe EV rebound on high oil prices and subsidies. Preliminary Q3 revenue was about 4.6856 trillion won with operating profit of 407.8 billion won, the strongest quarterly operating profit in 12 quarters.

### 6. Korea Battery Industry Association: cell-maker recovery has yet to reach materials firms; Posco Future M cathode/anode orders may hit 2.7 million tons (batteries)
**Summary:** Ahead of Korea Battery Industry Day on October 29, association chairman and Posco Future M CEO Eom Ki-cheon said stalled global EV projects restarted late last year, while ESS demand and European EV growth of about 20%–30% are offsetting a slower U.S. EV market. LG Energy Solution, Samsung SDI, and SK On are seeing improving profitability, but materials and components suppliers remain largely in the red, so the industry has not fully recovered. Near-term indicators are utilization and shipments; longer-term signals are order backlogs that convert into revenue over two to three years. Posco Future M has secured about 1.5 million metric tons of cathode and anode contracts since 2H25 and may add about 1.2 million tons by year-end for a cumulative 2.5–2.7 million tons, though shipments and utilization are expected to rise mainly from 2H27. The association wants direct cash-refund support for loss-making firms beyond production tax credits.

## III. Policy, Trade, and Logistics

### 8. U.S. Section 232 robotics and industrial-machinery probe: talks for 180 days instead of immediate tariffs (policy)
**Summary:** Supply Chain Dive reported on October 29 that President Trump signed a proclamation on October 28 following a Section 232 investigation launched in September 2025, which found U.S. reliance on imported robotics, industrial machinery, and related parts threatens the industrial base and national security. Rather than imposing immediate tariffs, he directed the commerce secretary and USTR to pursue or continue negotiations with trading partners and report progress within 180 days. It is the third time in about three months he has preferred talks after a 232 probe (after similar moves on wind equipment and commercial aircraft/engines); the proclamation leaves room for tariffs or other remedies depending on negotiation outcomes.

**Links:**

- [Supply Chain Dive — Trump opts for trade talks over tariffs for robotics, industrial machinery](https://www.supplychaindive.com/news/trump-opts-for-trade-talks-over-tariffs-for-robotics-industrial-machinery/873166/)
