## I. Chips & Critical Materials

### 1. AI advanced packaging becomes the bottleneck: Nvidia reportedly finances SPIL CoWoS expansion; 2026 advanced-packaging capex near $28B (Packaging)
**Summary:** Reuters reported on Nov 6 (still widely followed on Nov 8) that AI chip demand has outrun advanced-packaging capacity, with CoWoS and related processes now a delivery chokepoint. TSMC still leads high-end packaging, but tight capacity is pushing designers to seek alternatives; an industry executive familiar with the matter said Nvidia is providing billions of dollars in financing to help ASE unit Siliconware Precision Industries (SPIL) expand factory space and equipment for more CoWoS capacity—amount and terms undisclosed; Nvidia did not respond, and ASE said it does not comment on market rumors involving customers. Yole Group expects advanced-packaging capital spending near about $28 billion in 2026, with advanced-packaging revenue set to overtake traditional packaging for the first time. AMD has also pledged more than about $10 billion across Taiwan’s semiconductor industry, including packaging ties with ASE, SPIL and Powertech; CEO Lisa Su said more capacity is still needed overall.

### 2. TSMC denies DRAM entry: reaffirms logic foundry focus after reports Nvidia asked it to weigh 3D DRAM (Memory)
**Summary:** CHOSUNBIZ reported on Nov 8 that, responding to The Information and related coverage that Nvidia—facing memory shortages and rising prices—asked TSMC to produce 3D DRAM and that TSMC had begun drafting plans, a TSMC spokesperson said the company always focuses on its core semiconductor foundry business and that its mission is to remain a trusted provider of technology and capacity in the global logic semiconductor industry. Winbond Electronics chair Chao Yu-chun also said a direct TSMC DRAM push is unlikely, citing proprietary process needs and complex patents, and noting TSMC’s close advanced-packaging work with Samsung, Winbond and others. Earlier reports said options under review included building a new memory fab or acquiring and converting an existing plant, with no location, budget, product or start date set.

### 3. Chip-equipment orders lock in through 2028–2029: lead times roughly double as majors talk three-year deliveries (Equipment)
**Summary:** The Electronic Times English site, citing industry sources as of Nov 3, said Applied Materials, ASML, Lam Research, Tokyo Electron and KLA are in final stages of 2028 equipment supply contracts, with some already discussing purchase orders for 2029 delivery. Versus the prior pattern of talks 6–12 months ahead and delivery 3–6 months after order, some tool lead times have more than doubled to about two years or longer, forcing fabs to lock machines 1–2 years earlier to keep AI buildout schedules. ASML said at its last earnings that planned 2027–2028 capacity still will not fully meet customer demand and is reviewing further expansion beyond a previously guided ~30% increase; some customers have also expressed interest in 2029–2030 orders. A senior equipment executive called three-year-ahead delivery talks unusual but likely to persist toward about 2030 at the current fab-investment pace.

**Links:**

- [Electronic Times — Semiconductor Equipment Orders Stretch Three Years Ahead Amid AI Boom](https://en.etnews.com/20261104200007)
- [CNBC — ASML says it's getting order interest through 2030 on AI demand](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/14/asml-3q-earnings-ai-demand-orders-capacity.html)

**Commentary:** Whoever books the tools first ships first—equipment lead times have become the entry ticket to the AI capacity race.

## II. Batteries & Materials

### 4. Korea’s LFP cathode “alliance” firms up: domestic contracts estimated above 210,000 tons; L&F targets 120,000 tons by 2028 (Batteries)
**Summary:** MoneyToday reported on Nov 8 that Korean cathode makers have accelerated into lithium iron phosphate (LFP) since the third quarter. Industry sources estimate LFP cathode supply contracts between domestic cell makers and materials firms have surpassed about 210,000 tons, driven by North America China-free supply-chain orders. POSCO Future M plans to lift Pohang LFP capacity from about 20,000 tons a year to as much as about 105,000 tons by end-next year, and to use up to about 50,000 tons from its JV with CP New Materials. L&F’s LFP lines are running near 90% utilization; after deals with Samsung SDI (~1.6 trillion won) and SK On (~161.7 billion won), it signed an ~1 trillion won supply contract with LG Energy Solution this month and aims to expand annual LFP capacity to 120,000 tons by the second half of 2028. Ecopro BM is also pivoting toward North American ESS LFP demand, discussing ~4,000 tons/year of supply and reviewing medium-term capacity near 50,000 tons. LGES and Samsung SDI are said to have locked volumes for up to about six to seven years each.

**Links:**

- [MoneyToday — Korea's battery 'LFP alliance' solidifies… Material makers weigh successive capacity expansions](https://www.mt.co.kr/en/industry/2026/11/08/2026110613205737343)

**Commentary:** North American “non-China” cathode orders are yanking Korean materials makers from NMC into an LFP capacity race.

### 5. Korea’s third ESS central auction: ~1,180 MW / up to ~9 GWh battery need; supply tight and quotes up 30–40% (Energy storage)
**Summary:** Aju Press reported on Nov 8 that Korea’s third central ESS contract market is the largest yet: bid proposals closed Nov 4 at about 1,180 MW (mainland ~1,100 MW, Jeju ~80 MW), above the first two rounds’ combined ~1,128 MW, equating to roughly 7.08 GWh of battery storage; with equipment reserve rates, actual battery need is estimated near 9 GWh. LG Energy Solution opted out, leaving Samsung SDI (~4–5 GWh estimated supply) and SK On (~3 GWh) as primary suppliers—even at full stretch they may struggle to cover demand. Insiders say battery supply quotes are about 30–40% higher than prior rounds, which could lift generator bid prices. The Korea Power Exchange requires final documentation including business plans by Nov 11 and expects preferred negotiation partners later this month; the government aims to establish related ESS facilities by about February 2029 through the program.

**Links:**

- [Aju Press — Potential Battery Supply Shortage Looms in Major Energy Storage Auction](https://www.ajupress.com/view/20261108160470742)

**Commentary:** Storage orders are stacked at the cell makers’ gates—the next contest is delivery schedules, not just clearing prices.

### 7. HP, Asus shift some PC making from Thailand/Vietnam back to China as AI lifts BOM costs; TrendForce sees non-China notebook share falling (Migration)
**Summary:** TechRadar on Nov 4, citing Nikkei Asia, reported that HP, Asus and Acer are moving some PC production from Thailand and Vietnam back to China and leaning more on Chinese assemblers such as Luxshare and Huaqin to offset AI-driven memory and storage price spikes that are squeezing thin hardware margins; Dell is also said to be evaluating a similar shift. TrendForce estimates the share of global notebooks built outside China will fall from about 24% in 2025 to about 21% in 2026 and could slip below about 20% in 2027. Processors, DRAM and SSDs rose from roughly 45% of a mainstream notebook BOM in Q1 2025 to about 68% in Q3 2026, per TrendForce. Traditional Taiwan ODMs are pouring capacity into AI servers, opening room for Chinese assemblers on PC work; brands have also begun shipping small volumes of CXMT DRAM in some non-U.S. notebooks. The move partly reverses “China+1,” though most firms still keep Southeast Asian capacity as a tariff hedge.

**Summary:** The Telegraph reported on Nov 8 that Port of Long Beach CEO Noel Hacegaba said U.S. midterm results are unlikely to end tariff turmoil and global trade dislocation; uncertain tariff plans, plus disruption from the Iran war, Red Sea threats and Panama Canal logjams, will keep stressing supply chains. **Summary:** Multiple policy trackers through early November and around Nov 7–8 still show that MOFCOM Announcement No. 70 of 2025 suspends the October 2025 rare-earth and related export-control package through Nov 10, 2026; the Sept 28 statement extending the Kuala Lumpur arrangement to Jan 10, 2027, does not name rare earths or amend No. 70’s text, and no matching numbered extension notice appears on public lists. ### 10. Visakhapatnam port congestion delays imported PVC/PE to southern India; Guntur–Vijayawada pipe belt sees up to ~5-day dispatch lags (Logistics)
**Summary:** Polymers Bazaar reported on Nov 8 that operational bottlenecks and container-yard congestion at Visakhapatnam (Vizag) Port have slowed imported polyvinyl chloride (PVC) and polyethylene (PE) resins to southern Indian processing hubs. Vessel dwell times have lengthened over the past week, yard density is above optimal, and trailer shortages are adding several days from berth to inland depots. Importers relying on Vizag for Andhra Pradesh, Telangana and Tamil Nadu are renegotiating schedules; pipe processors in the Guntur–Vijayawada belt report dispatch delays of up to about five days. Paper availability of imported suspension PVC and film-grade LLDPE remains adequate—the squeeze is haulage, not a global resin shortage. Smaller converters with thin buffers face hiccups, while larger buyers shift toward domestic petrochemical producers via road. Local spot imported PVC K-67 and LLDPE film grades are commanding slight premiums on prompt availability, and freight to Vijayawada, Hyderabad and Chennai has edged up.
