## I. Chips & Critical Materials

### 1. Samsung and SK hynix expand multi-year memory LTAs; Hynix capacity roughly locked into 2029 (memory)
**Summary:** Korea JoongAng Daily reported on Nov 2 that amid an AI-driven memory shortage, Samsung Electronics and SK hynix are expanding long-term agreements (LTAs) with customers. Samsung said on its third-quarter call it has signed deals with 10 customers and is negotiating with about 10 more, advancing toward allocating 60%–70% of total capacity to LTAs by 2030; demand for such contracts is spreading from AI firms to mobile, auto and networking buyers. SK hynix said it has signed typically five-year LTAs with 14 customers, including major cloud providers, locking more than half of 2027–2028 capacity, and with deals still in negotiation its DRAM and HBM output is effectively sold out through 2029. Both plan to pace capacity adds carefully against the risk of oversupply as new fabs ramp around 2028.

**Links:**

- [Korea JoongAng Daily — Samsung, SK hynix lock in long-term memory chip deals](https://www.koreajoongangdaily.com/business/samsung-sk-hynix-build-longterm-order-pipelines-on-back-of-continued-ai-chip-demand/12949893)

**Commentary:** Memory has moved from a price cycle to a capacity-booking cycle—whoever holds LTAs holds the entry ticket to compute for years ahead.

### 2. TrendForce: GPUs tighten, MLCC lead times stretch to ~38 weeks; high-capacity DRAM stays very tight (components)
**Summary:** TrendForce’s Weekly Radar dated Nov 2 showed mixed supply signals: GPUs shifted from Balanced to Tightening as the Rubin transition begins; MLCC lead times extended to about 38 weeks; DRAM eased to about 19 weeks but remains Very Tight, especially for 32GB/64GB RDIMMs. Memory suppliers are posting record profits while their largest customers face rising cash outflows. The backdrop is simultaneous scramble for HBM, high-capacitance passives and advanced packaging for AI servers, leaving delivery times and pricing power with suppliers.

**Links:**

**Commentary:** The bottleneck is no longer only advanced logic—GPUs, DRAM and MLCCs tightening together show the AI hardware pipe is under stress end to end.

### 3. SEMI: 2026 silicon-wafer shipments forecast up ~11.8% on AI and fab expansion (wafers)
**Summary:** SEMI wrote on Nov 2 that the Q3 2026 Silicon Wafer Market Monitor forecasts global wafer shipments to grow about 11.8% in 2026, mainly on AI demand expected to support shipments through 2029. Rising process-material sales, memory demand, precautionary inventory building and global fab expansions also help. SEMI notes a widening gap between fab-equipment and wafer-manufacturing-equipment spending growth, suggesting investment support for wafer demand may last longer—but the next test is inventory absorption; if expansions outrun end-chip demand, wafer orders could still pull back.

**Links:**

- [SEMI — What Is Driving Silicon Wafer Demand Recovery?](https://www.semi.org/en/blogs/what-is-driving-silicon-wafer-demand-recovery)

**Commentary:** Wafer recovery shows front-end expansion is real, but if buying is mostly precautionary, the slope still depends on fabs consuming those inventories.

### 4. After a ~45% October plunge, Intel’s foundry and capacity allocation face a critical test (foundry)
**Summary:** CNBC reported on Nov 2 that Intel’s roughly 29% drop on Friday left the stock down about 45% in October, among its worst months on record. ## II. Capacity Layout & Reshoring

### 6. Lockheed Martin adds ~$420M to expand missile output in Camden, Arkansas (defense manufacturing)
**Summary:** The Arkansas Economic Development Commission announced on Nov 2 that Lockheed Martin will invest about $420 million in Camden, expecting to create about 650 full-time jobs over five years to expand PAC-3, THAAD and Precision Strike Missile (PrSM) capacity. The roughly 2.2-million-square-foot site on 2,427 acres has delivered more than 700,000 missiles and rockets over four decades; in January 2026 Lockheed broke ground on a Munitions Acceleration Center aiming to raise THAAD interceptor output from about 96 to about 400 a year. The site currently employs about 1,100 people.

**Links:**

- [Arkansas EDC — Lockheed Martin Expands in Camden with $420M Investment](https://www.arkansasedc.com/news-events/newsroom/detail/2026/11/02/lockheed-martin-expands-in-camden--arkansas-with--420m-investment)

**Commentary:** Defense supply-chain capacity races are landing in heartland plants—interceptors and precision munitions are becoming measurable industrial-policy targets.

## III. Activity, Trade & Logistics

### 10. U.S. factories keep expanding but delays worsen: ISM PMI 55.5 as factory-gate inflation hits multi-year highs (activity)
**Summary:** Axios and S&P Global reported on Nov 2 that the U.S. ISM manufacturing index rose to 55.5 in October—a 10th straight expansion month—with production at its fastest since November 2021, while S&P Global’s manufacturing PMI printed 54.9, still among the best readings since early 2022. Both surveys show AI-related equipment demand straining supply chains and lifting prices: S&P said selling-price inflation was the steepest in about four years and supplier delays the most widespread since mid-2022; ISM’s prices index eased to 75.2% but still signaled broad raw-material increases. Energy costs, Middle East shipping disruptions and tariff-related frictions were cited alongside demand.

Some 178 China Section 301 product exclusions also point to the same expiry date.

### 13. Georgia’s Gainesville Inland Port celebrates opening; ~26,000 boxes may shift off highways in year one (logistics)
**Summary:** Norfolk Southern and Container News reported that the Georgia Ports Authority’s about $134 million Gainesville Inland Port held a grand opening on Oct 29 (operations began in May), with Norfolk Southern rail five days a week to the Port of Savannah. At full build-out the terminal can handle about 200,000 containers a year, serving roughly 300 Northeast Georgia manufacturers, and is expected to shift about 26,000 containers from truck to rail in its first year—an alternative to a roughly 600-mile round-trip truck haul to Savannah. SCMR’s Nov 2 briefing also flagged the inland port among key capacity items for the week.
