## I. Chips and Advanced Packaging

### 1. NVIDIA locks in substrate, packaging, test, and optical-engine expansion in Berlin (chips)
**Summary:** On October 21 at GTC Berlin, NVIDIA said it is expanding substrate, advanced-packaging, test, and optical-engine capacity with IBIDEN, Samsung Electro-Mechanics, Unimicron, Amkor, King Yuan Electronics (KYEC), and Siliconware Precision Industries (SPIL). NVIDIA plans to raise substrate capacity for its products 2.5 times over the next three years. A multibillion-dollar capacity-reservation advance for IBIDEN's Ono plant in Japan was signed and announced earlier this year; the expansion targets 3 times current capacity at that plant, with ramp-up from the first quarter of 2028. Samsung Electro-Mechanics is joining with existing capacity in South Korea and upgrades and new factories in Sejong and Busan; by 2028, output allocated to NVIDIA is projected to account for a significant share of its annual package-substrate capacity. Unimicron's KF substrate expansion in Taiwan is expected to start adding capacity in the second quarter of 2027 and reach full capacity by mid-2028. Amkor plans to raise interposer-wafer capacity in South Korea by more than 300 percent by the end of 2028. NVIDIA expects total semiconductor test capacity to double from calendar 2026 to 2027. A $1.1 billion capacity-reservation advance to KYEC supports new U.S. test facilities, with high-volume production targeted for April 2027; expanded testing in Singapore targets high-volume production in February 2027. SPIL is targeting a 15-fold increase in co-packaged-optics engine capacity by mid-2027 and another doubling by the end of 2028. Jensen Huang said demand is extraordinary and capacity is the constraint.

### 2. Samsung and SK Hynix upgrade NAND plants in China, with wafer targets from people familiar (chips)
**Summary:** Reuters reported on October 21, citing four people familiar with the plans, that Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix are upgrading factories in China to make higher-layer NAND flash, to compete with Yangtze Memory Technologies and meet AI demand. Both companies confirmed to Reuters that upgrades are underway in close consultation with Washington, but they did not disclose the production plans in the report. Reuters could not determine how far U.S. export licenses for the required equipment have been granted. People familiar said Samsung's Xian plant accounts for about 40 percent of its global NAND output and currently makes chips of about 200 layers. An internal project called Monami would convert it to 286-layer V9 NAND; one person said monthly output would be 5,000 wafers in December this year and 40,000 by December 2027. Samsung said the upgrade complies with U.S. export rules, is aimed at demand from global customers including those in the United States, and does not expand capacity or add new fabs. SK Hynix is looking to move its Dalian plant from 192 layers to 240 layers or more. One person said the company is authorized to make up to 240 layers in Dalian, may seek permission for research on chips above 300 layers there, and is discussing a new NAND factory in the United States with the Trump administration. The U.S. Bureau of Industry and Security did not answer directly on license status and pointed to its 2025 statement that it intended to grant licenses to keep existing China factories running, not to expand capacity or upgrade technology. Omdia said YMTC held 38.5 percent of China's NAND market in the second quarter, up from 28 percent for all of 2025, while Samsung slipped from 36 percent to 33.5 percent and SK Hynix from 20 percent to 17 percent.

### 3. Semiconductor Industry Association: about half of Tier-1 categories rate medium or high vulnerability (chip materials)
**Summary:** The Semiconductor Industry Association's October report, Understanding Dependencies in the U.S. Semiconductor Supply Chain, says that 95 percent of roughly $27 billion a year in equipment and materials consumed by the U.S. semiconductor ecosystem comes from suppliers headquartered in the United States, Europe, or Japan. Measured by physical production or assembly location, that share falls to 68 percent, while supply from China, Israel, Southeast Asia, South Korea, and Taiwan rises from 5 percent to 32 percent. The association's vulnerability index rates about half of the Tier-1 categories it analyzed as high vulnerability (11 percent) or medium vulnerability (38 percent). Nearly one-third of the upstream inputs analyzed are rated high vulnerability. Once sub-tier dependencies are included, more than three-quarters of Tier-1 categories map to at least one highly vulnerable upstream input, and more than half rely on two or more. The report says some domestic capacity can take a decade or longer to reach meaningful volume, and qualifying a new supplier or material for semiconductor-grade use can take another three to four years. Supply Chain Dive reported the study on October 20, and an October 21 industry briefing still listed it among the day's supply-chain items.

**Links:**

### 4. Siemens and NVIDIA move circuit-board optical-inspection prep into a digital twin (electronics manufacturing)
**Summary:** On October 21 in Plano, Texas, Siemens announced a collaboration with NVIDIA that uses Teamcenter, Process Preparation X, and NVIDIA Cosmos to prepare automated optical inspection before the first physical printed circuit board reaches the line. The workflow is being demonstrated at Siemens' electronics factory in Erlangen, Germany, where the goal is a factory that can introduce changes three to five times a week without stopping production. Project targets include up to 85 percent less manual engineering effort, more than 40 percent fewer false calls, and more than 5 percent improvement in first-pass yield. Rev Lebaredian, NVIDIA's vice president of physical-AI simulation, said manufacturers should not have to wait for the first physical board to start preparing inspection.

**Links:**

- [Siemens — Siemens and NVIDIA advance AI-powered PCB inspection from Digital Twin to factory](https://news.siemens.com/en-us/siemens-nvidia-pcba-digital-twin-factory/)

**Commentary:** The 85 percent, 40 percent, and 5 percent figures are targets for the Erlangen demonstration. The release does not report measured results from a production line.

### 5. A Japanese official says yttrium exports have stalled worldwide, with 4 tonnes to Japan in September (rare earths)
**Summary:** AFP reported on October 21 that an official at Japan's industry ministry, speaking on condition of anonymity, said yttrium exports have stalled not only to Japan but worldwide, causing a supply-demand crunch and soaring prices. The official said exports to Japan have decreased, including shipment delays, and that global volume itself is low. Tokyo is working with the United States and European countries on critical minerals and rare earths, including yttrium, the official said. AFP cited Chinese customs data showing yttrium-oxide exports to Japan of 4,000 kilograms in September, compared with 51,000 kilograms in the same month a year earlier. Foreign Ministry spokesperson Mao Ning said at that day's regular briefing that China imposed export controls on Japan in accordance with laws and regulations. The International Energy Agency was cited for the figure that China accounts for 92 percent of global refined rare-earth output. The comments followed a Financial Times report that Japan could run out of yttrium within months.

### 9. Westinghouse invests about $1 billion in South Carolina for LEU+ fuel, with operations in 2030 (nuclear fuel)
**Summary:** On October 21, Westinghouse Electric announced an investment of about $1 billion at its Columbia Fuel Fabrication Facility in Hopkins, South Carolina, to build what it called the first fleet-scale nuclear-fuel factory in the United States in more than 50 years. The plant will make low-enriched uranium plus (LEU+) fuel at 5 percent to 8 percent enrichment. Construction is underway, with operations planned for 2030, to support long-term operation of the existing fleet and industrialization of the AP1000. The Columbia site, built in 1968, employs more than 1,200 people. An economic-impact study commissioned by Westinghouse with the University of South Carolina's Darla Moore School of Business projects about $4.9 billion in positive impact across the state over 10 years, including about $3.8 billion in Richland County. Governor Henry McMaster said Westinghouse's fuel operations in Richland County support nuclear generation equal to about 10 percent of the United States. Ted Garrish, the Department of Energy's assistant secretary for nuclear energy, said the department is investing billions to restore U.S. uranium enrichment, and that Westinghouse's investment expands the manufacturing capacity that turns enriched uranium into fuel.

### 12. Audi trials mobile humanoid robots for parts sequencing in Ingolstadt (auto logistics)
**Summary:** Supply Chain Digital reported on October 21 that Audi is working with Agile Robots, Dassault Systemes, and NVIDIA on a proof of concept that uses mobile humanoid robots for parts sequencing at the Ingolstadt plant. Audi's demand-driven material supply handles about one million picks a day so the right part reaches the right station in assembly order. The trial is in Audi's Production Lab. Robots pick components from containers and place them in sequence for final assembly. The partners are building a virtual twin of the logistics operation in Dassault's DELMIA, with NVIDIA Omniverse libraries, to check cycle time, safety, and factory fit before deployment. The partners say this could cut engineering and commissioning from months to days. Agile Robots says it has deployed more than 20,000 robotic solutions worldwide. Audi board member for production and logistics Gerd Walker said picking inside the sequencing process has for decades been one of the hardest automation problems in automotive logistics.

**Links:**

- [Supply Chain Digital — Audi and NVIDIA Use Agile Robots for Parts Sequencing Trial](https://supplychaindigital.com/news/audi-and-nvidia-use-agile-robots-for-parts-sequencing-trial)

**Commentary:** This is a proof of concept in the Production Lab. The one million picks a day describe today's material operation. The robots have not taken over the sequencing line.
