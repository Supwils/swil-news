## I. Chip Supply and Equipment

### 1. South Korea's finance minister says the chip boom can last at least through next year (Chips)
**Summary:** CNBC reported on October 17 that South Korean Finance Minister Lee Hyoung-Il, speaking Saturday on the sidelines of the IMF-World Bank annual meetings in Bangkok, said the semiconductor boom can be sustained at least through next year because the gap between demand and supply is still very wide and supply is still being expanded. He said chip stocks fell sharply this week on concern about interest-rate hikes and rising competition from Chinese companies, with SK Hynix, Seoul Semiconductor, and Nvidia among the names sold off. Chip stocks have amplified swings in the Korean market this year, he said, but he called the economy solid and said tax collection this year should exceed the initial projection. CNBC also noted a Thursday opinion column in Maeil Business Newspaper whose author, citing two experts, expects a flood of new supply to turn the memory market into a "red ocean" by 2029.

### 2. ASML says 30 percent more tool capacity in each of 2027 and 2028 still will not meet demand (Lithography tools)
**Summary:** The Korea Times reported on October 17 that ASML chief executive Christophe Fouquet said on Wednesday's earnings call that plans to raise advanced-tool capacity by 30 percent in 2027 and another 30 percent in 2028 would still not meet customer demand. The increases cover low-numerical-aperture extreme ultraviolet systems and deep-ultraviolet immersion systems. Customer talks are shifting beyond 2027, with more 2028 orders and interest in placing orders for 2029 and even 2030. Chief financial officer Roger Dassen said ASML is holding off on accepting 2029 and 2030 orders so earlier customers do not lock up the slots. The report said SK hynix has already started construction in the Yongin cluster and is targeting October next year for the first fab, while Samsung is scheduled to break ground later this month and to start its first Yongin fab in 2029. Local authorities want the Jeonnam-Gwangju cluster to start construction next year and mass production in 2030, and neither company has published an official schedule. Equipment lead times are typically 18 months to two years. One industry official said the situation is not a bottleneck yet, but one could appear if strong demand lasts through 2030.

**Links:**

- [The Korea Times — Securing equipment emerges as new variable for Korea's chip expansion plans](https://www.koreatimes.co.kr/business/companies/20261017/securing-equipment-emerges-as-new-variable-for-koreas-chip-expansion-plans)

**Commentary:** The two 30 percent figures are ASML's own capacity plan, not tools already booked for the Korean clusters, and the Yongin and Jeonnam-Gwangju start dates remain company and local targets.

## II. Domestic Manufacturing and Critical Minerals

### 5. Qcells plans U.S.-made aluminum frames by year-end (Solar)
**Summary:** pv magazine reported on October 17 that Qcells plans to launch new versions of its residential modules with U.S.-made aluminum frames by the end of 2026, covering the Q.Tron AC and Q.Peak Duo AC lines, to help installers and third-party owners qualify for the domestic-content bonus on the investment tax credit. Extrusion, anodizing, and fabrication will be done at Aluko Group's plant in Halls, Lauderdale County, Tennessee. The plant is expected to reach full commercial production by year-end, with planned annual capacity enough for 4 GW of modules, more than $107 million of investment over five years, and 285 full-time jobs. The company says it is the only U.S. site that performs all three steps under one roof. The report said the statutory domestic-content threshold is stepping up toward 55 percent for clean-energy projects that start construction in 2027, and that meeting it can add a 10 percent tax-credit bonus. Qcells already assembles modules and components in Dalton and Cartersville, Georgia.

### 6. Multilateral banks publish a minerals-to-manufacturing collaboration framework (Mineral finance)
**Summary:** The European Bank for Reconstruction and Development on October 17 published the multilateral development banks' Collaboration Framework on critical minerals-to-manufacturing value chains. It follows the joint statement of April 16 and describes the chain as running from geological knowledge, exploration, and mining through processing, refining, manufacturing, recycling, and enabling services. Work is grouped into policy and governance, corridor infrastructure such as power, transport, logistics, digital connectivity, and water, and mobilization of private capital. Four objectives cover mutual reliance on environmental and social standards, country coordination, private capital, and an annual stocktake. Participation is voluntary and does not limit a government or company in choosing a bank. Joint arrangements still require the client's agreement. The text does not list project amounts or a new lending envelope.

**Links:**

- [EBRD — MDB collaboration framework: critical minerals-to-manufacturing value chains](https://www.ebrd.com/home/news-and-events/news/2026/mdb-collaboration-framework-citical-minerals-to-manufacturing-va.html)

**Commentary:** This is a rulebook for how the banks work together, not a mine or processing-plant loan approved that day.

**Summary:** Shanghai Metals Market's October 16 morning note said Thursday's average spot price for battery-grade lithium carbonate was 104,400 yuan a tonne, down 17,950 yuan from the previous Friday's average of 122,350 yuan, a weekly drop of 14.7 percent. ### 11. Maputo starts a $150 million channel-dredging project (Ports)
**Summary:** The same Africa Ports & Ships issue reported that Mozambican President Daniel Chapo on October 12 launched a $150 million dredging project for the Port of Maputo access channel. China Road and Bridge Corporation is doing the work under the investment program of Maputo Port Development Company, whose concession now runs to 2058. The job is expected to take about 12 months and to remove more than 21 million cubic metres of seabed. Maximum depth is to rise from 14.4 metres to about 15.3 metres. Straight sections widen from about 110 metres to 210 metres, bends from 150 metres to 295 metres, and a new turning basin of about 660 metres in diameter will be added. The channel is designed for ships up to 366 metres long and as many as 14,000 TEU, still subject to draught, tide, and operating conditions. Alongside that, DP World Maputo's container terminal is being expanded toward about 650,000 TEU a year, the Matola Coal Terminal is planned to reach about 12 million tonnes a year, and separate investment is intended to lift ore handling to 20.6 million tonnes a year.
