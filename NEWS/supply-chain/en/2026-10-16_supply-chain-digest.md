## I. Chip Inputs and Critical Minerals

### 1. Friday briefing separates tool spending, silicon germanium, gallium nitride, slurry, and test (Chip inputs)
**Summary:** The Electronics Brief on October 16 sorted the week's capacity news by who can actually use it. The briefing said SEMI on Tuesday projected $149 billion of worldwide spending on 300 mm front-end fab equipment in 2026, up 31% year on year, and $227 billion in 2029. Excluding facilities devoted purely to research and development, 20 new 300 mm fabs or production lines are expected to begin construction this year, and another 18 in 2027. The same piece noted that Semtech on Tuesday expanded a multi-year agreement with Tower Semiconductor and is investing in dedicated silicon-germanium capacity in the United States, Israel, and Japan. On Monday, Polar Semiconductor and IC-Link by imec said they would bring enhancement-mode gallium nitride into 8-inch production in Minnesota, initially from 40 V to 650 V. Fujifilm on Wednesday announced an $18 million expansion in Mesa, Arizona, for chemical-mechanical planarization slurries used in copper interconnects and advanced packaging, with the line scheduled to start operating in 2027. FormFactor and Teradyne on Monday announced a collaboration that includes Teradyne's agreement to invest $300 million in FormFactor equity for test work on high-bandwidth memory, advanced packaging, and silicon photonics. The investment remains subject to closing conditions and regulatory approvals. The briefing cited TrendForce's September 30 outlook for fourth-quarter contract-price increases of 10% to 15% in conventional DRAM and 15% to 20% in NAND, and a Wednesday TrendForce forecast that combined 2027 capital spending by nine leading cloud providers will exceed $1.5 trillion.

**Commentary:** The $149 billion and $1.5 trillion figures are spending forecasts from an industry association and a research firm, while the $18 million and $300 million figures are an announced expansion and an equity deal that still needs to close, not wafers delivered this year.

### 2. Project Vault prepares up to $1 billion in loans for manufacturers (Critical minerals)
**Summary:** MINING.COM reported on October 16, citing Bloomberg, that VaultCo, which runs the U.S. critical-minerals reserve Project Vault, is in talks with more than 70 companies and is preparing to issue up to $1 billion in loans to American manufacturers. Executive chairman Brett Lambert said the announcement would likely cover several hundred million dollars, maybe a billion dollars, of loans to original-equipment manufacturers, with more than a dozen firms in the first tranche and another group soon after. Recipients would not be named at this stage, though the types of minerals requested would be disclosed. The project launched in February as a public-private partnership backed by a $10 billion loan from the U.S. Export-Import Bank and nearly $2 billion in private investment. Glencore and Mercuria recently committed $500 million each. The same article said Mercuria has delivered 12,000 tonnes of copper from the Democratic Republic of Congo, the first metal reported into the reserve. A Reuters story on October 15 said Mercuria's global head of metals, Kostas Bintas, told the FT Metals and Mining Summit in London on Thursday that this copper "has gone into the vault this week" and sits inside a broader Congolese commitment to ship metal to the United States. MINING.COM also said U.S. imports of Congolese copper reached a record 53,290 tonnes in July. Lambert said companies have asked for raw metals and also for specialized magnets, powders, alloys, and other processed materials.

### 3. MH Materials signs with Korea's materials institute to develop a dysprosium- and terbium-free magnet (Rare earths)
**Summary:** CHOSUNBIZ reported on October 16 that MH Materials signed a business agreement at 1:30 p.m. that day with the Korea Institute of Materials Science to jointly develop and commercialize a next-generation permanent magnet called MH HIMAG. The approach cuts neodymium use by 30%, substitutes cerium, and excludes the heavy rare earths dysprosium and terbium. The company said that, for 1 kilogram of permanent magnet, the materials change can reduce cost by about 34%, and a hot-deformation process can cut process cost by a further roughly 15%. It plans a pilot line of 50 tons a year, applied first to motor magnets for robot actuators under development at affiliate MH Dynamics. After prototypes and customer verification, it is reviewing a step-up from 1,000 tons to 10,000 tons a year in line with orders. The report contrasted this with Japan's Daido Electronics, which already supplies Honda with magnets that do not use heavy rare earths but has not reduced neodymium use.

**Links:**

- [CHOSUNBIZ — MH Materials and Korea institute of Materials Science develop Dy/Tb-free magnet](https://biz.chosun.com/en/en-finance/2026/10/16/KISVZWLUYNEO3DWGEOSOAYSC4U/)

**Commentary:** The 34% and 15% figures are the company's cost and process estimates, and 50 tons is the planned pilot line, not magnets already shipped.

## II. Batteries and Defense Manufacturing

### 4. Korean cell makers shift LFP cathodes toward POSCO and L&F (Battery materials)
**Summary:** The Chosun Ilbo's English edition reported on October 16 that Samsung SDI and SK On have contracted for lithium-iron-phosphate cathode material from POSCO Future M and L&F, while LG Energy Solution is in final negotiations with both Korean suppliers to diversify away from Chinese sources. Samsung SDI signed on the 6th to take 6 trillion won of LFP cathode from POSCO Future M from 2027 through 2032, and in March signed with L&F for about 1 trillion won over three years starting in 2027. SK On agreed to take 1 trillion won from POSCO Future M and 160 billion won from L&F. LG Energy Solution already has a 2024 long-term contract with China's Shangrao Liwon, so a Korean deal would add a second source. The report said LG Energy Solution will produce 1 GWh a year of energy-storage LFP cells at Ochang next year. Samsung SDI plans to start mass production of energy-storage LFP this quarter at its Indiana plant and to supply LFP batteries from next year. SK On is converting part of its Seosan plant and plans to use part of its Georgia plant, with mass production expected next year. POSCO Future M plans to start mass production of LFP cathode at Pohang by the end of this year. A joint venture with Chinese precursor maker CNGR and Korean subsidiary Pino is building a plant in the Yeongilman 4 General Industrial Complex in Pohang. L&F's wholly owned L&F Plus completed a Daegu plant in May, announced plans to start mass production of 30,000 tons a year by the end of the third quarter, and aims for 60,000 tons a year in the first half of 2027. In September the climate and power-exchange authorities opened a third central energy-storage contract round for 1,180 MW, with price and non-price scores split evenly and 25 of 100 non-price points tied to industrial and supply-chain contribution.

### 5. Samsung SDI aims to mass-produce cylindrical LFP for servers in Malaysia as early as next June (Batteries)
**Summary:** The Herald Business reported exclusively on October 13 that, according to industry sources, Samsung SDI has decided on a new investment and has begun installing lithium-iron-phosphate cathode-plate equipment at its second plant in Seremban, Malaysia, for battery backup units used in artificial-intelligence servers. Mass production is targeted as early as next June. The report called this the first time a Korean battery maker has applied LFP chemistry to cylindrical backup cells. The plant is expected to add mixers and tanks so the new chemistry does not cross-contaminate existing nickel-cobalt-manganese lines, while later steps reuse current equipment. Samsung SDI has invested about 1.7 trillion won ($1.27 billion) in the second plant since 2022. It now makes 21700 cylindrical cells for power tools, e-bikes, and electric vehicles. Utilization at the small-battery plants was 50.4% last year and returned to 73% in the first half of this year. Cho Han-je, head of strategy and marketing, said on the second-quarter call that the company holds roughly 40% to 50% of the battery-backup-unit cell market and that related sales would grow more than 70% this year. Global Info Research forecast the AI data-center server backup market to rise from $989 million last year to $2.8 billion in 2032. Shinyoung Securities estimated third-quarter small-battery sales of 1.31 trillion won and operating profit of 20 billion won. The cylindrical LFP backup cell was first shown on September 29 at Data Centre World Asia in Singapore.

**Links:**

**Commentary:** Next June is the mass-production target in the industry-source account, the 70% sales increase is a second-quarter call outlook, and 20 billion won is a brokerage estimate for third-quarter profit.

## III. Tariffs, Export Orders, and Warehouses

### 7. U.S.-Mexico auto and metals talks slow, and both sides deny a breakdown (Tariffs)
**Summary:** A Quartz report updated on October 16, citing The Wall Street Journal, said Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick has opposed deep cuts to the 25% tariff on Mexican automobiles and the 50% tariffs on steel and aluminum, and has also objected to lower duties on finished goods made from those metals, slowing a deal that would have traded tariff relief for Mexican concessions. Officials on both sides said talks continue and rejected the idea of a standstill. A Reuters story on October 15, also citing the Journal and people familiar with the talks, reported that Mexico's deputy trade minister, Luis Rosendo Gutierrez, told the Journal there was progress, and that a U.S. Commerce Department spokesperson said the department is in constant dialogue with Mexico. Quartz added that Mexico has proposed taxing only the share of a vehicle's value that originates outside North America, while the United States has pushed for 50% American-made parts as a condition for lower rates. President Claudia Sheinbaum said on Tuesday that her government is buying more American goods and curbing imports from third countries. Quartz wrote that Mexico's goods-trade surplus with the United States rose 51% in the first eight months of the year to more than $280 billion, and that more than 80% of Mexico's exports go to the United States.

## IV. Fuel and Ocean Freight

### 10. The IEA agrees to release more than 20 million barrels of diesel as soon as possible (Fuel)
**Summary:** After its October 15 meeting, the International Energy Agency Governing Board said about 330 million barrels have been released and agreed to complete the remaining roughly 100 million barrels in a timely way consistent with national circumstances. Some members have released more than they pledged in March, and others intend to do so, so the total is set to exceed the original 400 million barrels. The statement said members agreed to accelerate the remaining volumes, with specific plans already in place, including more than 20 million barrels of diesel as soon as possible, and it encouraged members with production and refining capacity to boost crude and products, particularly diesel. A Reuters story carried by Mining Weekly on October 16 said the board took that decision on Thursday, after the Iran war and the Russia-Ukraine conflict pushed diesel prices to record highs in many markets. Abhishek Kumar of Sparta Commodities said the 20 million-plus barrels look like an acceleration of existing commitments rather than genuinely new volumes. Reuters added that, earlier this month, EU governments had discussed a French proposal for 50 million barrels of diesel plus 50 million barrels of crude. Spain said on Thursday it would release 4.4 million barrels from strategic reserves in October. It has released 3.7 million of the 11.5 million barrels it pledged in March.

### 11. Asia-Europe spot rates stop falling, and October 19 all-in prices may already be revised down (Containers)
**Summary:** The Loadstar reported on October 16 that Drewry's World Container Index rose 6% on Shanghai-Rotterdam to $3,522 per 40-foot container and 7% on Shanghai-Genoa to $3,945, ending about three months of declines on the Asia-Europe trades. Carriers moved new freight-all-kinds prices to October 19: Hapag-Lloyd at $4,200 to North Europe and $4,600 to the Mediterranean; CMA CGM at $4,600 and $4,700; MSC at $4,500 to both North and South Europe. A European forwarder told the paper that day that those prices had already been revised down, almost back to the first half of October, and that demand was still flat. MSC and CMA CGM set the validity of their prices through October 31. Jon Charles, head of product at WiseTech, said only 9.9% of Asia-Europe shipments arrived on time in September and that delays of eight days or more now account for almost half of volume. MSC said its Asia-North Europe Britannia service would be blanked in weeks 45 and 46. On the trans-Pacific, Shanghai-Los Angeles rose 3% to $7,884 and Shanghai-New York fell 1% to $10,075. West Coast forwarder Freight Right said carriers spent the week clearing Golden Week backlogs, so tight space for new bookings does not by itself mean stronger demand.

### 12. Very large crude carriers from the Gulf of Oman to China fall from 841 to about 776 (Tankers)
**Summary:** The American Journal of Transportation on October 16 carried a Reuters report that the cost of shipping oil on the largest tankers fell this week from record highs. Baltic Exchange data put very large crude carriers from the Gulf of Oman to China at about 776 on the Worldscale basis, down from 841 last week. That 841 print was the highest since the rate was launched earlier this year, after gains that followed the outbreak of the Iran war. Kpler analyst Matthew Wright said demand on the route softened after the latest attacks, because higher Middle East Gulf freight and lower export volumes reduced demand. Michael Ryan of Sparta Commodities said Trump administration comments about possibly removing sanctions on some shadow-fleet tankers pushed rates down, since a removal would add ships that can carry oil. The report said rates remain close to record highs and also eased this week on routes such as West Africa to Asia. The war has increased ship-to-ship transfers used to move oil out of the Strait of Hormuz, which supports tanker demand.

**Links:**

- [American Journal of Transportation (Reuters) — Supertanker rates fall from record highs](https://www.ajot.com/news/supertanker-rates-fall-from-record-highs)

**Commentary:** The 776 and 841 readings are Worldscale points, not a dollar rate per barrel, and the drop is from a level the report still describes as close to the high.
