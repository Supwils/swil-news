### 1. Reuters: Advanced packaging becomes AI’s next battleground; Nvidia said to fund billions for ASE/SPIL CoWoS (Packaging)
**Summary:** Reuters reported from Taipei on Nov 6 that the race to build AI infrastructure is pouring billions into advanced chip packaging, once a back-office step and now a critical bottleneck, with Nvidia, AMD, and Taiwanese specialists expanding capacity while toolmakers develop new equipment. Yole Group forecasts advanced-packaging revenue to overtake traditional packaging for the first time in 2026, with capital spending near about $28 billion. TSMC dominates leading technologies including CoWoS, but capacity limits are pushing designers to diversify; an industry executive said Nvidia is providing billions of dollars in financing to expand CoWoS capacity at ASE unit Siliconware Precision Industries (SPIL) for new plants and tools—scale not previously reported. Nvidia did not respond; ASE declined to comment on customer-related market rumors. AMD has also pledged more than $10 billion across Taiwan’s semiconductor industry, including ASE, SPIL, and Powertech; Powertech plans about $400 million for an advanced-packaging JV with Broadcom in Singapore, with production possibly in 2028.

### 2. Tool lead times stretch: front-end equipment makers advance 2028 supply deals, some open 2029 PO talks (Equipment)

ASML’s recent results said lithography supply through 2028 looked intact while it weighs further capacity expansion. Korean observers warned Samsung and SK hynix must move early on tool slots as front-end and back-end buildouts collide.

### 3. Shanghai copper spot premiums hit a record: ~2,615 yuan/mt average on Nov 6 as inventories plunge (Copper)
**Summary:** Shanghai Metals Market (SMM) analysis on Nov 6 said #1 copper cathode spot premiums versus the SHFE copper 2611 contract were quoted at about 2,480–2,750 yuan/mt, averaging about 2,615 yuan/mt—up about 515 yuan/mt from the prior session and a record high—while the average spot price was about 109,600 yuan/mt. As of Nov 5, social inventories in major Chinese regions fell to about 62,600 mt versus about 134,400 mt a year earlier; SHFE copper warrants fell another about 2,247 mt to about 4,327 mt on Nov 6. Drivers include earlier U.S. refined-copper tariff expectations that diverted metal toward the U.S., with China’s Jan–Sep cathode imports at about 2.1383 million mt, down about 16.34% year on year; October maintenance at 11 smelters cut about 103,200 mt of output; and CIIE-related waterway controls through Nov 10 may slow some arrivals. Tight prompt supply into the 2611 delivery window continues to support premiums.

### 4. Rare-earth pause clock ticks to Nov 10; European dysprosium/terbium still many times China’s price (Rare earths)
**Summary:** Early-November reviews note that China’s partial suspension of October 9, 2025 export-control measures—covering five additional medium/heavy rare earths and related technology/extraterritorial rules—runs legally through Nov 10, 2026; without a new formal notice, those measures could resume from Nov 11. Broader U.S.–China trade messaging has pointed to an extension toward about Jan 10, 2027, but whether legal texts already carry the suspensions past Nov 9–10 remains contested, so buyers should watch official notices rather than verbal truce talk. Separately, April 2025 licensing for seven medium and heavy rare earths including dysprosium and terbium was never paused, and European spot quotes remain far above China. Critical Minerals Signals, citing SMM around Nov 5, put European dysprosium oxide near $3,300–4,200/kg versus about $215–220/kg in China—roughly a 15–19× gap—with thin European inventories and “prices but little available metal.” Motley Fool on Nov 5 also flagged the Nov 10 pause window for non-China supply pricing.
