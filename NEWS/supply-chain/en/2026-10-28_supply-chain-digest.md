## I. Chips and Critical Materials

### 1. SK hynix says memory shortages may last through 2030 as HBM demand exceeds supply by more than 2x (chips)
**Summary:** SK hynix reported on October 28 third-quarter revenue of 104.61 trillion won and operating profit of 83.33 trillion won, up about 327.9% and 557.2% year over year, a sixth straight record quarter, with operating margin above 80%. The company said virtually all DRAM and HBM capacity is sold out through 2029, more than half of 2027–2028 capacity is locked under long-term agreements, and the global memory shortage is expected to persist through 2030. On the earnings call, it said current HBM customer demand is more than twice what it can supply; lower stack counts by some buyers were framed as a way to secure more cubes at the same capacity, raising demand for base dies and back-end steps. Capex this year is planned at least about 40 trillion won, and HBM4 is set to become the main product from the fourth quarter.

### 2. Nvidia signs capacity deals with six suppliers: substrates up about 2.5x and some optical-engine capacity targeting a 15-fold jump (chips)
**Summary:** Supply Chain Dive on October 28 reviewed Nvidia's October 21 supplier expansion plan: agreements with Ibiden, Samsung Electro-Mechanics, and Unimicron to raise substrate manufacturing capacity about 2.5 times in aggregate over three years; an Amkor pact to lift interposer-containing wafer processing capacity in South Korea by about 300% by end-2028; about $1.1 billion for KYEC U.S. test facilities targeting high-volume production around April 2027; and a SPIL plan to expand advanced optical-engine capacity in Taiwan about 15-fold by mid-2027, with another doubling targeted by end-2028. Ibiden received a multibillion-dollar capacity reservation to roughly triple its Japan plant, with new output ramping from the first quarter of 2028; Unimicron's Taiwan expansion is slated to start in the second quarter of 2027 and reach full capacity by mid-2028. CEO Jensen Huang said demand is extraordinary and capacity is the constraint.

### 3. Samsung Electro-Mechanics says AI data-center FCBGA remains short and will seek price talks (substrates)
**Summary:** At its third-quarter earnings call on October 28, Samsung Electro-Mechanics said demand for flip-chip ball grid array (FCBGA) package substrates for AI data centers has surged beyond what production can fill, and it expects the shortage to continue. As AI server CPUs and accelerators get larger and more layered, and advanced packaging spreads, the company expects the gap to deepen for high-spec parts. It said it has signed long-term supply contracts with major North American tech customers to expand high-value data-center products and will negotiate prices with customers in light of higher raw-material costs and tight supply-demand conditions to improve profitability.

## II. Batteries, Industrial Base, and Capacity Relocation

### 4. DOE launches BattMan battery manufacturing accelerator to move lab tech onto domestic lines (batteries)
**Summary:** The U.S. Department of Energy's Office of Critical Minerals and Energy Innovation announced on October 28 a national consortium, the Battery Manufacturing Accelerator (BattMan), led by Pacific Northwest National Laboratory with Argonne, Oak Ridge, and the National Laboratory of the Rockies, plus universities and industry. The goal is to shorten the path from lab discovery to commercial battery manufacturing, cut costs, and strengthen the U.S. battery manufacturing supply chain. BattMan plans to combine super intelligence and advanced manufacturing in a data-driven ecosystem, aiming over time for self-optimizing lines with real-time process monitoring, digital twins, and closed-loop control. It is supported by the Advanced Materials and Manufacturing Technologies Office and is opening industrial and academic partnerships.

**Links:**

- [U.S. Department of Energy — DOE Launches Battery Manufacturing Accelerator to Strengthen U.S. Competitiveness](https://www.energy.gov/cmei/ammto/articles/doe-launches-battery-manufacturing-accelerator-strengthen-us-competitiveness)

**Commentary:** Policy emphasis is shifting from opening mines to scaling new chemistries in production—the pilot and process gap.

### 5. MIIT and partners issue the 15th Five-Year Plan for industrial base capability, targeting relief by 2030 (policy)
**Summary:** China's government portal, citing People's Daily on October 27, reported that the Ministry of Industry and Information Technology and seven other departments jointly issued the 15th Five-Year Plan for Improving Industrial Base Capability, described as the first publicly released plan focused on the industrial foundation. Anchored in basically realizing new industrialization, it targets clear relief of industrial-base weaknesses by 2030, with broad gains in innovation, self-reliance, and high-quality product supply, higher basic-product quality, a set of key foundational products and internationally influential brands, and new manufacturing single champions and specialized "little giant" firms. The plan sets 22 priority tasks across seven areas covering self-reliance, innovation capacity, technology foundations, quality, firm competitiveness, product adoption, and the development ecosystem.

**Summary:** Shanghai's Economy and Informatization Commission published on October 27 the Action Plan for Accelerating Next-Generation Smart Manufacturing (issued October 22), centered on AI fusion across manufacturing and smart factories as the key vehicle. ## III. Logistics, Tariffs, and Organizational Capability

### 8. About 35,700 import boxes await rail at LA–Long Beach, with more than 10,000 dwelling at least nine days (logistics)
**Summary:** FreightWaves reported on October 28 that as of October 26 about 35,688 import containers were waiting for on-dock rail at Los Angeles and Long Beach, including about 10,562 that had been on terminals at least nine days. At Los Angeles, 8,133 of 19,626 rail-bound boxes (about 41.4%) hit that threshold; at Long Beach it was 2,429 of 16,062 (about 15.1%). Pacific Merchant Shipping Association data put average rail-container dwell at 7.19 days in September; the ports handled about 1.04 million and 997,177 TEUs that month, both records. C.H. Robinson cited Southern California intermodal railcar shortages extending import dwell; a watchmen's union has also authorized a strike as early as November 1 that could affect terminals handling about 85% of Long Beach and 33% of Los Angeles cargo.

### 9. U.S. revises Section 338 tariff list on selected Canadian goods effective October 30 (tariffs)
**Summary:** World Ports Organization reported on October 28 that a presidential proclamation dated October 23 under Section 338 of the Tariff Act of 1930 revises the roughly 50% additional duties on selected Canadian goods, effective 12:01 a.m. Eastern Time on October 30 for goods entered for consumption or withdrawn from warehouse. Certain plywood, paper and paperboard, packaging materials, and selected industrial machinery and refrigeration equipment leave the surcharge list, while more than 100 product lines are added, including certain paints, soaps, artificial waxes, vinyl flooring, fiberboard, carpets, and industrial materials. Coverage turns on specific HTS classifications, applies whether or not goods qualify as USMCA originating, and may stack with Section 232 duties; CBP will issue implementing guidance.

**Links:**

- [World Ports Organization — US revises tariffs on Canadian goods ahead of October 30 changes](https://www.worldports.org/us-revises-tariffs-on-canadian-goods-ahead-of-october-30-changes/)

**Commentary:** Rates are not uniformly cut, but the list keeps shifting—compliance and schedule uncertainty remain the real cost for cross-border chains.

### 10. Gartner: nearly half of supply chains cut headcount by an average 10.5% due to AI, mostly at entry level (organization)
**Summary:** Gartner said on October 28 that a survey of 303 supply-chain leaders across North America, Western Europe, Asia-Pacific, and Latin America from May 5 to June 11, 2026—all from organizations already using AI to some degree—found nearly half cut headcount by an average of 10.5% over two years primarily because of AI. Sixty-six percent of leaders said reductions concentrated at entry level, more than double the share reporting cuts at other levels. Analyst Tess Frenzel warned that entry-level thinning is outpacing talent-pipeline redesign and could weaken future AI capability; high-performing organizations are raising entry-level expectations for domain knowledge, analytics, and supply-chain experience while shifting work away from repetitive tasks toward consequential decisions.

### 11. Durban port congestion halts some South African production lines as firms shift to air freight (logistics)
**Summary:** Freight News reported on October 28 that vessel delays and congestion at Durban Gateway Terminal are disrupting South African manufacturing schedules. Zoeller Waste Trucks said six containers on two vessels sat at anchorage outside Durban for more than a month, stopping refuse-truck assembly for lack of German and Polish kit imports. An anonymous supplier said customers considered canceling contracts and the firm switched to air freight while absorbing margin hits; a South African Freight and Logistics Association adviser said delays have caused plant stoppages and production postponements. Association modeling pointed to about 18 extra days through affected chains, and even about 4,600 moves a day would take roughly a month to clear the queue. The terminal operator said it has committed more than 2 billion rand over three years to modernize equipment and infrastructure.
