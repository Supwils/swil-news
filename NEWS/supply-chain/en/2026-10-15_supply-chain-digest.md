## I. Chips and Memory

### 1. TSMC says demand still exceeds supply and lifts 2026 dollar revenue growth to about 44% (Wafers)
**Summary:** TSMC on October 15 reported third-quarter revenue of NT$1,494.24 billion, up 50.9% year on year, or $46.72 billion, up 41.2% year on year and 16.2% from the prior quarter. Net income was NT$763.06 billion, up 68.7% year on year. Gross margin was 66.4% and operating margin was 58.7%. By wafer revenue, 2 nm was 8%, 3 nm 33%, 5 nm 28%, and 7 nm 10%, so 7 nm and more advanced nodes were 79%. Fourth-quarter revenue guidance is $52.3 billion to $54.3 billion, with gross margin of 65% to 67%. On the call, management raised full-year 2026 revenue growth to about 44% in dollar terms and reiterated a 2026 capital budget of $60 billion to $64 billion. C.C. Wei said that after weighing cloud-provider spending, data-center construction, power, and other components, demand is still higher than supply and the gap remains large. The company said nearly 20 fabs are in progress, 13 of them leading-edge and advanced-packaging fabs in Taiwan and another five to six overseas. Arizona Fab 2 is built and tools are being installed, with volume production in the third quarter of 2027, slightly ahead of the prior schedule. An additional 3 nm fab in Tainan is scheduled for volume production in the first half of 2028. Teams have been sent to evaluate Texas, and TSMC is discussing a customer Terafab that TSMC would run. No conclusion yet.

### 2. Reuters: Solidigm ties a U.S. NAND fab to a Dalian equipment license (NAND)
**Summary:** A Reuters story datelined October 14 said two people familiar with the matter spoke on Wednesday: SK Hynix's U.S. unit Solidigm is seeking Washington's approval to export more U.S. chipmaking tools into unused NAND space in Dalian, and is tying that license to building a NAND factory in the United States. SK Hynix told Reuters that equipment installation for Mod B is being carried out in close consultation with and under authorization from the U.S. government, and that no capacity expansion is being sought. The space was completed in 2023. Sources said Mod B already has some tools and room for many more. One person said the Commerce Department has not yet issued a license for more U.S. equipment and technology in 2027, and that Solidigm has applied for additional tools for Mod B. One person said more output in China would help fund a U.S. fab. Another said Solidigm is weighing an IPO as early as next year. TechInsights analyst Walt Coon said NAND pricing has increased sevenfold since mid-2023 and is expected to stay tight for the next few years. Reuters added that SK Hynix's Seoul-listed shares slumped 11% on Thursday amid a broader tech selloff.

**Links:**

**Commentary:** The link between a U.S. fab and the Dalian license comes from two people familiar with the matter. The company's public line is that Mod B is not a capacity expansion, and Commerce had not responded.

### 3. Memory stocks drop over five sessions as the market prices CXMT and a 2029 glut (DRAM)
**Summary:** CNBC reported on October 15 that over the last five trading days SK Hynix fell 26%, Samsung fell 22%, and Micron fell about 11%. Earlier in the week, ChangXin Memory Technologies announced a server-memory product on a new architecture. Higher density raised share concerns. Ben Barringer of Quilter Cheviot said an architectural change that yields only 50% would scrap half the chips. Kevin Cassidy of Rosenblatt said he does not think the development swings the supply-demand balance. Traders were also discussing a Maeil Business Newspaper column in which Professor Hwang of Seoul National University and Ahn Ki-hyun of the Korea Semiconductor Industry Association expect a flood of new supply and a "red ocean" in 2029, and a possible end to the supercycle by 2030. Elon Musk replied on X that China will be making a lot of RAM in 2028. Jeongku Choi of Counterpoint told CNBC that DRAM prices in 2028 could be about 23% lower than in 2027 and still about 230% higher than in 2025, with suppliers and buyers signing three- to five-year agreements that include upfront payments and price bands.

## II. Batteries, Materials, and Factory Automation

### 4. Changan and CATL raise the joint-venture target from 50 GWh to 75 GWh (Batteries)
**Summary:** electrive reported on October 15 that Changan Automobile, on the Shenzhen Stock Exchange investor-interaction platform, said joint cell capacity with CATL will rise from a previously planned 50 GWh to 75 GWh, with closer work on solid-state and sodium-ion cells. CarNewsChina on October 14, describing that platform reply, said the addition is 25 GWh on a site of about 700,000 square meters, with investment of 5 billion yuan, in a high-tech zone between Chongqing and Sichuan. Operations are planned for 2027, and the extra 25 GWh is to be reached within three years. The joint venture has existed since 2023 and already supplies Avatr, Deepal, and Nevo. electrive also said Changan's Golden Bell solid-state targets, including 400 Wh/kg and more than 1,500 kilometers of range, are development targets whose series performance is not yet confirmed.

### 5. UK castings report: offshore wind needs more than 2 million tonnes by 2050 (Castings)
**Summary:** The Cast Metals Federation and the Offshore Wind Growth Partnership on October 15 published the UK Castings & Forgings Bill of Works. The text says the UK offshore-wind pipeline from 2026 to 2050 will require more than two million tonnes of castings and forgings. If manufacturers have or develop the capability, they could supply components accounting for 37% of that demand by weight, generating up to £14.6 billion of gross value added by 2050, equal to 41% of the market by value, and potentially more than 10,800 new jobs. The same page's headline says 13,000 jobs. Domestic suppliers now operate in markets representing about 8% of demand. Existing capability could lift participation to 14%, and further investment in forged rings, hub castings, and drivetrain components could reach 37%.

**Links:**

- [Cast Metals Federation — New report reveals 13,000 jobs boost for UK castings and forgings from offshore wind push](https://www.castmetalsfederation.com/news/2026/10/new-report-reveals-13000-jobs-boost-for-uk-castings-and-forgings-from-offshore-wind-push)

**Commentary:** The 10,800 figure is the job potential in the body text, while the page title says 13,000. Both are scenarios through 2050, not hiring announced for this year.

## III. Trade Routes and Investment Policy

### 7. WTO chief: some costlier detour routes will stay (Trade)
**Summary:** Reuters reported from Bangkok on October 15 that WTO Director-General Ngozi Okonjo-Iweala, interviewed on the sidelines of the IMF and World Bank annual meetings, said the wars in Ukraine and the Middle East and a wave of tariffs will have a lasting effect on trade flows, and that companies will keep some costlier alternative routes as a hedge. The WTO forecast a 17% drop in Middle East exports this year and imports down about 15%, tied to the Iran war. Private-sector experts expect a rebound once the Strait of Hormuz is open, because it is the cheaper route. She said supply chains are reconfiguring. Last week the WTO raised its 2026 world merchandise trade-volume growth forecast to 3.9%, from a 1.9% baseline in March, with AI-related demand offsetting the Middle East war. She added that the impact of AI is masking other tendencies in trade that are not positive. Intra-African trade is about 20% of African trade, intra-EU trade about 60%, and trade still governed by WTO rules is nearly three-quarters of all trade.

**Links:**

- [Euronext (Reuters) — WTO chief sees lasting impact on trade from Middle East war and geopolitics](https://live.euronext.com/en/financial-news/wto-chief-sees-lasting-impact-trade-middle-east-war-and-geopolitics)

**Commentary:** The 17% and 15% figures are the WTO's forecast for Middle East trade this year. The claim that detours will endure is the director-general's judgment, not a published list of new routes.

### 8. Akazawa on Thursday: the Japan-U.S. chip project must be repayable and useful to Japanese firms (Policy)
**Summary:** A Reuters story with an updated October 15 dateline says Japan will invest up to $21 billion in two New York chip factories jointly owned by the U.S. government and Japanese interests and operated by GlobalFoundries, which the company announced on Wednesday. Trade Minister Ryosei Akazawa told reporters on Thursday that expanding semiconductor capacity with the United States matters for the economic security of both countries. He rejected criticism that the initiative makes Japan subservient, saying projects face rigorous bilateral reviews so they are financially viable, capable of repayment, and beneficial to Japanese companies. Reuters also wrote that available free cash flow is split evenly until a set allocation is reached, after which 90% goes to the United States, and that Japan has announced two batches worth over $100 billion combined under the $550 billion U.S. investment arrangement. GlobalFoundries CEO Tim Breen said at least some of the Japanese capital will flow back to toolmakers such as Tokyo Electron.

**Commentary:** The two fabs and the $21 billion cap were announced on Wednesday. What Thursday added is Akazawa's public reply and the cash-flow split as Reuters described it.
