## I. Chips & Critical Materials

### 1. Nvidia Server Partners Halt Southeast Asia Shipments as U.S. Export Controls Spill Over (Semiconductors)
**Summary:** Nikkei Asia reported on Nov 21 that at least two of Nvidia's server partners have suspended shipments to Southeast Asian customers and are urgently reviewing orders in response to the latest U.S. AI-chip export controls. The U.S. Commerce Department has brought data centers in Singapore, Malaysia, Indonesia, Thailand and Brazil into a higher-risk licensing perimeter: overseas facilities that provide Chinese firms with compute above a set threshold must obtain licenses within 90 days or face future export restrictions. One supplier said it halted all Southeast Asia shipments from last week and stopped taking new regional customers for now; approvals to ship Blackwell products into the region had already tightened under stricter end-customer checks. Southeast Asia is among the fastest-growing AI data-center hubs, so the compliance pause directly disrupts local rack and server delivery schedules.

**Commentary:** Controls have moved from direct China sales to third-country data centers and server deliveries, turning Southeast Asia from a capacity destination into a compliance front line.

### 2. Samsung Memory Capacity Reportedly Booked Through 2029; Micron Warns 2027 Will Be Tighter (Memory)
**Summary:** Wccftech reported on Nov 21, citing South Korea's Economy Tribune, that most of Samsung Electronics' 2027–2029 memory capacity is already locked via long-term agreements, with some customers seeking deals that extend into 2031. Samsung had previously confirmed multiyear deals with about 10 major customers and talks with another 10 nearing completion, aiming to place roughly 60%–70% of medium- to long-term capacity under multiyear contracts by 2030. Industry sources estimate leading makers can meet only about 60%–70% of customer demand; 2027 HBM supply was already sold out, and conventional DRAM wafer capacity is also nearly exhausted. Micron CTO Scott DeBoer told Yahoo Finance on Nov 20 that supply-demand imbalance will persist and that “2027 is expected to be tighter than 2026,” even as the company invests more than $250 billion in fab expansion that still takes years to ramp.

**Links:**

- [Wccftech — Samsung's Memory Production Reportedly Almost Fully Booked Through 2029](https://wccftech.com/samsungs-memory-production-reportedly-almost-fully-booked-through-2029-as-customers-seek-supply-deals-until-2031/)
- [Yonhap Infomax — Micron CTO Warns Memory Chip Supply Constraints to Intensify in 2027](https://en.infomaxai.com/news/articleView.html?idxno=149417)

**Commentary:** AI is turning memory from a cyclical commodity into multiyear strategic stockpiling, and capacity adds are not keeping up with order locks as shortages spread from HBM into conventional DRAM.

### 3. TrendForce: HBM4 Prices May Rise 1.5–2.7x Next Year as More DRAM Wafers Shift to HBM (Memory)
**Summary:** Seoul Economic Daily reported on Nov 19 that TrendForce, at its Korea roadshow, projected HBM4 prices next year at about $3–$5.5 per gigabit, roughly 1.5 to 2.7 times this year's about $2.05. HBM4E terms above $5 per gigabit are also being discussed. Samsung, SK hynix and Micron may devote nearly 30% of DRAM wafer input to HBM next year, up from about 23% this year; HBM consumes roughly three to four times as many wafers as conventional DRAM for the same capacity. TrendForce expects conventional DRAM contract prices to rise about 10%–15% quarter on quarter in Q4, with further but decelerating gains through the first half of 2027. China's CXMT may lift monthly capacity only from about 30,000 to about 36,000 wafers next year, limiting how quickly the HBM gap with Korean suppliers can close.

- [Seoul Economic Daily — Latest HBM Prices to Jump Up to 2.7-Fold Next Year](https://en.sedaily.com/business/2026/11/19/latest-hbm-prices-to-jump-up-to-27-fold-next-year)

### 4. Niron Magnetics Plans $1.8 Billion Rare-Earth-Free Magnet Campus in Alabama (Critical Materials)
**Summary:** Alabama Gov. Kay Ivey and Niron Magnetics announced on Nov 20 that the company will build an about $1.8 billion, roughly 1.6-million-square-foot advanced manufacturing campus at Dothan's Westgate Super Site, targeting about 10,000 tons a year of iron-nitride permanent magnets and about 700 high-wage jobs within five years. Construction is expected in 2028, with production in 2029. Minneapolis-based Niron makes magnets that do not rely on rare earths; its first full-scale plant is planned for Sartell, Minnesota, in 2027 at up to about 1,500 tons a year, with Alabama as the second site. The company notes that roughly 90% of global rare-earth magnet output is concentrated overseas, and its products serve AI infrastructure, automotive, industrial, defense and consumer electronics. Reuters also reported the investment the same day.

**Summary:** Batteries News reported on Nov 21 that POSCO group signed a long-term lithium supply agreement with Contemporary Amperex Technology (CATL), which holds about 40% of the global EV battery market, covering lithium for EV battery production from 2026 through 2028, with deliveries already underway. POSCO is extending resource-securing capabilities built in steel into lithium and using the deal to strengthen its sales base in China, the world's largest EV battery market. The move echoes Korean materials majors' strategy of locking customers and resources, matching cell makers' demand for multiyear feedstock visibility.

### 6. China Launches “Green Chain Together” Initiative at COP31 (Green Supply Chains)
**Summary:** CGTN reported on Nov 21 that China launched a global green and low-carbon supply-chain initiative (“Green Chain Together”) at a China Pavilion side event at COP31 in Antalya, Türkiye. The initiative focuses on full life-cycle environmental data, green assessment rules and mutual recognition of certifications, calling for a collaborative global supply-chain mechanism. Co-initiator Chinese Research Academy of Environmental Sciences outlined three priorities: shared life-cycle datasets covering raw materials, manufacturing, logistics, consumption and disposal; jointly developed classification, management and supplier-evaluation standards; and a unified disclosure and verification framework to ease cross-border green trade. A Ministry of Ecology and Environment climate official said inconsistent data, differing assessment rules and weak certification recognition remain barriers in cross-border chains.

**Links:**

**Commentary:** Green compliance is shifting from ESG language to contests over mutual-recognition rules that may reshape supplier screening alongside tariffs and market access.

## III. Logistics, Trade & Manufacturing Disruptions

### 7. LA/Long Beach Port Watchmen Reach Tentative Deal, Averting Immediate Strike Risk (Logistics)
**Summary:** The Pacific Maritime Association said on Nov 21 it reached a tentative agreement with ILWU Local 26 covering about 250 port watchmen, subject to member ratification, with the current contract extended pending that vote. The prior contract expired at 12:01 a.m. Saturday after the union authorized a strike. Watchmen control terminal-gate access, conduct security patrols and report safety issues; they are only about 2% of ILWU port labor but work at terminals handling roughly 85% of Long Beach and 33% of Los Angeles cargo. If other longshore workers had honored picket lines, a wider stoppage could have followed. Reuters, NBC Los Angeles and others reported that the deal temporarily removes an immediate disruption risk at the two busiest U.S. container hubs.

### 10. Bangladesh RMG Unrest Disrupts Hundreds of Factories and Threatens Export Orders (Manufacturing)
**Summary:** Dhaka Tribune reported on Nov 21 that Bangladesh's ready-made garment sector remains under pressure from wage-related unrest, with stoppages, road blockades and clashes across Savar, Ashulia, Gazipur and Mirpur. The Bangladesh Garment Buying House Association estimates losses of Tk3,000–4,000 crore over recent weeks; BGMEA data showed production disrupted at about 341 factories on Thursday, including 194 closed indefinitely under the Labour Act. Industry voices warn that October–December is critical for winter-garment shipments, delays may force air freight or discounts, and some international buyers are already cutting new orders, raising the risk of shifts to competing countries.
