## I. Chips and Memory

### 1. CXMT says a 4F² server memory product is due by year-end, with no yield disclosed (memory)
**Summary:** Bloomberg, via The Edge Singapore on October 13, reported that CXMT president Cao Kanyu told an industry conference in Shanghai the company will launch a data-center server memory product on a self-developed 4F² architecture before the end of the year. A company spokesperson confirmed the remarks. Seoul Economic Daily the same day identified the event as the 4th Integrated Circuit and Chiplet Conference on October 9–10, and said the first product is a server DDR5 RDIMM. Bloomberg wrote that success would put CXMT ahead of Samsung and SK Hynix on that threshold even though U.S. sanctions block the firm from ASML's most advanced tools, and that Cao gave no further detail. On Tuesday Samsung fell 7.4 percent and SK Hynix 10 percent, after Micron closed down 4 percent the night before. Counterpoint data put CXMT's DRAM share at 10 percent by the second quarter of this year, from 4 percent a year earlier. A Morningstar analyst said commercialization could raise memory density by 30 percent, with a meaningful gap before profitable yields. The report said the claim is not an immediate threat to Samsung, SK Hynix, and Micron in high-bandwidth memory.

**Summary:** The Cyprus Mail on October 13, citing Reuters, reported that Broadcom chief executive Hock Tan said on Monday at a panel in Singapore that capital availability will become the primary constraint on artificial-intelligence growth as the buildout scales, while semiconductor producers are getting past earlier hardware bottlenecks such as persistent wafer and memory shortages. A Reuters analysis the same day said selling in South Korean chip shares ran into a fifth day on Tuesday, with the KOSPI down more than 6 percent during the session, and the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index on track for a sixth straight decline, about 1.5 percent lower intraday. The piece tied the mood to the cost of capital: Nvidia-backed data-center operator Firmus last week shelved a $5 billion IPO in Australia, and the yield on a bond CoreWeave sold in June rose to 15 percent from 9.625 percent at issue. The International Monetary Fund warned on Tuesday, in its financial stability report, that the consequences of an AI correction could be global.

### 3. DHL plans India's first dedicated chip warehouse in Dholera, with an initial outlay of about ₹50 crore (chip logistics)
**Summary:** Mint reported on October 13 that Vikas Anand, managing director of DHL Supply Chain India, said the company will build a semiconductor logistics and warehousing facility in Dholera, Gujarat, near Tata Electronics' fab. The initial investment is about ₹50 crore, operations are expected by August 2027, and spending may rise three or four times by 2030 depending on customer expansion. It would be DHL's first dedicated semiconductor warehouse in India; the company already stores some semiconductor raw materials in Gujarat and Assam. The money sits inside a €1 billion India program announced on November 13, 2025, of which about ₹5,000 crore is earmarked for DHL Supply Chain India and about 10 percent of that for semiconductors. Japan's NX Group signed a logistics partnership with the India Semiconductor Mission on September 9. Mint had reported on September 11, 2025, that Tata Electronics' 28-nanometre fab is expected to start in Dholera in 2027. Anand said the warehouse is still in planning, though preparatory work began two years ago.

## II. Rare Earths and Magnets

### 4. Aclara finishes basic engineering for a Louisiana heavy-rare-earth plant, and the capital estimate rises to $494 million (rare earths)
**Summary:** Aclara said on October 13 that Hatch has completed basic engineering for Project Dynamo, its planned heavy-rare-earth separation facility in Louisiana. Site preparation is targeted for the first quarter of 2027 and construction completion for the second half of 2028, both subject to financing and other customary conditions. The AACE Class 3 construction estimate is about $494 million, up from a $277 million conceptual estimate. Life-of-project average operating cost, excluding feedstock, is estimated at $26 per kilogram of oxide, down from $38. Average annual separated output, sized to full feed from Carina and Penco, is about 200 tonnes of dysprosium oxide (previously 140), 35 tonnes of terbium oxide (previously 25), 1,350 tonnes of yttrium oxide, 180 tonnes of gadolinium oxide, 190 tonnes of samarium oxide, and 1,300 tonnes of neodymium-praseodymium oxide (previously 1,100). Using independent Argus price assumptions, the company estimates an after-tax net present value of about $773 million at an 8 percent discount rate, an after-tax IRR of 27.9 percent, and a 2.6-year payback. A September 4 letter of interest from the U.S. Export-Import Bank considers up to $750 million and is non-binding.

**Links:**

- [Le Lézard (CNW) — Aclara completes basic engineering for its U.S. heavy rare earths separation facility in Louisiana](https://www.lelezard.com/en/news-aclara-completes-basic-engineering-for-its-us-heavy-rare-earths-separation-facility-in-louisiana-22399084.html)

### 5. HyProMag's Texas magnet-recycling hub secures power, with phase one still aimed at mid-2028 (magnets)
**Summary:** HyProMag USA said on October 13 that it has secured electrical capacity with Oncor for its Texas hub, covering cutting and finishing and phase one, with a substation upgrade as the path for phase two. Commissioning of cutting and finishing is targeted for the first half of 2027, initially finishing neodymium-iron-boron magnet blocks supplied from other HyProMag Group operations. Phase one is targeted for mid-2028 and is designed for about 550 tonnes a year of sintered NdFeB magnet blocks and finished magnets, plus about 484 tonnes of co-products, or about 1,034 tonnes of payable production. After phase two, blocks and cylinders are put at about 1,048 tonnes and co-products at about 478 tonnes, or about 1,526 tonnes in total. The company says its short-loop recycling uses 88 percent less energy and emits 85 percent less carbon than conventional methods. The release lists output, timing, and power as forward-looking.

**Summary:** The North Carolina governor's office announced on October 13 that employee-owned defense contractor Corvid Technologies will add 423 jobs in Iredell County and invest $95 million to expand its Mooresville campus, including a new 400,000-square-foot advanced manufacturing facility for engineering and production of vehicle launch systems. Chief executive David Robinson said manufacturing space has grown from 8,000 square feet to 800,000 square feet in two and a half years. Over that term the project is estimated to grow the state economy by $1.7 billion. State and company leaders discussed the project at the 2026 Farnborough International Airshow.

### 8. Lockheed Martin and Avio agree to make solid rocket motors, with no volume stated (munitions)
**Summary:** Lockheed Martin and Avio announced on October 13, at the Association of the United States Army annual meeting, a strategic cooperation agreement under which, after qualification, initial work will produce solid rocket motors for Extended Range GMLRS and the Precision Strike Missile. Avio USA chief executive James Syring said the factory is still under construction and the team is still growing its U.S. workforce and preparing to manufacture.

## IV. Ocean Freight and the Strait

### 9. Xeneta: September global on-time performance falls to 27 percent, and Far East–Europe is at 4 percent (containers)
**Summary:** FreightWaves reported on October 13, citing Xeneta's September schedule-reliability scorecard, that global container on-time performance fell for a fourth straight month, down 2 percentage points to 27 percent, and that the average delay across all arrivals rose to 6 days, 2.4 days more than at the start of the second quarter. Far East–Europe on-time performance fell to 4 percent, with an average delay of 9.9 days, the worst month for that lane since September 2021, when it was 3 percent. Asia–North America stayed at 20 percent on time, while the average delay rose 0.8 days from the prior month to 6.9 days. Xeneta's China-to-U.S. West Coast spot average reached $7,717 per FEU in early October, more than four times the $1,778 of September 2025 and 8 percent above the start of September 2026. Long-term rates rose to $3,649 per FEU on October 1, up 26 percent from the prior month. Singapore's average daily vessel queue nearly doubled to 22 ships, and the average wait rose from 1.9 days to 3.1 days. Announced October blank sailings cover at least 17 percent of planned Far East–Europe capacity and 13 percent on Far East–North America.

**Links:**

- [FreightWaves — Shippers waiting an extra week for US-bound containers](https://www.freightwaves.com/news/shippers-waiting-an-extra-week-for-us-bound-containers)

**Commentary:** The 4 percent and 9.9 days are September's Far East–Europe reliability and delay, and $7,717 is the early-October spot average, not a price for the whole market.

### 10. Iran International: reported attacks on commercial ships from October 5 to 11 rise to 12 (shipping)
**Summary:** Iran International reported on October 13 that UK Maritime Trade Operations warnings show 12 attacks on commercial vessels in and around the Strait of Hormuz and the Persian Gulf between October 5 and 11. Six of them were on October 11: five tankers and a container ship, struck in the strait and near Oman's Sohar anchorage. Several incidents were reported only after they occurred. The Joint Maritime Information Center's October 11 advisory had listed three confirmed attacks in the strait over the preceding 72 hours and another in the Persian Gulf. The center kept the Hormuz threat at SEVERE and said attacks were spreading from transit lanes to anchorages. U.S. Naval Cooperation and Guidance for Shipping data showed about 29 U.S.-facilitated transits a day over the preceding 72 hours, against a historical average of about 138 a day. Independent tracking indicated substantially fewer vessels actually passing the strait.
