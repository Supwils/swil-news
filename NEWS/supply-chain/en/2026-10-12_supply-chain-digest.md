## II. Batteries, Nickel, and Grid Equipment

### 3. EnergyX signs a $2 billion offtake with Trafigura, with output written for 2028 and 2029 (lithium)
**Summary:** Reuters reported on October 12 that lithium startup EnergyX signed a $2 billion, 10-year binding offtake with commodity trader Trafigura, under which Trafigura will buy 100,000 metric tons of lithium on a take-or-pay basis, with the value tied to prices near $20,000 a metric ton. The split is 5,000 metric tons a year of lithium hydroxide and 5,000 metric tons a year of lithium carbonate. Texas hydroxide is planned at 15,000 metric tons a year from 2029, of which Trafigura would buy 33 percent, and Chilean carbonate at 7,500 metric tons a year from 2028, of which Trafigura's share is 66 percent. The company, backed by General Motors and Eni, said it aims to start producing lithium in 2028 and that the deal helps a $690 million loan application to the U.S. Export-Import Bank, while the Chile and Texas plants still need more funding. Trafigura plans to sell first to Chinese customers and later into the U.S. market once a domestic battery supply chain exists.

**Commentary:** The 100,000 metric tons are a take-or-pay commitment on projects that are not yet built, and commercial production is still written for 2028 and after.

### 5. Rystad says the executive order hits storage harder than transformers, with rules due by December 24 (batteries)
**Summary:** Rystad Energy analysis published by OilPrice on October 12 says Executive Order 14421, signed by President Trump on August 26, covers substation transformers, grid-connected inverters, battery energy storage systems, high-voltage circuit breakers, and power-generation turbines, citing electricity demand from data centers and artificial intelligence. In the 12 months to June 2026, Rystad says China accounted for 50 percent of U.S. lithium-ion battery imports by value, South Korea 15 percent, and Japan 13 percent, and that neither Korea nor Japan can replace the Chinese volume on its own. Over the same period China supplied 3 percent of U.S. transformer imports and under 1 percent of circuit-breaker imports. South Korea, Mexico, and Canada together supplied 74 percent of breaker imports and 48 percent of transformers. China's reported inverter export value to the United States fell 34 percent from 2022 to 2025, and U.S. inverter capacity is set to reach 126 gigawatts by 2028. Department of Energy rules are due by December 24, and a broad definition of covered foreign-entity ownership could pull in suppliers assembling in South Korea or Mexico with Chinese-controlled components or capital.

## III. Reshoring and Defense Manufacturing

### 6. Kentucky launches ReshoreKY on October 14; $29.7 billion is an import-substitution estimate (reshoring)
**Summary:** The Lane Report on October 12 said the Kentucky Association of Manufacturers and the National Reshoring Center will launch ReshoreKY on October 14 at the Kentucky Industry Conference in Lexington, to match goods Kentucky companies now buy overseas with products local plants might make. The National Reshoring Center estimates about $29.7 billion in potential reshoring opportunities, roughly 31 percent of the state's imports. Organizers also cite about 6,000 manufacturers, 254,500 manufacturing workers, and $49.8 billion in manufacturing value added, against about $96.5 billion in imports and $50.6 billion in exports. The report says the $29.7 billion is not committed investment or new production, and the next step is to judge which overseas orders can actually move into the state.

**Summary:** MeriTalk reported on October 12 that Acting Army Secretary Adam Telle, speaking at the Association of the United States Army annual meeting in Washington, said the Army plans a "manufacturing rodeo" early next year to test whether the defense industrial base can scale to military demand and to find where the supply chain breaks. He said the test covers both the Army's organic industrial base and the wider U.S. manufacturing network. ### 10. Ningbo-Zhoushan berth waits fall to about one day, and September container volume sets a monthly record (ports)
**Summary:** World Ports Organization reported on October 12, citing the port operator, that average container-ship berth waiting time at Ningbo-Zhoushan had fallen to about one day by October 9 after most of a backlog from three typhoons was cleared, and that some terminals were again taking ships on arrival.
