## I. Chips, Equipment, and Distribution

### 1. GlobalFoundries will operate two new New York fabs under a Japan-funded plan capped at $21 billion (wafers)
**Summary:** On October 14, GlobalFoundries said it has been selected as technology provider and operator for two new fabs at its Malta, New York, site under a U.S.-Japan initiative providing up to $21 billion. Japan supplies the capital; GlobalFoundries supplies technology and operations. An initial investment of about $10 billion is meant to speed a new 300mm fab expected online in 2029, adding about 900,000 wafers a year when fully operational. A further $10 billion would fund a second fab of roughly the same scale. The company expects about 27,000 construction and ecosystem jobs and more than 1,000 high-skilled manufacturing jobs, and it plans to break ground on the first fab later this year. It also agreed with the Commerce Department to reallocate $955 million of its existing CHIPS and Science Act award into silicon photonics, silicon-germanium, and advanced packaging in Malta and Burlington, Vermont. Commerce will receive equity of about 4 percent. Reuters, citing chief executive Tim Breen, reported that the U.S. stake rises from about 3 percent to about 7 percent, that the new factories will be jointly owned by the U.S. government and Japanese interests with Japan providing all the capital, and that the partners will decide on the second fab before the end of 2028. Breen named Renesas and Apple as initial customers. Executives said fee revenue lasts at least 20 years, with about $50 million next year for managing construction of the first fab. President Trump said on Truth Social that prior U.S. grants to the company were $1.63 billion and valued the 7 percent stake at more than $2 billion.

### 2. ASML says planned lithography supply for 2027 and 2028 still falls short of demand (equipment)
**Summary:** On October 14, ASML reported third-quarter net sales of €11.7 billion, a gross margin of 57.5 percent, and net income of €5.0 billion, with installed-base management sales of €3.3 billion. It sold 113 new lithography systems and 8 used systems. Fourth-quarter net sales are guided at €14.2 billion to €15.2 billion with a gross margin around 57 percent, and full-year 2026 sales at €44 billion to €45 billion with a gross margin around 56 percent. Chief executive Christophe Fouquet said in the release that some customers are interested in placing orders for 2029 and even 2030, that supply as currently planned for both 2027 and 2028 will not fully meet demand, and that the company is investigating a 2028 capacity increase beyond the 30 percent it had previously discussed. A footnote points back to last quarter's plan to raise 2027 low-NA EUV and DUV immersion capacity 30 percent from the 2026 base, with a further 30 percent for 2028 under study. On the earnings call, chief financial officer Roger Dassen said low-NA EUV output for 2026 is roughly 65 systems, nearly 50 percent more than the 44 shipped last year, while DUV immersion deliveries this year are expected to match 2025. The statements show a €509.7 million gain on equity investments; Dassen attributed about €500 million of net income to a higher valuation of the stake in Mistral AI.

Advanced packaging, test, and wafer-fab capacity are listed as areas still to watch.

## II. Critical Minerals and Batteries

### 5. Reuters: internal EU papers argue for closer critical-minerals ties with Vietnam (tungsten, rare earths)
**Summary:** Reuters reported on October 14 that internal EU documents it reviewed argue for securing more critical minerals from Vietnam to reduce reliance on China. Vietnam's deposits include tungsten, rare earths, antimony, bismuth, and bauxite, with gallium available as a bauxite by-product. Exports to the EU have been largely limited to tungsten and antimony. A paper from the EU delegation in Hanoi says Europe cannot afford to ignore Vietnam's rare earths and tungsten given the pace of rearmament and de-risking. The report and an annex, dated June 2026, recommend financial support for mining and processing to maintain and expand access to tungsten, bismuth, antimony, and titanium, and say China's export restrictions last year disrupted European supply chains. Four European and Vietnamese officials said party general secretary To Lam will visit Brussels next week, with meetings with EU leaders set for October 22. The visit has not been officially announced. A European Commission spokesperson said the executive is assessing concrete cooperation with Vietnam on critical raw materials. Vietnam is not among the EU's 16 strategic raw-material partners. The documents also treat a Lynas Rare Earths and LS Eco Energy project to produce rare-earth metals in Vietnam from imported feedstock as a risk of leaving the EU behind.

### 7. GAC-backed GBT says all-solid-state cells are in series production in Nansha at 1 GWh (batteries)
**Summary:** electrive reported on October 14 that Greater Bay Technology, backed by GAC Group, began series production of all-solid-state cells on October 13 in Nansha, Guangzhou, on a converted lithium-ion line. Current solid-state capacity is 1 GWh, and the rest of the plant can be converted up to 4 GWh. The company says a liquid precursor is injected and then solidified inside the cell, while electrodes, stacking, and formation stay on conventional lithium-ion equipment. Founder and chief technology officer Huang Xiangdong said the project moved from A-samples in April to series production in October. Cited energy density is 260 to 480 watt-hours per kilogram depending on cell generation and materials. The first generation now in production uses automotive pouch cells and established electrode materials. The company said two production cells passed tests at the National Power Battery Innovation Center and described them as tested under Chinese standard GB/T 48093.1-2026, which was published on August 28 and takes effect on January 1, 2028. Hubei Liming Lithium Technology is to integrate the cells into vehicle and stationary systems. GBT also cited a collaboration with an eVTOL development center in Shenzhen and said it is in advanced talks with several automakers.

**Links:**

- [electrive — GBT begins series production of solid-state battery cells](https://www.electrive.com/2026/10/14/gbt-begins-series-production-of-solid-state-battery-cells/)

**Commentary:** One gigawatt-hour is the current solid-state capacity on a converted line; the energy-density range and the "first certified" claim are the company's, and the cited national standard does not take effect until 2028.

**Summary:** electrive reported on October 14 that BMW said on Tuesday it will invest more than €100 million at Steyr, Austria, in a new plant for inverter power-module components, with production planned for summer 2029. The site already builds sixth-generation electric drives, introduced in February 2025 for Neue Klasse, on two lines. Harald Gottsche, managing director of BMW Motoren, said in-house power modules will let the plant source semiconductors and similar parts more flexibly on global markets. Steyr still supplies more than one million gasoline and diesel engines a year to the group's vehicle plants and employs about 4,700 people. The company did not say how many jobs the new investment adds.

### 10. Daktronics confirms the Ennistymon, Ireland, plant will close, with more than 100 jobs affected (manufacturing exit)
**Summary:** The Journal reported on October 14 that U.S. electronic-display company Daktronics confirmed on Wednesday it will exit manufacturing in Ireland. Closure of the Ennistymon plant in north Clare is expected to be substantially complete by mid-fiscal 2028, with more than 100 jobs affected. The company tied the shutdown to leaving the highly customized part of its International Transportation business, which serves rail, bus, and roadway customers outside North America. Chief executive Ramesh Jayaraman said exiting Ireland and aligning capacity is meant to improve factory utilization across the global network. Daktronics expects $13.5 million of cumulative pre-tax charges, about $5.3 million of that in the second quarter of fiscal 2027, and about $7.7 million of annualized pre-tax savings once the exit is fully in place. The Journal cited Irish accounts through the end of April 2025: pre-tax profit of €586,003, revenue down 8.5 percent from €17.98 million to €16.44 million, and 116 employees at period end.

## IV. Trade and Shipping

### 11. China's September exports rose 36.3 percent, and the monthly surplus was $133.53 billion (trade)
**Summary:** Reuters reported on October 14 that customs data released Wednesday showed China's September exports up 36.3 percent from a year earlier, above August's 25.0 percent and above a 25.5 percent forecast, the fastest gain since February's 39.6 percent. Imports rose 31.0 percent, compared with 28.2 percent in August and a 24.5 percent forecast. The September surplus widened to $133.53 billion, including a 20-month-high $34.01 billion surplus with the United States and a $34.8 billion surplus with the European Union. The surplus for the first nine months was $938.11 billion. The surplus topped $1 trillion in November last year and the full-year record was $1.189 trillion. Semiconductor exports rose 108.1 percent in value in the first nine months, and auto exports rose more than 50 percent in both value and volume. September crude-oil import volumes fell 21.5 percent year on year, and soybean import volumes fell 7.7 percent. The story also said September factory-gate prices rose at the fastest pace in more than four years, without giving that index a figure. A September summit between Xi Jinping and Donald Trump in Washington extended the tariff truce and agreed tariff cuts on $30 billion of goods from each side.
