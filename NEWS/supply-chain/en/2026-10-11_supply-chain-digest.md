## I. Chips and Packaging Materials

### 1. Samsung directs nearly 8 trillion won to Vietnam testing and substrates, leaving wafers in Korea (chips)
**Summary:** The Korea Herald reported on October 11 that Samsung affiliates plan to invest a combined 12.26 trillion won ($9.15 billion) in chip operations, with nearly 8 trillion won directed to Vietnam while high-end capacity expands in South Korea. Samsung Electronics submitted a plan to invest 105.79 trillion Vietnamese dong ($4.09 billion) in memory-chip testing at Yen Binh Industrial Park in Thai Nguyen, northern Vietnam, with the first line scheduled for the fourth quarter of 2027 and another for 2028. The lines will run final tests on DRAM and NAND already packaged at Cheonan in Korea, and about 66.64 trillion dong, roughly 63 percent of that investment, is to come from reinvested profits of the Vietnam business. Samsung Electro-Mechanics said on September 29 it would invest 6.78 trillion won in flip-chip ball-grid-array substrates, 4.27 trillion won in Sejong and 2.51 trillion won in Vietnam, with Sejong mass production planned for September 2028 and Vietnam targeted for June 2030.

**Links:**

- [The Korea Herald — Samsung to expand chip capacity in Vietnam, boost high-end production in Korea](https://www.koreaherald.com/article/10898561)

**Commentary:** The Vietnam money is for final test and part of the substrate build, the first test line is set for the fourth quarter of 2027, and about three-fifths of the electronics outlay depends on future local profits.

### 2. Chey says the Honam cluster can be built alongside Yongin; 400 trillion won is still the planning figure (chips)
**Summary:** Seoul Economic Daily reported on October 11 that, according to industry sources, SK Group Chairman Chey Tae-won made his first visit on October 9 to the Gwangju air base chosen as the site of SK hynix's Honam semiconductor cluster. He said plants in Yongin and Jeonnam-Gwangju may have to be built in parallel, that the current plan is about two-thirds the size of Yongin, and that more land and more demand could make the site larger. The Yongin cluster is a 600 trillion won project with four fabs, scheduled for completion by 2033, and the 400 trillion won now earmarked for Honam, about $280 billion, equals two-thirds of that outlay. Jeonnam-Gwangju Mayor Min Hyung-bae said on the 30th of last month that he had heard discussion of a possible slowdown, and the paper says Chey restated the commitment nine days later, adding that demand is rising faster than expected and that moving Yongin forward by 12 years may still fall short; SK is also pursuing a 100 trillion won fab in Cheongju and a $3.87 billion facility in Indiana.

**Links:**

- [Seoul Economic Daily — SK Chairman Dispels Doubts Over 400 Trillion Won Honam Chip Project](https://en.sedaily.com/finance/2026/10/11/sk-chairman-dispels-doubts-over-400-trillion-won-honam-chip)

**Commentary:** What reached print on October 11 is an October 9 site visit, the 400 trillion won figure remains a plan, and the report gives no groundbreaking date for Honam.

### 3. Sumitomo Bakelite adds encapsulation lines in Suzhou and Singapore without stating the investment (packaging materials)
**Summary:** Sumitomo Bakelite said on October 9 that it will expand capacity for advanced semiconductor encapsulation materials in Suzhou, China, and Singapore, adding lines for SUMIKON EME mold underfill (MUF) and compression-molding granules at both sites, and the South China Morning Post reported the decision on October 11. The company said that after the expansion, MUF capacity is expected to be about double the current level and compression-molding granule capacity about triple; Suzhou will extend a plant completed in 2024, with production scheduled for fiscal 2030, and Singapore will add lines inside the existing plant, with production scheduled for the end of fiscal 2028. The statement does not disclose the investment amount, and it says a planned acquisition of Kyocera's chemical business at the end of October would add Kyocera's existing MUF and granule capacity.

**Links:**

- [Sumitomo Bakelite — Expansion of encapsulation materials capacity in Suzhou and Singapore](https://www.sumibe.co.jp/english/topics/2026/it-materials/1007_01/index.html)

**Commentary:** The doubling and tripling are expected capacities after the lines are in, Suzhou production is scheduled for fiscal 2030, and the spending figure was not published.

## II. Batteries and Rare-Earth Licenses

### 4. CATL and Seres sign; the in-plant second phase is planned at 10 to 12 GWh (batteries)
**Summary:** A CATL statement carried by Sina Finance on the evening of October 10 said the company and Seres signed a long-term strategic agreement that day in Chongqing, attended by Chongqing officials Xu Jian and Zheng Xiangdong, Seres founder and chairman Zhang Xinghai, and CATL chairman and CEO Zeng Yuqun. Aito has used CATL batteries across its lineup since launch, cumulative deliveries have passed 1.2 million vehicles, and the brand will keep using CATL batteries across the range. The companies will speed up phase two of the plant-inside-a-plant at the Seres super factory, with planned annual capacity of 10 to 12 GWh and combined annual capacity above 20 GWh once both phases are complete, and they will also work on supercharging, battery services, and markets in Europe, the Middle East, and Central Asia. CnEVPost noted on October 11 that the announcement gives neither the contract term nor a start date for phase two; two CTP 2.0 pack lines at the site began operating on June 30, 2025.

**Links:**

- [CnEVPost — CATL signs long-term strategic deal with Seres to expand Aito battery capacity](https://cnevpost.com/2026/10/11/catl-deal-seres-expand-aito-battery-capacity/)

### 5. The China-EU list names a green channel for rare-earth and magnet licenses and publishes no quota (rare earths)
**Summary:** A CCTV account of the joint statement, carried by Guangzhou Daily, says Minister of Commerce Wang Wentao and European Commission trade and economic security commissioner Maros Sefcovic held the second session of the China-EU trade and investment consultation mechanism in Beijing on October 8 and 9 and agreed a 16-point list. Export-control item 12 says China is willing to keep facilitating licenses for rare-earth and permanent-magnet exports to the EU through a green-channel mechanism, while the EU will keep working with member states to facilitate priority dual-use license cases bound for China. The list also records an understanding on hybrid-vehicle trade consistent with WTO rules and continued work on company price undertakings and the review in the electric-vehicle anti-subsidy case, with a third session set for March 2027 and a ministerial video meeting in January before that. A Tonghuashun reading on October 11 said easier approval is not a cancellation of export controls, and the public text does not set a fixed supply volume.

**Links:**

- [Guangzhou Daily — Joint statement of the second China-EU trade and investment consultation](https://news.dayoo.com/world/202610/09/139998_55011172.htm)
- [Tonghuashun — Can a rare-earth green channel ease Europe's supply-chain anxiety](https://news.10jqka.com.cn/20261011/c680557946.shtml)

**Commentary:** The list puts licensing convenience and a follow-up calendar into writing, and tonnage still depends on case-by-case permits.

### 8. An Ohio aluminum firm says this year's extra tariff cost is $4.5 million to $5 million, with surcharges up to about $6,500 a container (tariffs)
**Summary:** An AFP story published by The New Indian Express on October 11 quotes Doug Rende, chief executive of Shapes Unlimited in North Jackson, Ohio, who said aluminum tariffs rose from 10 percent in Trump's first presidency to 25 percent and then to 50 percent last year, adding $4.5 million to $5 million in cost this year alone. He also said fuel prices after the U.S.-Israel war with Iran have pushed surcharges on supplier shipments to as much as about $6,500 per container, and the company has paused hiring while adding robotic assembly. Guy Coviello, president of the Youngstown Warren Regional Chamber, said Canada is Ohio's largest export market, about a third of the state's exports go there, drone attacks in the Middle East are also hitting aluminum supply, and the United States lacks new aluminum smelters.
