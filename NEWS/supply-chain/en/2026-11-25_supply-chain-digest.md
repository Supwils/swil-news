## I. Chips & Critical Materials

### 1. Samsung Faces HBM Base-Die Crunch as 2027 Internal Demand Seen Doubling to ~30K Wafers/Month (Chips)
**Summary:** TrendForce reported on Nov 25 that industry sources say Samsung is facing a severe shortage of HBM base-die foundry capacity, with memory-division wafer needs projected to double in 2027 to roughly 30,000 4nm-equivalent wafers per month. To ease the gap, the company is said to be expanding base-die capacity across Giheung, Pyeongtaek and the upcoming Taylor P2 fab in the United States: converting a Giheung R&D line to 4nm base-die production, dedicating part of Pyeongtaek P5 to base dies, and tentatively earmarking Taylor P2 for HBM5 base dies that could be paired with Korean DRAM for AMD and Nvidia. Korean media also say Samsung is developing custom HBM4E for Meta’s in-house AI chips and validating its own 2nm process as a fallback for Nvidia custom HBM. Overall HBM4/HBM4E output is targeted to double in 2027, with monthly HBM wafer input rising from about 180,000 to about 250,000 wafers.

**Commentary:** The AI memory bottleneck has moved beyond DRAM dies into advanced-node base dies and packaging, pulling captive foundry capacity into HBM and intensifying competition for custom logic.

### 2. Nvidia GPU Resales Surge After U.S. Curbs on Chinese Remote Access to Advanced Compute (Chips)
**Summary:** CNBC reported on Nov 25, citing three people familiar with the matter, that an unusually large volume of Nvidia GPUs has been offered for sale since the Trump administration announced on Nov 16—and put into effect on Nov 19—an interim final rule tightening Chinese companies’ ability to rent advanced AI compute remotely in overseas data centers. GPU Mart president Jack Duhamel and others said the surge is directly tied to the rule, with at least one seller linking the offering to the new controls; sources said some chips had been destined for facilities serving China-linked remote tenants. Deals involving thousands of advanced B300 GPUs have been floated since the announcement. The Bureau of Industry and Security said the rule closes loopholes around third-country remote access; Nvidia declined to comment and BIS did not immediately respond. USTR Jamieson Greer separately said the measures aim to shut indirect channels such as smuggling and remote access from third countries.

**Links:**

- [CNBC — Nvidia sellers seek buyers after U.S. China AI remote-access rule](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/25/nvidia-china-ai-chip-remote-access.html)

**Commentary:** Export controls are shifting from “no chip sales” to “no remote compute,” creating a short-term secondary-market jolt and a longer-term reshuffle of where and for whom GPUs are deployed.

## II. Batteries & Critical Materials

### 3. Korea’s Battery Rebound Still Runs Through China: Congo Cobalt Ban Meets Expiring Material-Control Pause (Batteries)
**Summary:** Aju Press reported on Nov 25 that AI data-center storage demand is pulling LG Energy Solution, Samsung SDI and SK On out of an EV retreat, but a Democratic Republic of Congo cobalt-export suspension from Nov 30 and China’s pause on export controls covering NCM precursors, synthetic graphite anodes and high-end cells—due only through Jan 10, 2027—show how exposed the recovery remains. Congo accounts for about 75% of mined cobalt; much of it reaches Korean makers as NCM precursor after Chinese refining, which held about 79% of global refined supply last year, while Korea once took about 95% of China’s NCM precursor exports. Chinese cobalt futures hit a more-than-three-month high this week. Storage demand is shifting toward cobalt-free LFP, yet LFP technology and supply chains remain China-centric; Ford has begun shipping LFP cells from Marshall, Michigan, under CATL-licensed technology. Analysts note cobalt prices are still well below mid-year peaks, so the ban’s shock may be milder than the prior round.

**Links:**

- [Aju Press — Korea's battery recharge hinges on China](https://www.ajupress.com/view/20261125153610810)
- [Europe Mining News — Four-month Congo review to shape 2027 cobalt contracts](https://euromining.news/four-month-congo-review-to-shape-battery-makers-2027-cobalt-contracts/)

**Commentary:** Korea’s pivot from EV to storage does not escape Chinese refining and materials nodes; the cobalt ban and January control window hit NCM and LFP along different paths but leave both chains tethered.

### 4. WoodMac: Indonesian Nickel Hit by Quotas, Sulphur Shock and Drought; Refined Cobalt Use Seen Contracting in 2026 (Critical Materials)
**Summary:** Wood Mackenzie said on Nov 25 that takeaways from the 2026 Antaike Nickel and Cobalt Conference point to concurrent Indonesian pressures from tighter mining quotas, a sulphur supply shock and El Niño-related drought. Before the Middle East conflict, about 80% of Indonesia’s sulphur imports came from the region; related disruptions cut total sulphur imports about 29% year over year in H1 2026 and nearly tripled average prices, putting more than 600 kt of nickel in incoming MHP capacity at risk. IMIP-area smelters reported 30%–45% production cuts in late September amid barging and water shortages. Falling saprolite grades are driving a 2026 contraction in nickel pig iron output, with stainless mills raising primary nickel and scrap substitution. The firm also forecasts a contraction in global refined cobalt consumption in 2026, while battery recycling remains early-stage ahead of a larger post-2030 retirement wave.

**Links:**

- [Wood Mackenzie — Indonesian supply constraints and shifting battery chemistry reshape nickel and cobalt markets](https://www.woodmac.com/press-releases/indonesian-supply-constraints-and-shifting-battery-chemistry-reshape-global-nickel-and-cobalt-markets/)

**Commentary:** Nickel-cobalt balances now hinge as much on intermediate chemicals and climate logistics as on mine output, adding new choke points for battery metals.

## III. Capacity & Relocation

### 5. TSMC Seeks Texas Site for ~$22.6B Advanced Fab, Still Weighing Arizona Alternative (Capacity)
**Summary:** Manufacturing Dive reported on Nov 25 that TSMC has filed a JETI tax-incentive application with the Texas Comptroller for a roughly $22.6 billion advanced wafer facility on more than 3,200 acres in Brazos County, with at least one fab using 2-nanometer or more advanced processes for AI, high-performance computing and mobile chips, and seeking about $354 million in school-district property-tax relief. The filing calls the tax break a “determinative and compelling factor,” while still treating an Arizona expansion as an alternative that could leverage existing fabs, workforce, logistics and supplier ties. The project is projected to create at least 2,099 direct jobs by 2032 when fully operational; College Station ISD will hold a Dec 10 hearing, and both the district and the governor must approve before a tax-limitation agreement. No final investment decision has been made.

## IV. Policy, Tariffs & Logistics

### 8. USTR Proposes Section 301 Tariffs on 14 Economies Over Structural Excess Manufacturing Capacity (Policy)
**Summary:** On Nov 25 the Office of the U.S. Trade Representative determined that acts, policies and practices of 14 economies—including China, the EU, Japan, Korea, Vietnam, India and Taiwan—relating to structural excess capacity and production in manufacturing are unreasonable and burden or restrict U.S. commerce, and proposed responsive tariffs; Mexico and Norway were not determined at this time. Proposed capped rates include up to about 20% for China (consistent with existing arrangements), or up to about 40% if engagement fails; about 15% for the EU, Japan, Korea, Taiwan and Switzerland (net of MFN and certain existing Section 301 duties); about 20%/35% for Vietnam with or without a deal; about 17%/25% for India; and as low as about 10% for Singapore under reciprocal arrangements. Written comments and hearing requests are due Jan 5, 2027, with hearings on Jan 25, 2027, so duties would not take effect before next year. China’s embassy in Washington rejected “overcapacity” as a false pretext and urged delivery on leader-level understandings. Reuters said the move seeks to rebuild tariff leverage under court-tested Section 301 authority.
