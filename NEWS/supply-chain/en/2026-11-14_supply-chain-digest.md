## I. Chips & Critical Materials

### 1. SMIC: AI-related semiconductor supply to stay tight through 2027; some tool deliveries exceed 24 months (Foundry)
**Summary:** Reuters reported on Nov 14 that SMIC Co-CEO Zhao Haijun, asked whether AI is a bubble, said the industry remains early-stage with strong demand and that many customers still face shortages of computing resources. He said the semiconductor supply chain for AI applications—driven by data centers and private clouds—will stay tight through 2027, while memory supply and demand should roughly balance toward the middle to end of 2028. SMIC faces longer equipment lead times, with some orders taking more than 24 months, which would affect capital spending this year; depreciation expense is set to rise about 30% year on year in 2026. TrendForce and earlier coverage put SMIC’s third-quarter utilization near 97.3% and new-tool quotes at about 16–24 months.

## II. Rare Earths & Battery Critical Minerals

### 3. China extends some rare-earth export-control pauses to Jan 10, 2027; USTR still unhappy with shipment pace (Rare earths)
**Summary:** S&P Global and others reported that China’s Ministry of Commerce on Nov 9 extended suspensions—originally due to expire Nov 10—on export controls covering five rare-earth elements plus related processing technology, certain lithium-battery and graphite items, and extraterritorial rules on foreign goods made with Chinese-origin rare earths, through Jan 10, 2027. Licensing introduced in April 2025 for seven medium and heavy rare earths (samarium, gadolinium, terbium, dysprosium, lutetium, scandium and yttrium) remains in force. The IEA estimates China holds about 91% of global refining/processing capacity for major rare earths; Platts assessed dysprosium oxide CIF North America at about $4,100/kg on Oct 30, sharply above its late-March launch near $1,200/kg. InsideTrade on Nov 13–14 cited USTR Jamieson Greer saying China is only partly meeting commitments and rare-earth shipments remain slower and smaller than the U.S. wants.

**Links:**

- [S&P Global — China extends rare earth export suspension to January 2027](https://www.spglobal.com/energy/en/news-research/latest-news/metals/110926-china-extends-rare-earth-export-suspension-to-january-2027)
- [InsideTrade — Greer: U.S. dissatisfied with China's progress on rare-earth shipments](https://insidetrade.com/critical-minerals-news/greer-us-dissatisfied-chinas-progress-rare-earth-shipments)

**Commentary:** A calendar pause is not a supply reset—heavy-element licenses and offshore premiums show the squeeze is still on.

### 4. India bans lithium-ion battery black-mass exports to lock feedstock for domestic recycling (Battery recycling)
**Summary:** India’s Directorate General of Foreign Trade, via Notification No. 49/2026-27 dated Nov 10, changed the export policy for lithium-ion battery black mass under ITC(HS) 85491400 from “Free” to “Prohibited,” effective immediately until further orders, with FTP transitional arrangements applying. Officials said black mass holds recoverable lithium, cobalt, nickel and manganese, and exports divert feedstock that domestic recyclers need; the ban complements the National Critical Mineral Mission and a roughly ₹1,500 crore recycling incentive scheme aimed at lifting utilization and cutting import dependence. Indian press noted the move follows a cluster of critical-mineral processing policies as black-mass processing capacity remains feedstock-constrained.

**Links:**

- [PIB — DGFT prohibits export of Lithium-Ion Battery Black Mass](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleseDetailm.aspx?PRID=2335019&lang=1&reg=1)
- [The Indian Express — India bans lithium-ion battery black mass exports](https://indianexpress.com/article/business/india-bans-lithium-ion-battery-black-mass-exports-10958913/)

**Commentary:** Resource nationalism now reaches “urban mining”—keeping black mass at home folds secondary feedstock into industrial policy.

## III. Capacity Layout & Migration

### 5. Topre to invest $140 million in U.S. stamping: new Alabama plant plus Tennessee presses for hybrid ramp (Auto supply chain)
**Summary:** Nikkei Asia and a Nov 13 company filing show Japanese press-parts maker Topre will invest about $140 million through Topre America: a new plant in Cullman, Alabama (construction targeted for early 2027) and additional presses at its Tennessee site, with commercial operations aimed for fiscal 2028. The Alabama line will integrate stamping through structural subassembly. The company cited rising North American hybrid demand and customer capacity plans; the outlay equals roughly 66% of Topre America’s paid-in capital of about $211 million and is expected to have only a minor effect on consolidated results for the year ending March 2027. The move follows Honda’s roughly $3 billion Ohio hybrid-assembly plan.

### 7. Caixin/Nikkei: Chinese manufacturers shift Africa strategy from trade to local factories under tariff pressure (Migration)
**Summary:** A Caixin deep dive republished by Nikkei Asia on Nov 14 says Chinese firms facing softer domestic demand and fierce home competition are building African factories rather than only exporting finished goods—to dodge high finished-goods tariffs, cut freight and reach neighboring markets. China–Africa trade topped about $348 billion in 2025 (up about 17.7% YoY) and rose about another 22.3% in the first nine months of 2026. Examples include Kenya’s roughly 60% tariff on finished vehicles (total burden can exceed 70%) versus about 5% on components; Kaili and Beiqi Foton’s Nairobi assembly with about 3,000 vehicles a year capacity; Foton’s about 12 knockdown plants across Africa; and Sunda International’s about 32 factories on the continent. The same reporting stresses that power, logistics, financing and local-content rules still make profits hard to lock in.

**Links:**

- [Nikkei Asia — Chinese makers flock to Africa as trade gives way to local production](https://asia.nikkei.com/spotlight/caixin/chinese-makers-flock-to-africa-as-trade-gives-way-to-local-production)
