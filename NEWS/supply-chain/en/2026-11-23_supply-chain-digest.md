Earlier plans still target about a 2.5-fold substrate increase over three years, doubled test capacity in 2026–2027, and roughly a 15-fold rise in SPIL optical-engine capacity by mid-2027.

### 2. Global Electronics Association Warns PCB Shortages Threaten AI and Broader Electronics Output (Critical Materials)
**Summary:** The Economic Times Electronics edition on Nov 23 cited a Global Electronics Association study warning that escalating printed-circuit-board shortages could threaten AI production and electronics across automotive, industrial, medical, defense and telecom. The association said a typical PC board has about 12 layers while AI-server boards are moving to more than 40–60 layers, with each added layer consuming more glass fabric, resin, copper foil and laminate; bookings rose 56.1% in the year to September while shipments rose only 13.3%, and lead times for high-layer boards now run 16–20 weeks or more. Asia makes over 90% of the world’s boards, and more than $10 billion has been invested in new PCB plants in Southeast Asia and at least as much in China over three years, yet constrained materials mean more board lines alone cannot close the gap. The shortage is expected through at least the first half of 2027, with meaningful relief unlikely before mid-2028, as hyperscalers secure supply and push other sectors back in the queue.

**Links:**

- [ET Electronics — PCB shortages threaten AI, electronics production: Global Electronics Association](https://electronics.economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/components-hardware/pcb-shortages-threaten-ai-electronics-production-global-electronics-association/136092822)

**Commentary:** Material-intensive multilayer boards have become an invisible AI-infrastructure bottleneck; without glass fabric and copper foil catching up, auto and industrial electronics stay at the back of the line.

Softer growth partly reflected constrained capacity, with backlogs rising further and firms hiring more staff, especially in manufacturing. The survey said input shortages lengthened manufacturers’ supplier delivery times in November to the greatest extent since August 2022, often linked to global trade disruptions and widely cited for metals and electronics components. Selling-price and input-cost inflation cooled but remained elevated, with manufacturing selling prices still rising sharply. Affordability, supply disruptions and high inflation continued to weigh on prospects, though confidence showed some revival on hopes of lower political instability after the midterms.

## II. Batteries & Critical Materials

### 4. BASF Starts CAM Deliveries to CATL from Germany and Agrees to Sell Battery Recycling to cylib (Batteries)
**Summary:** BASF announced on Nov 23 that it has begun initial deliveries of high-performance cathode active materials (CAM) from its Schwarzheide, Germany, plant to CATL, while also supplying CATL in China from its Changsha plant; the shipments follow a July 2025 global CAM framework agreement and mark the move from development and qualification into European production. The same day, BASF said Battery Materials will focus exclusively on CAM and confirmed that on Nov 20 it signed an agreement for cylib to acquire its battery-recycling business in Europe and North America, including IP, customer contracts, and the black-mass and prototype plants at Schwarzheide. Closing is expected in Q2–Q3 2027, subject to approvals; financial terms were not disclosed. cylib said recovery rates exceed 90% and that the deal expands its European network and North American market access.

### 5. Fortum Produces Battery-Grade Recycled Nickel, Cobalt and Lithium Samples in Harjavalta (Recycling)
**Summary:** Fortum Battery Recycling said on Nov 23 that a pilot campaign in Harjavalta, Finland, has produced the first customer samples of battery-grade nickel sulphate, cobalt sulphate and lithium hydroxide using its next-generation recycling process. The materials are in customer qualification, with offtake talks progressing with several buyers. The process relies on internal reagent circulation to cut chemical use and waste and is powered by clean electricity. The European Commission named the Harjavalta facility a Critical Raw Materials Act Strategic Project in 2025; Business Finland granted about EUR 86.4 million for the extension, and a CINEA Innovation Fund grant agreement covers up to EUR 40 million.

### 6. Vietnam’s Khanh An Breaks Ground on $61 Million High-Purity Rare-Earth Materials Plant (Critical Materials)
**Summary:** Reuters/Mining Weekly reported on Nov 23 that private Vietnamese firm Khanh An Industry broke ground Monday on a high-purity rare-earth materials plant in Haiphong’s Lach Huyen Non-tariff Industrial Zone. The roughly 14-hectare project is valued at about 1.58 trillion dong ($61 million) and aims for 10,000 tons a year of high-purity materials for permanent magnets, EVs, electronics and energy. Feedstock will include mixed rare-earth oxides from the Ben Den mine in Lao Cai province plus other legally sourced supply. The plant plans hydrometallurgical processing and continuous counter-current solvent extraction, targeting individual rare-earth oxide purity above 99%.

### 7. Thailand Plans to Restrict Chemical Exports Used in Myanmar Rare-Earth Mining (Critical Materials)
**Summary:** Mining South East Europe reported on Nov 23 that Thailand said over the weekend it plans to block exports of chemicals used in rare-earth mining in neighbouring Myanmar, intensifying pressure on an unregulated mining boom blamed for contaminating rivers in the country’s north. Mines in Myanmar’s conflict-hit border regions have become a major heavy-rare-earth feedstock source for Chinese refiners, so cutting leaching chemicals could tighten supply of elements used in EV, wind and defense magnets. A deputy prime minister said measures would target chemicals bound for Myanmar and other relevant countries, without naming products or timing; Thailand also plans to designate the Thai side of the Kok River a pollution-control zone. Thailand, China and Myanmar are due to hold talks Nov 24–26. Impact will depend on enforcement along a porous border and whether flows simply shift routes.

## III. Capacity Migration & Policy

### 8. EU Opens Safeguard Probe into Glass-Fibre and Composite Input Imports (Policy)
**Summary:** The European Commission announced on Nov 23 a safeguard investigation into imports of certain composite input materials to assess whether quotas or tariffs are needed to protect EU industry. The probe takes a supply-chain approach covering glass-fibre reinforcements (chopped strands, rovings, mats), composite resins (epoxy, unsaturated polyester, vinyl ester) and upstream inputs such as epichlorohydrin (EPI), isophorone diamine (IPDA) and maleic anhydride (MAN)—materials used in wind energy, power infrastructure, aerospace and defence. The Commission said the import surge reflects global overcapacity and trade diversion from restrictions elsewhere; official notice data show imports rising from about 697,000 tonnes in 2019 to 898,000 tonnes in 2025 (about +29%), with import market share up from 25.9% to 38.2%. Provisional measures could follow in four to five months if conditions are met; safeguards would need qualified-majority Member State support and would apply to all origins, broader than existing anti-dumping/anti-subsidy measures on specific products and countries.

**Links:**

- [European Commission — Safeguard investigation into imports of certain composites](https://policy.trade.ec.europa.eu/news/commission-initiates-safeguard-investigation-imports-certain-composites-2026-11-23_en)
- [WTO — EU launches safeguard investigation into certain composite input materials](https://www.wto.org/english/news_e/news26_e/safe_eec_23nov26_561_e.htm)

**Commentary:** Brussels is pushing trade defence upstream into the glass-fibre–resin chain shared by wind and defence, putting a wider border gate on global overcapacity seeking an EU outlet.

### 10. AI Power Demand Squeezes Turbine Supply Chains Across Energy, Aviation and Shipping (Capacity)
**Summary:** ET Supply Chain on Nov 23, citing Bloomberg, reported that AI’s electricity hunger is feeding through turbine supply chains, lifting costs and delays across aviation, shipping, gas infrastructure and power projects. Highly specialised single-crystal nickel-alloy blades shared by jet engines and gas turbines sit in a market about 80% controlled by Berkshire Hathaway’s Precision Castparts and Howmet Aerospace; GE said in September it planned to spend about $11.8 billion on Consolidated Precision Products, while UK castings producer DPC Holdings raised about $1 billion in a June IPO. Howmet’s CEO has said AI power demand is testing an industry already struggling to keep up. Compressor orders that once needed about 40 weeks now require about 200 weeks; gas plants in Ohio and South Korea’s Jeju Island have been delayed for years, a mothballed Philippine generator sold already-purchased turbines to U.S. data centres, and baseload gas-generating equipment prices are said to have roughly tripled. Shipping deliveries have slipped on auxiliary-engine shortages, and aviation previously cut Airbus delivery targets amid blade shortfalls.

**Links:**

- [ET Supply Chain — AI boom squeezes turbine supply chains across energy and aviation](https://supplychain.economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/ai-and-technology/ai-boom-squeezes-turbine-supply-chains-across-energy-and-aviation/136102537)

**Commentary:** AI infrastructure has welded compute–power–turbine castings into one chain, so scarce high-temperature alloy capacity now stalls data centres, aircraft and ships at once.
