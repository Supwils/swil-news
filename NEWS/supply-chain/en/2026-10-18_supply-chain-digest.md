## I. Chip Supply and Capital Spending

### 1. Samsung and TSMC share the base die for Nvidia's custom high-bandwidth memory (chips)
**Summary:** The Seoul Economic Daily reported on October 18, citing industry sources, that Nvidia is co-designing the base die of its proprietary high-bandwidth memory, NVHBM, with Samsung Electronics and TSMC. The base die takes on the memory controller that previously sat on the graphics processor. The physical-layer circuitry is 60 percent smaller than in standard high-bandwidth memory, bandwidth can widen by up to 30 percent, and power use is 15 percent lower than seventh-generation HBM4E. The industry estimates TSMC's 3-nanometer yield at more than 90 percent, against about 60 percent for Samsung Foundry's comparable 2-nanometer SF2 process, so Nvidia wants the base die on TSMC's 3-nanometer logic process. UBS projects next year's high-bandwidth-memory demand at about 61.5 billion gigabits, up 91 percent from this year, against about 54.6 billion gigabits of new shipments and a shortfall of about 10 percent. The report said TSMC will add two 3-nanometer lines at Fab 18 in Tainan, with mass production aimed at the first half of 2027 and the first half of 2028, and plans 3-nanometer lines at its second Arizona plant and its second Kumamoto plant for the third quarter of 2027 and 2028. Samsung's core die uses 1c DRAM. Its 4-nanometer base-die capacity is limited to Hwaseong Line 17 and part of Pyeongtaek P3, and the company is also validating an NVHBM base die on its own 2-nanometer process.

**Commentary:** The bandwidth, yield, and roughly 10 percent gap come from industry accounts and a UBS estimate. Samsung and TSMC did not jointly announce that day that all base-die capacity is locked in.

## II. Battery Materials and Factory Automation

### 3. Korea's battery-materials chain converts lines toward lithium iron phosphate (batteries)
**Summary:** The Asia Business Daily reported on October 18 that energy-storage demand is shifting South Korea's battery industry from ternary materials toward lithium iron phosphate. Samsung SDI signed a cathode contract of about 1.6 trillion won with L&F in March and a long-term supply agreement of about 6 trillion won with POSCO Future M on October 6. LG Energy Solution and L&F are still discussing LFP cathodes, and the timing and scale are not set. Last month SK On signed separate ESS LFP cathode contracts with POSCO Future M and L&F, worth about 1.1 trillion won and 160 billion won. POSCO Future M has converted part of a high-nickel line at its Pohang plant to LFP and plans to start mass production and supply by the end of this year. It is also building a dedicated plant of up to 50,000 tons a year at the Yeongil Bay 4th General Industrial Complex, targeting mass production next year. EcoPro BM plans to invest a total of 1.5 trillion won in a nickel smelter in Indonesia and keep its high-nickel ternary business. One industry source said price competition with Chinese companies is intense, new plants and line conversions both cost money, and the approach is closer to spreading the product mix while keeping ternary materials competitive.

**Links:**

- [The Asia Business Daily — ESS boom drives a shift from ternary batteries to LFP](https://www.asiae.co.kr/en/article/2026101514205438112)

**Commentary:** Most of the large contracts were signed before October 18. The day's development is cathode lines being converted to lithium iron phosphate, and the cells have not been delivered to storage projects on the strength of that report.

### 4. Hyundai and Kia set robot-led plants for 2030 and full automation for 2032 (auto manufacturing)
**Summary:** The Seoul Economic Daily reported on October 18 that Hyundai Motor and Kia will show robotics, artificial intelligence, and digital twins at E-FOREST Tech Day from October 19 to 21. At a media briefing on the 16th at the Uiwang research center, the companies said they will fold plant-by-plant systems into one manufacturing platform, push robot-led autonomous plants by 2030, and apply robotics and factory autonomy to new plants from 2032. A physical-AI virtual plant developed with Nvidia is meant to shorten new-model development and train robots and equipment. Polaris, a manufacturing AI agent, retrieves information workers need. Quality tools on display include a hand-like gripper, a three-dimensional tunnel inspection for vehicle exteriors, and automatic path setting for more than 1,700 weld points. The companies plan to bring these into mass production in phases from next year. Verified tools will go to the new Ulsan electric-vehicle plant that starts mass production this month and to later plants. The Singapore innovation center is the testbed, with a target of completing a robot-linked virtual autonomous manufacturing system in 2030.

## III. Solar Controls, the Canton Fair, and Cross-Border Trucking

### 6. China proposes export controls on 12 solar-manufacturing technologies (solar)
**Summary:** Reuters reported on October 18 that China's Ministry of Commerce on Friday proposed adding 12 solar-equipment manufacturing technologies to the export-control list, citing growing use in aerospace and national defense. The revision also covers artificial intelligence and biotechnology. Public consultation closes on November 15. Until the catalogue is formally updated, certain overseas technology transfers must be registered. Technologies proposed for a ban include some thin wafers, perovskite and tandem cells, and large-scale fluidized-bed polysilicon. Advanced crystalline-silicon cell technologies including TOPCon, heterojunction, and back contact (XBC) would be restricted rather than banned. pv magazine reported on October 16, citing Joint Announcement No. 48 of 2026 from the Ministry of Commerce and the Ministry of Science and Technology, that the consultation covers 40 technologies, including solar, artificial intelligence, and biomedicine. Proposed bans include perovskite-silicon tandem processes on wafers of at least 182 millimeters with conversion efficiency of 30 percent or higher, and slot-die coating of perovskites at least 1.1 meters wide. Technologies below the thresholds, and finished goods made with controlled technologies, sit outside the proposed scope. The bans and licensing rules are not yet in force, but an interim registration requirement took effect on October 16 and covers intellectual-property transfers, overseas licensing, and transactions that are not yet complete.
