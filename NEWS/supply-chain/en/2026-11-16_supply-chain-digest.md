### 1. Auto Chips Tighten as AI Data Centers Absorb Mature-Node Capacity (Semiconductors)

**Summary:** At the Electronica fair in Munich on Nov 9–10, STMicroelectronics CEO Jean-Marc Chery said foundries and packaging firms are shifting resources away from automotive and industrial products: "We see many bottlenecks." He added that investment in mature technologies such as diodes and transistors has lagged for years.

### 2. China Extends Rare Earth Export-Control Suspension to Jan 10, 2027 (Rare Earths)

**Summary:** According to S&P Global, China's Ministry of Commerce announced on Nov 9 that it would extend the pause on its expanded October 2025 rare earth export controls by two months, from Nov 10 to Jan 10, 2027. The April 2025 licensing regime for seven medium and heavy rare earths (samarium, gadolinium, terbium, dysprosium, lutetium, scandium, yttrium) remains in force. S&P says North American rare earth oxide prices have risen significantly in recent months. (The core facts here come from S&P's reported summary; the full article could not be opened.)

## II. Policy, Tariffs & Trade

### 4. US Revises Canada's 50% Section 338 Tariff List; USMCA Moves to Annual Reviews (Tariffs)

**Summary:** Per C.H. Robinson's November update, effective Oct 30 the US revised the list of Canadian products subject to the additional 50% Section 338 tariff: certain plywood, paper products, machinery, refrigeration equipment and packing containers were removed, while certain valves, carpets and waste products were added. USMCA eligibility does not exempt covered goods, and Section 232 tariffs may stack on top. The US also did not agree to extend USMCA by another 16 years at the July review, so the pact moves to annual reviews; comments for the 2027 review are due Jan 12, 2027.
