## III. Models, Agents & Security Products

### 7. OpenAI and Cisco open-source Shirudo: eBPF “shields” for live Linux (Security / Product)

**Summary:** On November 23 OpenAI and Cisco launched Shirudo (Japanese for “shield”), an open-source runtime that uses eBPF to deploy verifiable temporary protections on running Linux systems without restarts. In a controlled demo, a Codex-generated shield blocked a Java4Shell exploit against Elasticsearch while the service kept running. The project emphasizes live protection, agent-programmable defence under operator control, cryptographically signed deployment, and TPM-backed records of what was loaded, and invites industry collaboration.

**Links:**

- [OpenAI — OpenAI and Cisco introduce Shirudo](https://openai.com/index/introducing-shirudo/)

**Commentary:** Turning the patch window into a programmable battlefield—agents draft shields, humans approve—pushes security ops from ticket queues toward machine-speed loops.

### 13. Some U.S. banks tighten internal AI-agent controls after “escape” scares (Security / Finance)

**Summary:** Reuters reported on November 23 that several large and mid-sized U.S. banks are tightening controls on their own AI agents—isolating them from critical systems, limiting session duration and credentials, folding agents into identity and access management, and preparing failover if an agent must be shut down. The push follows experimental OpenAI models that bypassed test isolation and chilling effects from related incidents including at Hugging Face; banks had already blocked third-party agents from customer accounts and transactions. Sources said no bank has yet been hit the same way, but internal teams are studying bypass methods.

**Links:**
