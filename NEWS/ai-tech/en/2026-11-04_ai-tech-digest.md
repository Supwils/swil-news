## I. Policy, Regulation, and Geopolitics of Compute

### 1. Oracle reportedly cancels ~$7B Tencent chip lease amid dual pressure (Policy · Chips)

**Summary:** The Financial Times reported on November 4 that Oracle canceled an estimated $7 billion, five-year AI chip leasing arrangement with Tencent after US officials raised concerns. The deal would have given Tencent access to roughly 100,000 advanced AI chips across Oracle data centers in Southeast Asia, a path around bilateral export/import curbs. The FT also said Guangdong officials visited Tencent and urged more support for domestic Chinese chips. Reuters said it could not immediately verify the report; Oracle, Tencent, and the White House did not immediately comment.

### 2. Google turns school AI off by default, but Search stays tied to AI Overviews (Policy · Education)

**Summary:** Google said Gemini, Gemini Notebook, Gemini in Classroom, and Google Search will be entirely off for K-12 student accounts until administrators explicitly enable them. Education VP Chris Phillips said Search and AI are deeply integrated, so schools that block AI entirely may also lose Search. After Tuesday backlash, Google clarified admins can enable Search and Gemini products separately—but still cannot keep Search while disabling AI Overviews / AI Mode. DuckDuckGo pitched alternative search to districts the same day; WIRED reported schools still hunting workarounds around AI summaries.

### 3. NDRC: China to accelerate foundational, comprehensive AI legislation (Policy · China)

**Summary:** At a November 3 briefing, Wang Ruomeng, a deputy director in China’s National Development and Reform Commission innovation department, said that during the 15th Five-Year Plan period China will accelerate foundational, comprehensive AI legislation to build the “pillars” of a national legal system. Rules covering data, compute, algorithms, and key application scenarios will also be expanded, with lifecycle safety standards, ethics guidelines, and sector-specific regulatory sandboxes. Caixin’s November 4 coverage stressed China still lacks a unified national AI statute. Officials also cited new occupations such as model trainers and data labelers, plus progress toward a World AI Cooperation Organization with about 38 founding members.

**Links:**

- [China.gov / Xinhua — NDRC briefing on deepening “AI+” action](https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/202611/content_7084907.htm)
- [Caixin — China to accelerate work on comprehensive AI legislation](https://china.caixin.com/2026-11-04/102500735.html)

**Commentary:** Moving from nimble sector rules to a unifying basic law signals that deployment scale now demands a national compliance floor.

## II. Models, Products, and M&A

### 5. OpenAI to acquire Pierre, folding Code Storage into Codex (M&A · Developer Tools)

**Summary:** On November 4 OpenAI announced it will acquire Pierre Computer Company, Inc., the firm behind Code Storage and open-source tools including Trees and Diffs. Terms were undisclosed; closing needs regulatory approvals, and the companies stay independent until then. Codex already uses Pierre’s Trees and Diffs for file browsing and change review; after closing, OpenAI plans to bring Code Storage into Codex while keeping Pierre’s open-source projects open and letting existing customers keep using the service. Axios and others framed the deal as a challenge to Microsoft’s GitHub in an agent-coding era.

### 7. Baidu unveils Apollo World to virtualize Apollo Go road testing (Product · Autonomous Driving)

**Summary:** At its AI Day Apollo session, Baidu disclosed Apollo World, an agent-driven multimodal world model for physical AI, and proposed a “2+3” bar (causality and interactivity; physical accuracy, persistence, and real-time performance). Traffic participants in its virtual cities are modeled as decision-making agents; the system generates camera and lidar-style observations and supports full trip loops from dispatch to drop-off. Baidu says the model already supports Apollo Go global testing; Apollo Go covers about 28 cities with more than ~350 million autonomous kilometers and over ~240 million fully driverless kilometers. Industry coverage cites virtual testing on the order of ~100,000 km per day, while still requiring real-world validation.

**Links:**

- [Guandian — Baidu Apollo discloses Apollo World](https://www.guandian.cn/m/show/623109)
- [AutoCN — Apollo World debut; ~100k virtual km/day](https://www.auto-cn.com/views-14328)

**Commentary:** L4’s bottleneck is shifting from “can drive” to “can afford to test”—world models that eat long tails are what compress overseas city timelines.
