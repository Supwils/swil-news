## I. Export Controls, Security & Regulation

### 1. Nvidia halts AI server shipments to four Southeast Asian countries (Export controls)

**Summary:** The Financial Times reported on November 26—and Reuters relayed—that Nvidia told manufacturing partners including Dell and Supermicro to stop shipping servers containing its chips to Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand, and Indonesia; it is unclear whether the pause is temporary while compliance paths are worked out. The move follows a U.S. Commerce Department rule effective November 19 that tightens licensing for advanced chips and remote access, aiming to block Chinese entities from renting high-end compute via third-country data centers; cloud providers have roughly 90 days to seek licences. Reporting notes Nvidia booked about $104 billion of 2025 revenue through its Singapore unit, and regional data-center financing has also been disrupted.

**Commentary:** Licensing has moved from paper to the loading dock—Southeast Asia’s compute hubs became the enforcement frontier overnight, narrowing both diversion and rental workarounds.

### 3. Switzerland confirms first AI-agent cyberattacks, including on critical infrastructure (Security)

**Summary:** Switzerland’s National Cyber Security Centre (NCSC/BACS) said it has received its first confirmed reports of attacks using “Agentic Offensive Frameworks”—AI agents that search for vulnerabilities, select tools, evaluate results, and plan next steps. Officials told media there were about five such incidents in recent days, with multiple successful compromises and operators of critical infrastructure in at least two sectors (power, water, telecoms among those cited) affected; attackers were not named. NCSC said publicly available attack agents have nearly doubled since October 2026 and urged patching internet-facing systems within 24 hours, universal MFA/passkeys, and a smaller exposed attack surface.

**Links:**

- [NCSC — Cyberattacks involving AI agents: First confirmed cases in Switzerland](https://www.bacs.admin.ch/en/26-ki-agenten-1-en)
- [SWI swissinfo — Switzerland reports first AI-enabled cyberattacks on critical infrastructure](https://www.swissinfo.ch/eng/swiss-ai/switzerland-reports-first-ai-based-cyberattacks-on-critical-infrastructure/92440400)

**Commentary:** Agents have crossed from office assistants into offensive automation—defense windows shrink to minutes, forcing critical operators to shrink their internet exposure fast.

### 4. New York governor signs five-year ban on AI chatbot toys (Policy / Child safety)

**Summary:** Governor Kathy Hochul signed S.9408A/A.11144B, placing a five-year moratorium on manufacturing, distributing, and selling toys in New York with embedded or integrated AI chatbots—such as internet-connected stuffed animals, dolls, and robots. Violations can draw civil penalties of up to $15,000 per day, enforced by the attorney general. The law takes effect 90 days after signing (around February 22, 2027), so holiday shelves are still legal; the state urged caregivers to weigh risks now. California already enacted a similar ban; New York is another major market joining the patchwork.

**Links:**

- [New York Governor — Hochul signs five-year ban on AI chatbot toys](https://www.governor.ny.gov/news/consumers-begin-their-holiday-shopping-season-governor-hochul-signs-five-year-ban-ai-chatbot)

**Commentary:** Companion chat for kids is getting a state-level pause button—federal rules lag, but shelf space is already being rewritten by a patchwork of deadlines.

### 5. 42 state AGs oppose Character.AI auction of private chat histories (Privacy / Bankruptcy)

## II. China Policy & Compute Self-Reliance

### 6. China’s National Data Administration seeks comment on data rules for AI (Policy / China)

**Summary:** Xinhua reported on November 26 that the National Data Administration released a draft “Several Opinions on Improving Data Rules to Empower AI Innovation” for public comment. The draft sets out twelve measures in three areas: applying data-property rules so data can be used orderly and lawfully for AI training and inference; balancing data processors and entrusted parties, encouraging payment for data, and building risk controls for AI enterprise outputs; and clarifying liability and security governance for data circulation. Agencies say they will keep updating rules as new AI business models emerge.

**Links:**

- [Xinhua — National Data Administration seeks to improve data rules empowering AI](http://www.news.cn/tech/20261126/134151d541c445c0987c3f8fd3b012c8/c.html)

**Commentary:** Training data is moving from gray-area custom to property, payment, and liability checklists—higher compliance cost, but also a path to legitimate supply.

## III. Products, Platforms & Sovereign Models

### 9. FCC authorizes SpaceX to deploy up to ~1 million orbital AI compute satellites (Infrastructure)

**Summary:** The U.S. Federal Communications Commission authorized SpaceX’s “Starmind” constellation of up to about one million AI-chip satellites to process workloads with space-based solar power and return results via laser links. The order requires at least one authorized space station by 2033, with build-out continuing through about 2040. Chair Brendan Carr framed the move as opening a frontier for “super intelligence”; the American Astronomical Society, Viasat, and Amazon’s Kuiper were among opponents, and scientists question heat rejection, debris, and sky interference, calling million-satellite deployment still constrained by physics and cost.

**Links:**

- [NPR — FCC says SpaceX can deploy 1 million satellites for data centers in space](https://www.npr.org/2026/11/26/g-s1-155940/fcc-spacex-satellites-data-centers-in-space)

**Commentary:** Regulatory green lights are not engineering proofs—orbital data centers push the power-bottleneck narrative into space; heat, cost, and debris remain the real tests.
