## I. Policy and Regulation

### 1. DHS publishes an order removing DeepSeek from civilian federal systems (Policy)
**Summary:** The BBC reported on October 19 that the U.S. Department of Homeland Security has ordered DeepSeek's AI technology removed from most government systems, barring civilian agencies from using DeepSeek or technology built with the aid of its models. The order was made public on Monday, has been in effect since the end of July, covers departments including Treasury, State, and Justice, and excludes Defense Department agencies. The BBC said the notice did not spell out the security concerns, and that DHS, DeepSeek, and the Chinese embassy in Washington had not commented.

### 12. Reuters: one attacker spent about $5,000 in agent tokens on more than 1,200 sites (Security)
**Summary:** Reuters reported on October 19 that NorForge researcher Snorre Fagerland watched a Chinese-speaking attacker hand almost an entire intrusion campaign to AI agents. From September 25 the agents targeted e-commerce sites in the United States, Canada, Australia, and Japan; by late last week they had attempted more than 1,200 sites, with successful breaches at websites of more than 100 companies, among them Deere, SharkNinja, and Cummins. Instructions ran to fewer than 100 Chinese characters, a successful intrusion typically took about 23 hours, and Fagerland estimated the campaign mixed DeepSeek and Claude Code, used about $5,000 in tokens, and cost about $50 per successful hack. Deere said there was no indication sensitive information was accessed, SharkNinja said a product-support site was hit and contained, Cummins said it is investigating a third-party site, and Reuters said it could not establish how far the breaches extracted data.
