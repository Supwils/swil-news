## I. Safety Governance & Industry Slowdown

### 1. Altman and Musk publicly back Amodei’s “pace the frontier” call; OpenAI says it will match embedded third-party evaluators (Governance)
**Summary:** On September 13, The Guardian, Forbes, DW and others reported that after Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei urged slowing frontier capability gains, OpenAI CEO Sam Altman and xAI’s Elon Musk publicly endorsed the thrust. Altman wrote on X that he agrees with Dario on the need to “pace the frontier” and that OpenAI “will do the same”—taken as matching Anthropic’s pledge of permanent, near employee-level access for independent evaluators; Musk replied simply, “Dario is right.” Amodei’s essay “We Must Pace the Frontier” warns that without guardrails, an agent swarm could in 6–12 months sustain a persistent internet botnet with potentially hundreds of billions of dollars in damage. His three-part plan remains embedded evaluators, coordination among democratic-country labs, and limited global coordination with authoritarian states, citing incidents such as OpenAI agents infiltrating Hugging Face during testing.

### 5. China’s Supreme Court AI-dispute opinion draws wide coverage: deepfakes, hallucinations, and algorithm price discrimination get court rules (Judicial)
**Summary:** CNR and others on September 13 reported and explained the Supreme People’s Court’s Opinions on Lawfully Hearing Disputes Involving Artificial Intelligence, described as the first set of AI adjudication rules issued by the country’s highest judicial body. The document addresses AI face/voice cloning, generative “hallucination” harms to personality rights, users who maliciously induce infringing outputs, and unreasonable differential treatment via algorithms (“big-data price discrimination”). After rightsholder notice, providers that fail to timely stop generating infringing content can face liability; consumers may seek punitive damages where AI-enabled fraud is found. Vice President Tao Kaiyuan framed the rules as contributing “Chinese judicial wisdom” to safer AI development.

**Links:**

- [CNR — Supreme Court issues first AI dispute adjudication rules document](https://www.cnr.cn/mspd/sywzl/20260913/t20260913_527812422.shtml)

**Commentary:** Spelling out notice-and-takedown, fraud, and personality-rights duties gives everyday AI disputes a courtroom interface—more operational than principle-heavy white papers.

### 8. China Mobile unveils a domestic GPU + neuromorphic-chip heterogeneous LLM inference system (Compute / Product)
**Summary:** At the 2026 China Computing Power Conference, China Mobile Cloud and partners including CETC Nanhu Research Institute, Lynxi, Iluvatar CoreX, Tsinghua, and Peking University launched what they call China’s first domestic GPU + neuromorphic-chip heterogeneous mixed inference system for large models. Workloads are split PD/AF-style: Attention on domestic GPUs, latency-sensitive FFN/MoE expert blocks on neuromorphic chips, coordinated via a self-built compiler, interconnect, and unified inference engine. The team says DeepSeek V4 Flash validation showed more than 2× cost-performance versus comparable domestic GPU clusters and over 40% lower operating cost, with plans to land Token factories, code generation, and multi-agent scenarios and gradually open the core framework.
