### 2. OpenAI publishes community compact and compute-campus policy blueprint (Policy · Infrastructure)

**Summary:** Responding to midterm voter backlash against data centers, OpenAI on November 5 released a National Community Compact and a Compute Campus Policy Blueprint. For campuses it owns and operates, it pledges early community input, published construction plans, paying the full cost of power and related infrastructure with financial assurances that protect other ratepayers, prioritizing closed-loop or low-water cooling with public reporting on water, electricity, and noise, and avoiding NDAs with public officials except narrow security/trade-secret cases. The blueprint urges utility regulators to make large users cover incremental grid costs and Congress to speed transmission upgrades and clean-power incentives.

**Links:**

- [OpenAI — Setting a new standard for responsible AI infrastructure](https://openai.com/index/setting-new-standard-responsible-ai-infrastructure/)
- [CNBC — OpenAI pledges to halt NDAs in new data center policy proposal](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/05/openai-to-pay-for-data-center-power-after-election-shows-ai-concern.html)

**Commentary:** Ballot pressure is being translated into industry self-legislation—voluntary pledges only work if lawmakers harden them into rules.

## II. Compute Infrastructure and Capital Structure

### 4. Google backstops Anthropic’s ~$19B Kentucky data-center lease (Infrastructure · Financing)

**Summary:** TeraWulf disclosed on November 4 (widely covered November 5) that Google signed credit-support agreements tied to roughly $19 billion of 20-year Anthropic lease commitments (~401 MW critical IT load) at its Hawesville, Kentucky “Justified” campus, improving project-finance terms; it also signed a Fluor EPC deal for three buildings, with phased ready-for-service dates from late 2027 through Q2 2028. If Anthropic defaults or becomes insolvent, Google may assume the lease or, in some cases, pay a termination fee for equity in the project subsidiary. Support activates after each lease’s final hall is delivered and can fall away if Anthropic sustains investment-grade ratings for a set period.

## III. Models, Security, and Products

### 7. OpenAI launches GPT-6 Cyber: Critical-tier cyber model finds 948 Linux privilege escalations (Product · Security)

**Summary:** OpenAI on November 5 introduced GPT-6 Cyber, saying it is stronger at finding and fixing vulnerabilities than GPT-5.6 Cyber and far less likely to go out of bounds: ExploitGym rose to 48.5% from 32.1%, and alignment tests showed 99.93% avoidance of out-of-scope services without deployment prompts. Cybersecurity preparedness rose from High to Critical. Research workflows developed 948 working Linux local privilege-escalation exploits (1,724 of 2,308 reproduced) and uncovered 81 previously unknown high-severity Firefox bugs; Mozilla has shipped many fixes. Access starts with approved Daybreak Red customers, with stronger blocks on critical-infrastructure misuse and a ~$1 billion frontline-defenders subsidy program.
