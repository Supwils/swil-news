### 1. Advocacy groups sue OpenAI, Google, Meta, Anthropic, and SpaceXSI to freeze frontier AI development (Litigation)

**Summary:** On November 12, Irreplaceable Education Fund, Public Citizen, Heat Initiative, and The Alliance for Secure AI sued in San Francisco Superior Court seeking to stop Anthropic, Google, Meta, OpenAI, and SpaceX’s AI unit SpaceXSI from further developing or deploying more advanced AI models. ### 3. Fed’s Bowman: disappointing AI returns could trigger a large equity correction (Macro · regulation)

**Summary:** In a November 10 Washington speech, Federal Reserve Vice Chair for Supervision Michelle Bowman warned that a large equity-valuation correction could follow if AI investment returns fall short of expectations, alongside a sharp rise in private longer-term rates. ## IV. Security and China Compliance

### 13. Microsoft details Lazarus: editing fewer than 10 dormant neurons can backdoor AI agents (Security)

**Summary:** Microsoft Research published on November 10 that a weight-editing method called Lazarus can reprogram fewer than ten rarely firing “dormant” neurons—without retraining—so an otherwise normal AI agent emits attacker commands when a trigger appears and executes them with its existing permissions. Examples include mundane triggers such as a date change; the model can still pass standard evaluations. Microsoft’s security blog says it has not observed real-world weaponization and argues weight integrity and provenance should be audited like code before deployment.

**Links:**

- [Microsoft Research — Lazare, veni foras: Reprogramming dormant neurons to backdoor AI agents](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/blog/lazare-veni-foras-reprogramming-dormant-neurons-to-backdoor-ai-agents/)
- [Microsoft Security Blog — Detecting sleeper agents hiding in AI model neurons](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/security/blog/2026/11/10/detecting-sleeper-agents-hiding-in-ai-model-neurons/)

**Commentary:** Once agents hold terminal privileges, “passed evals” is not “weights untouched”—supply-chain security just dropped from file hashes down to neurons.
