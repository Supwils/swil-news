## I. Capital & Chips

### 1. Anthropic sets IPO terms: $1.3–1.45T valuation, Nasdaq debut Thursday

**Summary:** The National reports Anthropic plans to offer 825 million shares at $70–78, implying a market value of roughly $1.3–1.45 trillion, below earlier hopes of up to $2 trillion; it will trade as "ANT" on Nasdaq, expected this Thursday. Nine-month revenue was $33.24 billion (up about 1,200% year on year) with a net loss above $104 billion, mostly non-cash adjustments to financing instruments. Google, Nvidia, AMD, Broadcom, Micron, Microsoft and TSMC agreed to buy $26 billion of non-voting shares at the IPO price.

### 2. Jensen Huang resigns as Nvidia CEO; CFO Colette Kress named interim CEO

**Summary:** Nvidia's SEC Form 8-K says Huang resigned as president, CEO and director, accepted by the board on Nov 11; CFO Colette Kress becomes interim CEO while keeping her CFO role, Harvey Jones becomes the first board chair, and a search for a permanent CEO has begun. The filing gives no reason; several outlets link the exit to personal scandal and Chinese-espionage allegations, which remain unconfirmed. Nvidia reports earnings Tuesday (Nov 17), with Q3 revenue expected near $109 billion.

**Links:**

**Commentary:** With its founder gone, this week's earnings become a test of governance and succession as much as of results, and the most Nvidia-dependent customers such as OpenAI feel it first.

### 3. Congress uses the defense bill to push tighter AI chip export limits on China

**Summary:** Reports say the AI Overwatch Act, Chip Security Act and MATCH Act are all in the Senate version of the defense authorization bill. House Speaker Johnson and Senate Majority Leader Thune publicly backed tighter limits on Nov 12, with Thune citing a "high probability" of new restrictions on chip exports to China. Coverage links Huang's departure to better odds for the bills; the Trump administration has not said whether it would sign them.

**Links:**

- [Daily Caller — What Jensen Huang's Exit Could Mean For Washington's Chip Controls](https://dailycaller.com/2026/11/16/jensing-huang-nvidia-chip-controls/)

**Commentary:** Losing Nvidia's strongest lobbying voice gives Congress a chance to turn export controls from executive discretion into statute, though the White House is still the final gate.
