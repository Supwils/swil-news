### 1. Bill Gates urges Congress to legislate AI safeguards, warning of a billion deaths (Policy)
**Summary:** Reuters and The Guardian reported on September 27 that Microsoft co-founder Bill Gates, in an NBC Meet the Press interview airing Sunday, said AI needs safeguards beyond corporate self-regulation: "No one thinks self-regulation is enough," and he answered "absolutely" when asked whether Congress should pass legislation. He said a kill switch alone is not enough, because systems are not yet at the point of autonomously seizing computers that cannot be shut down, and he wants to meet Trump to discuss AI.

### 2. Singapore proposes a UN framework convention on AI safeguards (Policy)
**Summary:** CNA reported on September 27 that Singapore Foreign Minister Vivian Balakrishnan, in the country's national statement at the 81st UN General Assembly, urged states to explore options including a UN framework convention on AI safeguards. He said the UN is the only apex institution with universal membership, and that countries may also need a body that performs some of the functions the ITU provides for technical standards or the IAEA provides for verification. Balakrishnan named three risk categories: humans losing control of autonomous systems, rogue actors using AI to create biological or other weapons of mass destruction or disruption, and economic, social, and political upheaval. He said "the genie is already out of the bottle" and it is too late to call a halt, so states should keep investing while requiring pre-deployment testing, limits on autonomous systems, and human intervention, adding that it is too early to conclude a convention is necessary but "at least the discussions have begun."

**Links:**

- [CNA — Singapore proposes UN framework for global AI safeguards](https://www.channelnewsasia.com/singapore/un-ai-safeguards-singapore-national-statement-6413236)
- [The Straits Times — Singapore proposes a UN framework convention on AI safety](https://www.straitstimes.com/world/united-states/singapore-proposes-a-un-framework-convention-on-ai-safety)

**Commentary:** A small state treats "too late to pause" as an institutional problem—the ask is a shared rule-making door, not another voluntary principle.

### 7. Lambda plans an Oklahoma data center: about $500 million in taxes over ten years, power costs self-funded (Infrastructure)
**Summary:** AI cloud company Lambda said on September 27 that it will build a data center at MidAmerica Industrial Park in Mayes County, Oklahoma, near Chouteau. At current rates it expects about $500 million in taxes over ten years, up to 1,000 construction jobs, and up to 100 permanent roles. Lambda said it will follow Oklahoma's Data Center Customer Ratepayer Protection Act, pay 100 percent of the site's energy costs, honor the White House Ratepayer Protection Pledge, shift or cut some compute at peak demand, and use closed-loop cooling to limit water use. Governor Kevin Stitt said those costs will not be passed to residential customers; the announcement did not disclose power capacity or a go-live date.
