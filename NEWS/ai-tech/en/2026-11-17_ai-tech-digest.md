## I. Corporate Governance and Capital

### 1. Nvidia: Jensen Huang resigns as president, CEO and director; CFO Kress becomes interim CEO

**Summary:** According to Nvidia's 8-K filing with the SEC, the board accepted Jensen Huang's resignation as president, CEO and director on November 11, effective immediately. CFO Colette Kress is interim CEO while the board searches internally and externally for a successor, and Harvey C. Jones becomes the board's first-ever chair. The filing gives no reason; the board had announced an independent investigation on November 8, which media link to reports about his personal relationships and a foreign intelligence probe (via Reuters, details differ by outlet).

**Commentary:** The world's most valuable AI chip company has lost its founder amid new-product and export-control pressure, making governance uncertainty a risk factor for the whole AI supply chain.

## II. Cloud and Infrastructure

### 3. Microsoft 365 / Exchange Online outage lasts more than 30 hours

**Summary:** Exchange Online suffered a prolonged outage on November 12-13, reported at more than 30 hours (sources count slightly differently), affecting Outlook, Teams meeting scheduling and Copilot mail access. Microsoft attributed it to a network gateway issue triggered by an earlier network capacity upgrade, not a cyberattack. Business, enterprise and education customers can file SLA credit claims.

**Links:**

- [Microsoft 365 for IT Pros — The Great Exchange Online Meltdown of November 2026](https://office365itpros.com/2026/11/16/exchange-online-outage-nov26/)

**Commentary:** With Copilot embedded in daily work, one low-level network change can take down both email and the AI assistant, exposing the fragility of concentrated cloud dependence.

## III. Space and Satellite Networks

### 4. SpaceX Starship Flight 15: ship returns from orbit and is caught by the tower for the first time

**Summary:** Starship launched from Starbase, Texas on November 13 (US time) and released 26 Starlink V3 satellites about 40 minutes in. After 14 orbits and roughly 22.5 hours, the ship was caught by the launch tower arms on November 14, the first upper-stage catch. The Super Heavy booster's tower catch was called off and it was diverted to the Gulf of Mexico, with sources disagreeing on the cause.

### 5. ULA Vulcan return to flight fails, destroying 40 Amazon Leo satellites

**Summary:** On November 15, a Vulcan launched from Cape Canaveral suffered an anomaly about 10 minutes into flight, and its 40 Amazon Leo satellites, the largest Leo batch yet, did not reach orbit. It was Vulcan's first flight in about nine months and, per Spaceflight Now, its second failure in four flights. The cause is unknown, and Reuters reports Blue Origin is assisting the investigation.
