### 2. Sanders and Casar introduce Ban Artificial Superintelligence Act: permanent ASI ban, advanced-AI pause, cabinet-level AI department (Policy)
**Summary:** Sen. Bernie Sanders and Rep. Greg Casar on September 23 introduced the Ban Artificial Superintelligence Act, which would permanently prohibit developing or deploying “artificial superintelligence” (defined as systems that exceed human cognition or can plan and execute the destruction or disempowerment of humanity) and pause advanced AI development and unreleased advanced deployments until a new federal body sets rules. The bill would create a cabinet-level Department of Artificial Intelligence to approve advanced-model deployments and oversee removal of dangerous capabilities; individual violations could carry up to about 20 years in prison. Some employees at leading AI labs have endorsed the measure; passage remains doubtful under a Congress and White House resistant to sweeping federal brakes.

### 3. Newsom names expert panel for AI executive order: independent onsite verification and a frontier “kill switch” (Regulation)
**Summary:** California Gov. Gavin Newsom on September 23 announced experts—including Jason Goldman, Gillian Hadfield, Alondra Nelson, and Rob Reich—to deliver recommendations under his recent AI executive order, due around November 16. Topics include embedding designated independent verification organizations inside frontier labs for periodic audits; independently verifying safety frameworks and transparency reports; advancing a “kill switch” for frontier models whose efficacy is continually verified; and expanding reportable critical safety incidents to cover loss-of-control cases. The order itself does not enact a mandatory switch; it accelerates implementation of SB 813 and AB 1405 on independent verification and auditor registries.

### 5. Anthropic’s wet lab posts a first public result: nearly 1,000 Claude agents flag a novel phage enzyme system, framed like CRISPR (Science)
**Summary:** Anthropic said on September 23 that the first result from its new wet lab came after roughly 950 Claude agents spent 21 hours and about 210 million tokens searching DNA-sequence data, spotted an unusual repeating pattern, and—after human review and lab work—identified a previously uncharacterized enzyme system in bacteriophages.
