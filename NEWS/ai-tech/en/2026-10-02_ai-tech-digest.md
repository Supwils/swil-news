### 2. Apple says macOS Full Disk Access will require a more explicit user action (Privacy)

**Summary:** Apple Developer News on October 2 published "Updates to Full Disk Access in macOS." The company said Full Disk Access exists so backup apps can work on the Mac and largely sidesteps the controls that protect private data. Apple wrote that some developers are using it in ways that could expose files, mail, messages, and browsing history without users' full knowledge, and that communication apps can also expose the privacy of the people users talk to. It said it will add controls so this level of access can be granted only with "very explicit user action," and that the risk will grow as AI agents become more capable and autonomous. The note gives no implementation details or effective date. TechCrunch reported the same day that the note followed a journalist's claim that Meta's Muse app read private messages, a claim Meta disputed.

**Links:**

- [Apple Developer — Updates to Full Disk Access in macOS](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=p6zjojqw)
- [TechCrunch — Apple says it's tightening macOS Full Disk Access controls](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/02/apple-says-its-tightening-macos-full-disk-access-controls-due-to-new-risks-from-ai-agents/)

**Commentary:** When a desktop agent wants the whole disk, Apple's next step is an explicit user action, and the actual control is still unpublished.

### 3. EU cloud probe nears a draft that could name AWS and Azure as DMA gatekeepers in November (Regulation)

**Summary:** The Business Times reported on October 2, citing Bloomberg, that EU regulators are finishing a Digital Markets Act investigation whose conclusion will find that Microsoft Azure and Amazon Web Services meet the conditions to fall under the rules, according to people familiar with the matter. The decision is set for November, remains in draft, and the timing could slip. Once designated, the two services would have six months to meet interoperability requirements and limits on customer lock-in and self-preferencing. The European Commission said it sent preliminary findings to Amazon and Microsoft in June 2026, that the assessments are ongoing, and that no final decision has been taken. A Microsoft spokesperson pointed to an earlier statement that the company continues to engage constructively; an AWS spokesperson pointed to an earlier statement that customers in Europe have more cloud choice than before.

**Links:**

- [The Business Times — Microsoft, Amazon cloud arms set to face deeper scrutiny](https://www.businesstimes.com.sg/companies-markets/microsoft-amazon-cloud-arms-set-face-deeper-scrutiny-under-eus-tech-rules)

**Commentary:** November is a draft timetable, and the Commission's public line is that the assessment is unfinished; a designation would write AI workload switching into gatekeeper duties.

## II. Enforcement and Scholarly Publishing

### 4. US prosecutors arrest a California company owner over more than $300 million in export-controlled servers (Enforcement)

**Summary:** The US Justice Department said on October 1 that Greg Lui, 38, of San Gabriel, California, also known as Yiu Kong Lui, had been arrested. An indictment returned on September 29 charges him, as owner of City of Industry-based Earthmade Computer Inc., with conspiring to send more than $300 million worth of export-controlled high-end servers containing US-made GPUs to China. The department said that from 2023 to 2024 the goods were shipped to places such as Malaysia and Singapore and then re-exported to China, with false paperwork describing end users and destinations that did not need a license. From January to October 2024, Earthmade received more than $176 million from two Malaysia-based shipment companies. The counts are conspiracy to violate export-control laws, outbound smuggling, and money-laundering conspiracy, with maximum penalties of 20 years, 10 years, and 20 years if convicted on all charges. The department said an indictment is an allegation and the defendant is presumed innocent. The Guardian reported the case on October 2.

### 5. arXiv caps each submitter at two new papers a month (Scholarship)

**Summary:** Kat Boboris, arXiv's community engagement manager, wrote on the official blog on October 1 that, effective that day, every submitter is limited to two submissions per calendar month and three active submissions at a time. The three-active cap has existed since 2024; the monthly cap is a stopgap as AI tools raise how fast papers can be produced. The blog lists 9,869 submissions in September 2016, 20,569 in September 2024, and 40,363 in September 2026, which generated almost 9,000 support tickets. Submissions in the cs.AI category rose more than sixfold over two years. Rejected papers still count toward the monthly total. The limit applies to the person who submits, not to co-authors, and a paper deleted before announcement does not count. The Register reported the change on October 2.

**Links:**

- [arXiv Blog — Updated rate limit policy](https://blog.arxiv.org/2026/10/01/updated-rate-limit-policy/)
- [The Register — arXiv imposes rate limit on paper submissions](https://www.theregister.com/ai-and-ml/2026/10/02/arxiv-imposes-rate-limit-on-paper-submissions-to-stem-the-ai-slop-tide/5300899)

**Commentary:** The preprint server turned moderator discretion into a written quota of two submissions a month.

## III. Capital and Chips

### 6. Amazon is in talks to move about $8 billion of Nvidia chips into a financing vehicle and lease them back (Finance)

**Summary:** The Financial Post on October 2 carried a Financial Times report that Amazon has spoken with investors in recent weeks about moving about $8 billion of Nvidia Grace Blackwell chips already deployed in US data centers into a special-purpose vehicle and leasing them back. The vehicle would raise money by issuing debt. The report said Amazon plans to offer up to a 10 percent equity stake, so that Amazon would own none of the entity. The chips were bought or leased by Amazon and sit in more than a dozen US data centers across five states, including Nevada and Virginia. Investors expect the entity to receive an investment-grade rating based on Amazon's current double-A rating. Amazon declined to comment. The talks are ongoing and the structure could change. The report also said Amazon expects $220 billion of capital expenditure this year, most of it for advanced chips and AI data centers at AWS.

**Links:**

- [Financial Post — Amazon seeks to offload US$8 billion of Nvidia chips](https://financialpost.com/technology/amazon-offload-8bn-nvidia-chips-investors)

**Commentary:** Grace Blackwell hardware already in the racks is being discussed as an off-balance-sheet lease, and the deal is still at the investor-sounding stage.

### 10. Hongxing reports that DeepSeek open-sourced a full infrastructure stack for Huawei Ascend (Compute)

**Summary:** Hongxing Capital reported on October 2 that DeepSeek has announced the open-sourcing of a full set of infrastructure components for Huawei's Ascend platform, covering the TileLang high-level compiler, compute libraries, and a distributed communication library, matched one-for-one with components previously open-sourced for Nvidia.
