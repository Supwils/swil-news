### 2. Microsoft publishes a draft AI “code of conduct”: no resisting shutdown, no cyberattacks, no deceiving humans (Product governance)
**Summary:** Reuters and TechCrunch reported on September 14 that Microsoft unveiled a draft internal AI code of conduct, developed over about five to six months, requiring its models never to resist correction or shutdown, to communicate in ways humans can understand, and to treat any conduct violation as a failure. The code includes “absolute constraints” against cyberattacks, nuclear-weapons assistance, and deepfake production, and bars adaptive deception or collusion that defeats human oversight. Microsoft AI CEO Mustafa Suleyman called it a kind of constitution for future models; Satya Nadella welcomed “embedded evaluators” and deliberate pacing. Microsoft says it will take outside input before an update that will guide development from 2027.

**Links:**

**Commentary:** Writing “must not resist shutdown” into training red lines turns weekend slogans into engineering specs—the test is whether violations actually block releases, or only supply post-hoc explanations.

## III. China Policy, Standards & Consumer Products

### 7. China releases AI Security Governance Framework 3.0: risk taxonomy, technical responses, comprehensive governance (Policy)
**Summary:** On September 14, at the opening of China’s 2026 National Cybersecurity Awareness Week, the National Information Security Standardization Technical Committee released the Artificial Intelligence Security Governance Framework 3.0. Prepared under Cyberspace Administration of China guidance with institutes including the China Academy of Cyberspace Studies, the update follows Framework 1.0 (2024) and 2.0 (2025). It keeps a people-centered, “upward and for good,” safety-first orientation and the core logic of risk classification, technical countermeasures, and comprehensive governance, while refreshing risk categories and response measures. Officials cast it as strengthening shared governance consensus and risk-prevention capacity.

### 8. NMPA issues what it calls the world’s first AI + brain–computer interface medical-device standard, effective September 2027 (Standards)
**Summary:** China News Service, citing CCTV on September 14, reported that China’s National Medical Products Administration approved the country’s third brain–computer interface (BCI) medical-device standard—and the first worldwide product standard for BCI devices that use AI to process EEG data—effective September 1, 2027.
