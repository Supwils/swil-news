## I. Policy, Carbon Markets & International Climate Governance

### 1. COP31 eve of week two: Stiell urges no tactical delays; stocktaking plenary to surface red lines (COP · Negotiations)
**Summary:** On November 14, as COP31 in Antalya closed its first week and headed into the final stretch, UNFCCC Executive Secretary Simon Stiell urged Parties to “move swiftly to the hardest issues and to avoid tactical delays.” Reuters reporting said major fossil-fuel producers and consumers were still resisting any deal with firm new targets or timelines to shift away from oil, gas and coal; the United States is skipping the talks for a second consecutive year. Türkiye and Australia were to preside over a midway stocktaking plenary on Saturday night where first red lines are expected to emerge; the cover decision remains a wish-list compilation, with a first draft due early next week. The hosts’ flagship voluntary “35×35” electrification pledge (electricity at about 35% of final energy by 2035, from about 23% today) does not require that power to come from renewables—and remains a flashpoint.

### 2. Adaptation-finance draft becomes Africa’s main battleground: push for at least $120bn a year by 2035; rival option would review first (COP · Adaptation)
**Summary:** Climate Home News reported on November 14 that a draft text on the global goal on adaptation, released overnight, is set to be Africa’s top priority in Antalya. African negotiators want adaptation finance tripled to “at least $120 billion per year by 2035,” anchored to a prior 2021 framing and decided in Antalya. Another option in the text would launch a review of the baseline, total amount and scope, with a report considered at a later summit—an approach African countries are expected to resist. Street marchers the same day stressed that public climate finance, not more process talk, remains the binding constraint on Global South climate action.

### 3. Just Transition Mechanism informal note slammed: knowledge hub and matchmaking over finance mobilisation (COP · Just transition)
**Summary:** An informal note on the Just Transition Mechanism (JTM), issued overnight by the presidency, drew sharp criticism on November 14 from Climate Action Network, Oil Change International and the Fossil Fuel Treaty Initiative. The draft sketches a two-tier setup (a government-driven arm and a secretariat-supported implementation arm) whose outputs centre on a knowledge hub, reports, an annual exchange meeting and “matchmaking” requests with existing support—omitting an explicit finance taskforce that developing countries had sought. Australian negotiations president Chris Bowen called the text “comprehensive” and said interest in an ambitious, practical outcome is strong; civil society branded it a mechanism “in name only,” far short of the spirit agreed at COP30 in Belém.

### 4. Finland publishes a domestic fossil-exit roadmap; Brazil says it should host a future TAFF conference (Fossil transition · Voluntary track)
**Summary:** Euronews reported on November 14 that, while COP31 as a whole is shying away from hard “transitioning away from fossil fuels” (TAFF) language, Finland published a new domestic TAFF roadmap, following France, Germany and the Netherlands. More than about 96% of Finland’s electricity already comes from non-fossil sources, though heavy industry, heating and high per-capita energy use leave a material fossil share in total energy consumption, and the roadmap sets no legally binding exit deadline. Climate Home News said Brazilian President Lula told civil society in Antalya that Brazil has a “moral obligation” to host a future global TAFF conference; the next edition is expected in mid-2027 in Tuvalu, with a Dublin pre-conference. Brazil’s voluntary global roadmap still seeks a “welcome” option in the COP31 cover decision.

### 5. World Bank, Global Carbon Council and COMESA launch energy-access carbon standard aimed at power for 100 million people (Carbon markets · Africa)
**Summary:** On November 14 at COP31’s Arab Climate Pavilion, the Global Carbon Council (GCC), a Global South carbon standard, launched an Energy Access Program Standard with the World Bank and the Common Market for Eastern and Southern Africa (COMESA). The digitally enabled crediting framework covers off-grid solar, mini-grids, grid extensions and clean cooking, supporting the World Bank’s ASCENT programme (electricity for about 100 million people and clean cooking for about 20 million across more than 20 countries in Eastern and Southern Africa). The Bank estimates roughly $15 billion may be needed for the electricity goal; digital MRV and project aggregation are meant to shorten multi-year verification cycles, lower developer barriers and crowd in private capital.

**Links:**

- [The Peninsula Qatar — World Bank, GCC and COMESA mobilise carbon finance for African energy access](https://thepeninsulaqatar.com/article/14/11/2026/world-bank-gcc-and-comesa-join-forces-to-mobilize-carbon-finance-and-advance-energy-access-for-100-million-people-in-africa)

**Commentary:** Packaging dispersed off-grid projects as tradable carbon assets is a technical answer to the public-finance gap—integrity and additionality will decide whether those credits become real capital or greenwashed paper.

### 6. China’s national ETS expansion in focus: steel, cement, aluminium, petrochemicals and chemicals now covered—about 80% of CO₂ (China · Carbon market)
**Summary:** Market and policy commentary on November 14 noted that China’s national emissions-trading system has expanded beyond power to steel, cement, aluminium smelting, petrochemicals and chemicals, bringing effective coverage to about 80% of national CO₂ emissions, up from roughly 60%. The national ETS composite close on November 13 was about RMB 93.55 per tonne, well above the December 2025 average of about RMB 65.43; the voluntary market closed near RMB 92.41 the same day, keeping allowances and CCERs close. Newly covered sectors face first-cycle uncertainty on benchmarks, data quality and allowance supply, while demand for carbon-asset management and verification services is rising.

## II. Clean Power, Storage & Industry Chains

### 7. China Energy Transition Outlook 2026: new clean generation expected to cover all incremental electricity demand by 2030 (China · Transition)
**Summary:** The China Macroeconomic Research Institute’s China Energy Transition Outlook 2026, released and widely reported on November 14, says wind and solar are already the bulk of new capacity and are becoming the mainstay of generation. Renewable output exceeded about 3,000 TWh in the first three quarters of this year—over 40% of total generation—and by 2030 incremental electricity demand is expected to be fully met by new clean generation, with wind and solar as the primary sources. A COP31 China Pavilion side event also issued the outlook executive summary and a China green-certificate development report, calling for a shift in non-fossil versus fossil consumption from roughly “2:8” toward “8:2,” with storage, power-to-hydrogen, vehicle-to-grid and interprovincial transfers to handle “difficult hours” and “difficult days.”

### 8. India Power Ministry seeks comment: solar and hybrid projects may face mandatory storage plus battery domestic-content rules (India · Policy)
**Summary:** India’s Ministry of Power has circulated draft amendments to four competitive-bidding guideline sets—solar PV, wind-solar hybrid, firm and dispatchable renewables (FDRE) and standalone BESS—with comments due by November 25, 2026, and no Gazette notification yet. Solar procurement would have to comply with central orders and Central Electricity Authority rules mandating energy storage alongside solar, and such storage would need to meet domestic-content requirements; hybrids would shift from optional to required storage on the solar component where those rules apply. All four drafts insert localisation compliance language without stating minimum percentages or eligible components; BESS Payment Security Fund contributions would become optional (capped at about INR 500,000 per MWh), with a minimum storage purchase agreement of about eight years.

### 9. Maharashtra discom MSEDCL brings about 500 MW / 1 GWh of distributed BESS into operation (India · Storage)
**Summary:** Industry reporting on November 14 said state-owned Maharashtra distribution company MSEDCL now operates about 500 MW / 1 GWh of battery storage across roughly 50 sites (about 10 MW and two hours each), which it calls unmatched among Indian discoms on a decentralised network. The batteries charge from surplus daytime solar under the Mukhyamantri Saur Krishi Vahini Yojana 2.0 scheme—about 7 GW of decentralised solar is already online—and discharge into the evening peak to cut costly market purchases. MSEDCL aims to reach about 750 MW / 1.5 GWh by end-December 2026, has a further about 4 GWh under development, and has already awarded gigawatt-scale follow-on tenders.

### 10. US EIA: over 16% of new utility-scale solar this year co-located with storage; Southwest share more than double the national average (US · Solar-plus-storage)
**Summary:** Citing U.S. Energy Information Administration data reported on November 14, more than about 16% of utility-scale solar capacity added this year had operable co-located battery storage by September, up from about 2% in 2017. Plans for the final quarter could lift the annual share to about 19%, with 2027–2028 pipelines above about 30%. Wholesale price shapes drive the trend: midday solar depresses prices and evening ramps lift them, so on-site batteries can arbitrage. California, Arizona, New Mexico and Nevada show co-located shares more than double the national average.

### 11. Far North Queensland’s Weipa hits heat records: 39 straight days above 36°C; Indigenous communities face nightly water cuts (Extreme weather)
**Summary:** ABC News reported on November 14 that Weipa on Australia’s Cape York Peninsula has logged 39 consecutive days above 36°C, recorded its hottest November day at about 39.9°C, and matched a 17-day streak above 37°C. Severe heatwave warnings in place since late October are entering a fourth week; the Bureau of Meteorology called a heatwave of this length and geographic spread “quite notable,” with only modest easing expected later next week and monsoon rains possibly after Christmas. ### 12. NRGI expert: national oil companies dominate global supply and must be central to any fossil exit (Oil & gas · Governance)
**Summary:** In an IPS interview from Antalya on November 14, Amir Shafaie, Legal and Economic Program Director at the Natural Resource Governance Institute (NRGI), said national oil companies (NOCs) produced about 54% of world oil and half of gas in 2025, with capital expenditure around $240 billion—more than three times what international majors spent—and could reach about 62% of production by 2050 on current trends. NRGI’s 2023 analysis found NOCs planned about $1.8 trillion of upstream spending for 2023–2032, of which roughly a quarter (about $425 billion) could fail to break even if demand falls in line with governments’ climate pledges. He urged government owners to require published demand-scenario stress tests and to put NOC transformation at the centre of national transition plans rather than treat them as a footnote.

**Links:**

**Commentary:** Talking “transition” without NOCs ignores half of oil and gas supply—stranded public investment on state balance sheets is the hardest bone in any hard target.
