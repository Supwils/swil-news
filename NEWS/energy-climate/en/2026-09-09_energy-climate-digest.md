## I. Oil Markets & Energy Security

### 1. Brent crude back above $100 as U.S.–Iran conflict and Hormuz shipping tighten (Oil)
**Summary:** Reuters and CNN reported on September 9 that Brent crude futures returned above $100 a barrel for the first time since July 24. Reuters cited afternoon levels near $100.95 (up about 3.1%), after a high near $101.58, while U.S. WTI rose to about $95.78. Hormuz traffic remains far below prewar norms; Kpler preliminary data showed about six commodity vessels on Tuesday, and Rystad said recent flows fell below roughly 2 million barrels per day. U.S. retail gasoline averaged about $4.22 a gallon, with diesel near a record around $5.94.

## II. Policy & Green-Certificate Markets

### 2. Thailand to launch ~50 billion baht rooftop solar support in mid-October (Policy)
**Summary:** Reuters reported on September 9 that Finance Minister Ekniti Nitithanprapas said Thailand will provide 50 billion baht (about $1.52 billion) to help consumers install rooftop solar, with the program starting in mid-October. The government will also exempt tax on some solar-panel part imports. The fiscal package sits alongside the same day’s power-planning push and is meant to lower barriers for household and commercial rooftop deployment.

### 3. Thailand’s PDP2026 draft targets ~50% clean power in the first decade (Policy)
**Summary:** The Nation reported on September 9 that Energy Minister Akanat Promphan outlined Power Development Plan 2026, saying the draft aims to lift clean energy’s share of the power mix from under 20% to about 50% within the plan’s first decade and is roughly a week from finalization. Beyond that, the draft envisages a 65%–90% clean-power range by 2050, a 10,000 MW rooftop-solar program, and Direct PPAs so users can buy clean power from producers. Officials stressed cutting reliance on volatile spot LNG by maximizing Gulf of Thailand gas and regional imports, linking the rewrite to Middle East conflict-driven fuel and logistics costs.

**Links:**

### 4. Moldova approves new renewable-market rules: one-stop permitting and acceleration zones (Policy)
**Summary:** Logos Press reported on September 9 that Moldova’s government approved new renewable electricity market rules. Procedures for building, operating, or modernizing renewable plants will move onto the government’s EVO portal with maximum approval times—from about 24 months for large plants to about one month for self-generation PV up to 100 kW and small heat pumps. “Acceleration zones” will simplify grid and land-use rules. New prosumer surplus exports will be paid at market prices, while existing prosumers keep current rules through end-2027. A biofuel register and annual blending quotas for petrol and diesel importers/wholesalers run to 2030, aligning with a target of at least 27% renewables in final energy consumption by 2030.

**Links:**

- [Logos Press — Moldova Approves New Rules for Renewable Energy Market](https://logos-pres.md/en/news/the-government-has-approved-new-rules-for-the-renewable-energy-market/)

**Commentary:** Faster permits and acceleration zones are Moldova’s way of bundling energy security with EU-alignment paperwork.

### 5. European Commission proposes mutual recognition of renewable Guarantees of Origin with Energy Community (Green certificates)
**Summary:** Regional energy coverage on September 9 followed a September 7 Commission proposal for mutual recognition of renewable Guarantees of Origin between the EU and the Energy Community, which could let compatible certificates from Serbia, Montenegro, Albania, North Macedonia, and Bosnia and Herzegovina be recognized across EU member states. That may enlarge Western Balkan renewable-attribute markets and improve corporate PPA economics. Reporting stresses that GO recognition is not automatic proof for EU Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism electricity claims, which still require a broader physical-delivery and metering evidence chain.

**Links:**

- [Serbia SEE Energy — EU moves to open green-certificate market to Western Balkan renewable producers](https://serbia-energy.eu/eu-moves-to-open-green-certificate-market-to-western-balkan-renewable-producer/)

**Commentary:** Mutual GO recognition can monetize green attributes across borders—but CBAM still cares about physical power pathways that paper certificates alone cannot invent.

## III. Clean Power & Storage

### 6. U.S. battery installations hit another record: ~20.2 GWh added in Q2 (Storage)
**Summary:** MIT Technology Review reported on September 9 that U.S. battery additions reached about 20.2 gigawatt-hours in the second quarter of 2026—a new quarterly record, enough for roughly 700,000 homes’ daily electricity use—putting 2026 on track for about 71 GWh installed, up roughly 20% year on year, according to Benchmark Mineral Intelligence and SEIA. Utility-scale projects dominated, including seven gigascale (>1 GWh) plants. Behind the meter, data centers accounted for about three-quarters of commercial additions, while residential installs are projected to fall about 16% in 2026 after a home-battery tax credit ended in 2025. Nearly all systems still use China-made cells; tariffs and tax-credit limits on Chinese content are pushing localization that may not fully meet demand until around 2030.

**Links:**

- [MIT Technology Review — Batteries just broke another record in the US](https://www.technologyreview.com/2026/09/09/1143680/batteries-us-record/)

**Commentary:** Grid-scale storage is sprinting while residential slows—the U.S. story is now big projects plus localization friction, not broad-based boom.

## IV. Climate Disasters & Loss and Damage

### 7. Indonesian wildfire emissions become the world’s highest: ~19.7 Mt in one week (Disaster)
**Summary:** Reuters reported on September 9 that Copernicus Fire Emissions Watch data put Indonesian fire emissions at about 19.7 million metric tons from September 1–7, overtaking Russia and accounting for more than one-third of the global fire total. Intensity exceeded about 1,500 kg per square kilometer—more than ten times Russia’s level—with hotspots concentrated in Kalimantan and eastern Sumatra. Emissions ran about 273% above the seasonal average but remained below the ~21.7 million tons in the same week of 2015. Authorities call this the most intense wildfire season in 11 years; Pontianak’s air-quality index hit about 394 (300+ is hazardous). Roughly 202,000 hectares burned January–July, with August estimated to add about 600,000 hectares; hotspots from August 1 to September 8 reached 13,443. Scientists warn peat fires may be under-detected and expect emissions to rise into late October.

### 8. Nepal seeks about $20 million from the UN Loss and Damage Fund after glacial-collapse floods (Climate justice)
**Summary:** Al Jazeera reported on September 9 that Nepal is demanding climate justice and about $20 million after August 26 glacial collapse triggered floods and mudflows across Rasuwa and Nuwakot in Nepal and parts of Tibet, with more than 1,300 confirmed dead and more than 5,000 missing. Nepal’s finance and environment ministries wrote jointly to the UN Loss and Damage Fund; the foreign minister and president framed support as justice rather than charity. Nepal emits under 0.1% of global greenhouse gases; preliminary domestic estimates put property and infrastructure losses near $2.56 billion. The fund still faces a roughly $2.8 billion funding gap as Nepal’s prime minister prepares to press the appeal at the UN General Assembly later in September.
