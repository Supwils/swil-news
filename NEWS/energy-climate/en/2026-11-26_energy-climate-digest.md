### 1. OPEC rejects “petrostate wrecked the talks” narrative, pins COP31 collapse on the EU (geopolitics)
**Summary:** OPEC Secretary General Haitham Al Ghais, in a post titled “What Really Happened at COP31?,” said oil- and gas-producing countries negotiated in “good faith” in Antalya and were not the parties that walked out or paralyzed talks, challenging media blame aimed at Saudi Arabia, Russia and other producers. Producers had opposed language on transitioning away from fossil fuels and backed references to carbon capture, utilization and storage (CCUS). The summit ended without agreement after the EU and others rejected a political text from co-hosts Türkiye and Australia that omitted a fossil-fuel transition. Al Ghais accused the EU of hypocrisy for delaying methane accounting rules for oil and gas importers until 2029; COP President Murat Kurum said the EU’s focus on the “definition of fossil fuels,” along with climate-finance disputes, blocked consensus. Marshall Islands lead negotiator Kathy Jetnil-Kijiner said she would “sleep well” knowing she did not endorse a CCUS-heavy deal.

### 2. Australia’s annual climate statement: emissions down 1.3% to June 2026, far short of the 2035 path (policy)
**Summary:** Australia’s annual climate statement on Nov 26 showed greenhouse gas emissions fell 1.3% in the year to June 2026, a cut of about 6.2 million tonnes. Climate and Energy Minister Chris Bowen said the pace was about 1.5 times the average of the past decade. Climate Change Authority chair Matt Kean warned the country needs cuts of 25–30 million tonnes a year—“a formidable task”—and that hitting the ambitious 70% end of the 2035 target range would require about 30 Mt a year for nine years, nearly five times the latest drop. Electricity emissions fell 3.6%, with renewables supplying a record 45.9% of National Electricity Market power; transport emissions edged up 0.1% as diesel and domestic aviation offset petrol declines and EV gains. Experts said the 82% renewables-by-2030 goal looks out of reach on time; the bottleneck is turning approved projects into grid electrons.

**Links:**

- [ABC News — Australia's emissions fall but not fast enough to reach 2035 climate target](https://www.abc.net.au/news/2026-11-26/australia-emissions-fall-short-2035-climate-target/107418552)

**Commentary:** Directionally right, slope far too shallow—power is leading, but the 2035 band demands roughly fivefold delivery speed, not a polite annual win.

### 3. U.S. House debates BAAJA permitting reform as advocates warn of weakened clean-water rules (policy)
**Summary:** The Guardian reported on Nov 26 that the Bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act (BAAJA), which passed the Senate 62–33, is now before the House. The bill aims to shorten federal approvals for energy and infrastructure such as power lines, fossil pipelines, gas plants and data centers, but also includes provisions that would make it harder for states to set water-pollution protections, stretch automatic water-permit reviews from five to ten years, and reduce pollution transparency. More than 50 groups in PFAS-affected communities oppose the measure; Clean Water Action called it a handout to oil and gas with little link to real permitting bottlenecks. Supporters say faster builds will aid the clean-energy transition; critics say that narrative is cover for fossil and chemical deregulation. A House vote is expected next week.

**Commentary:** “Speed the grid” and “ease pollution rules” are stapled into one bill—the lame-duck vote tests whether the U.S. will trade water safeguards for build-out pace.

### 4. Türkiye unveils National Energy 2040: 77% renewable share of capacity, about $190 billion investment (policy)
**Summary:** Energy and Natural Resources Minister Alparslan Bayraktar on Nov 26 presented Türkiye’s National Energy 2040 roadmap: total installed capacity rising from about 123 GW in 2025 to 281 GW by 2040, with renewables’ share of capacity rising from about 63% to 77%. Investment is pegged at around $190 billion, with about 95% directed to zero- or low-emission sources. Combined wind and solar would reach 175 GW (including offshore), requiring about $100 billion; a first offshore wind tender is planned for April 2027 toward a 6 GW offshore target by 2040. Electricity’s share of final energy use would rise to 36%, with demand seen reaching about 680 TWh; nuclear capacity is targeted at 10 GW, including Akkuyu plus about 5 GW of new build. Bayraktar cited an annual energy import bill of roughly $65–70 billion and said oil, gas and a separate mining roadmap (by 2027) remain part of the strategy.

### 5. India draft solar rules would curb profitable net metering and mandate batteries from 2027–2028 (policy)
**Summary:** India’s central government released draft amendments that would empower state regulators to limit profitable net metering and, from April 1, 2027, levy a special grid charge on consumption above a commission-set threshold. Systems above 10 kW would face mandatory battery storage of up to 20% of generation from April 1, 2028, alongside time-of-day tariffs. The draft also allows off-site installations, capacity above sanctioned load, and requires distributors to review bills that spike fivefold or drop to one-fifth. Coverage notes the move echoes Kerala rules previously stayed by a high court and would likely cut returns for many distributed solar owners while raising bills.

**Links:**

- [Mathrubhumi — Solar policy shift: Centre proposes net metering curbs and mandatory battery storage](https://english.mathrubhumi.com/news/india/solar-policy-shift-centre-proposes-curbs-on-profitable-net-metering-and-mandatory-battery-storage-cx9wmik1)

**Commentary:** India is twisting rooftop solar from “sell-and-arbitrage” toward “self-consume plus store”—grid externalities are finally getting an explicit price tag.

### 6. Australia CER Q3 report: large-scale battery FID hits 20 GWh YTD; ACCU issuance sets a quarterly record (carbon markets)
**Summary:** The Clean Energy Regulator on Nov 26 updated its September-quarter 2026 carbon market report: large-scale renewables reaching final investment decision totaled 5.0 GW in the first three quarters—already above the prior full-year record of 4.9 GW—and about 5.5 GW by mid-November, with more than half CIS-supported. Home batteries under the Cheaper Home Batteries Program reached 10.3 GWh installed and approved by end-Q3, lifting the 2026 outlook to 13–15.5 GWh. Large-scale storage FID was 13.8 GWh in Q3 and 20.0 GWh for 2026 to date; average battery capacity entering service rose from 265 MWh in 2025 to 621 MWh in 2026. ACCU issuance hit a record 8.2 million in Q3 and 19.1 million year-to-date. Preliminary Safeguard Mechanism data showed emissions at previously covered facilities down 0.5%, while total covered emissions rose 0.4% on newly covered oil and gas sites. CER said distributed solar and storage helped push NEM wholesale prices about 27% lower than Q3 2025.

**Links:**

## II. Clean Power, Storage & Power Markets

### 7. China’s first northwest–east China joint green-power trade clears ~61.7 TWh across 1,615 participants (power markets)
**Summary:** China’s National Energy Administration reported on Nov 26 that the northwest–east China joint green-power market launched in Beijing and completed its first clearing: 1,615 market participants (1,188 generators and 427 demand-side entities) across ten provinces and regions; cleared volume was 61.7 TWh, including 19.5 TWh inter-provincial and 42.2 TWh intra-provincial, at an average price of 0.204 yuan/kWh. For the first time, inter- and intra-provincial green-power markets cleared under one rule set with a single bid. Xinhua estimated nearly 5 million tonnes of CO2 avoided. The move implements State Council guidelines (Guobanfa [2026] No. 4) on a unified national electricity market; State Grid said trading will become routine and expand in scope and tenor.

### 8. France triples technology-neutral renewables auction to 1.5 GW and adopts a single baseload reference price (clean power)
**Summary:** pv magazine reported on Nov 26 that the French government plans to raise its upcoming technology-neutral renewables auction from 500 MW in draft CRE rules to 1.5 GW and open it to all renewable generation technologies after industry pushback. A second change introduces a single M0 baseload reference price for all technologies, replacing technology-specific profile prices (e.g., solar M0 tied to midday hours) with the national monthly average of wholesale spot prices across all hours. That exposes producers more directly to wholesale swings. The auction is expected to launch within days.

## III. Climate Monitoring & Energy Security

### 13. Planet logs consecutive days above 2°C: Copernicus shows a peak daily anomaly near 2.24–2.25°C (climate)
**Summary:** The Guardian, El País and others, citing Copernicus Climate Change Service data, reported that global mean surface temperature stayed more than 2°C above pre-industrial levels for at least six to seven consecutive days, with a Nov 23 anomaly of about 2.24–2.25°C and a record ~1.34°C above the 1991–2020 baseline. Scientists stressed this does not mean the Paris Agreement’s long-term 2°C limit is breached—that metric uses multi-year averages—but it is an early signal of what may come, amplified by a strong El Niño nearing peak plus ongoing fossil emissions. Copernicus climate lead Samantha Burgess said El Niño will pass; greenhouse-gas warming will not unless emissions fall. Figures remain preliminary, typically within about ±0.02°C.
