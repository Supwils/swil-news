## I. Policy & Carbon Markets

### 1. CCICED opens: Huang Runqiu cites green-power gains and a glacial collapse warning (Policy)
**Summary:** Xinhua and Cover News reported on September 27 that the 2026 annual meeting of the China Council for International Cooperation on Environment and Development opened in Beijing under the theme of practical innovation and joint green progress. Environment Minister Huang Runqiu, the council's Chinese executive vice chair, said a glacial collapse this year on the Nepal side of the China-Nepal border caused large loss of life and property, and that climate change is now an immediate threat. He cited 1.17 billion kilowatts of coal units and 990 million tonnes of crude-steel capacity retrofitted to ultra-low emissions; 101 nuclear units operating or under construction, the most of any country; 2.455 billion kilowatts of renewable capacity, about 60.7% of total installed power, of which solar and wind are 1.951 billion kilowatts, about 48.3%; and nearly 4 of every 10 kilowatt-hours consumed nationwide coming from green power. New-energy vehicle production and sales each exceeded 16 million in 2025, leading the world for an 11th year. Coal's share of power generation fell below 50% for the first time in the first half of this year, and non-fossil energy already exceeds 21.7% of the energy mix.

### 2. EU letter urges gas and power demand cuts, with storage near 70% and TTF near €72 (Policy)
**Summary:** Reuters on September 25 and Euronews on September 27 reported that EU Energy Commissioner Dan Jorgensen wrote to member-state energy ministers describing a price crisis linked to a supply crisis, and inviting measures for as long as necessary to keep gas flowing into storage or to cut gas and electricity demand. Gas Infrastructure Europe data put EU storage at about 70% full, roughly 12 percentage points below the same point last year. Foreign suppliers cover about 80% of EU gas needs, and the Strait of Hormuz normally carries about 20% of the world's oil and liquefied natural gas. Euronews said the Dutch TTF benchmark was trading around €72 per megawatt-hour, up about €40 since the February 28 U.S.-Israeli strikes on Iran, with analysts warning of spikes above €100 if Gulf LNG exports fail to grow. Jorgensen stopped short of mandates, pointing to voluntary 2022-style steps such as cutting peak power use, limiting temperatures in public buildings, restricting outdoor heating and switching off unnecessary public lighting, and suggested aiming for 80% storage rather than a late rush to 90% to avoid panic buying. The letter still says there is no immediate risk to security of supply.

**Links:**

- [Euronews — EU tells countries to curb energy demand as gas prices face winter squeeze](https://www.euronews.com/2026/09/27/eu-tells-countries-to-curb-energy-demand-as-gas-prices-face-winter-squeeze)
- [Reuters — EU warns of energy price crisis, asks countries to consider curbing demand](https://www.reuters.com/business/energy/eu-warns-energy-price-crisis-asks-countries-consider-curbing-demand-letter-shows-2026-09-25/)

**Commentary:** Brussels has recast the winter risk from whether gas runs out to whether demand will fall—the storage gap is being filled with voluntary restraint.

## II. Green Power & Storage

### 3. Ordos 314 MW wind-solar project for full on-site use targets year-end connection (Green power)
**Summary:** Etuoke Qianqi's official account, carried by Ifeng on September 27, said CHN Energy's Guodian Power Shanghaimiao company is building a 314 MW renewable project in Shanghaimiao town for full self-consumption, with investment of about 770 million yuan: 214 MW of solar and 100 MW of wind. Piling on the solar site is complete and racking is underway; wind construction is about to start. The solar field covers about 4,680 mu, uses bifacial glass modules, and includes a substation, export lines and storage, with power absorbed by the Guoneng Shanghaimiao plant. Full connection is planned by year-end. First-year output is put at 420 million kilowatt-hours, saving about 122,300 tonnes of standard coal and 332,800 tonnes of carbon dioxide a year, and cutting the captive plant's power bill by more than 32 million yuan annually.

### 4. NineDot cuts ribbon on a nearly 20 MW Bronx battery aimed at next summer's peaks (Storage)
**Summary:** Interesting Engineering reported on September 27 that NineDot Energy held a ribbon-cutting for a battery system in Hunts Point, the Bronx, rated at nearly 20 MW and able to cover about 20,000 homes at peak, with full operation expected before next summer's heat waves. More than 10,000 diesel trucks pass through the area each day, and two high-emitting peaker plants operate in nearby Port Morris. The company said asthma-related emergency visits in Hunts Point-Mott Haven in 2023 were two to three times the citywide average; the site previously housed a fuel-oil trucking firm. During the July 1-4, 2026 heat wave, nine operating NineDot projects exported nearly 34 MW at peak to Con Edison's local network and the statewide transmission system. NineDot now operates 11 projects at six locations in Staten Island and the Bronx totaling 39 MW, with a development pipeline above 1 GW, and says it is on track for 400 MW operating, under construction or in development by the end of 2026.

### 5. Nigeria commissions a 581 kW solar mini-grid with 1.4 MW of storage in Kwara (Green power)
**Summary:** The Sun reported on September 27 that Nigeria's federal government commissioned a 581 kWp solar mini-grid backed by a 1.4 MW battery in Oke-Oyi, Ilorin East, Kwara State, intended to serve about 1,800 households and businesses and to extend solar power past sunset. Power Minister Joseph Tegbe said the project was delivered under the Interconnected Mini-Grid Acceleration Scheme with support from the Nigerian Energy Support Programme, the European Union, Germany's GIZ and UNICEF, using public funds to draw private capital and commercial rules rather than lasting subsidy dependence. The Rural Electrification Agency described the model as linking distributed renewables to existing distribution networks. Ibadan Electricity Distribution Company and Nayo Tropical Technologies took part in delivery.

### 6. Ireland moves to open solar grants to newer homes and add a €600 battery grant (Green power)
**Summary:** The Irish Independent reported on September 27 that newer homes will be eligible for solar-panel grants from next month, and that the package includes a €600 grant for storage batteries, aimed at cutting household energy bills. The Irish Times reported on September 25 that the battery grant would sit on top of the Sustainable Energy Authority of Ireland grant of up to €1,800 for solar PV systems, as a core piece of Energy Minister Darragh O'Brien's household clean-energy proposals. More than 100,000 homes have installed solar with SEAI support, including about 33,000 in 2025; about half of new installations already include storage, and batteries typically cost more than €4,000.

Water Resources and Energy Minister Biraj Bhakta Shrestha said flooding on the Rahuganga River damaged structures and transmission towers of a 40 MW hydropower project. Floods in late August had already killed more than 1,400 people in Nepal.
