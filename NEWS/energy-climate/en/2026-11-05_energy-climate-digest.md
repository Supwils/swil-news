## I. Policy & International Climate Governance

### 1. Türkiye unveils 2040 energy policy: 175 GW wind and solar, over $330 billion for electrification (Policy)
**Summary:** On Nov 5, Energy Minister Alparslan Bayraktar presented Türkiye’s 2040 national energy policy: total installed power capacity is projected to rise from about 123 GW to 281 GW, with renewables’ share climbing from roughly 63% to 77%. Wind and solar are targeted at 175 GW, including 6 GW of offshore wind with a first tender planned for April next year; nuclear is targeted at 10 GW (5 GW beyond Akkuyu), covering about 11% of electricity demand, with decisions on Sinop and Thrace plants planned for 2027. Electrification would lift electricity’s share of final energy to about 36% and electricity use to about 680 TWh; gas and oil demand are projected below business-as-usual paths, with emissions down about 40%. Total energy investment is put at about $500 billion, including more than $330 billion for generation, grids and storage, with state firms providing about $210 billion, aiming to cut an energy import bill that has averaged $65–70 billion a year.

### 2. Allianz Green Transition Tracker 2026: renewables overtook coal in 2025; fossil CO₂ may fall outside a recession (Net zero)
**Summary:** Allianz Research on Nov 5 released the second Green Transition Tracker. In 2025 renewables supplied about 33.5% of global electricity, edging past coal at 33.2%, and provided about 99% of new generation, slowing emissions growth to about 0.7% from 1.5% in each of the prior two years. Annual battery-storage additions rose from about 2 GWh in 2015 to 307 GWh in 2025 as installed costs fell about 91%; clean-energy investment is set to rise about 6.5% to $2.2 trillion in 2026, with grids up about 21% and storage up about 36%. The Hormuz oil shock is likened to an implied carbon price of more than €200/t on diesel. Fossil CO₂ emissions could fall about 0.5% in 2026 even with solid growth, though AI and data centers could add about 0.6 percentage points to global emissions by 2030. On the current path, global net zero arrives only around 2064—about 14 years late—and only about one-third of 65 tracked economies are keeping pace with Paris-aligned pathways.

**Links:**

- [Allianz — Green Transition Tracker 2026: the clean-tech turning point](https://www.allianz.com/en/mediacenter/news/media-releases/261105-allianz-green-transition-tracker-2026.html)

**Commentary:** “Peak emissions” enters the research headline, but the authors themselves stress it would be a by-product of expensive oil and cheap technology—not a climate-policy victory.

## II. China–India Policy & Energy Security

### 5. India Cabinet clears ₹14,500 crore VGF for 92 GWh of grid-scale storage (Storage)
**Summary:** India’s Union Cabinet approved a Viability Gap Funding scheme for grid-scale energy storage, with ₹14,500 crore of support from FY 2026–27 to FY 2030–31 for 92 GWh of still-to-be-commissioned capacity: 60 GWh pumped hydro, 30 GWh batteries and 2 GWh emerging technologies. Estimated total investment is ₹97,600 crore—about 170% of today’s grid-scale storage stock—and would contribute toward a 411 GWh requirement by 2031–32. Capacity will be awarded through tariff-based competitive bidding; battery projects face a minimum 40% domestic-content requirement. New and Renewable Energy Minister Pralhad Joshi said on Nov 5 that future SECI solar and renewable tenders may mandate three-to-four hours of storage, moving away from standalone solar to ease evening peaks and daytime curtailment.

### 7. Five ministries issue Comprehensive Conservation Strategy Action Plan: 150 Mtce-plus savings from key industries by 2030 (Policy)
**Summary:** China’s NDRC, MIIT, Ministry of Natural Resources, Ministry of Water Resources and Government Offices Administration jointly issued the Comprehensive Conservation Strategy Action Plan, widely covered on Nov 5. By 2030, energy use per unit of value added in above-scale industry should fall more than 10% from 2025, with energy-saving and carbon-cutting retrofits in key industries delivering over 150 million tonnes of coal equivalent in savings; energy and water intensity of GDP are to fall about 10% and about 10%/15% respectively versus 2025 (per the plan’s wording), and construction land use area by 15%. Energy measures include sector-by-sector retrofits in steel, nonferrous metals and petrochemicals; low-carbon upgrades of coal power and lower grid losses; new-energy heavy trucks and green marine fuels; efficiency upgrades for compute and telecom facilities; and building retrofits. The plan also seeks to curb unreasonable growth in total energy use, require equal or reduced carbon displacement for high-emitting projects, and apply coal equal/reduced substitution in key air-pollution control regions.

### 8. CREA: China’s clean power and EVs buffered the Hormuz shock; renewables and nuclear saved about $9.1 billion in fossil imports (Energy security)
**Summary:** The Centre for Research on Energy and Clean Air said on Nov 5 that after the Hormuz crisis China’s crude imports fell sharply, but stock draws, clean power and transport electrification limited the shock. Growth in renewables and nuclear since 2020 is estimated to have avoided about $9.1 billion in fossil-fuel import costs—the largest such saving of any country. From March to August 2026, EVs displaced about 38 million tonnes of oil equivalent, roughly 13% of China’s crude imports in the same period a year earlier. Hydro, nuclear, wind and solar generation rose about 139 TWh over the same window, far exceeding the gas-power shortfall, while coal generation stayed broadly flat. Sustained clean-power growth could lift the non-fossil share of generation to about 54% by 2030, above the ~50% 15th Five-Year energy-plan target; China has already set a domestic goal of 35% electrification of final energy by 2030—five years ahead of Türkiye’s proposed global pledge.

**Summary:** Sichuan’s Development and Reform Commission issued a Three-Year Action Plan for New Energy Storage Project Construction (2026–2028), widely reported on Nov 5. ## IV. Climate Risk, Adaptation & Carbon Prices

### 12. UNEP Adaptation Gap Report: developing countries need about $305 billion a year by 2035; WHO says six climate-health actions could save 1.6 million lives annually (Adaptation)
**Summary:** UNEP’s 2026 Adaptation Gap Report continued to circulate on Nov 5: developing countries will need roughly $305 billion a year by 2035 for climate resilience, while international public adaptation finance reached only about $28.2–31.7 billion in 2024—about $1 billion more than the prior year, with growth flattening. Even if that public finance triples under the COP30 Global Mutirão goal, inflation-adjusted coverage would reach only about 20–29% of estimated needs. WHO analysis released around the same window finds that scaling six proven measures—heat-health warnings, climate-resilient water and sanitation, hygiene, cleaner household energy, and efficient fossil-fuel pricing—could prevent about 1.6 million deaths a year and yield about $3.9 trillion in economic welfare benefits; fossil-fuel pricing alone accounts for about 1.2 million of those avoided deaths and about $5.4 trillion in fiscal benefits. The findings land days before COP31 opens in Türkiye.

**Links:**
