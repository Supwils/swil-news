### 1. Six dead in Mexico as Simon and Rachel's remnants flood the coast; the hurricane still at 150 km/h (Hurricane)

### 2. Eastern Pacific accumulated cyclone energy passes the 2018 record (Hurricane)
**Summary:** Associated Press science writer Seth Borenstein reported Tuesday that accumulated cyclone energy east of the international date line passed the 2018 record on Sunday, a measure that combines wind speed and longevity. Colorado State University's Phil Klotzbach said that on Tuesday the basin would tie the record for days with a hurricane spinning and was likely to break it soon after. The last five Pacific hurricanes — Nolo, Odalys, Polo, Rachel, and Simon — all reached major-hurricane status, and Polo spent two and a half days at Category 5, a record at that strength. Atlantic accumulated cyclone energy was under 18 percent of normal for the date. The National Hurricane Center gave the next disturbance, Trudy, a 90 percent chance of becoming a tropical storm.

**Links:**

- [PBS News — Pacific hurricane region sets a record for big, long-lasting storms](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/science/pacific-hurricane-region-sets-record-for-big-long-lasting-storms)

**Commentary:** The record is storms that last and intensify, not the peak wind of a single landfall.

### 3. Super El Niño: central Pacific weekly sea temperatures reach a recorded high (Climate)
**Summary:** BBC Weather reported Tuesday that Australia's Bureau of Meteorology now shows average weekly sea-surface temperatures in the central Pacific at their highest level on record, with readings above 30C for the first time in parts of the tropical Pacific. A September daily record in the central-eastern Pacific already exceeded the very strong El Niño of 2015. Zeke Hausfather of Berkeley Earth called the event unprecedented in the historical record. The European Centre for Medium-Range Weather Forecasts and the U.S. National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration expect a December peak. Guangming Daily the same day carried a Xinhua report that China's National Climate Center found a super event had formed in September, and that the World Meteorological Organization said on October 8 that October–December sea temperatures would reach record levels, peak in December, and had a near-100 percent chance of lasting through February 2027.

**Links:**

- [Guangming Daily — El Niño keeps strengthening and the world faces multiple risks](https://news.gmw.cn/2026-10/13/content_39042108.htm)

## II. Policy and the Power Grid

### 4. U.S. EPA proposes to restore all 770 million extra 2025 renewable-fuel credits (Biofuels)
**Summary:** EPA Administrator Lee Zeldin, speaking Tuesday in Peosta, Iowa, proposed revisions to Renewable Fuel Standard "Set 2" volumes. Small-refinery exemption decisions issued on August 31, 2026, exempted 770 million more renewable identification numbers for 2025 obligations than the agency had projected. Both options would reallocate 100 percent of that gap: one splits it equally between the 2026 and 2027 compliance years, and the other places all of it in 2027. Comments run until December 4, 2026. A revision of the 2026 standards may also extend the compliance-reporting deadline now set for March 31, 2027.

### 5. DOE emergency order for PJM takes effect today, with demand forecast near 111,500 MW (Grid)
**Summary:** On October 12 the U.S. Department of Energy issued emergency Order No. 202-26-50 under section 202(c) of the Federal Power Act, effective October 13 through October 16. PJM's October 11 application forecast demand at or around 111,500 MW or higher on October 13, within about 900 MW of its reserve margin on October 14, and generation outages of 61,591 MW, with the tightest conditions in the BGE, PEPCO, and Dominion zones. The order directs PJM to dispatch specified units as needed and authorizes it to call backup generation at large loads, including data centers, as a last resort before or during an Energy Emergency Alert 3. Those units may run beyond environmental operating limits.

### 7. Bulgaria's energy committee adopts renewable-energy amendments at second reading (Renewables)
**Summary:** The Bulgarian News Agency reported Tuesday that the parliamentary Energy Committee, meeting in Sofia, adopted amendments to the Renewable Energy Sources Act at second reading to finish transposing Directive (EU) 2023/2413, whose deadline was May 21, 2025. The changes extend the validity of wind-connection opinions, encourage decentralized generation and storage, and cover heating and cooling so the power system can be balanced. They also create a mechanism to issue and trade renewable-electricity credits in transport. The Energy Ministry said the amendments leave the procedures in the Environment Protection Act and the Biological Diversity Act unchanged, and that wind priority zones still exclude protected areas, bird migration routes, forests, and permanent grassland.

**Links:**

- [Bulgarian News Agency — Energy committee adopts amendments to ease renewable electricity purchases](https://www.bta.bg/en/news/economy/1223619-energy-committee-adopts-amendments-to-facilitate-electricity-purchases-from-rene)

## III. Renewables, Storage, and Nuclear

### 8. California says the September 9 peak hit 50,335 MW without emergency measures (Grid)
**Summary:** Governor Gavin Newsom said Tuesday that California's high-voltage grid had handled its highest electricity use in four years without emergency measures or a call for extra conservation. The peak came on September 9, 2026, at 50,335 megawatts, about 3 percent below the all-time record of 52,061 megawatts set in the September 2022 heat event. The state says it has added more than 39,000 megawatts of utility-scale clean energy since 2019, and that batteries serving California grew from fewer than 700 megawatts to more than 23,000. As of the end of 2024, clean energy supplied 67 percent of retail electricity sales, and last year clean energy met all demand for at least part of the day on 279 days.

### 9. HELLENiQ brings a 100 MW / 200 MWh battery online in Thessaloniki (Storage)
**Summary:** The Cyprus Mail reported Tuesday that HELLENiQ Energy has started its first large battery at the group's industrial site in Thessaloniki after an investment of about €50 million. The plant is rated at 100 megawatts and 200 megawatt-hours, among the largest power-storage projects in Greece and southeastern Europe. It was selected in the first storage tender by Greece's Regulatory Authority for Waste, Energy and Water and funded through the Greece 2.0 recovery plan and NextGenerationEU. The site covers about 100,000 square meters, uses lithium iron phosphate with liquid cooling, and required a new 33/150-kilovolt substation plus about five kilometers of underground high-voltage line. The company said the plant will complete at least one full cycle a day. A second project in Florina, 50 MW / 200 MWh, is nearing completion. More than 700 MW of renewables are already operating, and the development portfolio exceeds 6 GW.

### 10. Google subscribes to Georgia nuclear uprates, expected to add about 96 MW (Nuclear)

## IV. Oil, Gas, and Markets

### 11. Burnham has still not decided Rosebank and Jackdaw; climate protesters gathered in London (Oil and gas)
**Summary:** The Associated Press reported Tuesday that Prime Minister Andy Burnham has not decided whether to approve the Rosebank oil field and the Jackdaw gas field north of Scotland. Public consultations closed in August, and a court in Edinburgh ruled last year that the earlier approvals were unlawful. Rosebank, about 130 kilometers north of Shetland, is reported to hold 300 million to 500 million barrels of oil equivalent over about 25 years. Shell expects Jackdaw to supply about 6.5 percent of U.K. gas at peak, and the Conservatives said approval could heat 1.4 million homes this winter. Climate protesters gathered Tuesday outside the Department for Energy Security and Net Zero. The Press and Journal reported an Adura-commissioned analysis finding that blocking Rosebank and other planned North Sea developments would mean £10.8 billion less economic value over the next decade, while approving Jackdaw and Rosebank alone would mean about £4.1 billion in tax revenue over ten years.
