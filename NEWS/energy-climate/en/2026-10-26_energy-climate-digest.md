## I. Policy and Markets

### 1. China's wind and solar fleet reaches 2.01 billion kilowatts by the end of September (capacity)
**Summary:** Xinhua reported on October 26 that the National Energy Administration announced the same day that combined wind and solar capacity reached 2.01 billion kilowatts by the end of September 2026, with solar at 1.31 billion kilowatts, up 16.5%, and wind at 700 million kilowatts, up 20.4%. From January through September, wind and solar generated 1,933.3 billion kilowatt-hours, 193.7 billion more than a year earlier, and that increase accounted for 64% of the growth in electricity use. On the summer peak-load day, maximum midday wind and solar output exceeded 40% of load at the time. Over the same nine months, wind and solar investment was about one-third of completed energy investment. Liu Zhiqiang, deputy director of planning and development at the China Electricity Council, said the milestone consolidates renewables as the mainstay of installed capacity. The renewable-energy plan for the 15th Five-Year Plan period calls for more than 300 million kilowatts of additional reliable peak renewable capacity.

### 2. Environment ministry report: coal-fired generation falls to 49.7% in the first half (climate policy)
**Summary:** Caixin reported on October 26 that the Ministry of Ecology and Environment released its annual climate-policy report ahead of the U.N. climate summit. Coal-fired plants supplied 49.7% of electricity generation in the first half of 2026, the first time a half-year share has fallen below 50%, and by the end of July installed solar capacity had surpassed coal, making photovoltaics the largest source of installed capacity. The report says carbon dioxide per unit of GDP in 2025 was 17.7% below 2020, non-fossil energy was 21.7% of energy consumption, combined wind and solar capacity was 1.84 billion kilowatts, and new-energy vehicles were 50.8% of new vehicle sales. Renewable capacity in the first half of 2026 was 2.455 billion kilowatts, about 60.7% of national installed capacity. The national carbon market covers about 8.3 billion metric tons of carbon dioxide, more than 65% of national emissions, with cumulative allowance trading above 1 billion metric tons, and authorities plan to add petrochemicals, chemicals, and other industries by 2027. Vice Minister Li Gao said climate change has become an immediate crisis, and the ministry is guiding 39 cities in expanded climate-adaptive city pilots. COP31 is scheduled for November 9–20 in Antalya, Turkey.

### 3. The southern regional power market moved to formal operation on October 24 (power market)
**Summary:** Science and Technology Daily reported on October 26 that China's first regional power market, covering Guangdong, Guangxi, Yunnan, Guizhou, and Hainan, moved to formal operation on October 24. Since continuous settlement trials began in June 2025, coal, hydro, nuclear, wind, solar, and gas have bid under one set of rules. From January through September, traded electricity was nearly 900 billion kilowatt-hours, more than 70% of regional sales; renewables supplied 40% of the increase in electricity sold; the largest single-day clearing exceeded 4 billion kilowatt-hours; and registered market entities passed 290,000. Xinhua the same day reported Hainan's side of the market: renewables are above 50% of installed capacity, one year of settlement trials covered 30.978 billion kilowatt-hours, 55% of provincial consumption, with 1.301 billion kilowatt-hours bought from other provinces and 357 million exported, and more than 1.6 billion kilowatt-hours moved on the cross-sea cables, sending out surplus midday solar and importing power at night.

### 4. The EU launches the T-MED platform with up to €4.5 billion in guarantees (cross-border investment)
**Summary:** The European Commission on October 26 launched the T-MED Investment Platform under the Trans-Mediterranean Renewable Energy and Clean Tech Cooperation Initiative. Commissioner for the Mediterranean Dubravka Suica presented it at a stakeholder event in Brussels. EU support includes up to €4.5 billion in guarantee capacity under the European Fund for Sustainable Development Plus, to reduce risk in renewable generation, renewable hydrogen, transmission and distribution, storage, cross-border interconnectors, and clean-tech manufacturing. The Commission said that by 2035 the initiative aims to mobilise up to €25 billion and train up to 10,000 workers. Projects already cited include modernisation and expansion of Egypt's national grid, with a total investment of €1.6 billion; the first green-ammonia project in North Africa at Ain Sokhna, a €575 million operation; a hybrid solar project in Egypt worth about €430 million; the Niat and Rasgha wind projects, 500 MW combined on the Gulf of Suez; and two 100 MW solar plants plus transmission in Tunisia, with EU support of about €92 million and €80 million.

### 5. The EU opens consultation on a new hydrogen strategy, with comments due November 23 (hydrogen)
**Summary:** The Commission's Directorate-General for Energy announced on October 26 a four-week call for evidence and a targeted stakeholder consultation on the forthcoming hydrogen strategy. Comments are due by 23:59 CET on November 23, 2026. The strategy is meant to support a resilient and competitive European hydrogen value chain and give investors stability and predictability. The Commission said it will be developed in parallel with the renewable-energy framework through 2040, so that renewables serve the EU's 2040 climate target while responding to geopolitical pressure to cut dependence on imported fossil fuels. The consultation page says that, since the 2020 hydrogen strategy, a regulatory framework is in place, while production and use of renewable and low-carbon hydrogen are still slower than first expected, with limited operating capacity and projects that have not reached sufficient maturity.

### 6. Maryland opens a $200 million Megawatts for Maryland clean-energy auction (power prices)
**Summary:** Governor Wes Moore announced on October 26 the launch of Megawatts for Maryland, administered by the Maryland Energy Administration under the Utility RELIEF Act he signed earlier this year. The two-year program draws up to $100 million a year, $200 million in total, from the Strategic Energy Investment Fund and awards competitive grants to renewable generation and storage projects that have already completed some early development. The aim is several hundred megawatts of in-state supply, enough for more than 150,000 homes a year. Stage-one applications are due November 30, 2026, and eligible bidders must submit prices by January 22, 2027. Maryland Matters reported that energy-administration director Kelly Speakes-Backman said funds will go to projects delivering the most megawatt-hours per dollar, that projects must come online within seven years of the grant, and that the money will not fully replace federal investment tax credits that were canceled. The governor said regional and national power costs are rising and the program is meant to add cleaner, cheaper in-state generation.

### 8. SEIA publishes a distributed capacity procurement framework (distributed energy)
**Summary:** The Solar Energy Industries Association and Current Energy Group on October 26 released the Distributed Capacity Procurement Framework, on how regulators and grid operators can procure distributed solar and storage as demand rises, prices stay high, and grid equipment ages. The paper sets out three pathways, from least to most prescriptive: a programmatic tariff for system-wide capacity needs, a standing tariff whose price is calibrated by competitive bids, and a competitive solicitation only for a specific site. Two of its ten principles are to use the least restrictive pathway that meets the need, and to make customer-owned or third-party-owned resources the default, with utility ownership only in limited cases. SEIA said competitive procurement of third-party resources can reduce utilities' purchases of higher-cost wholesale capacity. The paper keeps utility-owned distributed resources out of the first two pathways and allows them only as bounded exceptions in the third, such as storage co-located with a substation and used strictly for real-time grid control.

**Commentary:** The framework makes customer and third-party ownership the default and leaves utility-built assets as an exception inside site-specific bids.

### 9. Eleven states oppose MISO's 120-day fast study for large loads and paired generation (large loads)
**Summary:** Utility Dive reported on October 26 that clean-energy groups, ratepayer advocates, and 11 of the 15 states in the Midcontinent Independent System Operator's footprint urged the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission on Friday to reject the operator's Large Load Addition Resource Study. Earlier this month MISO proposed a 120-day joint study for loads larger than 200 MW and generation in the same local resource zone; there are 10 such zones. The Organization of MISO States said the plan lacks protection against shifting transmission costs onto existing customers and could affect competition in the generator interconnection queue, and it said any similar program should end after five years. Commissions in Arkansas, Louisiana, and Mississippi supported the plan. Google said in a separate filing that the cost-allocation rules will not be filed until next month, so existing customers could pay for network upgrades needed by new loads and generation, conflicting with its Ratepayer Protection Pledge. The American Clean Power Association and SEIA said only load-serving entities that are transmission customers may apply, so an independent producer needs one of them to sponsor the request. Duke Energy Indiana, Entergy, and other utilities supported the filing.

**Summary:** The Guardian reported on October 26, citing MetDesk, that Typhoon Champi recorded sustained winds of 185 mph last week and was declared the strongest storm of the year so far. From late Thursday to early Saturday it struck Taiwan and Japan's southern islands, with gusts of 141 mph and 48-hour rainfall above 620 mm in parts of Yilan. The toll stands at one death and 527 injuries. More than 288,000 homes lost power at one point and most have been reconnected. Cyclonic Storm Arnab in the Bay of Bengal is expected around Wednesday near the north Andhra Pradesh and south Odisha coasts, with gusts up to 85 mph and six-hour rainfall up to 100 mm at landfall.
