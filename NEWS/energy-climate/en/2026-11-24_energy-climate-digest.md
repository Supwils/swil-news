## I. Climate Diplomacy & International Cooperation

### 1. EU drafts overhaul of UN climate summits after COP31 collapse over fossil-fuel text (Policy)
**Summary:** Euronews reported on Nov 24 that the European Commission is preparing plans to reform the UN climate summit process, warning that consensus rules let a single country among nearly 200 parties block agreement. The push follows COP31 in Antalya, which failed to adopt a political declaration reaffirming the Dubai commitment to transition away from fossil fuels. Brussels said a Saudi-led group sought to replace that language with a greater emphasis on carbon capture, including a gigaton-scale CCS target by 2035 that the EU called “unacceptable.” The Commission estimated 80–90 countries objected to the final draft, including all 27 EU members, the UK, Norway, Switzerland and more than 40 least-developed countries. Officials also cited progress on carbon markets and about $103 billion in development-bank climate finance, and stressed coalitions of the willing outside formal consensus.

### 2. Analysts: COP31 deadlock unlikely to derail clean-energy shift; IEA sees ~$2.2T clean investment in 2026 (Transition)
**Summary:** According to CNA on Nov 24, experts at the Australian National University and E3G said COP31 exposed deep rifts over fossil fuels and climate finance but is unlikely to reverse the clean-energy transition. The IEA estimates global clean-energy investment at about $2.2 trillion in 2026, nearly double the roughly $1.2 trillion projected for fossil fuels; falling renewable and storage costs, electrification and energy-security concerns are the main drivers. The UN climate body said countries representing about half the world economy support or plan to meet earlier a goal of roughly 35% electrification by 2035. Analysts also noted that supply shocks around the Strait of Hormuz reinforce the case for renewables and storage as a hedge against price volatility.

## II. Policy & Regulation

### 3. Spain approves emergency decrees freeing grid capacity for wind repowering and industrial self-consumption, and regulating energy communities (Policy)
**Summary:** Spain’s Council of Ministers on Nov 24 approved a royal decree amending energy rules to free and auction grid-access capacity for wind farms older than 15 years seeking up to 25% additional capacity, and to release reserved nodes for manufacturing self-consumption, hydrogen and renewable-gas projects. The package also lets pumped-hydro plants upgrade power electronics and concentrating-solar plants expand solar fields and add thermal storage. A second royal decree sets legal requirements for renewable energy communities and citizen energy communities, including a 40% per-member voting cap and a two-year release of 10% of reserved contest nodes for community generation, implementing EU directives and recent emergency legislation.

### 4. India draft: larger rooftop solar may face mandatory storage and net-metering charges; ToD tariffs expand from 2028 (Storage)
**Summary:** Business Standard reported on Nov 24 that India’s power ministry proposes amending the Electricity (Rights of Consumers) Rules so state regulators can set net-metering limits and charges; where no limit is set, the current cap remains 500 kW, with charges beyond the threshold reflecting implied storage and network-loss costs. The draft would also empower SERCs to mandate energy storage equal to at least 20% of daily generation above a capacity threshold—defaulting to systems above 10 kW if unspecified—effective April 1, 2028. Time-of-day tariffs would apply to all consumers above 10 kW from that date, and to other non-agricultural consumers from April 1, 2029. Officials cited weak fixed-cost recovery for discoms and the “free battery” effect of exporting midday power and drawing evening peak power.

### 5. Ireland publishes National Energy Affordability Report and Action Plan: 116 measures betting on household solar-storage and grid investment (Policy)
**Summary:** On Nov 24, Ireland’s Minister for Climate, Energy and the Environment Darragh O’Brien published the cross-government National Energy Affordability Report and Action Plan, with 116 actions spanning energy poverty, business competitiveness, flexibility and active participation, electricity-market outcomes, and balancing grid investment with consumer costs. Key measures include fully funded solar PV and batteries for low-income households, a boiler scrappage scheme, stronger business solar supports, and wider participation in smart tariffs and demand response; a companion Plug-in Solar Working Group options report explores safe standardised deployment. ### 6. More than 120 climate scientists urge U.S. lawmakers to reject BAAJA permitting bill over fossil lock-in and weakened reviews (Policy)
**Summary:** The Guardian reported on Nov 24 that more than 120 climate and social scientists wrote House members urging a no vote on the Bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act (Baaja), which the Senate passed rapidly last week. The letter warned the bill weakens environmental laws, locks in fossil projects with little recourse and delivers only limited emissions cuts; scientist Michael Mann called it deeply problematic and backed by fossil and dark-money interests. Supporters, including Sen. Sheldon Whitehouse, cite gigaton-scale cuts, freed clean power, about $10 billion a year in consumer electricity savings and a roughly $35 billion “whack” at data centers. The bill would slash barriers to transmission and infrastructure while shrinking Clean Water Act and Endangered Species Act scrutiny; a House vote is expected next week, with signature by Donald Trump if it passes.

**Summary:** Berliner Zeitung and related reporting citing Argus/FT and CRU said European fertiliser producers have shut at least about 5 million tonnes a year of ammonia capacity—roughly 28% of regional capacity, with some estimates of disruption near one-third—as natural gas accounts for about 70% of production costs. ### 9. U.S. data centers rush behind-the-meter gas plants: >115 GW announced; BNEF flags emissions lock-in (Fossil lock-in)
**Summary:** MIT Technology Review reported on Nov 24 that Cleanview data show developers have announced more than 100 behind-the-meter or off-grid gas plants for U.S. data centers as of October 2026, totaling over 115 GW of planned capacity—more than double California’s peak demand—with most announcements since early 2025. Only about 4.9 GW are operating and 15.8 GW under construction. BloombergNEF tracks about 181 GW of related announcements globally, roughly 90% in the U.S.; if all U.S. plans proceed, related emissions could reach about 345 million tonnes of CO2e a year by 2035—about 152 million tonnes more than grid power. Most planned behind-the-meter plants favour less-efficient simple-cycle turbines, and higher gas demand could lift costs for the roughly 60% of U.S. households that heat with gas.

## IV. Clean Power, Storage & China Markets

### 10. First units online at Tibet Qiongjie wind base Phase I: 150 MW at average elevation above 5,100 m (Clean power)
**Summary:** Xinhua reported on Nov 24 that the first turbines of the 150 MW Phase I project at the Qiongjie County wind base in Shannan, Tibet, connected to the grid the same day. It is Tibet’s only wind project selected for the National Energy Administration’s new-type power-system capability pilot; total planned capacity is 500 MW. Phase I deploys 24 turbines of 6.25 MW each with grid-forming storage and a 220 kV substation expansion, invested by China Huadian; full operation is expected to generate enough electricity for about 175,000 households a year. Sites sit on ridges at roughly 4,800–5,350 m with average wind speeds above 8 m/s, aiming to fill winter hydropower shortages and night-time solar gaps.
