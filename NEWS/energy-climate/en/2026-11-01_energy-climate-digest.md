## I. Global Climate Governance & Policy

### 1. UNEP Emissions Gap Report: the United States is a major reason global pathways worsened (Emissions gap)
**Summary:** UNEP’s Emissions Gap Report 2026 finds that continuing current policies could put the world on track for about 2.8°C of warming this century, while full delivery of Nationally Determined Contributions (NDCs) would still leave about 2.6°C—both worse than last year’s outlook, reflecting in part the U.S. withdrawal from the Paris Agreement and revised historical emissions. Preliminary estimates show U.S. greenhouse-gas emissions rose about 133 MtCO2e in 2025, the largest increase among major economies, driven by higher electricity demand and a coal-power rebound. To follow a “lowest possible” 1.5°C overshoot-and-return path, global emissions would need to fall about 18% by 2030 and 45% by 2035 relative to 2025. AP and other outlets carried the findings into November 1 coverage as a core COP31 backdrop.

### 2. EU proposes law requiring countries to plan for roughly 3°C of global warming (Adaptation)
**Summary:** The European Commission has proposed a European Climate Resilience Law requiring national climate-risk assessments by end-June 2029 and national resilience plans by end-2030, using a shared reference trajectory of about 1.5°C by 2030, 2°C by 2050 and 3°C by 2100. Plans must cover health, water, energy, transport, food and public finances, name accountable authorities and set out funding; the Commission estimates adaptation needs near €70 billion a year but the proposal creates no matching dedicated fund. Climate Commissioner Wopke Hoekstra said that even doing mitigation “right today” is “no longer nearly good enough” to keep citizens out of harm’s way.

**Links:**

### 3. Egypt approves updated NDC 3.0 and advances Article 6 project authorizations (NDC)
**Summary:** On November 1, Prime Minister Mostafa Madbouly chaired Egypt’s National Council for Climate Change Supreme Committee, which approved launching updated Nationally Determined Contributions (NDC 3.0) for upload to the UNFCCC. Versus the prior plan, mitigation sectors rise to eight (adding water resources and irrigation) with 37 mitigation policies and measures; adaptation sectors rise to five (adding health) with 29 measures, and 2035 reduction targets for electricity, oil and gas, and transport are updated. The committee also approved an authorization letter for international use of emission-reduction outcomes and letters of intent for two nitrous-oxide reduction projects at nitric-acid plants under Paris Agreement Article 6.

**Links:**

- [SIS Egypt — PM chairs National Council for Climate Change Supreme Committee](https://sis.gov.eg/en/media-center/news/pm-chairs-meeting-of-national-council-for-climate-change-s-supreme-committee/)

**Commentary:** A key North African emitter is refreshing its negotiable text before COP31—and Article 6 letters are the gate to carbon-credit monetization.

### 4. Trump EPA advances methane-rule reconsideration: super-emitter program and associated-gas controls in play (United States)
**Summary:** EPA has published in the Federal Register a reconsideration proposal for the 2024 oil-and-gas methane NSPS and emission guidelines, with comments due December 16, 2026. Core elements include rescinding the Super Emitter Program, revising marginal- and conventional-well site categories and monitoring burdens, and replacing the phase-out of routine associated-gas flaring with routing through a closed vent system to a control device or flare achieving at least 95% reduction. The administration projects roughly $45 billion in savings and additional oil and gas output; critics note that low-volume wells contribute a disproportionate share of oilfield methane emissions.

**Links:**

- [Federal Register — Oil and gas methane standards reconsideration](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/29/2026-22082/additional-reconsideration-standards-of-performance-for-new-reconstructed-and-modified-sources-and)

**Commentary:** Swapping third-party leak triggers for operator self-attestation reopens the near-zero-leak bargain through administrative procedure.

## II. China Supply Security & Power Markets

### 5. NDRC issues rules bringing coal, power and gas supply LTCs under one framework (Supply security)
**Summary:** China Business Journal reported on November 1 that the National Development and Reform Commission issued Trial Measures for Managing Medium- and Long-Term Energy Supply Contracts—six chapters and 45 articles that for the first time place thermal-coal, electricity and natural-gas supply LTCs in a unified institutional frame, implementing the 15th Five-Year Plan call to strengthen such contracts. The rules assign enterprise primary responsibility with government oversight and graded accountability, keep port coal on a “benchmark-plus-float” price with monthly origin adjustments, and ban reselling or diverting contracted coal. Analysts say annual circulars are being upgraded into an enforceable institutional tool.

**Links:**

- [Sina Finance / China Business Journal — Energy supply LTCs brought under unified management](https://cj.sina.com.cn/articles/view/1650111241/625ab30902001hywi?finpagefr=p_104)

**Commentary:** Writing supply contracts into a justiciable checklist shows Beijing fears price spikes and hollow delivery more than missing capacity slogans.

### 6. Shandong consults on generation-side reliable-capacity payments: wind and solar eligible; double storage multipliers scrapped (Capacity mechanism)
**Summary:** Shandong’s development and reform commission is consulting through November 15 on detailed rules for generation-side reliable-capacity compensation, implementing national guidance on capacity pricing. Reliable capacity is defined as power that can sustainably serve annual system peaks; coverage includes market-participating coal, gas and qualifying grid-side independent new-type storage, while government-priced units are excluded. Wind and solar reliable capacity would be set from the prior year’s peak contribution and true-up after the operating year. Once in force, prior coal capacity prices, market capacity top-ups and double compensation for demonstration and long-duration storage pilots would be repealed.

**Links:**

- [Sina Finance — Shandong capacity-compensation draft: wind and solar can earn capacity fees](https://finance.sina.com.cn/stock/stockzmt/2026-11-01/doc-inixetzw4270073.shtml)

**Commentary:** Paying for peak-reliable megawatts without technology favoritism raises the value of dispatchable renewables—and ends storage’s double-subsidy diet.

### 7. NEA: Q1–Q3 supply balanced; solar 1.31 TW, new-type storage 167 GW (Data)
**Summary:** At an October 30 briefing, China’s National Energy Administration said energy supply met demand through the first three quarters. Solar capacity historically surpassed coal in July and reached 1.31 TW by end-September; coal’s share of generation fell to 49.1%, while clean energy supplied more than 80% of incremental generation. New-type storage stood at 167 GW / 435 GWh (+63% YoY) with average duration 2.61 hours; renewables totaled 2.518 TW (~60.94% of national capacity) and wind-plus-solar 2.01 TW. Investment in grids, storage and charging grew 15.3% combined; hydrogen investment roughly doubled. Domestic media continued amplifying the figures on November 1.

**Links:**

## III. Clean Power & Storage Projects

### 8. Italy’s budget eyes about €1.2 billion to accelerate sub-1 MW solar incentives (Policy / solar)
**Summary:** Il Sole 24 Ore reports that Italy’s government plans to use EU safeguard-clause room in the budget to grant €1.2 billion in capital support for small photovoltaic plants up to 1 MW that qualify for direct FerX incentives without auctions, capped at 40% of eligible costs for roughly 3 GW of quota. Disbursements are sketched as €800 million in 2028 and €200 million in each of 2029 and 2030, with €400 million recovered via electricity-bill system charges including the ASOA component. Implementing criteria are to be set by environment and energy authorities with the finance ministry within 120 days of enactment.

### 10. Xinjiang Fukang 100 MW molten-salt tower CSP comes online with 900 MW PV hybrid (CSP)

The CSP block is expected to generate about 120 GWh a year, saving roughly 40,000 tonnes of standard coal and cutting about 140,000 tonnes of CO2. State Grid Xinjiang said the hybrid supports diversified renewables and a multi-energy complementary supply system.

### 12. Southwest U.S. atmospheric river keeps flooding risk elevated; local evacuations continue (Extreme rainfall)
**Summary:** On November 1 the Weather Prediction Center kept a Moderate Risk of excessive rainfall across parts of the Four Corners from Arizona into southern Utah, citing an anomalously strong atmospheric river with precipitable water several times seasonal normals atop soils already saturated by last week’s storms; widespread 3–5 inches are possible on the Mogollon Rim and San Juan Mountains. Weather.com reported mandatory evacuations near Sedona, Arizona, and in Archuleta County, Colorado, with the San Juan River threatening record crests. The pattern fits a stronger El Niño–favored wet West Coast regime.

### 14. Bolivia declares nationwide emergency over El Niño impacts (Climate emergency)
**Summary:** Al Jazeera reported on November 1 that Bolivia declared a nationwide state of emergency to free resources for dealing with El Niño–linked extreme weather. El Niño alters global winds and rainfall via Pacific warming; intensity varies by place, and reporting noted this cycle could be among the strongest on record, peaking late in 2026 with effects possibly lasting into 2027. The decree mainly unlocks budget and administrative capacity; detailed loss figures and affected zones await further official updates.

**Links:**

- [Al Jazeera — Bolivia declares state of emergency over El Niño](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/11/1/bolivia-declares-state-of-emergency-over-el-nino-weather-pattern)

**Commentary:** A national emergency is adaptation finance’s fast-open valve—and a reminder Andean states remain highly exposed to ENSO phase shifts.
