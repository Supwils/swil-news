## I. Climate Negotiations & International Cooperation

### 1. COP31 overtime deadlock: ~130 countries press Saudi Arabia as fossil-fuel language becomes a “red line” (Policy)

**Summary:** After missing Friday’s scheduled close, the 31st UN climate conference remained deadlocked late Saturday in Antalya. A loose coalition of about 130 countries insists the final outcome must reference a “transition away from fossil fuels,” while developing countries also demand stronger rights to adaptation finance; Saudi Arabia and allies oppose that language and want strong carbon-capture-and-storage (CCS) recommendations in the cover decision. Thursday’s revised draft had already stripped fossil-fuel wording and a roughly USD 120 billion adaptation-finance target. On Saturday evening the EU, UK, least-developed countries, small-island states and several African nations jointly pressed the presidency to restore the language. Tuvalu’s climate minister called fossil-fuel wording a “red line”; if talks drag into Sunday morning, departures could threaten quorum.

### 2. China backs low-carbon electrification at COP31, echoing “35 by 35” and offering to help others (Policy)

**Summary:** Wang Yi, a Chinese Academy of Sciences professor and senior adviser to China’s delegation, said Saturday that end-use electrification is a clear direction while electricity supply itself must also be cleaned. The presidency’s “35 by 35” goal—raising electricity to 35% of final energy consumption by 2035—remains central; China and Australia signed a joint statement on Friday welcoming voluntary cooperation toward that target. Beijing stresses “clean” electrification because the draft does not require new power to come from renewables, raising concerns that coal- or gas-fired generation could count. As the world’s largest greenhouse-gas emitter, China’s stance weighs heavily on whether electrification lands in the final text.

### 3. MEE: greenhouse gases enter EIA; 49 new thermal projects unlock ~1.7 Mt CO₂ cuts a year (Policy)

**Summary:** At the COP31 China Pavilion, Wang Zhihua, director-general of the Ministry of Ecology and Environment’s EIA and emissions management department, said China has systematically advanced greenhouse-gas environmental impact assessment since 2021, covering CO₂ and methane and linking with the emissions-trading system on accounting, data and evaluation. Figures show 49 new thermal power projects, after option screening, unlocked about 1.7 million tonnes of annual CO₂ reduction potential; a coal-chemical project using process optimisation and carbon capture and utilisation can cut about 1.6 Mt a year. Next steps include refining sectoral technical standards and indicators, widening coverage, and tighter links with pollution permits.

## II. Energy Security & Markets

### 4. Rare early polar-vortex breakdown plus super El Niño lifts Europe gas-price risk (Markets)

**Summary:** CNBC reported on Nov 21 that an unprecedented early breakdown of the stratospheric polar vortex, colliding with a powerful El Niño, has traders fretting about colder spells in Europe and the eastern United States. Europe entered winter with the lowest gas storage in more than a decade and has seen “very, very strong” withdrawals over the past two weeks; Dutch TTF December futures were about €87.28/MWh on Friday after touching €103.9 in late October. Analysts warn a colder winter would accelerate drawdowns and deepen reliance on imported LNG while Middle East supplies remain disrupted. The IEA has also flagged that low European storage and limited storage in several Asian and South American LNG importers could intensify cargo competition.

### 5. China enters winter peak-supply push: ~1.3 TW grid capability; gas peak capacity 1.2 bcm/day (Security of supply)

**Summary:** Xinhua reported that successive cold air masses have pushed China’s winter heating season into a critical phase; forecasters expect somewhat more frequent cold waves and extreme weather. State Grid expects a winter peak load of 1.24 TW and reliable peak supply capability of about 1.3 TW, with interregional transfer capacity topping 200 GW for the first time. The National Energy Administration said coal stocks at centrally dispatched plants stand at 216 Mt—about 32 days of burn; peak winter gas capability is more than 70 mcm/day above last year’s peak, PipeChina’s “national one network” peak capacity has risen to 1.2 bcm/day, and LNG terminal tank inventory exceeds 1.05 bcm. China Southern Power Grid warned that southern ice and freeze spells could be the strongest in nearly a decade.

### 6. Lithium carbonate near CNY 90,000/t; time-of-use and system-cost reforms improve storage economics (Storage / Markets)

**Summary:** Securities Daily reported on Nov 21 that battery-grade lithium carbonate rose from about CNY 120,000/t early in the year to near CNY 200,000/t in mid-May before falling to about CNY 90,000/t in November—down more than CNY 100,000/t from the peak. CITIC Securities estimates that drop cuts cell costs by over CNY 0.06/Wh and still leaves more than CNY 0.04/Wh of net cost relief after higher processing fees and a 2% battery consumption tax. On the revenue side, the 15th Five-Year Plan New-Type Energy Storage Implementation Plan calls for better charge/discharge pricing and system operating-cost allocation; Zhejiang already folds system costs into industrial/commercial time-of-use floats, and Shaanxi will raise peak/off-peak spreads from 1 January 2027. Analysts say independent storage charging in off-peak hours could cut system-fee burdens sharply.

## III. Renewables & Storage

### 7. Global Energy Storage Alliance launched at COP31 China Pavilion: CATL, BYD, ACWA among 27 co-initiators (Storage)

**Summary:** pv magazine reported on Nov 21 that the Global Energy Storage Alliance (GESA) was formally launched on Nov 13 at the COP31 China Pavilion in Antalya. Founding organisations are the China Energy Storage Alliance, the China Renewable Energy Engineering Institute and China Southern Power Grid Energy Storage; 27 co-initiators include CATL, BYD Energy Storage, EVE Energy, Hithium and Sungrow, plus CGN Energy International, POWERCHINA International and Saudi developer ACWA Power. Seven bodies—including the Long Duration Energy Storage Council, the International Hydropower Association, Energy Storage Europe and WRI—are exploring participation. The alliance targets dialogue, a global project/standards database, R&D, market mechanisms, supply chains and investment mobilisation in developing economies, aiming at weak valuation of short- and long-duration storage, fragmented standards and emerging-market finance barriers.

## IV. Extreme Weather & Climate System

### 10. Super El Niño shatters records: equatorial Pacific anomalies above 3°C reorder global drought and flood patterns (Climate)

**Summary:** Science News and European outlets, citing Copernicus Climate Change Service and others, say the 2026 super El Niño has already set multiple observational records: daily average sea-surface temperature anomalies in the monitoring region once exceeded about 3.03°C and peaked near 3.5°C—above the 2015 super event—while the WMO expects further intensification around January. Hot Pacific water has fuelled a record-active eastern Pacific hurricane season while suppressing Atlantic storms; wider impacts include Central American drought emergencies, Southeast Asian drought and wildfires, East African flood risk, and higher global mean temperatures that raise heat-related health burdens. Scientists stress that natural variability on an already warmer baseline amplifies extremes.
