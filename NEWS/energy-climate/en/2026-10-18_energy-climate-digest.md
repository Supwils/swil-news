## I. Energy Security, Policy, and Carbon Markets

### 1. The Hormuz shock turns Europe's electrification debate into orders (energy security)
**Summary:** El País reported on October 18 that the war in the Middle East and the closure of the Strait of Hormuz, following the war in Ukraine, amount to a second energy crisis in five years and are weakening resistance to leaving fossil fuels. Renewable installations rose 15.5% worldwide last year, a record, and global electricity storage is expected to double this year. Electric-vehicle sales have jumped in major European markets since spring: fully electric cars are about one in two new cars in Portugal and Belgium and about one in three in France and Germany, while Spain already has 400,000 electric cars on the road. The EU spent more than €300 billion on oil and gas last year; Jan Rosenow of the University of Oxford says the Hormuz closure added about €50 billion, and he cites a European Commission estimate that electrification could cut that burden by up to €260 billion by 2040. Bruce Douglas, chief executive of the Global Renewables Alliance, says importers are spending an extra $1.8 billion a day and cites a 16% year-on-year drop in China's oil demand for transport. COP31 president Murat Kurum has proposed raising electricity's share of global final energy from about 23% to 35% by 2035. Council of the EU papers preparing the bloc's position say the proposal does not commit to supplying that increase only with clean power.

**Commentary:** Fuel prices have made electric cars and heat pumps an economic case; the 35% electrification target still does not say where the electricity comes from.

### 2. Italy opens Fer X expressions of interest on October 22, with 27.15 GW of auction quotas (renewables)
**Summary:** Il Sole 24 Ore reported on October 18 that the final Fer X support scheme, run by the energy services manager GSE, is under way for solar, wind, hydro, and sewage-gas plants whose costs are close to market rates, and remains in force through December 31, 2030. The window for expressions of interest runs from noon on October 22 to noon on November 6. Projects above 1 MW compete in auctions and must obtain preliminary qualification by November 30. Auction quotas total 27.15 GW: 16.50 GW of wind, 10 GW of solar, 0.63 GW of hydro, and 0.02 GW of sewage gas, with another 10 GW reserved for direct access by plants up to 1 MW. A decree of October 2 sets targets for the first round rather than volumes that are awarded automatically: 350 MW for conventional solar, 150 MW for solar under the EU Net-Zero Industry Act, 1.5 GW for conventional wind, and 750 MW for NZIA wind. The solar reference operating price is €80/MWh and the auction ceiling is €95/MWh; for wind the figures are €85 and €95. Winners receive a 20-year two-way contract for difference and repay GSE when the market price is above the awarded price.

### 3. Bangladesh's cabinet clears a rooftop-solar draft aimed at 3,000–4,000 MW before next summer (solar)
**Summary:** The Daily Star reported on October 18 that a cabinet meeting chaired by Prime Minister Tarique Rahman approved the draft Special Solar Power Installation Ordinance, 2026, targeting 3,000–4,000 MW of solar before next summer to reduce dependence on imported fossil fuels. Cabinet Secretary Nasimul Gani said the recent war had exposed the country's energy vulnerability, that systems could go on government and private buildings and unused land, and that the ordinance was expected to be issued the same day. A cabinet division statement said the Public Procurement Act, the Invest Bangladesh Act, and the Electricity Act do not cover installation and service arrangements under the capital-expenditure model, while the operating-expenditure model lacks rules on 15-year power prices, rooftop rent paid in electricity, and maintenance. The Power Division has drawn up a National Renewable Energy Development Strategy for 2026–2030 under which investors would finance rooftop systems on public buildings and institutions with their own revenue could build their own.

### 4. Brazil sets up a carbon-market working group on leakage and border adjustments (carbon market)
**Summary:** Carbon Pulse reported on October 18 that Brazil has established a working group, through a federal resolution, to examine the competitiveness of entities covered by its emerging emissions trading system, including carbon border adjustment mechanisms and carbon leakage. The text is CTCP/SBCE Resolution No. 5, dated October 14, adopted after a September 28 plenary decision of the standing technical advisory committee and published in an extra edition of the Federal Official Gazette on October 15. The group is to recommend responses on regulatory and competitiveness risks, shifts of production to jurisdictions without equivalent carbon pricing, equalization of carbon costs, decarbonization pathways, use of system revenues, and the effect of other jurisdictions' border adjustments. It is coordinated by the Subsecretariat of Regulation and Methodologies in the Finance Ministry's extraordinary carbon-market secretariat. Participating bodies have ten days from publication to name representatives. The group lasts up to one year from its first meeting, and the committee plenary may extend it for another year.

### 6. California law requires an electrification option before a gas service line is replaced (electrification)
**Summary:** Grist on October 18 republished a Canary Media report on California's Home Energy Choice Act, Assembly Bill 2313, which requires utilities to offer households scheduled for a gas service-line replacement either the new line or a financial incentive, worth less than the replacement, to switch to a heat pump, induction range, and heat-pump water heater. In Pacific Gas and Electric's service area a replacement typically costs $20,000 to $30,000 and is recovered from all gas customers over the life of the asset; the utility replaces about 15,000 service lines a year. The Natural Resources Defense Council calculates that giving 1,000 households $17,500 each instead of replacing the lines would save gas customers $20 million in total. That figure is a scenario, not a subsidy set in the statute. The version that passed caps programs at 1% of a utility's customers, and the law expires on January 1, 2035, though it can be renewed. The Public Utilities Commission must write the framework, and utilities have until January 1, 2028, for their programs to be approved.

**Links:**

- [Grist — A new gas line or cash to electrify: California lets households choose](https://grist.org/energy/a-new-gas-line-or-cash-to-electrify-california-lets-households-choose/)

**Commentary:** The law attaches an exit option to the moment a line would be replaced; the incentive level waits on the utilities commission.

### 11. ASEAN raises its southern transboundary haze alert to the highest level (wildfire)
**Summary:** France 24 reported on October 18 that months of forest and peatland fires in Indonesia have sent toxic haze into Malaysia, Singapore, and Timor-Leste. The ASEAN Specialised Meteorological Centre on Friday activated Alert Level 3 for the southern ASEAN region, the top rung of a four-tier scale, used when there is a high risk of severe transboundary haze and widespread moderate to dense smoke has been observed for two or more consecutive days. Environmental group Auriga said more than 637,000 hectares burned from January through August, about nine times the area of Singapore; deforestation monitor Nusantara Atlas put the figure closer to 900,000 hectares. Indonesia's disaster agency said 2.4 tonnes of dry ice were dispersed by plane on Saturday at a main airport in South Sumatra in an attempt to clear the haze. Singapore said Friday it would warn seven companies in Indonesia that it identified as responsible for fires. On Saturday, IQAir ranked Singapore and Kuala Lumpur second and third among major cities for pollution, at "very hazardous" readings. A Philippines foreign-department spokesman for ASEAN issues, Dominic Xavier Imperial, said Friday the haze would be discussed in meetings ahead of a November ASEAN summit in Manila.

### 12. A strong El Niño is expected to intensify West Coast atmospheric rivers, with the first wave penciled in after mid-November (extreme weather)
**Summary:** USA Today reported on October 18 that climate scientist Daniel Swain says a stronger El Niño brings stronger atmospheric rivers rather than more of them, and also raises the frequency of other storms in California. Writing on Weather West, he said that if this event is not already the strongest and easternmost-centered on record, it will become so, and that the tilt toward a wetter, stormier California will be pronounced. AccuWeather forecaster Alex DaSilva told the paper the first atmospheric river could arrive as early as mid-November, with the most frequent period in January and February. Swain said he would not be surprised to see little of note in October. DaSilva also warned of beach erosion, strong winds, and significant flooding from winter atmospheric rivers, and of flooding from snowmelt later in spring. Both noted that autumn precipitation has little statistical connection to even an extremely strong El Niño that would let it predict the winter.
