## I. The Emissions Gap, Carbon Markets and Industrial Policy

### 1. UNEP: overshooting 1.5°C is now unavoidable; the lowest path still caps the peak at 1.7–1.8°C (emissions gap)
**Summary:** The UN Environment Programme published the Emissions Gap Report 2026, The Point of Return, on October 21. It finds that the chance to limit warming to 1.5°C with no or only limited overshoot has been lost. The remaining "lowest possible" overshoot pathway has a 66% chance of holding the mid-century peak to 1.7–1.8°C and about a 50% chance of returning to 1.5°C or below by 2100. Against 2025 levels, global greenhouse-gas emissions would have to fall 18% by 2030, 45% by 2035 and 69% by 2040. Full delivery of current nationally determined contributions would cut emissions only 5–8% by 2030 and 11–15% by 2035. If current policies continue, there is a 66% chance that warming is limited to 2.8°C by 2100 and keeps rising after that; if the contributions are met and similar efforts continue, the figure is 2.6°C, worse than last year's projection because the United States left the Paris Agreement and historical emissions were revised up. Emissions in 2025 were about 58.4 billion tonnes of CO2 equivalent, a preliminary 0.1 billion tonnes below 2024, but the uncertainty band is ±5.5 billion tonnes, so the report says a peak cannot yet be declared. Carbon dioxide from fossil fuels was still rising, while the land-use sector fell by about 0.5 billion tonnes. Since 2015, renewable power capacity has grown about three times faster than the current-policy scenarios of that year expected, and solar about seven times faster.

**Links:**

- [UN News — Global warming will breach 1.5°C, but deep cuts can still bring it back](https://news.un.org/en/story/2026/10/1168634)

**Commentary:** The report moves the task from holding 1.5°C to keeping the overshoot short and then reversing it, while existing pledges deliver only a 5–8% cut by 2030.

### 2. EU Council and Parliament reach a provisional deal on the emissions-trading market stability reserve (carbon market)
**Summary:** The Council of the EU said on October 21 that it and the European Parliament had reached a provisional agreement on October 20 to amend the invalidation rules for allowances in the market stability reserve of the EU emissions trading system. Both institutions still have to confirm the deal before formal adoption. The invalidation mechanism stays, and the threshold rises from 400 million allowances to 700 million, taking effect as soon as the amendment is formally adopted. The European Commission had proposed halting invalidation indefinitely. The Council said more than 3 billion allowances have been invalidated since the mechanism began in 2023. The larger reserve is meant to handle possible tightness after the mid-2030s as the cap keeps falling. A gradual lowering of the threshold is left to the wider ETS review still under way. The change is described as a short-term response to high and volatile energy prices and does not redesign the reserve.

**Links:**

- [Council of the EU — Provisional agreement on a targeted MSR amendment](https://www.consilium.europa.eu/en/press/press-releases/2026/10/21/ets-market-stability-reserve-council-and-parliament-reach-provisional-agreement-on-targeted-amendment/)

**Commentary:** Invalidation remains, but the threshold moves from 400 million to 700 million allowances, and the rule in force does not change until the amendment is adopted.

### 3. China's environment ministry: the carbon market covers about 8.3 billion tonnes and will add petrochemicals and chemicals by 2027 (carbon market)
**Summary:** Vice Minister of Ecology and Environment Li Gao said at a State Council Information Office briefing on October 21 that renewable capacity reached 2,455 GW in the first half of 2026, 60.7% of total generating capacity, and that solar capacity passed coal at the end of July. The national emissions-trading market now covers about 8.3 billion tonnes of carbon dioxide, more than 65% of the national total. He said cumulative allowance turnover passed 1 billion tonnes last week. During the 14th Five-Year Plan, more than 1,100 major emitters gained about 36 billion yuan from emissions cuts, and the market lowered economy-wide abatement costs by 56.8 billion yuan. The October 20 close was 98.11 yuan a tonne, more than double the price on the 2021 launch day. The voluntary market has registered 44 projects and more than 25 million tonnes of reductions, with turnover above 24 million tonnes and value close to 2 billion yuan. By 2027 the compliance market is to expand to petrochemicals, chemicals and other industries, with covered sectors cutting emissions per unit of product by about 3%. Sectors with relatively stable totals would move first to a cap, and the paid share of allocation would rise over time.

### 5. Industry ministry publishes a dual-control plan: industrial carbon intensity down more than 17% by 2030 (industrial policy)
**Summary:** On October 21 the Ministry of Industry and Information Technology published the 2026–2030 plan for a carbon dual-control system in industry and information technology. The notice is dated September 30. By 2030 the plan calls for about 400 carbon-footprint accounting rules for key industrial products, about 500 national zero-carbon factories and zero-carbon computing facilities, a cut of more than 17% in carbon dioxide per unit of industrial value added among larger firms versus 2025, and a drop of more than 10% in energy use per unit of value added. Over the 15th Five-Year Plan period, accounting, evaluation and assessment systems for the sector are to be basically in place, covering local carbon assessment, sector controls, company carbon management, project evaluation and product footprints. The plan also steers new projects toward zero-carbon parks, supports energy-and-carbon models, and calls for coordination on carbon displacement and evaluation of energy- and emissions-intensive industrial projects.

## II. Power Systems, Projects and Oil & Gas

### 6. Energy administration publishes coal-and-renewables integration rules as nine-month power use rises 3.9% (power system)
**Summary:** On October 21 the National Energy Administration published opinions on integrating coal power with new energy, document Guoneng Fadianli [2026] No. 69, dated September 28. Integration must not weaken coal's ability to support and balance the system, and the renewable capacity must match the extra flexibility gained from retrofits. By the end of the 15th Five-Year Plan the integration models are to be in mature use, and annual emissions of projects should be significantly lower than before the rebuild. The five settings are nearby renewables sharing a public coal plant's export route, combined heat and power for cleaner heat, coastal coal plants taking offshore wind ashore, captive coal plants bundled with nearby renewables, and on-site or nearby renewables covering a coal plant's own use. For shared export, offshore wind and captive plants, the document encourages an annual emissions cut of more than 5% and strives for more than 10%. The edge of a renewable site should in principle sit within 100 kilometres of the coal plant. Curtailment from these projects is excluded from renewable-utilization statistics and assessments, and qualifying projects can receive central funding. The same day's consumption release put January–September electricity use at 8,073.4 billion kilowatt-hours, up 3.9%. Charging and swapping services rose 55.4% and internet data services 41.6%. Residential use in September fell 6.0% year on year, which the administration attributed mainly to cooler weather from Typhoon Sader in high-load regions.

### 7. Britain clears the Jackdaw gas field and a £2 billion Energy Wealth Fund; Rosebank is still open (oil and gas)
**Summary:** Energy Secretary Miatta Fahnbulleh agreed on October 21 that the North Sea Transition Authority may consent to production at the already licensed Jackdaw gas field. The government said gas could reach British users by the end of this month, lifting domestic gas output by about 6% at peak over a life of up to 11 years. The developer estimates 27 new direct jobs, 273 existing jobs safeguarded, about £2.9 billion added to the economy, and output with the energy of about 40 million barrels of oil. Chancellor John Healey announced an Energy Wealth Fund inside the National Wealth Fund, starting with £2 billion and later taking North Sea oil and gas tax revenue into renewable and low-carbon projects. The government restated that it will not issue new exploration licences. Fahnbulleh told the Guardian the decision "is not about bills." The paper, citing officials, said she is minded to reject the larger Rosebank oilfield, with a final decision expected before year-end and Prime Minister Andy Burnham still able to overrule her. The energy department called that speculation. The Guardian said Rosebank would emit about seven times the carbon of Jackdaw, and that Jackdaw itself is estimated to add about 36 million tonnes of carbon emissions.

### 8. India is drafting an agrivoltaics policy, with an initial 5 GW target (solar)
**Summary:** Pralhad Joshi, India's minister for new and renewable energy, said on October 21 at the Agrivoltaics World Conference in New Delhi that the government is writing a policy to grow crops and generate power on the same land. He said about 30% of India's 160 million hectares of cropland is suitable, and that Maharashtra, Rajasthan, Andhra Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh, Karnataka and Uttar Pradesh together hold about half of the estimated potential. About 85% of farmers are small or marginal and cannot take land out of cultivation; about four acres are needed per megawatt. Joshi said agrivoltaic plants cost 25–30% more than ground-mounted solar because of elevated structures, at about 3.20 rupees per unit against 2.60 rupees for ground-mounted projects. Ministry secretary Santosh Kumar Sarangi told reporters the draft will be published for comment, that the initial target is 5 GW over the next few years, and that a dedicated 10 GW agri-PV component is also expected in the proposed PM-KUSUM 2.0 scheme. Joshi said global agrivoltaic capacity has passed 18.4 GW.

### 10. Kazakhstan aims to mobilise about €17.4 billion through a just-transition platform (investment)
**Summary:** Euronews reported on October 21 that Kazakhstan aims to mobilise $20 billion, about €17.4 billion, for cleaner energy through its just energy transition platform, QaJET, with support from the European Bank for Reconstruction and Development. The platform is due to be launched officially at COP31 in Antalya in November. About half the money, roughly €8.7 billion, is intended for 10 GW of new renewables by 2035, about a quarter for integrating that power, and the rest for electrification in transport, industry and smaller firms plus network upgrades. The platform is expected to cut more than 20 million tonnes of greenhouse gases a year, about 7% of the country's energy-related emissions. Projects already in the pipeline exceed €1 billion, including the Mirny wind farm in Zhambyl, developed by TotalEnergies and Kazakh partners: 1 GW, 150 turbines and 600 MWh of batteries, due in 2028, about 4 TWh a year, up to 3.5 million tonnes of carbon dioxide avoided, under a 25-year contract. The EBRD is also lending up to 102 billion tenge, €194 million, to Almaty's distribution company for metering and low- and medium-voltage upgrades. The policy frame is carbon neutrality by 2060 and a cut in net emissions of up to 25% by 2030 versus 1990, subject to international support. Euronews, citing LowCarbonPower, said fossil fuels still supply about 85% of electricity.

## III. Extreme Weather and Climate Risk

### 12. WMO: this El Niño could push Pacific sea-surface anomalies to 3.8°C, and the UN asks for action now (climate risk)
**Summary:** Celeste Saulo, secretary-general of the World Meteorological Organization, said on October 21 that the current El Niño could be off the monitoring charts used for the past four decades, peaking toward the end of 2026 with impacts lasting well beyond that. The latest global seasonal outlook says tropical Pacific sea-surface temperatures may rise to 3.8°C above average from November 2026 through January 2027. An anomaly of 0.5°C is enough for a WMO El Niño alert, and events linked to anomalies around 3°C have already had severe worldwide effects. UN relief chief Tom Fletcher called it a wrecking ball likely to hit Latin America, eastern and southern Africa, and Asia and the Pacific with extreme heat, drought and floods. He said some 50 million people could face increased hunger, and cited an African Development Bank estimate of $10 billion to $20 billion in costs for African countries and a 1 to 2 percent hit to GDP. He has released more than $50 million from the Central Emergency Response Fund for 11 front-line countries, including Chad, El Salvador, Ethiopia, Somalia and South Sudan, and said the next two weeks are critical for getting supplies into Somalia, Ethiopia and Kenya before heavy rains arrive.

**Links:**

- [UN News — UN agencies appeal for help ahead of an unprecedented El Niño](https://news.un.org/en/story/2026/10/1168639)
- [WMO — Celeste Saulo on El Niño and humanitarian action](https://wmo.int/secretariat/celeste-saulo/el-nino-and-humanitarian-action)
