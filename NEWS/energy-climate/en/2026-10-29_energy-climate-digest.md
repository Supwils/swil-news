## I. Policy & Carbon Markets

### 1. UK chancellor unveils “radical” energy-market reform to cut bills while restating 2050 net zero (Policy)
**Summary:** In the Autumn Budget, Chancellor John Healey set a “new course on energy”: a £100 Household Energy Payment for up to 20 million England band A–D homes, and an extension of the household electricity VAT cut to July 2027, expected to take about £50 off the April 2027 price cap. Longer-term measures include a Cost Control Framework before the 2027 spending review with no new policy costs on bills until then; scrapping the planned Gas Shipper Obligation so hydrogen is publicly funded; approving new generation only when it lowers bills and can connect; shifting more older renewables onto fixed-price contracts; and faster connections via GB Grid. Healey cited Middle East conflict-driven fossil-fuel spikes while saying the government remains “unshakably committed” to net zero by 2050.

### 2. EU energy ministers meet as TTF gas tops €100/MWh and short-term tools run dry (Energy security)
**Summary:** EU energy ministers met in Luxembourg on Oct 29 on winter security and prices. Wholesale TTF gas had briefly topped €100/MWh for the first time since December 2022; EU storage is about 75% full versus roughly 83% a year earlier, with Middle East conflict and late refill buying lifting prices. Ministers acknowledged subsidies, windfall taxes and renewables schemes have not calmed protests; Ireland’s energy minister said there is no “magic wand.” Energy Commissioner Dan Jørgensen warned of a “difficult” winter, but capitals rejected formal demand-reduction mandates and instead discussed “targeted and temporary” subsidies that could include fossil fuels. Multiple states also pushed back against Commission tax provisions meant to make electricity cheaper relative to gas, citing unanimity rules on taxation.

### 4. EPA formally publishes methane-rule reconsideration; agency sees only ~0.14% crude-price relief (Oil & gas)
**Summary:** The Federal Register on Oct 29 published EPA’s additional reconsideration of oil-and-gas NSPS/EG rules, proposing to rescind the Super Emitter Program, subcategorize marginal and conventional well sites, and revise requirements on liquids unloading, process controllers, pumps, fugitives, storage vessels, covers and closed vent systems, associated gas and control devices—including treating ≥95% efficient flaring/control as a BSER option for associated gas. CNBC reported EPA estimates of an 11-cent-per-barrel (~0.14%) crude-price drop in 2027, a ~0.09% production rise, about 60 million additional short tons of methane from 2027–2050, and roughly $45 billion in economy-wide savings including about $42 billion in lower compliance costs. The proposal remains subject to comment and finalization.

**Links:**

### 6. RFF models U.S. bipartisan permitting bill: ~30% more transmission, ~1.8 Gt less power CO2 (Permitting)
**Summary:** Resources for the Future on Oct 29 modeled power-sector effects of the Senate’s Bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act (BAAJA). In its central scenario, overall U.S. transmission capacity rises about 30% by 2041 versus baseline and interregional capacity more than doubles; average annual electricity-system costs fall 3.5–7.0%, saving over $350 billion through 2050 in present-value terms; near-term clean capacity expands and cumulative power-sector CO2 falls by about 1.8 Gt through 2041. Axios notes the analysis will shape lobbying ahead of a Nov 9 test vote. Gas additions still occur, but more clean generation and a stronger grid are projected to displace higher-emitting output.

**Summary:** The China Electricity Council on Oct 29 projected that by end-2026 national installed capacity will reach about 4.3 TW, of which non-fossil capacity about 2.7 TW (~63% of the mix); wind and solar together would be about half of total capacity, with coal’s share falling to about 31%. Full-year demand is seen near 10.8 trillion kWh (+4–4.5%), with more than 350 GW of additions possible in 2026.

### 8. Southern China regional power market moves to formal operation; ~900 TWh traded Jan–Sep (Power markets)
**Summary:** Economic Daily reported on Oct 29 that China’s first regional power market—the Southern regional market covering Guangdong, Guangxi, Yunnan, Guizhou and Hainan—has shifted from continuous settlement trial operation to formal operation, a milestone for the national unified market. Coal, hydro, nuclear, wind, solar and gas compete under one price formation set; cross-zone corridors now allocate transmission capacity via market bidding, not energy-only trades. January–September traded volume approached 900 TWh, over 70% of regional retail sales, with renewable utilization above 95%. More than 200 Hong Kong, Macao and Taiwan firms bought 1.28 million green certificates. Operators plan to bring more storage and flexible demand into ancillary services across energy, flexibility, environmental and capacity value stacks.

**Links:**

### 9. CREA: China’s 2026 clean-tech exports on track to avoid ~450 MtCO2 a year abroad (Exports)
**Summary:** Analysis for Carbon Brief by the Centre for Research on Energy and Clean Air finds that if first-nine-month trends hold, China’s 2026 exports of solar, wind, EVs and batteries could avoid about 450 million tonnes of CO2 a year once deployed—roughly Brazil’s annual fossil CO2 and about 2% of emissions outside China. Coverage links the export surge partly to renewable demand amid the Middle East energy shock.

### 10. Portugal tenders ~1,050 MVA of grid capacity and mandates grid-forming for large BESS (Storage)
**Summary:** Energy-Storage.News reported on Oct 29 that Portugal launched auctions for about 1,050 MVA of connection capacity—roughly 750 MVA for standalone storage and 300 MVA for renewables with co-located storage. Standalone projects need at least four hours at full injection power and must size nominal power to 100% of allocated injection/charging capacity. A decree last week requires standalone BESS above 45 MW to operate in grid-forming mode with synthetic inertia, reflecting post–Iberian blackout security concerns; the 2030 storage target is about 6.9 GW. The TSO may curtail up to about 500 charge/discharge hours a year for standalone projects. IPP Sonnedix also commissioned two solar-plus-storage plants totaling nearly 70 MWp PV and about 140.5 MWh of batteries.

**Links:**

- [Energy-Storage.News — Portugal mandates grid-forming BESS and launches ~1 GW tender](https://www.energy-storage.news/portugal-makes-grid-forming-mandatory-for-large-scale-bess-and-launches-1gw-tender/)

**Commentary:** Connection rights are now sold with grid-forming duties—storage is being rewritten as system hardware, not just a spread trade.
