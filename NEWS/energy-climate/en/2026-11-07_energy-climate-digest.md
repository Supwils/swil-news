### 2. Allianz Tracker: At Current Pace, Global Net Zero Slips to 2064 as Renewables Overtake Coal Power (Climate)
**Summary:** Allianz Research on November 5 released the second Green Transition Tracker 2026, scoring 65 economies that account for about 81% of global greenhouse-gas emissions. Most countries are improving, yet at today’s reduction speed the world would reach carbon neutrality only in 2064 — 14 years past the 2050 benchmark. A bright spot is power: in 2025 renewables supplied 33.5% of global electricity, edging past coal’s 33.2% for the first time, with roughly 99% of new generation coming from renewables. The report sees a possible 2026 decline in fossil CO₂ even with solid growth, but grid bottlenecks, rising demand and policy uncertainty could slow progress; the EU’s Paris-aligned path points near 2057, while the U.S. lags with a second straight emissions rise.

### 3. EU Council Backs One-Year Delay to Methane Import Rules, Citing Energy Security (EU)
**Summary:** On November 6 the EU Council agreed its negotiating position on targeted methane-regulation amendments: monitoring, reporting and verification duties for importers of natural gas, crude oil and coal would start January 1, 2028 instead of 2027; methane-intensity reporting and maximum intensity limits would slip to August 2029 and August 2031. Ministers cited Middle East conflict disruption and supply-security pressure; Ireland’s climate and energy minister stressed reliable supplies amid high prices. The text also tasks the Commission with assessing by end-September 2027 whether third-party verifiers and related rules are ready, allowing further delay via secondary law if not. Early agreement with the European Parliament is needed before year-end.

## II. China, Canada & Emerging Markets

### 5. Global Energy Transition Report (2026): China Runs the World’s Largest Renewables System as Coal Power Share Falls Below 50% (China)
**Summary:** At the November 7 Energy Transition Conference in Beijing, the China Enterprise Reform and Development Society released the Global Energy Transition Report (2026). It says China has built the world’s largest and fastest-growing renewables system; coal’s share of generation fell to 49.7% in the first half of 2026, below 50% for the first time. Global renewable capacity reached about 5,149 GW by end-2025, supplying 33.8% of electricity and overtaking coal for the first time in a century. China supplies roughly 70% of wind equipment and 80% of solar modules worldwide, helping cut wind and solar costs by more than 60% and 80%; 2026 energy investment is projected near $940 billion, first globally. Separately, CEC data show solar capacity at 1.31 TW by end-September — historically surpassing coal as China’s largest installed source — with wind plus solar above 2 TW.

### 6. Canada Budget 2026: Over CAD 50 Billion for a National Electricity Strategy Aiming to Double the Grid by 2050 (Canada)
**Summary:** Canada’s Budget 2026 elevates the “Power Canada Strong” National Electricity Strategy, committing over CAD 50 billion (about CAD 52 billion in some departmental tallies) to help double electricity-system capacity by 2050 and accelerate electrification. ### 8. India Cabinet Clears ₹145,000-Crore VGF for 92 GWh of Grid-Scale Storage (India)
**Summary:** India’s Union Cabinet approved a Viability Gap Funding scheme for grid-scale storage with about ₹14,500 crore (~$1.5 billion) of support from FY2026–27 through FY2030–31, targeting 92 GWh not yet commissioned: 60 GWh pumped hydro, 30 GWh BESS and 2 GWh alternative technologies, expected to mobilize roughly ₹97,600 crore of investment. Capacity will be allocated via tariff-based competitive bidding; BESS projects face a minimum 40% domestic-content requirement. Officials say the package equals about 170% of today’s grid-scale storage and supports a 411 GWh need by 2031–32. On the developer side, Adani Green said transmission constraints cost about ₹700 crore of first-half EBITDA and has commissioned 3 GWh of batteries at Khavda, taking total storage to 6.6 GWh to blunt curtailment.

## III. Clean Power, Storage & Offshore Wind

### 9. U.S. Revolution Wind Fully Online: 704 MW Offshore Project Survives Stop-Work Orders to Serve Rhode Island and Connecticut (Offshore Wind)
**Summary:** Ørsted’s Q3 report confirmed that the 704 MW Revolution Wind project with Skyborn reached full commercial operation in October. Sixty-five Siemens Gamesa SG 11.0-200 DD turbines now supply Rhode Island (~400 MW contracted) and Connecticut (304 MW), enough for about 350,000 homes. The farm endured Trump administration stop-work orders and permit challenges yet became the third major U.S. utility-scale offshore wind project at full commercial ops after South Fork and Vineyard Wind. Rhode Island’s energy office projects about $374 million in PPA savings over 20 years plus roughly $139 million in lower wholesale prices. In October the Justice and Interior departments still asked a court to remand the construction and operations plan for review, so political risk has not fully cleared.

### 10. Spain’s Endesa Starts First Grid-Forming Solar-Hybrid Batteries, ~60 MW / 120 MWh (Storage)
**Summary:** Endesa has begun building three grid-forming LFP battery systems to hybridise solar plants in its Masdar partnership on the Iberian Peninsula: about 60.16 MW / 120.34 MWh total and €25.2 million of investment, selected in Spain’s first innovative storage aid call. Ardila in Badajoz (~36.1 MW / 72.2 MWh) and La Vega I/II in Teba, Málaga (~12 MW / 24 MWh each) aim for end-2027 operation. Endesa says grid-forming inverters can deliver synthetic inertia and near-instant response, emulating conventional thermal or hydro machines. Recovery Plan and FEDER funding support parts of the spend, with Comillas and others validating grid-forming performance.
