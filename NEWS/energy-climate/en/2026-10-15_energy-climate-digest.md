## I. Policy and Oil

### 1. The IEA agrees to speed the release of about 100 million barrels still outstanding, including more than 20 million barrels of diesel (Oil)
**Summary:** The International Energy Agency published a Governing Board statement on Thursday saying oil markets remain tight, especially for diesel. Members have released about 330 million barrels so far. The board agreed to finish the remaining releases of around 100 million barrels in a timely way consistent with national circumstances, and to accelerate volumes already planned, including more than 20 million barrels of diesel as soon as possible. Some members have already released more than they pledged in March, and others intend to do so, so the total will exceed the original 400 million barrels of the March collective action. The statement also urges members with production and refining capacity to boost crude and products, particularly diesel, and underlines free energy trade. The secretariat will keep tracking progress against the October 2 G7 statement.

**Links:**

- [IEA — Governing Board statement on energy markets and security](https://www.iea.org/news/iea-governing-board-statement-on-energy-markets-and-security)

**Commentary:** What was decided today is to bring forward diesel from stocks already pledged, not to open a larger new release.

### 2. The EU summit stalls between short-term price relief and the integrity of the carbon market (Carbon market)
**Summary:** European Council leaders met in Brussels on Thursday with energy prices at the center of the agenda. Euronews reported that the energy debate had ended while conclusions were still being drafted. Slovakia, Hungary, the Czech Republic, and Poland, backed by Italy, are pushing to delay until 2030 the second emissions trading system covering road-transport and building fuels, now due in 2029. Several diplomats said that proposal will not get unanimity. Finland, Denmark, and Sweden warned in a letter that weakening the ETS would undermine the predictability investment depends on. Il Sole 24 Ore, citing EU sources, said leaders shared a view that the Middle East is lifting oil and gas prices, that the transition remains the long-term strategy, and that short-term measures and the Commission's existing proposals should be supported, while ETS wording in the conclusions was still deadlocked. ANSA reported that the only tool described as having a very near-term effect is an extension of the temporary energy state-aid framework. Dutch TTF gas was quoted around €85.69 to €87.32 per megawatt-hour.

### 3. Poland's president signs a renewable-energy amendment that auctions biomethane plants above 1 MW (Renewables)
**Summary:** Poland's Ministry of Climate and Environment said Thursday that the president signed on October 15 an amendment to the renewable-energy law and related acts (UD332), which the Senate had already passed without changes. The law introduces auction support for biomethane installations above 1 MW and sets rules for direct pipelines carrying biogas, agricultural biogas, or biomethane. It also simplifies how prosumers use energy storage, clarifies energy settlement, and adjusts energy cooperatives and the efficiency of renewable support schemes. The ministry said the changes are meant to speed renewable investment and raise biomethane and biogas output.

**Links:**

- [Ministry of Climate and Environment — Renewable-energy amendment will enter into force](https://www.gov.pl/web/klimat/nowelizacja-przepisow-o-odnawialnych-zrodlach-energii-wejdzie-w-zycie)

**Commentary:** What took effect today is the presidential signature; the auction threshold is biomethane above 1 MW, not a subsidy for every biogas project.

### 7. Ember: national 2030 storage targets total 822 GW, 55 percent of the 1,500 GW pledge (Storage)
**Summary:** Energy-Storage.News reported Thursday that Ember's tracker, covering 98 countries and the European Union, puts national 2030 storage targets at 822 GW. That is up 190 percent since the COP29 energy-storage and grids pledge, and it is still only 55 percent of the 1,500 GW global commitment. Almost all of the increase comes from China's 15th Five-Year Plan and the EU's new target: China aims for 300 GW of batteries plus 160 GW of pumped hydro, 460 GW in all and 330 GW above its previous target; the EU targets 200 GW by 2030, from about 55 GW at the start of this year. Citing IEA figures, Ember said annual battery additions rose from 9.5 GW in 2021 to 108 GW in 2025, total storage additions last year were 118 GW, and cumulative capacity reached 430 GW, with additions up 40 percent from 2024 to 2025. Ember calculates that 78 GW of additions a year are needed from 2026 to 2030, and that holding the 2025 pace would take the world to 1,020 GW, 24 percent above the 822 GW of national targets.

**Links:**

- [Energy-Storage.News — World falls well short of the storage pledge despite higher ambition](https://www.energy-storage.news/world-falls-well-short-on-commitment-to-energy-storage-despite-increased-ambitions-think-tank-ember-says/)

**Commentary:** Targets on paper lag the 1,500 GW pledge, while the 2025 build rate is already ahead of those national targets.

### 8. Malaysia sets corporate renewable access at 14 sen per kWh for developers and 4 sen for green users (Green power)
**Summary:** Bernama reported Thursday that Malaysia's Energy Commission will fix the system access charge for eligible developers under the Corporate Renewable Energy Supply Scheme acceleration package at 14 sen per kilowatt-hour for 10 years. Green consumers pay a separate 4 sen per kilowatt-hour under the Regulatory Period 4 tariff. The fixed rate applies to eligible projects that reach commercial operation by December 31, 2028, with a contract of at least 10 years. About 3 GW of projects are in the pipeline. The ministry announced the package on September 18, and the commission briefed industry in Putrajaya on October 13 with Tenaga Nasional, the Single Buyer, and the grid operator. The commission did not quantify savings versus the previous charge. Guideline amendments are expected by November 2026.

## III. Storage and Transition Projects

### 9. IRENA's 32nd Council opens as the new outlook puts electrification on the path after overshoot (Transition)
**Summary:** The Emirates News Agency reported Thursday that the 32nd Council of the International Renewable Energy Agency is meeting in Abu Dhabi from October 14 to 16, with more than 300 government representatives. The agency said renewable deployment is still setting records, technology costs have fallen, and end-use electrification is advancing, but implementation is too slow, electricity demand is rising, and efficiency and enabling infrastructure lag. The new World Energy Transition Outlook 2026 looks at how to return to a 1.5°C-compatible pathway after the current overshoot in carbon emissions. A central finding is the growing role of renewables-based electrification. Director-General Francesco La Camera said the outlook sets an agenda for 2026 to 2035. The Council will also discuss the 2026-2027 work programme and budget, the 2028-2032 medium-term strategy, and the next stage of selecting a director-general, with candidates to be recommended to the 2027 Assembly.

## IV. Climate and Disasters

### 12. Indonesia drops 1.5 tons of dry ice over South Sumatra to break up its worst haze in a decade (Disaster)
**Summary:** The BBC reported Thursday that Indonesia scattered dry-ice pellets over South Sumatra, including Palembang and nearby areas, to disperse toxic haze from forest and peat fires. Coordinating Minister Dudung Abdurachman said the dry ice is meant to break up low-level haze so it can rise, not to seed rain, and that a successful trial would be extended to Central Kalimantan and other haze centers. Authorities said the trial uses 1.5 tons of dry ice. Indonesia is in its most severe wildfire season in a decade, with an unusually strong El Niño worsening drought. New cloud seeding is also set for Central Kalimantan on Thursday and Friday. Nusantara Atlas has recorded more than one million fire hotspots this year and almost 900,000 hectares burned. Police have named more than 200 people and nine companies as suspects. Smoke has crossed into Singapore and Malaysia.

**Links:**

- [BBC — Indonesia douses its skies with dry ice to clear toxic haze](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/ck1l3djr5d5do)

**Commentary:** The 1.5 tons of dry ice is a low-level haze trial; more than a million hotspots and nearly 900,000 hectares burned are the scale of the season.
