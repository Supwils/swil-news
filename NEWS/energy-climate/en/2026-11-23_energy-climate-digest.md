## I. Climate Talks & International Cooperation

### 1. Day-after COP31 blame game: Australian opposition calls for energy minister’s resignation as Pacific nations voice despair (Policy)
**Summary:** Reuters reported on 23 November that finger-pointing began on the first working day after UN climate talks in Antalya, Türkiye, closed without a final agreement. Australian Climate Change and Energy Minister Chris Bowen, who co-led negotiations, faced opposition calls to resign at home, while Pacific island nations most at risk expressed despair over the failure of collective action. It was the first COP without a final deal since Copenhagen in 2009, after the EU and others rejected a text that omitted any mention of fossil fuels’ role under pressure from Saudi Arabia and other oil producers. The summit was also the first since the UN’s environmental body confirmed global temperatures are set to exceed the 1.5°C threshold.

### 2. China and Australia issue joint statement on climate change and clean-energy transition, reaffirming 1.5°C and “triple renewables” cooperation (International cooperation)
**Summary:** Xinhua on 23 November released the China–Australia Joint Statement on Climate Change and Clean Energy Transition. ## II. Policy & Carbon Markets

### 3. Eight EU countries unite to defend ETS2, warning further delays would weaken energy security and clean-investment signals (Carbon market)
**Summary:** Euronews reported on 23 November that climate ministers from Denmark, Finland, Germany, Luxembourg, the Netherlands, Portugal, Spain and Sweden wrote to Climate Action Commissioner Wopke Hoekstra opposing further delays or dilution of ETS2, which prices carbon in road-transport and building heating fuels. The 23 November letter cites Strait of Hormuz supply shocks: the EU imported about €340 billion of fossil fuels in 2025, and extra import costs since the strait’s closure have reached about €127 billion. The Visegrad Four, Italy and Greece have pressed to soften or postpone the scheme; the October summit agreed to assess impacts before the January 2028 start after a one-year delay already. The eight insist the Social Climate Fund and regulatory certainty must not be reopened in isolation.

**Commentary:** Europe is rewriting the carbon-price calendar as an energy-security brief—geopolitical shocks are changing the politics of household heating and transport bills.

### 4. South Korea to phase out renewable quotas from next year in favour of competitive long-term PPAs with KEPCO (Policy)
**Summary:** South Korea’s Ministry of Climate, Energy and Environment said on the 23rd it will enforce an amended renewable-energy promotion act next year, gradually dismantling the RPS that requires generators above 500 MW to source a set share of power from renewables, and shifting to competitive auctions for long-term fixed-price PPAs with state-run KEPCO. REC spot prices have swung hard—above 100,000 won per unit in 2017, down to around 30,000 in 2021, and recently near 70,000—creating financing uncertainty; the existing REC spot market will keep a three-year grace period through 2029 after enactment to ease the transition and strengthen sector competitiveness.

### 5. Saudi Arabia raises renewable self-consumption cap to 50 MW with tiered grid rules and mandatory net billing (Policy)
**Summary:** The Saudi Electricity Regulatory Authority (SERA) updated rules for renewable self-consumption—covering solar, wind, waste-to-energy and biofuels—raising the maximum system size under the framework from 2 MW to 50 MW; generation and consumption must be at the same facility, with integrated storage allowed. Grid-connected systems fall into four tiers (1–11 kW, 11–50 kW, 50 kW–2 MW, 2–50 MW): small residential systems need no prior approval but must notify the utility, while larger ones require studies, licences and higher fees. Surplus exports use net billing with credits carried forward, usable across accounts of the same consumer and valid for 12 months. The Saudi Press Agency said the update aims to simplify installation and connection while clarifying safety and consumer rights.

### 6. China’s MIIT rolls out industrial carbon dual-control plan: ~400 product carbon-footprint standards and ~500 zero-carbon factories/compute sites by 2030 (Policy)
**Summary:** Economic Daily reported on 23 November that the Ministry of Industry and Information Technology issued an implementation plan (2026–2030) to build a carbon emissions dual-control system for industry and IT. By 2030 it targets roughly 400 carbon-footprint accounting standards for key industrial products, about 500 national-level zero-carbon factories and zero-carbon compute facilities, and cuts of more than 17% in CO₂ per unit of value-added and 10% in energy use per unit of value-added for above-scale industry versus 2025. ## III. Energy Crisis, Green Power & Storage

### 7. IEA Energy Efficiency 2026: primary intensity may improve nearly 3% under Hormuz shock as oil and gas demand fall and electricity rises ~3.6% (Transition)
**Summary:** The IEA’s latest annual efficiency assessment says primary energy intensity is expected to improve by nearly 3% in 2026 (about 2% in 2025), as the Strait of Hormuz crisis lifts prices and holds global energy demand roughly flat while GDP grows about 3%. Around 120 countries introduced conservation, relief or resilience measures, and more than 30 added longer-term efficiency or electrification policies; oil demand is set to fall in most regions and global gas demand to decline, while electricity demand grows about 3.6%, with EV charging power use in China up more than 50% so far this year and EU battery-EV sales up about 50% in January–September versus 2025. The IEA cautions that part of the intensity gain reflects forced cutbacks, so emergency steps must harden into structural electrification and efficiency investment.

**Links:**

- [IEA — Energy crisis disrupts global demand trends in 2026, underscoring benefits of efficiency](https://www.iea.org/news/energy-crisis-disrupts-global-demand-trends-in-2026-underscoring-benefits-of-efficiency)
- [IEA — Energy Efficiency 2026](https://www.iea.org/reports/energy-efficiency-2026)

**Commentary:** Crisis-era “efficiency leaps” are half forced austerity—durable security gains still depend on whether heat pumps, EVs and grid investment stick.

### 8. Qinghai completes first hourly green-power trade: two retailers and renewables generators settle 1.89 GWh (Green power)
**Summary:** Xinhua reported from Xining on 23 November that Qinghai Power Exchange said the province’s first hourly green electricity trade cleared that day on the Beijing power trading platform, with two provincial retailers and renewables generators transacting 1.89 million kWh. Unlike conventional annual or monthly green-power organisation and settlement, hourly trading matches generation, load and contracted volumes to each hour, enabling hour-level traceability via generation, load and contract curves. State Grid Qinghai said it will refine organisation rules and platform functions and tighten metering, traceability and settlement links with standard green-power products.

### 14. U.S. courts dismiss further challenges to Gulf of America oil-and-gas Endangered Species Act national-security exemption (Oil & gas)
**Summary:** The U.S. Justice Department said on 23 November that the U.S. District Court for the District of Columbia dismissed on Friday and today the second and third lawsuits challenging the Endangered Species Committee’s national-security exemption for oil and gas activities in the Gulf of America, after a parallel case was dismissed on 16 November. The court again held that challenges to the Committee’s exemption decisions belong in the courts of appeals. On 31 March the Committee unanimously exempted Gulf oil and gas activities from Endangered Species Act requirements based on the Secretary of War’s national-security finding; the exemption remains in effect.
