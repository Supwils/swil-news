## I. Policy, Carbon Markets & International Climate Governance

### 1. Climate Action Tracker: current policies point to about 2.7°C and “irrecoverable overshoot” (science · COP)
**Summary:** On Nov 12, Climate Action Tracker (CAT) released its global warming-path update at COP31. Under policies in place today, end-of-century warming is about 2.7°C (slightly up from 2.6°C last year), with collective climate action effectively stalled for a fifth straight year. Meeting 2030 and 2035 targets would still yield about 2.6°C; adding long-term targets about 2.1°C; and the most optimistic scenario including net-zero pledges about 1.9°C. CAT says long-term warming is set to cross 1.5°C around 2030, so the task shifts from holding the limit to limiting the size and duration of overshoot, with emission cuts needing to accelerate from about 4% a year to 2030 to about 7% in the early 2030s and about 9% over 2035–2050. It also notes that clean electricity met all additional global power demand in 2025 and that China, India, EVs, and Amazon deforestation trends remain bright spots—yet fossil investment and rising energy demand still outweigh those gains.

**Commentary:** While negotiators still fight over a cover decision, science has rewritten 1.5°C from a guardrail into an overshoot-management problem—each year of delay steepens the return path.

Climate Home reports that Russia, Saudi Arabia, and the Like-Minded Developing Countries group (including China and India) have questioned the legitimacy and necessity of an Australia–Türkiye-led cover decision; Australia asked for written inputs by Saturday. Urban resilience featured in a leaders’ dialogue for the first time; UN-Habitat says cities need about $4.5 trillion a year in climate investment through 2030, with current finance covering only about one-fifth.

### 4. Indonesia–Norway sign Paris Agreement Article 6.2 MOPA for up to 12 million tonnes of credits by 2038 (carbon markets)
**Summary:** At COP31, Indonesia and Norway concluded a Mitigation Outcome Purchase Agreement (MOPA)—Indonesia’s first such deal to operationalize Paris Agreement Article 6.2—facilitated by the Global Green Growth Institute and Norway’s Article 6 Climate Action Fund. Norway will buy up to about 12 million tonnes of CO₂-equivalent emission reductions from solar and related projects linked to state utility PLN through 2038, at roughly $20–$40 per tonne, described by multiple parties as among the largest transactions recorded under the UNFCCC cooperative framework. The deal supports Indonesia’s Generation-Based Incentive programme, initially prioritising floating solar and favouring projects paired with battery storage to deliver power in high-emission hours; projects must show additionality, measurable reductions, and sustainable-development impacts, with payments after digital MRV and independent audit.

### 5. Singapore and World Bank launch carbon-market action plan; global carbon-finance network debuts (carbon markets)

### 10. Inner Mongolia completes first cross-province gas-replacement trade: 50.49 GWh of green power displaces Beijing gas generation (China · power markets)
**Summary:** Inner Mongolia Power Exchange, working with Beijing Power Exchange Center, completed the region’s first Beijing gas-replacement cross-province trade, totaling about 50.49 million kWh. Through bilateral negotiation, local renewables in surplus hours substituted for gas-fired generation in Beijing—a generation-rights swap between power plants rather than the usual generator-to-consumer incremental trade. Officials said the model widens renewable offtake and cross-provincial trading, with further coordination planned with Beijing and Guangzhou exchange centers to support a unified national power market.
