## I. Supply Security, Policy and Levies

### 1. Birol says the energy crisis will run into 2027 and proposes lifting electricity's share to 35% (supply security)
**Summary:** International Energy Agency executive director Fatih Birol told the Investment Summit Towards COP31 in Istanbul remotely on October 23 that he sees no convincing case for a return to normal in 2027, and that the crisis will continue in some form into that year unless new shocks appear. He proposed a commitment, for countries to sign in Antalya, to raise electricity's share of global energy from 23% to 35% by 2035, arguing that greater electrification would strengthen energy security and cut emissions. An Al Jazeera interview published the same day reported that Birol, speaking in Berlin on Wednesday, said Europe is entering winter with gas stocks far below historical averages, that a harsh winter could push gas prices higher, and that this year and next will not be easy. The report said EU gas storage is about 72% full, against a usual level near 90%, a shortfall roughly equal to the energy used by 10 to 12 million homes; gas prices are up 140% since February, Brent is trading well above $100 a barrel, and about 80% of EU emergency stocks remain.

### 2. US Energy Department authorizes an exchange of up to 400,000 reserve barrels for the Lake Charles refinery (oil)
**Summary:** The US Department of Energy announced on October 23 that it has authorized an emergency exchange of up to 400,000 barrels of crude from the West Hackberry Strategic Petroleum Reserve site to the Phillips 66 Lake Charles refinery in Louisiana. The oil is meant to offset a disruption in crude deliveries to the refinery and to keep transportation-fuel production going for Louisiana and nearby markets. Borrowed crude must be returned with additional premium barrels, which the department said adds no cost for taxpayers. Under Secretary of Energy Kyle Haustveit said Phillips 66 must return more barrels than are withdrawn. The notice does not state the cause of the disruption or a return schedule.

**Links:**

**Commentary:** This is a loan of crude that must be returned with extra barrels, sized for one refinery's short interruption, not a free release.

### 3. China's planning agency puts coal, power and gas supply contracts under one rule (supply security)
**Summary:** The National Development and Reform Commission on October 23 issued trial measures on medium- and long-term contracts for energy supply security, Order No. 50 of 2026, effective December 1, 2026. The rule brings thermal coal, electricity and natural gas into one framework: energy companies carry a duty to keep supply safe and stable, buyers and sellers negotiate or trade on market terms and sign and perform contracts accordingly, and transport providers and government departments have defined roles under a chain of corporate responsibility, government supervision and tiered accountability. Contracts may be adjusted by mutual agreement only if safe and stable supply is maintained. Serious disruption of supply security can bring legal liability, and the rule also sets incentive and constraint mechanisms.

**Links:**

- [21st Century Business Herald — NDRC issues measures on medium- and long-term energy supply contracts](https://www.21jingji.com/article/20261023/herald/ea78cb33e56bc7bb5d14b934753f9012.html)
- [National Business Daily — Coal, power and gas brought into one contractual framework](https://www.nbd.com.cn/articles/2026-10-23/4615007.html)

**Commentary:** Three contract types now sit in one regulation; the published account stresses the chain of responsibility and negotiated changes, not fuel-by-fuel signing quotas.

### 4. EU advocate general says the 2022 fossil-fuel windfall levy complies with EU law (levy)
**Summary:** Advocate General Athanasios Rantos delivered opinions on October 22 in three preliminary references from courts in Italy, Belgium and Ireland. He found that the temporary solidarity contribution adopted by the Council in 2022, in response to the energy-price surge after the war in Ukraine, is consistent with EU law. It applies to the portion of 2022 and 2023 profits in petroleum, natural gas, coal and refining that exceeds the average taxable profits of the previous four fiscal years by 20%, and member states could adopt equivalent national measures that raise comparable revenue. Rantos said Article 122(1) of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union is a valid legal basis, because the contribution is an urgent, temporary and proportionate economic measure and does not harmonize direct taxes, so unanimity in the Council was not required. The opinion does not bind the Court, and judgment is still pending.

**Links:**

- [Court of Justice of the EU — Advocate general's press release on the temporary solidarity contribution](https://curia.europa.eu/site/upload/docs/application/pdf/2026-10/cp260153en.pdf)

**Commentary:** The advocate general sided with the emergency legal basis; the disputes in three countries still wait on the Court's judgment and on national judges.

## II. Renewables, Storage and Projects

### 5. Bulgaria's storage capacity rises to about 6 GW, and power exports are up 62% this year (storage)
**Summary:** Reuters reported on October 23 that EU grants of about 700 million euros and regulatory changes helped Bulgaria raise storage capacity from about 3 GW a year ago to about 6 GW, so daytime solar can be released at the evening peak. Figures from industry group APSTE show power exports up 62% this year, to 3.5 terawatt-hours. Some of those exports supplied neighbors during the July and August heatwaves; Romania shut a nuclear reactor in August after drought lowered the Danube, whose water cools the plant. The system operator said daily exports have reached 50,000 megawatt-hours, about 2,000 megawatts an hour on average. The energy ministry told Reuters that narrowing price spreads may let batteries cannibalize their own arbitrage revenue, so balancing markets and system services will matter more. Greek official figures expect about 1 GW of storage by year-end.

**Commentary:** The 6 GW bought summer exports, and the ministry is already warning that the price gap which pays for arbitrage is narrowing.

### 6. South Korea's renewable capacity overtakes coal for the first time, at 41.04 million kilowatts (renewables)
**Summary:** pv magazine reported on October 23, citing the Korea Power Exchange, that renewable capacity reached 41,040 MW on October 21, 283 MW above coal-fired capacity of 40,757 MW, the first time renewables have exceeded coal on an installed-capacity basis. Renewables are 25.4% of national generating capacity, still behind LNG at 46,788 MW, a gap of about 5,748 MW. The exchange said that if average annual additions from the end of 2020 to the end of 2025 continue, about 3,504 MW for renewables and about 807 MW for LNG, renewables could become the largest source of installed capacity within two to three years. The comparison is of capacity, not generation. An accompanying grid plan aims to raise transmission-level renewable hosting capacity from 119 GW to 171 GW by 2030, and distribution-level capacity from 84 GW to 110 GW.

**Links:**

**Commentary:** What passed coal is nameplate capacity, not electricity generated, and the hosting-capacity plan is the next constraint.

### 8. Bangladesh issues a special ordinance for distributed solar on public buildings (solar)
**Summary:** pv magazine reported on October 23 that Bangladesh has issued the Special Solar Power Installation Ordinance, 2026, published in the official gazette on Monday, to fill gaps in long-term tariffs, rooftop rent and maintenance for solar on government buildings, in support of a target to add 3 to 4 GW of renewable capacity before next summer. The ordinance covers systems of up to 5 MW at government, semi-government, autonomous and local institutions, state-owned companies and residential buildings, under both owner-financed and third-party operating models. Eligible projects include rooftop solar, solar irrigation, cold storage, electric-vehicle charging, floating solar and agrivoltaics. The consumer, the service provider and the competent authority must sign a tripartite agreement, and relevant procurement is exempt from mandatory use of the electronic government procurement system. An industry group welcomed the ordinance and said bidder qualifications and technical conditions still need to be clearer.

### 11. Finland grants an operating licence to the world's first deep geologic repository for spent fuel (nuclear)
**Summary:** On October 22 the Finnish government granted Posiva an operating licence for the spent-fuel encapsulation and final-disposal facility at Olkiluoto. Posiva said it is the first operating licence anywhere for the final disposal of spent nuclear fuel, and that the company will move toward production lasting more than a century, but the licence does not authorize disposal to start at once. Commissioning tests must still be finished and approved by the Radiation and Nuclear Safety Authority, and no detailed start-up schedule was given; the authority's safety assessment in August was the central basis for the decision. NucNet, citing the government, reported that the licence allows possession, handling, storage and disposal of up to 6,500 tonnes of uranium from the Olkiluoto and Loviisa plants, about 3,300 canisters, with a plan to dispose of at most 60 canisters a year. Operations are estimated to run into the 2120s, after which the repository would be backfilled and closed. The repository lies about 430 metres down in bedrock.

### 12. The European Investment Bank proposes at least 30 billion euros for climate adaptation by 2030 (adaptation)
**Summary:** On October 22 the European Investment Bank Group presented phase two of its adaptation plan for 2026 to 2030, committing at least 30 billion euros for climate adaptation by 2030, double the previous five years. The money covers energy networks, public buildings, water systems and climate-resilient agriculture, with guarantees and advisory support. President Nadia Calviño said every euro spent on prevention and adaptation saves 10 euros in damage and repairs. The boards also approved 11.8 billion euros of new financing, including 5.5 billion euros to strengthen Europe's energy autonomy and lower power costs: energy infrastructure in France, Germany, Hungary and Italy, renewables in Austria, the Netherlands and Romania, and energy efficiency in Spain and Sweden. The group will also support climate-resilient water infrastructure in Argentina and safe drinking water and sanitation in Ghana.

## III. Extreme Weather and Outages

### 13. Storm Austen cuts power across southern Britain, with Storm Boelo due over the weekend (storm)
**Summary:** Austen, the UK's first named storm of the season, hit southern England and Wales on October 23. The BBC reported gusts of up to 84 mph, or 135 kilometres per hour, in Jersey. National Grid said engineers had restored power to more than 60,000 properties since the storm began, while more than 15,000 homes were still without power; schools on Jersey and Guernsey closed for the day. The Guardian reported that just before 11 a.m. more than 16,000 homes in the south-west were without power, a gust of 76 mph was recorded at St Mary's airport in the Isles of Scilly, and National Grid said more than 12,000 properties had been restored by 8 a.m. Prime Minister Andy Burnham, at a wildfire summit in Liverpool, said Britain may be facing a winter of greater flooding. The Met Office issued an amber wind warning from Saturday night into Sunday for much of Scotland, the north of Northern Ireland and northern England, with coastal gusts possibly reaching 80 mph and a further risk of power cuts.

### 14. Typhoon Champi nears northeast Taiwan, with about 8,400 homes out in Okinawa (typhoon)
**Summary:** The BBC's live coverage on October 23 reported Typhoon Champi moving close to Taiwan's northeast coast. Taiwan's weather service put sustained winds near the center at about 155 kilometres per hour around noon on Friday. The Central Weather Administration judged a landfall unlikely but could not rule one out if the storm stalls or wobbles, and forecast as much as 90 centimetres of rain through Saturday. The fire department said that as of 15:00 local time the storm had killed one person and injured 171; the death was a person swept away by waves on Thursday at Liuqiu. Okinawa Electric Power said about 8,400 households were without power at 14:00 local time. China's meteorological authority issued a blue typhoon alert, with 50 to 80 millimetres of rain expected along the eastern coast, including Shanghai, and Zhejiang issued an orange alert for huge coastal waves.
