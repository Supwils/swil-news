## I. Policy, Carbon Markets & International Climate Governance

### 1. COP31 Opens in Antalya: Agenda Settled Fast as Electrification and Finance Take Center Stage (COP)
**Summary:** The 31st UN climate conference opened on November 9 in Antalya, Türkiye, running through November 20, with Environment Minister Murat Kurum as COP president and Australia’s climate and energy minister Chris Bowen leading formal negotiations. The agenda was adopted quickly, avoiding past standoffs, though contentious standalone items on trade and tripling adaptation finance were left off; as a compromise, a climate-finance work programme was added so developing countries can still pursue adaptation finance in talks. UN climate chief Simon Stiell said the fossil-fuel price crisis is “spreading inflation like a cancer” and urged the summit to “supercharge solutions”: deliver on last year’s pledge to triple adaptation finance, ease access to funds, triple renewables by 2030, move power across grids and borders, and slash methane from oil, gas and waste. Hosts are pushing a voluntary “35-by-35” electrification goal—raising electricity to about one-third of global final energy by 2035. IEA analysis finds cost-effective technologies could reach about 33% by 2035 at pre-crisis prices, with today’s high fuel costs making electrification even more economic and potentially cutting fuel-importing countries’ import bills by more than $400 billion combined by 2035 versus 2025.

### 2. WMO State of the Climate 2026 Update: Earth Accumulating Heat Faster as Super El Niño Extends into 2027 (Science)
**Summary:** On COP31’s opening day the World Meteorological Organization released its State of the Climate Update 2026: 2026 is on track to rank among the four warmest years on record; January–September global mean surface temperature was about 1.47°C above the pre-industrial average and may rise further as El Niño, strengthening since August, is expected to become the strongest on record by December and persist into early 2027. Earth’s energy imbalance reached its highest level since monitoring began; the ocean absorbs about 90% of excess heat, and 2026 is on course for a tenth straight year of record ocean heat, with sea level and sea-surface temperatures also at record highs. Secretary-General Celeste Saulo warned El Niño will be a “risk multiplier” as heatwaves, cyclones and rainstorms intensify beyond historical baselines; hundreds of millions have already faced extreme heat this year, while Nepal’s ice-and-rock collapse and flash floods underscored compound risks in warming high mountains. WHO for the first time classified expected El Niño health impacts as a health emergency.

### 3. EU Carbon Market Report: 2025 ETS Emissions Down 2%, Auction Revenues Hit €43.2 Billion (Carbon Markets)
**Summary:** The European Commission on November 9 published its annual carbon-market and climate-action progress reports: emissions covered by the EU Emissions Trading System fell 2% in 2025, while revenues rose 11% year on year to €43.2 billion, with about €23.9 billion earmarked for member states to fund climate, energy and social transition measures. Broader EU greenhouse-gas emissions fell about 1.6%; wind and solar capacity reached 611 GW, or roughly 49% of electricity generation capacity; heat-pump additions were estimated at 41 GW in 2025 (up from 26 GW in 2024); and battery-electric car registrations hit about 1.9 million, roughly one in six new cars. The same day the EU auctioned about 2.792 million spot allowances at €86.91/t. The reports also flagged that nine member states fell short of related targets, underscoring uneven progress.

### 4. China’s Legislature Begins Review of Renewable Energy Law Revision as NEA Maps 15th Five-Year “Reliable Substitution” (China · Policy)
**Summary:** Xinhua reported on November 9 that the 25th session of the 14th NPC Standing Committee (Monday–Friday this week) began first reading of a draft amendment to the Renewable Energy Law. The revised draft spans 10 chapters and 76 articles, strengthening resource surveys and planning, development and construction oversight, production and transmission, market trading and pricing, consumption, innovation, safeguards and supervision, legal liability, and implementation of the green electricity certificate system. Industry coverage focuses on deleting the 17-year full guaranteed-purchase regime, replaced by a unified power market, a sustainable development price settlement mechanism, and consumption-responsibility weights backed by green certificates—with non-compliant users facing fines of 5–10 times the cost of certificate shortfalls. The same day Economic Daily carried a bylined piece by National Energy Administration vice minister Wan Jinsong: by 2030 renewable energy consumption should reach about 1.8 billion tonnes of coal equivalent, total renewable power capacity about 3.5 billion kW, and wind-plus-solar above 2.8 billion kW; non-electric uses should hit 150 million tce and renewable hydrogen 2 million tonnes, shifting renewables from “whether it exists” to “how reliable it is” and from supplementary to mainstream energy.

### 5. Five Ministries: 100+ Product Carbon-Footprint National Standards for Energy and Four Other Sectors by 2028 (Standards)
**Summary:** According to a November 9 roundup by 21st Century Business Herald, China’s market regulator and four other ministries issued an Action Plan for Building a Key Product Carbon Footprint Standards System (2026–2028). It targets energy, raw materials, consumer goods, machinery and transport equipment, aiming to release more than 100 product carbon-footprint national standards within three years, advance some in sync with international standards, and largely cover key basic energy and materials plus intermediate products with strong supply-chain leverage. The plan calls for faster standards on data monitoring and metering, factor calculation and quality management, disclosure, factor databases, data security and interchange, explores grading and evaluation, and studies how to include green electricity certificates in product carbon-footprint accounting.

### 6. U.S. Senate Holds Procedural Vote on Bipartisan Energy Permitting Overhaul (United States)
**Summary:** AP, Reuters and The Guardian reported on November 9 that the Senate took a key procedural vote on the Bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act (BAAJA), the first major energy measure since last week’s midterms. The bill would shorten federal review timelines for power plants, transmission, pipelines, mines and other large projects, require large data centers to bear more grid-upgrade costs, and amend statutes including NEPA—benefiting both clean and fossil projects. Democratic sponsors Martin Heinrich and Sheldon Whitehouse argued weekend changes strengthen tribal consultation, states’ roles in transmission planning and community aid on data-center impacts; SEIA said more than 300 solar and storage firms backed the bill and estimated it could add about 167 GW of solar between 2028 and 2035 if enacted. Earthjustice, the Sierra Club and the National Congress of American Indians oppose it, warning of weaker environmental and tribal sovereignty protections.

**Summary:** ess-news reported on November 9 that Portugal launched two competitive procedures allocating up to 1,050 MVA of Public Electricity Service Network (RESP) connection capacity to storage: 750 MVA for standalone battery energy storage systems and 300 MVA for renewable plants with co-located storage, with applications due by December 22, 2026. ### 10. CGN’s Ali CSP Plant Synchronizes: World’s Highest-Altitude Grid-Connected Solar Thermal Station (China · Projects)
**Summary:** China Science Daily and related outlets reported on November 9 that the 50 MW concentrated solar power unit at CGN’s Ali plateau “zero-carbon” solar-storage-thermal demonstration project first synchronized on November 6, becoming the world’s highest-altitude grid-connected CSP plant and Tibet’s first. Located in Gar County’s Shiquanhe Township at about 4,300 m elevation, the project totals roughly RMB 2.4 billion and 150 MW, with 100 MW of PV already at full capacity; the CSP block includes 14-hour molten-salt storage and a 205 MW molten-salt electric heater on a “PV + salt heater + asynchronous interconnection storage” pathway, capable of grid-tied or islanded operation. Once fully online it is expected to generate about 270 GWh a year and avoid roughly 168,200 tonnes of CO₂.

### 11. European Dunkelflaute Sends Day-Ahead Prices to Multi-Year Highs; Evening Slots Top €600/MWh (Power Prices)
**Summary:** Northwestern and central Europe entered a prolonged low-wind, low-solar “Dunkelflaute” from November 9, especially acute in Germany, the UK and Benelux and expected to last at least through Wednesday. French Epex day-ahead power settled at €334.12/MWh, the highest since December 2022; Germany settled at €345.64/MWh, with some evening quarter-hours above €600/MWh. With gas already expensive, gas-fired plants continue to set the marginal power price; December futures in Germany and France are near €200/MWh, more than double a year earlier.
