## I. Policy, Carbon Markets & International Climate Governance

### 1. COP31 Energy Day: Brazil presents fossil-exit roadmap; “35×35” electrification pledge sparks clean-power dispute (COP)
**Summary:** On Day 2 of COP31 in Antalya—Energy and Transport Day—negotiators focused on the energy transition. Brazil formally presented a voluntary national roadmap for transitioning away from fossil fuels, covering demand, production/supply, and economic consequences. UN Secretary-General António Guterres urged countries to produce national plans with timelines and financing needs and to show measurable progress by 2028; he warned that since the Middle East escalation, the EU alone has paid more than €100 billion in extra fossil-fuel import costs, with petrol and diesel prices surging in 130 countries—“changing suppliers will not eliminate this exposure.” Host Türkiye is pushing a “35×35” Global Electrification Pledge to raise electricity’s share of final energy use from about 23% (some briefings say just over 20%) to 35% by 2035; reporting indicates the draft does not require clean or renewable power, and some EU negotiators want stronger wording. A UNECE official said roughly 2,500 GW of generation and related projects worldwide are stuck in grid-connection queues. Türkiye and COP32 host Ethiopia also launched an “Electrify Now” finance coalition, with the World Bank set to co-chair next year’s work plan.

### 2. Global Carbon Budget 2026: Fossil CO₂ may fall 0.4%, driven by Hormuz oil shock—not a structural peak (carbon accounting)
**Summary:** The Global Carbon Project’s 21st Global Carbon Budget projects fossil-fuel and cement CO₂ emissions of about 38.2 GtCO₂ in 2026, down roughly 0.4% from 2025 (range about −1.5% to +0.6%). Oil emissions are projected to fall about 1.9%, gas about 0.3%, while coal rises about 0.6%. Authors attribute most of the oil decline to higher prices and supply constraints after the Iran conflict and Strait of Hormuz disruption; EVs account for roughly one-fifth of the oil-emissions drop. China, the United States, and the EU are projected to see fossil emissions fall about 0.9%, 1.3%, and 1.5%, respectively, while India’s rise about 4.4%. Including land-use change, total CO₂ is projected to rise to about 43.3 GtCO₂ (from about 42.8 Gt in 2025), with El Niño-linked peat fires and forest degradation in Southeast Asia; atmospheric CO₂ may reach 427.7 ppm. Researchers stress a geopolitics-driven one-year dip is “not a sustainable climate solution” and clean-tech growth is still too slow for a durable peak.

- [University of Reading — Fossil CO₂ emissions decrease in 2026](https://www.reading.ac.uk/news/2026/Research-News/Fossil-CO2-emissions-decrease-in-2026)

**Commentary:** A war-bent emissions curve is not a climate win—if oil prices ease and coal fills the gap, this year’s 0.4% will vanish like a mirage.

### 4. Xinjiang pilots first provincial hourly green-power trade with time-stamped tracing (China · markets)
**Summary:** China’s National Energy Administration reported on Nov 10 that the Xinjiang energy regulator guided the provincial power exchange in its first wholesale-side pilot of intra-province hourly green electricity trading, with 106 MWh cleared that day to support exporters’ green consumption, product carbon-footprint accounting, and green certification. Trading runs alongside conventional green-power markets across 24 day-ahead hourly slots, tracing green attributes to each hour; settlement follows a “certificate-power unity” principle via the national green-certificate system, refining the “power–certificate–carbon” market link. Officials said multi-year and hourly products will expand with operating experience.

**Links:**

- [NEA — Xinjiang’s first hourly green-power trading pilot](https://www.nea.gov.cn/20261110/2d88c447346d4cad9b11452b476ae93f/c.html)

**Commentary:** Hourly green power compresses “green” from annual reporting into time slots—for trade and carbon footprints, traceable granularity beats slogans.

### 5. Hainan climate white paper: clean energy at 88% of capacity; carbon peak before 2030 (China · subnational)
**Summary:** Hainan released its 2026 climate policy and action white paper on Nov 10. The report also stresses typhoon/flood adaptation, blue-carbon monitoring, and free-trade-port international climate cooperation.

### 6. U.S. EIA: AI and electrification push power demand to record highs in 2026–2027 (United States)
**Summary:** In its November Short-Term Energy Outlook released Nov 10, the U.S. Energy Information Administration projects national electricity consumption rising from a record 4,216 billion kWh in 2025 to 4,306 billion in 2026 and 4,371 billion in 2027—a third straight record year—driven largely by commercial demand including AI data centers and broader electrification. 2026 sales are forecast at about 1,546 billion kWh residential, 1,557 billion commercial, and 1,061 billion industrial. Electric power-sector generation may near 4,390 billion kWh in 2026 (+2.7% YoY), with most growth from utility-scale solar; coal’s generation share may fall from about 17% in 2025 to about 15% in 2026–2027, while solar rises from about 7% to 8%–10%.

## II. Clean Power, Storage & Project Pipeline

### 7. Global Rooftop Solar Declaration at COP31: 300 million solar homes by 2030 (distributed)
**Summary:** On Nov 10, Australia, Bangladesh, Kenya, Nigeria, Palau, and Vanuatu endorsed the voluntary Global Rooftop Solar Declaration in Antalya, aiming to at least double rooftop-solar households from about 150 million to 300 million by 2030. Thirty-four parliamentarians from 21 countries and Indonesia’s utility PT PLN also signaled support. The declaration prioritizes faster permitting, affordable finance, distribution-grid modernization, equitable access, and workforce training, and calls for household batteries. AP notes the pledge is non-binding; experts say subsidies, storage, and grid upgrades must follow quickly for real emissions impact.

### 8. Uzbekistan’s Nukus 2: Central Asia’s first large-scale wind-plus-battery project nears completion (projects)
**Summary:** Euronews reported on Nov 10 that Saudi developer ACWA’s Nukus 2 project in Karakalpakstan is nearly finished: a 200 MW wind farm with 100 MWh of batteries—described by the Asian Development Bank as a Central Asian first at this scale. Twenty-five of 26 turbines are already generating; the battery system is in staged commissioning, with full operation targeted by end-November. The roughly €232 million project includes ADB, AIIB, and FMO financing plus a 25-year PPA, and a new ~44 km transmission line. Once online, it is expected to generate nearly 728,000 MWh a year and serve more than 300,000 people.

### 10. Liaoning sets 2027 reliable-capacity compensation at RMB 370/kW-year, including grid-side storage (China · storage mechanism)
**Summary:** Industry coverage on Nov 10 of a Liaoning Development and Reform Commission notice dated Nov 9 outlines a generation-side reliable-capacity compensation mechanism at RMB 370/kW-year (tax-inclusive) for 2027, effective Jan 1, 2027. Coverage initially includes compliant public coal units, grid-side independent new-type storage, and some interprovincial imports, with reliable capacity assessed by peak-contribution and paid “by performance.” Duration-limited resources such as storage are prorated by discharge duration; costs are socialized across commercial/industrial load, storage charging, and related volumes. The aim is a common peak-capability yardstick once the spot market runs continuously.

**Links:**

- [Sina Finance / Polar Star — Liaoning reliable-capacity pay at RMB 370/kW-year](https://finance.sina.com.cn/roll/2026-11-10/doc-iniyfzhw2674393.shtml)

**Commentary:** As peak-valley arbitrage thins, capacity “base pay” decides whether storage survives—Liaoning is using policy to fill the market spread gap by putting independent storage on the same peak yardstick.
