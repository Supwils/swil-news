## I. Policy and Carbon Markets

### 1. IRENA says record renewable growth is not enough; grids, storage and efficiency lag (outlook)
**Summary:** The International Renewable Energy Agency published its 2026 World Energy Transitions Outlook on October 20. pv Europe reported that the outlook says record renewable additions still cannot carry the transition. Under its 1.5°C scenario, renewable power capacity would rise from about 5.2 TW today to about 18.4 TW by 2035 and 38.2 TW by 2050, while electricity's share of final energy would rise from about 23% today to 35% by 2035 and more than 50% by 2050. Director-General Francesco La Camera said efficiency is falling short, grids are struggling, and demand is still surging, so renewables cannot simply be added to an unchanged system. The outlook estimates global GDP would be about 1.1% higher each year than business as usual, a cumulative gain of about $47 trillion by 2050, and that more than $256 trillion of investment and spending would be needed from 2026 to 2050, about $10.3 trillion a year. Household incomes are expected to stay below IRENA's planned scenario until around 2035 while investment is front-loaded.

**Links:**

- [pv Europe — IRENA: record renewables growth is not enough](https://www.pveurope.eu/markets/irena-record-renewables-growth-not-enough-deliver-transition)
- [IRENA — World Energy Transitions Outlook 2026](https://www.irena.org/Publications/2026/Oct/World-Energy-Transitions-Outlook-2026)

**Commentary:** The report moves the bottleneck from "not enough capacity" to grids, storage and efficiency that are not scaling with it.

### 2. Germany's parliament debates a renewable-subsidy reform that would end small rooftop support (policy)
**Summary:** Green Central Banking reported on October 20 that the Bundestag is debating a reform of Germany's 25-year-old Renewable Energy Sources Act, with a vote expected in the second week of November. EU state-aid approval for the current subsidies runs until the end of 2026. The draft would end feed-in tariffs for new wind, solar and biomass, end subsidies for new solar systems under 25 kilowatts, and cap feed-in from smaller solar at 50% of installed capacity to push operators toward batteries. It would introduce two-way contracts for difference while keeping the target of 80% renewable electricity by 2030, up from about 60% now. Economy minister Katherina Reiche said support of up to about €17 billion a year cannot be sustained. The grid package would also scale back connection priority for new renewables and limit redispatch compensation in congested areas; redispatch currently costs about €3 billion a year. Aurora Energy Research said the savings still depend on auction bids, and that onshore wind is more exposed to the grid changes.

**Links:**

- [Green Central Banking — Germany seeks savings by scaling back renewable subsidies](https://greencentralbanking.com/2026/10/20/germany-seeks-savings-by-scaling-back-renewable-subsidies/)

**Commentary:** The 80% target for 2030 stays in the draft; what comes out is the fixed price for new projects and the subsidy for small rooftops.

### 3. The EU's new Arctic strategy keeps oil and gas "in the ground" but drops the 2021 legal-ban pledge (Arctic)
**Summary:** On October 20 the European Commission presented a new Arctic strategy whose four priorities are security and resilience, faster climate change, a more economically secure Arctic, and support for local communities. The joint communication says the EU's position remains that oil, coal and gas stay in the ground, including in Arctic regions, and that the Commission will work with partners to prevent hydrocarbon developments that threaten the Arctic ecosystem. The Commission said the Arctic is warming nearly four times faster than the global average. Reuters reported the same day that the text does not repeat the 2021 pledge to work toward a multilateral legal obligation not to allow further hydrocarbon development in the Arctic and contiguous regions, or to buy products from those developments. An EU official said that, five years on, that kind of multilateral decision is harder given the positions of the parties involved. Norway has asked the EU to drop its opposition to new Arctic drilling.

**Links:**

- [European Commission — An EU strategy for a rapidly changing Arctic](https://commission.europa.eu/news-and-media/news/eu-strategy-rapidly-changing-arctic-2026-10-20_en)

**Commentary:** The text still says oil and gas should stay in the ground, but the 2021 multilateral legal ban and the pledge not to buy those products are no longer in it.

### 4. Brussels considers offtake contracts for 250 million carbon-removal credits (carbon market)
**Summary:** Argus reported on October 20 that Fabien Ramos, the European Commission's carbon-removal lead, told the Carbon Unbound conference that offtake agreements are the main option for central purchases of at least 250 million permanent removal credits under the EU emissions trading system in 2031-40, and that the contracts would probably have to be signed before the programme starts. The July ETS review proposed auctioning 250 million allowances in 2031-40 to fund an equivalent volume of domestic BioCCS and direct-air-capture-and-storage credits, plus a reserve of 10 million allowances. Ramos said purchases might reach about 40 million to 50 million credits a year by 2040. The impact assessment puts BioCCS at €214 per tonne in 2030 and direct air capture at €428 per tonne. The Commission is also considering making credits issued under the carbon-removal certification framework before 2031 eligible, and Ramos said the first direct-air-capture project selected for Innovation Fund support was announced on Monday.

**Links:**

- [Argus — Offtakes are the main option for EU ETS carbon-removal purchases](https://www.argusmedia.com/en/news-and-insights/latest-market-news/2893150-offtakes-main-option-for-eu-ets-cdr-purchases)

**Commentary:** The budget money does not arrive until 2031, but the offtakes have to be signed years earlier, and that gap is the point.

### 6. Draft spot-market rules would let storage and virtual power plants trade (power market)
**Summary:** Caixin reported on October 20 that the National Development and Reform Commission published draft Basic Rules for the Electricity Spot Market on October 19, with comments open until November 18. The draft revises the 2023 trial rules and would take effect on January 1, 2027, for five years, after which the trial rules would be repealed. It would bring storage, virtual power plants, load aggregators, smart microgrids and direct green-power connections fully into the spot market, and would allow renewables to bid jointly with co-located storage. A notice carried by China Energy News on October 19 confirms that the revision is meant to unify the basic rules of a national power market and to standardise spot-market operation.

**Links:**

- [Caixin — Draft spot-market rules open for comment, and new participants get a defined role](https://www.caixin.com/2026-10-20/102495045.html)
- [China Energy News — NDRC seeks comment on the basic spot-market rules](https://www.cpnn.com.cn/news/xwtt/202610/t20261019_1921106.html)

**Commentary:** Storage and virtual power plants are written in as market participants in a draft, with a proposed start date of January 1, 2027, not a rule already in force.

### 8. The Innovation Fund selects 77 net-zero projects, including a 5 GW French solar factory (manufacturing)
**Summary:** pv magazine reported on October 20 that the European Commission on October 19 selected 77 net-zero technology projects for about €3.4 billion from the Innovation Fund, financed by EU emissions-trading revenue. The projects are in 23 European Economic Area countries, and the Commission said they could avoid about 290 million tonnes of CO2 equivalent in their first ten years of operation. French manufacturer Holosolis was selected for Solar4EU, a 5 GW integrated cell and module factory in Hambach, with production planned from 2027 and full capacity by 2030. Twelve clean-tech manufacturing projects together requested almost €895 million. Winners now negotiate grant agreements with CINEA, which the Commission expects to finish in the first quarter of 2027. Individual grant amounts were not published.

### 10. Sources say Britain will clear the Jackdaw gas field on Wednesday; Rosebank is still open (oil and gas)
**Summary:** The Guardian reported on October 20, citing multiple sources, that energy secretary Miatta Fahnbulleh has decided not to block the Jackdaw gas field and will announce that on Wednesday morning in Aberdeen with chancellor John Healey and North Sea Transition Authority chief executive Stuart Payne. Shell and Norway's Equinor operate the field, which the paper said would account for 6% of North Sea gas production when fully operational. Tessa Khan of Uplift said it would cover only about 2% of UK gas demand for roughly ten years. The Court of Session ruled last year that the original approvals were unlawful because they ignored emissions from burning the oil and gas; the companies resubmitted environmental consent applications last September. The BBC said Whitehall sources expect an announcement on Wednesday morning and that approval is widely believed, but that the decision has not yet been published. No final decision has been made on the Rosebank oil field, which the Guardian said is expected later this year.

### 12. Vrije Universiteit Brussel attributes 665 deaths in the June heatwave to human-caused warming (attribution)
**Summary:** Vrije Universiteit Brussel on October 20 released a rapid attribution study finding that human-induced climate change made Belgium's June 17-28, 2026 heatwave 3.9°C hotter. Of an estimated 1,181 deaths statistically linked to the extreme heat, 665, or 56%, would not have occurred without that warming. On June 26 the observed average daily maximum in Belgium was 36.0°C, against 32.1°C in the counterfactual. People aged 85 and over accounted for half of the heat-related deaths, and the climate-attributable mortality rate was highest in Liège, at 9 to 11 deaths per 100,000 people. The university said version 1 was published on October 16, uses established peer-reviewed methods, and has not itself been peer-reviewed, so the results may still shift slightly. The figure is not the same as the 2,018 excess deaths Sciensano recorded during and just after the heatwave.

**Links:**

- [Vrije Universiteit Brussel — Climate change more than doubled heat-related deaths in the June heatwave](https://press.vub.ac.be/climate-change-doubled-the-number-of-heat-related-deaths-in-belgium-during-the-heatwave-of-june-2026)

**Commentary:** The university says the report is not yet peer-reviewed, and 665 is a statistical attribution, not a case-by-case medical finding.

### 13. Storm Adam has killed at least five people, with 600 litres in 24 hours at Oliva (extreme weather)
**Summary:** Euronews reported on October 20 that Storm Adam, a high-altitude cold-drop system, caused severe flooding in eastern Spain and has killed at least five people, up from four in Monday's reports. The Civil Guard has evacuated more than 2,100 people in the Valencia region and rescued almost 500. Oliva recorded 600 litres per square metre in 24 hours. More than 100,000 pupils had classes suspended, and Monday's Balearic closure affected about 160,000 students. The regional government used mobile emergency alerts, which the report contrasted with the delayed warnings criticised after the 2024 DANA floods.

### 14. Indonesian peat fires keep burning underground, and the haze reaches Singapore and Malaysia (disaster)
**Summary:** The BBC reported on October 20 from Palangkaraya in Central Kalimantan that peat fires are still burning underground. Toxic haze has lasted for weeks across Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore and parts of the Philippines. The BBC said tens of thousands of people have fallen ill in Indonesia alone, more than 2,600 domestic flights have been cancelled, and schools have closed in Malaysia. It said PM2.5 was reported above 3,000 micrograms per cubic metre at times. Singapore said it would take legal action against companies linked to the fire spots, and students in Jakarta protested to demand that the fires be declared a national disaster. Suharyanto, head of the national disaster agency, said new peat hotspots can appear even if surface fires are put out the same day. Police in South Kalimantan tested pumping water through pipes driven about two metres into the peat; at one trial site the underground temperature fell from about 120°C to 30°C.

**Links:**

- [BBC — Indonesian wildfire haze chokes Singapore and Malaysia](https://www.bbc.com/news/live/c6y93098e795t)

**Commentary:** Surface flames can be put out while the peat keeps smouldering, so a dip in hotspots is not the end of the haze.
