## I. Oil Markets & Energy Security

### 1. Saudi Arabia temporarily shuts East–West oil pipeline after drones from Iraq (oil)
**Summary:** The Guardian and ABC News reported on Sep 12 that Saudi Arabia condemned drone strikes launched from Iraq against the East–West crude pipeline in the Riyadh and Medina regions, causing injuries and damage; an energy ministry official told the SPA news agency the line was halted as a precaution. Since the Middle East war began, Riyadh has expanded use of the Abqaiq–Yanbu route to bypass Iran’s closure of the Strait of Hormuz; IEA data showed Red Sea exports via Yanbu had risen above 5 million barrels a day by early June. Iraq’s prime minister’s office said a military commander was dismissed after probes confirmed the latest attacks originated inside the country, while Houthi pressure on Red Sea shipping and Saudi infrastructure has intensified.

### 4. U.S. Commerce finalizes steep AD/CVD rates on solar from India, Indonesia, and Laos (trade)
**Summary:** The U.S. Commerce Department on Sep 11 issued final Solar IV antidumping and countervailing determinations on crystalline-silicon cells and modules: India AD 123.04% and CVD 126.09%; Indonesia AD 94.36% and CVD about 73.2%–173.7%; Laos AD 65.43% and CVD about 82.03%–153.67%. pv magazine USA reported combined ceilings near 234% (India), 178% (Indonesia), and 103% (Laos), stacking atop existing executive tariffs rather than replacing them. Petitioners include First Solar, Hanwha Qcells USA, and Mission Solar. The ITC’s injury vote is set for Oct 14, 2026; an affirmative finding would lead to duty orders around Nov 2. Solar III orders on Cambodia, Malaysia, Thailand, and Vietnam earlier cut those countries’ U.S. shipments from about $12.2 billion in 2023 to about $1.3 billion in 2025.

## III. Clean Power & Storage

### 7. World’s largest salt-cavern compressed-air storage project completes unit startup in Jiangsu (storage)
**Summary:** CCTV News and Global Times reported on Sep 12 that two units at Huaneng’s Jintan salt-cavern compressed-air energy storage Phase II project in Changzhou, Jiangsu, completed full startup. Each unit is 350 MW, giving the facility the world’s largest total installed capacity of its kind and marking a breakthrough in core technology and system integration for large-scale CAES. The plant uses caverns about 1,000 meters deep, compressing air above 130 atmospheres in off-peak hours and releasing it to drive turbines at peak demand. It features Asia’s largest single-set thermal water-tank matrix for CAES—16 spherical tanks of 3,500 cubic meters each, totaling about 56,000 cubic meters—to recover compression heat, with design conversion efficiency expected above 70%. Its largest intake/injection well can handle about 2.16 million cubic meters of air per hour.

### 8. China MIIT clarifies mandatory PV standards set to phase in from 2027 (policy)
**Summary:** TaiyangNews / Now.Solar reported on Sep 12 that China’s Ministry of Industry and Information Technology issued an FAQ (dated Sep 7) on five mandatory national PV standards covering polysilicon and monocrystalline energy use, module and inverter efficiency, plus module safety and nameplate rules. Export-only products are exempt unless diverted to domestic sale; noncompliant products cannot be produced, sold, imported, or used in distributed or utility-scale projects. Energy-consumption and efficiency standards are due Jan 1, 2027; safety and nameplate standards on Jun 1, 2027. MIIT frames the rules as tools for market discipline and capacity management; industry expects faster retirement of inefficient lines. Firms are advised to plan production through the transition, clear inventories, and renegotiate long-dated contracts that straddle the effective dates.

### 9. Vattenfall takes FID on 254 MW / 1 GWh battery at Germany’s former Brunsbüttel nuclear site (storage)
**Summary:** Energy-Storage.news reported on Sep 11 that Vattenfall took a final investment decision on a 254 MW / 1,000 MWh battery at the Brunsbüttel nuclear plant site in Schleswig-Holstein, targeting commercial operation by end-2028 on 50Hertz’s 380 kV grid.
