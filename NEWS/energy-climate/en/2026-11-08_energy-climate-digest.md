**Summary:** COP31 runs November 9–20 in Antalya, Türkiye, with Türkiye hosting and Australia leading formal negotiations as representatives of nearly 190 countries gather. ### 2. EU–UK Near Carbon-Market Linkage Deal, Aimed for November 20 Summit (Carbon Markets)
**Summary:** Bloomberg reported on November 8, citing people familiar with the talks, that the EU and UK are in the final stages of a deal to relink their emissions trading systems, potentially to be announced at the November 20 leaders’ summit. Negotiators have largely settled emissions caps, supply controls and free allowances; financial-derivatives rules remain the last open issue. Linkage would allow mutual recognition of permits for compliance, deepen liquidity and eventually open a path to CBAM exemptions — though waivers would not be automatic and the UK must meet EU legal tests of full linkage and effective carbon pricing without preferential rebates. UK December allowances closed Friday at £64.81/t versus about €86.55 for EU benchmarks; from 2031 annual UK issuance would be capped at 7.1% of EU allowance supply, with a UK market stability reserve of at least 29.5 million allowances at entry into force. Ratification on both sides is still required and is packaged with SPS and youth-mobility deliverables.

### 3. China Puts Renewable Energy Law Revision Out for Comment: Guaranteed Full Purchase Scrapped, Prices Shift to Markets (China · Policy)
**Summary:** China’s National People’s Congress website announced on November 6 that the Renewable Energy Law (revision draft) has entered a 30-day public consultation; industry coverage on November 8 focused on the core redesign. The draft deletes the full guaranteed-purchase regime, calls for a unified national electricity market and orderly participation of renewables in power trading; market-traded renewable volumes would clear at market prices, while non-market volumes would still follow state price policy. It also bars local governments from conditioning project approvals on mandatory supporting industry investment or unauthorized deposits, endorses both utility-scale and distributed buildout with self-consumption and surplus export, and supports wind–solar bases, hydro–wind–solar integration and orderly offshore wind. The draft — ten chapters and 76 articles — was earlier submitted for first reading at the 25th session of the 14th NPC Standing Committee.

- [NPC — Renewable Energy Law revision draft submitted for deliberation](http://www.npc.gov.cn/npc/c2/c30834/202611/t20261102_458170.html)

**Commentary:** Ending guaranteed offtake turns renewables from policy-backed kilowatt-hours into tradable commodities — the next contest is market design, offtake obligations and flexibility, not capacity slogans.

## II. China’s Winter Supply & 15th Five-Year Transition Path

### 5. NDRC: Winter Heating-Season Energy Supply Adequate Overall as Super El Niño Raises Uncertainty (Supply Security)
**Summary:** Science and Technology Daily reported on November 8 that NDRC economic operations deputy director Tang Zhen said a super El Niño implies a warmer-than-average winter overall, but with more frequent cold snaps, sharp swings and possible extremes that will challenge energy security; on balance, heating-season supply can meet demand. By end-October, coordinated power-plant coal stocks exceeded 210 million tonnes — about 36 days of cover; more than 200 billion cubic meters of gas have been secured for the season, with underground storage filled as planned; national generating capacity topped 4.13 billion kW by end-September, up 420 million kW year on year. Peak daily gas capability exceeds 1.75 bcm, about 70 million cubic meters above last season’s peak; peak power load is projected near 1.52 billion kW, up about 90 million kW. NEA power department deputy Tan Hongjiang said extreme cold or primary-fuel stress could tighten local peaks, manageable via inter-regional transfers.

## III. Clean Power, Storage & Grid Bottlenecks

### 7. Europe’s Winter “Dunkelflaute” Warning: Solar Share Slips as Gas Peakers and High Prices Stand Ready (Europe · Grids)
**Summary:** Euronews on November 8 reported Europe risking its first dunkelflaute of the season — prolonged cloudy, windless spells that crush wind and solar output — as winter cloud cover dampens the solar boom and lifts power prices. Solar has saved the EU an estimated €42.9 billion in avoided gas imports since the Iran war began, yet its share of EU generation fell from 25.4% in June to 13.2% in October. Bloomberg forecasts cited German day-ahead prices above €300/MWh on October 29 — roughly double October’s average — with evening peak hours above €500/MWh. Multi-day dunkelflauten push the system toward expensive gas and other backup, exposing seasonal and flexibility gaps in a high-renewables grid.
