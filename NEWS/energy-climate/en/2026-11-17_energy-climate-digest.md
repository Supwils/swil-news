## I. Climate Negotiations & Science

### 1. COP31 enters its second week in Antalya: US absent for a second year, Iran-war energy prices loom over talks

**Summary:** The 31st UN climate conference opened in Antalya, Turkey on Nov 9 and runs through Nov 20, with about 190 countries attending; Turkey's Murat Kurum is COP31 president and Australia's Chris Bowen leads negotiations. The US sent no official delegation for the second year running. Under the "COP of Implementation" theme, climate finance (the Baku goal of at least USD 300 billion a year by 2035, within USD 1.3 trillion from all sources) and tripling adaptation finance are central; the Iran war has lifted oil prices and pushed some governments to rethink energy policy and add wind and solar.

### 2. Fossil-fuel transition roadmap fight: parties still split on naming fossil fuels

**Summary:** Per Dialogue Earth and others, Brazil's COP30 roadmap on transitioning away from fossil fuels was left out of the final COP30 text; nearly 90 countries agreed to draft a voluntary roadmap that sits outside the UN negotiating track. Vulnerable states and phase-out advocates want fossil fuels named explicitly, while producers prefer broader wording on emissions, finance and national circumstances. Host Turkey emphasises electrification, with its "35 by 35" pledge for electricity to reach 35% of global final energy consumption by 2035.

**Links:**

- [Dialogue Earth — The world agreed to quit fossil fuels, now it disagrees over the plan](https://dialogue.earth/en/energy/the-world-agreed-to-quit-fossil-fuels-now-it-disagrees-over-the-plan/)

**Commentary:** A roadmap outside the formal negotiations means the transition remains a voluntary list, not a binding obligation.

### 3. Global Carbon Budget 2026: fossil CO2 down 0.4%, but total emissions hit a record 43.3 Gt

**Summary:** The Global Carbon Project's 2026 budget, released Nov 10, projects fossil-fuel and cement CO2 down 0.4% to 38.2 GtCO2 (range -1.5% to +0.6%). Oil emissions fall 1.9%, the largest drop since 2020, driven mainly by high prices after the Iran conflict and Strait of Hormuz disruption, while coal rises 0.6%. Land-use emissions climb to 5.1 Gt on El Niño-linked peat fires and forest degradation, pushing total emissions to a record 43.3 Gt. China is projected -0.9%, the US -1.3%, the EU -1.5% and India +4.4%; the remaining 1.5C budget is about 150 Gt, under four years of current emissions.

**Links:**

- [Carbon Brief — Analysis: Fossil-fuel CO2 emissions set to dip in 2026 as Iran conflict hits oil use](https://www.carbonbrief.org/analysis-fossil-fuel-co2-emissions-set-to-dip-in-2026-as-iran-conflict-hits-oil-use)

**Commentary:** The dip was bought with an oil-price shock rather than policy or technology, and the authors themselves say a peak is still "a few years away".

### 4. WMO: Jan–Sep 2026 about 1.47C above pre-industrial; strong El Niño to carry extremes into 2027

**Summary:** The WMO's State of the Climate Update 2026 says the past 12 years were the warmest on record and the last four the four warmest; January–September 2026 averaged about 1.47C above pre-industrial levels, with sea level at a record high. Copernicus reports October 2026 was the warmest October on record (1.75C above pre-industrial), and 2026 is virtually certain to be the second calendar year above 1.5C after 2024. The WMO expects a very strong El Niño to intensify heatwaves, floods and droughts into 2027.

## II. Energy Markets & Policy

### 5. OPEC+ keeps December output targets unchanged; 2027 framework deferred to Nov 29 ministerial

**Summary:** A seven-country OPEC+ subgroup led by Saudi Arabia and Russia decided on Nov 1 to hold December production targets, continuing the pause in quota increases through end-2026. Middle East conflict continues to curb output among Gulf members; London crude touched about USD 118 a barrel the previous week, but physical prices have weakened as Strait of Hormuz shipments recovered to roughly prewar levels. Whether to restore another layer of output halted in 2022 depends on an ongoing capacity audit, and 2027 policy will be set at the full ministerial meeting on Nov 29.

**Links:**

- [World Oil — OPEC+ subgroup keeps oil production quotas unchanged for December](https://worldoil.com/news/2026/11/1/opec-subgroup-keeps-oil-production-quotas-unchanged-for-december/)

## III. Clean Power & Storage

### 7. Bloomberg: China's fossil power falls for a fourth month as solar and wind surge in October

**Summary:** National Bureau of Statistics data released Nov 16 show China's coal and gas generation has fallen four months in a row and is 0.1% lower year-to-date through October; in October large-scale solar output rose 31% and wind 34% year on year. Solar overtook coal in July as the largest source of power capacity, and coal supplied under half of generation in the first nine months, versus nearly 70% a decade ago. Bloomberg cautions that a late cold snap could still push full-year thermal output up; China targets 3,600 GW of wind and solar by 2035, nearly double today's level.

**Links:**

- [Energy Connects (Bloomberg) — China's fossil power heads toward 2026 drop as renewables rise](https://www.energyconnects.com/news/renewables/2026/november/china-s-fossil-power-heads-toward-2026-drop-as-renewables-rise/)

**Commentary:** The structural substitution is real, but whether fossil power falls for the full year is one cold snap away.
