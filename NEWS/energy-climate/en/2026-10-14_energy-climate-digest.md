### 2. The European Commission proposes a one-year delay to methane rules for fuel importers (Methane)
**Summary:** Brussels Signal reported Wednesday that the European Commission has proposed delaying by one year a set of methane obligations due to apply from January 1, 2027. The change covers monitoring, reporting, and verification for companies buying natural gas, oil, and coal from outside the European Union. Methane-intensity reporting is also pushed back, for EU producers as well as importers. Energy and Housing Commissioner Dan Jorgensen said climate ambition was unchanged, but shielding people from price swings and supply risk was now the priority. Fines can reach 20 percent of annual turnover. The text still needs approval from the European Parliament and the Council. The Commission said the delay does not change the regulation's ambition, integrity, or scope, and on October 12 it opened a consultation on emissions-declaration templates.

**Links:**

- [Brussels Signal — Brussels proposes delaying methane rules for gas and oil importers](https://brusselssignal.eu/2026/10/brussels-proposes-delay-to-methane-rules-for-gas-and-oil-importers/)

**Commentary:** What moves back is the start date for importer checks and intensity reports, not the 2024 methane regulation as a whole.

### 3. Australia introduces a gas-reservation bill aimed at 110 percent of domestic demand (Natural gas)
**Summary:** The Australian Broadcasting Corporation reported Wednesday that Resources Minister Madeleine King introduced legislation requiring producers to sell up to one-fifth of gas slated for export into the domestic market. The energy regulator would set the annual volume, with a target of 110 percent of Australia's actual needs. At the National Press Club, King said the government wants a modest oversupply to push wholesale prices down, but she called the modeling commercially sensitive and declined to say how far prices would fall. Regulator data put wholesale gas at about A$10 to A$11.50 per gigajoule. Australian Energy Producers, citing the competition regulator, said the marginal cost of new supply is about A$9.50 to A$13 per gigajoule. The opposition said it would work to pass the bill while still asking how future supply and investment will be assured.

**Links:**

**Commentary:** The bill specifies the domestic share and a 110 percent surplus target; the size of any price cut was not published today.

**Summary:** OilPrice reported Wednesday that PJM, which serves about 67 million people, met unseasonable heat in the middle of its fall outage season. ## IV. Climate and Disasters

### 12. BioScience report: seven of 16 climate indicators at record extremes (Climate)
**Summary:** Eos reported Wednesday that the seventh annual assessment of Earth's planetary vital signs was published the same day in BioScience. Of 16 indicators, global sea level, global ocean heat content, worldwide glacier thickness, the mass of the Greenland ice sheet, and the atmospheric concentrations of the three main greenhouse gases are at record extremes. The report says atmospheric carbon dioxide breached 428 parts per million in May, about 50 percent above pre-industrial levels. The warming rate rose from about 0.19 degrees Celsius per decade from 1970 to 2010 to about 0.36 degrees per decade from 2010 to the present. Worldwide consumption of oil, coal, and gas rose 0.9 percent in 2025, while renewable electricity generation surpassed coal that year.
