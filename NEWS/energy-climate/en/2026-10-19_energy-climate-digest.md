## I. International Talks and Climate Policy

### 1. The biodiversity summit opens in Yerevan with 22 of 23 targets off track (nature)
**Summary:** AFP reported on October 19 that nearly 200 countries pledged four years ago to halt and reverse nature loss by 2030, and a United Nations scorecard released ahead of COP17 finds that all but one of the 23 targets in the so-called Paris Agreement for nature are off track. Astrid Schomaker, executive secretary of the Convention on Biological Diversity, said the biggest gaps are in areas that require deep economic and social change, including subsidy reform. Despite a pledge to cut harmful incentives, about $2.6 trillion a year in public spending still goes to activities that damage biodiversity, including coal and gas extraction and industrial farming. The deal promised to reduce harmful incentives by $500 billion a year by 2030. China's environment minister Huang Runqiu told the opening that incentives harmful to biodiversity should be reduced. UN Secretary-General Antonio Guterres said subsidising fossil fuels is subsidising suicide. Armenia's environment minister Hambardzum Matevosyan, who is presiding over the political talks, said countries have a unique chance in Yerevan to correct course. The summit runs for two weeks, with up to 15,000 people expected. The Guardian reported the same day that the UK has reinstated about £6 billion of international climate finance over the next three financial years, with about a quarter expected to be spent on nature.

**Commentary:** The hardest number on opening day is not a new protected area; it is the $2.6 trillion a year still subsidising damage.

### 2. IPCC-65 left the assessment timeline open, and Monday's summary says the mitigation volume could slip to 2029 (climate science)
**Summary:** The Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change said on October 16 that Chair Jim Skea suspended the 65th plenary in Addis Ababa after the meeting ran past its scheduled end and many delegations from least developed countries and small island developing states had left. The suspension was meant to protect consensus decisions that include those delegations. The timelines for the three Working Group contributions to the Seventh Assessment Report, and the IPCC budget, remain open. The plenary is to resume at the earliest opportunity. The Earth Negotiations Bulletin summary published on October 19 says about 334 people attended, including 225 Panel members from 115 countries. Delegates narrowed the timeline options from seven to four and still could not close the gap between those who want all three Working Group reports in time for the second Global Stocktake in November 2028 and those who want at least the Working Group III mitigation report delivered in 2029. The Chair proposed adopting a 2027 budget close to the indicative budget considered at IPCC-63 so the Working Groups could continue, and some members objected. Skea said the engine room of the IPCC is working and is healthy.

**Links:**

- [IPCC — 65th plenary session suspended](https://www.ipcc.ch/2026/10/16/65th-plenary-session-of-ipcc/)
- [Earth Negotiations Bulletin — Summary of IPCC-65 (October 19, 2026)](https://enb.iisd.org/sites/default/files/2026-10/enb12901e.pdf)

**Commentary:** The suspension preserves the rule that decisions wait until small-island delegations are still in the room; the 2028 stocktake may still lack a full mitigation assessment.

### 3. Statistics bureau: clean power reached 36.8% of generation in the first three quarters, and energy use per unit of GDP fell 3.1% (energy data)
**Summary:** China Energy News reported on October 19 that the National Bureau of Statistics released first-three-quarter economic data at a State Council Information Office briefing that day. Spokesperson and deputy director Mao Shengyong said gross domestic product was about 107.1 trillion yuan, up 4.6% in real terms. Above-scale industrial crude oil and natural gas output reached 165 million tonnes and 196.7 billion cubic metres, both record highs for the period. Domestic gasoline prices rose 7.3% year on year, far less than international crude. Hydropower, nuclear, wind, and solar accounted for 36.8% of above-scale industrial generation, 0.9 percentage points higher than a year earlier. A preliminary estimate shows energy use per unit of GDP down 3.1%, and energy use per unit of industrial value added down 4.3%. New-energy vehicle penetration stayed above 60% for six straight months. Mao said Chinese new-energy passenger cars accounted for more than 60% of the global market from January through August. An international commodity price index rose more than 50% this year, with energy prices up 80%. Oil and gas extraction, petroleum processing, chemicals, and chemical fibres together added about 0.81 percentage points to the year-on-year rise in producer prices.

### 4. 270 companies ask the EU to keep binding renewable-hydrogen targets after 2030 (hydrogen)
**Summary:** Hydrogen Europe said on October 19 that an industry letter asking the European Commission to keep the binding targets for renewable fuels of non-biological origin in the Renewable Energy Directive now has 270 signatories. The letter is coordinated by Hydrogen Europe, the Hydrogen Council, and the Renewable Hydrogen Coalition, and the text is dated September 23 and October 19. It says Europe has more than €15 billion committed to hydrogen end uses, that investment grew 35% over the past year, and that more than 4 GW of electrolysis capacity is under construction, with the growth driven mainly by transposition of the transport targets. The letter estimates that meeting the existing 2030 targets is a further €50 billion to €60 billion investment opportunity and, on its assumptions, about 30 million tonnes a year of carbon dioxide savings. Signatories wrote to Commission President Ursula von der Leyen, Executive Vice-President Stephane Sejourne, and Commissioners Valdis Dombrovskis and Maros Sefcovic, asking that binding targets and mandates in transport and industry remain in the post-2030 framework. They say obstacles to the industry target should be fixed, and that fixing them cannot mean scrapping the targets.

## II. Renewables, Storage, and Projects

### 5. India plans central support for agrivoltaics, with about 5 GW possible in three to four years (solar)
**Summary:** Business Standard reported on October 19 that Santosh Kumar Sarangi, secretary of India's Ministry of New and Renewable Energy, told reporters the government will soon launch a central scheme to cover the 25% to 30% cost premium of agrivoltaics over conventional ground-mounted solar, and may fold it into an existing scheme when that scheme is revised. He said about 5 GW could be built over the next three to four years, depending on whether states notify feed-in tariffs. Whether the support is an upgrade of the PM-KUSUM farm-solar scheme or a standalone programme is undecided, and the ministry is working out the details with the finance ministry. Panels would sit on structures about four metres high so farming can continue. Sarangi put India's potential at 1 to 2 terawatts, with 16% more land required. He estimated power at 3.20 to 3.30 rupees per kilowatt-hour, against 2.80 to 3.00 rupees for decentralised KUSUM plants. He named Madhya Pradesh, Odisha, Chhattisgarh, West Bengal, and Uttar Pradesh, moderate-irradiance states, as more suitable, and said fruits and leafy vegetables responded well in pilots. The Agrivoltaics World Conference is set for October 21 to 23.

**Links:**

- [Business Standard — Government to launch an AgriPV support scheme, with 5 GW possible](https://www.business-standard.com/economy/news/govt-to-launch-agripv-support-scheme-5-gw-capacity-possible-mnre-secy-126101901005_1.html)
- [The Economic Times — The Centre may nudge states toward agrivoltaic projects](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/industry/renewables/states-may-be-nudged-to-go-for-agrivoltaic-solar-projects/articleshow/135054873.cms)

**Commentary:** The 5 GW figure is the secretary's estimate of what three or four years could produce; the scheme itself is still being negotiated with the finance ministry, and states still have to set the tariff.

### 6. France plans to let solar-plus-storage reach 25 MW by year-end, with exports still capped at 17 MW (flexibility)
**Summary:** Energy Storage reported on October 19, citing pv magazine France, that Maud Bregeon, the minister delegate for energy, announced a new set of electrification and flexibility measures. The government confirmed a plan, before the end of 2026, to let photovoltaic projects paired with batteries raise installed capacity from the current 17 MW ceiling to 25 MW, while exports to the grid stay at or below 17 MW, so the extra energy can be stored on site without a larger connection. The government estimates that better control of residential heating, hot water, and electric-vehicle charging could cut the related household spending by 35%. From 2027, heat-pump subsidies would depend on the equipment being controllable, with estimated savings of €150 to €200 a year for affected households. Newly installed chargers are intended to default to smart charging, with estimated savings of €70 to €100 a year. Those savings are government estimates. Industry statements include a Gimelec pledge to mobilise 6 GW of flexibility in commercial and service buildings, an EDF aim to control energy-intensive equipment for five million customers by 2030, and a Neoen plan to deploy 1 GW of flexibility by 2030.

**Links:**

- [Energy Storage — France confirms a 25 MW ceiling exemption for solar-plus-storage](https://www.ess-news.com/2026/10/19/france-confirms-25-mw-limit-exemption-for-solar-plus-storage/)

**Commentary:** The 25 MW is installed capacity; the grid still sees a 17 MW export, and the difference has to stay behind the meter in a battery.

### 7. National storage targets total 822 GW, 45% below the 1,500 GW COP29 pledge (storage)
**Summary:** pv magazine USA reported on October 19 that national energy-storage targets compiled by the think tank Ember total 822 GW by 2030, 45% below the 1,500 GW global commitment made by 74 countries at COP29, a gap of 678 GW. Before the Baku summit, national targets totalled 284 GW. The current figure is about 190% higher and still below BloombergNEF's forecast of 1,200 GW of global battery storage by 2030. Installed storage reached 430 GW at the end of 2025. Additions that year were 118 GW, of which batteries were 108 GW, 40% more than in 2024. China accounted for 54% of the additions and the United States for 16%. Keeping the 2025 installation rate through 2030 would bring cumulative capacity to about 1,020 GW, still short of 1,500 GW. China raised its 2030 target from 130 GW to 460 GW, including about 300 GW of new-type storage, mostly batteries, and 160 GW of pumped hydro. The European Union adopted a 200 GW target in June 2026. China and the EU together account for more than 80% of the national targets Ember identified. Meeting the existing national targets would require an average of 78 GW of new storage a year from 2026 to 2030, below what was installed in 2024 and 2025.

**Links:**

- [pv magazine USA — National storage targets fall 45% short of the COP29 pledge](https://pv-magazine-usa.com/2026/10/19/national-storage-targets-fall-45-short-of-cop29-pledge/)

**Commentary:** Battery installations are already running ahead of the targets most countries have written down; the 45% gap is in the policy numbers, not in this year's shipments.

### 8. Norway's Green Party proposes legal plug-in solar and batteries by the end of 2027 (distributed energy)
**Summary:** pv magazine reported on October 19 that six Green Party members of Norway's current parliament tabled a proposal asking the government, by the end of 2027, to remove restrictions so that plug-in solar and batteries meeting established safety rules can connect to ordinary grounded sockets. Such equipment is currently prohibited in Norway. The proposal also asks, on the same deadline, for a buying-and-use guide, a digital registry, and a national specification covering electrical safety, fire safety, grid conformity, and end-user use, and for a study by October 1, 2027, of a value-added-tax exemption for household solar and batteries. It cites an 800 W plug-in system costing about 5,000 Norwegian kroner as enough, in an emergency, to provide hot food and water. The Green Party is not in the governing coalition. The article says Norway's cumulative solar capacity passed 1 GW last month, but deployment is slowing and 2026 is set to be the lowest annual addition since 2021, especially on rooftops. Analysts attribute part of that slowdown to Norgepris, an optional fixed household power price that has weakened the case for rooftop solar. The energy regulator has separately proposed a simpler connection process for small renewable systems.

**Links:**

- [pv magazine — Politicians propose plug-in solar and batteries in Norway](https://www.pv-magazine.com/2026/10/19/politicians-propose-plug-in-solar-batteries-in-norway/)

**Commentary:** This is an opposition proposal; balcony solar is still illegal in Norway, and the deadline written into the text is the end of 2027.

### 9. Colorado approves the largest solar lease on state trust land, advancing a 120 MW project (solar)
**Summary:** pv magazine USA reported on October 19 that the Colorado State Board of Land Commissioners approved a land-use lease for the Mirasol solar project on 1,140 acres of state trust land in Pueblo County. At 120 MW, it is the largest solar project in the agency's history, and the lease revenue goes to the Public School Fund. The developer, Mirasol Energy Center LLC, is a NextEra Energy subsidiary. The site is about five miles south of Avondale. County land-use filings authorise a facility of up to 200 MW of photovoltaics and 200 MW of battery storage, connecting through a new 230 kV line at the existing Mirasol switchyard to the Public Service Company of Colorado grid, part of Xcel Energy. After local permit extensions and the state lease, commercial operation is scheduled for late 2028. Land Board staff said the project helps an administrative target of hosting 750 MW of clean energy across the statewide portfolio. The public-school endowment recently passed $2 billion in assets. The article also cites Solar Energy Industries Association market figures of 5.77 GW dc of installed solar and 2.3 GWh of storage in Colorado.

### 10. Shengli's million-tonne-scale low-concentration CCUS project breaks ground, with startup planned for the end of 2028 (carbon capture)
**Summary:** People's Daily, carried on October 19, reported that on October 16 China started construction at Shengli Oilfield of its first full-chain demonstration of million-tonne-scale capture, use, and storage of low-concentration carbon dioxide. The project is planned to capture carbon dioxide from the low-concentration flue gas of coal-fired power plants and use all of it for oil recovery and storage. The gas would be injected through more than 70 wells, covering beach-bar sand, turbidite, and shale reservoirs. A paired system of wind, storage, a direct line, and capture is planned with 13 turbines totalling 98.8 MW and 217 million kilowatt-hours of green electricity a year, supplied directly to the capture plant. Completion and startup are expected at the end of 2028. The report uses "million-tonne scale" as the project description and does not give a separate annual capture tonnage.

**Commentary:** What was published today is an October 16 groundbreaking and a plan to start up at the end of 2028; the capture volume is still stated only as million-tonne scale.

## III. Climate and Disasters

### 11. Storm Dana Adam floods Valencia and Alicante, with four people dead (extreme weather)
**Summary:** El Pais reported on October 19 that the high-level cut-off low Adam dropped torrential rain on southern Valencia province and northern Alicante and caused rivers to overflow, with four deaths so far. Pilar Bernabe, the central government's delegate in the Valencian Community, confirmed that a 79-year-old woman and a man who died of cardiac arrest were found on Monday in Oliva, and that two women died on Sunday, one on a beach in Sagunto and one in Cabrera de Mar near Barcelona. L'Atzubia recorded 700 litres per square metre in less than 36 hours. Official stations logged more than 530 litres between 21:00 on Sunday and 08:00 on Monday at Vall d'en Gallinera and L'Atzubia, and the Valencian meteorological association said some municipalities briefly exceeded 700 litres. Around 05:30 the Vernissa river reached 260 cubic metres per second, against a usual flow of about 20. The Serpis ran at 80 to 100 cubic metres per second between 05:00 and 08:00 and later fell to about 30. The Girona overflowed at Els Poblets and El Verger. The regional government ordered about 900,000 people in 145 municipalities to stay home, then cut the order to eight municipalities. About 100,000 students in the two provinces had classes cancelled. The state weather agency's red-alert threshold for the northern Alicante coast and the southern Valencia coast remains 200 litres in 12 hours, with possible totals above 300 litres in 24 hours.
