## II. Grids, Storage, and Carbon Capture

### 3. India approves Green Energy Corridor Phase III with 50 GWh of storage (Grid)
**Summary:** ETEnergyworld reported on October 4 that India's Cabinet approved the third phase of the Green Energy Corridor on Wednesday, aimed at congestion and renewable curtailment as solar and wind grow. The outlay is 1,86,405 crore rupees, about 1.86 trillion rupees. Energy-Storage.News, in an October 2 report, converted that total to about $19.3 billion. Of the outlay, 1,36,378 crore rupees go to intra-state transmission and 50,000 crore rupees to battery storage over seven years, with 54,082 crore rupees in central support to offset intra-state transmission charges. The scheme will add 51,126 circuit kilometres of lines and nearly 229 GVA of transformation capacity to evacuate up to 135 GW of renewable energy, plus 50 GWh of batteries to store surplus green power and supply it outside solar hours. More than 8,000 GWh of renewable electricity was curtailed in the first quarter of fiscal 2026-27. Santosh Kumar Sarangi, secretary of the Ministry of New and Renewable Energy, said about 70 percent will be greenfield projects awarded through tariff-based competitive bidding, to be built, owned, operated, and maintained by transmission service providers. The other 30 percent will be brownfield upgrades recovered on a cost-plus basis. Battery deployment will be tied to domestic-content rules, with guidelines to follow technical consultations. The last Phase I project is expected by the end of the current fiscal year, and Phase II projects by March 2028.

### 4. The Dongfang 1-1 carbon-injection platform is built, designed to store more than 1 million tonnes of CO2 a year (Carbon capture)
**Summary:** China News Service reported on October 4 that Offshore Oil Engineering, based in Tianjin, said the topsides of the new central platform at the Dongfang 1-1 gas field, China's first offshore demonstration of carbon dioxide injection to boost gas recovery, are complete and have entered commissioning. Once in operation, the project is designed to store more than 1 million tonnes of carbon dioxide a year in subsea formations. Dongfang 1-1, China's first self-operated offshore gas field and a carbon-rich field, has produced more than 50 billion cubic metres of gas since it started up in 2003. The platform weighs nearly 17,000 tonnes, stands taller than a 28-storey building, and has more than 10,000 square metres of deck. It will capture and purify carbon dioxide produced with the gas and inject it back underground under pressure. Yu Fasong, the project lead at CNOOC Hainan, said the scheme closes the loop from capture and purification to storage and enhanced recovery, uses a combined cryogenic distillation and membrane process to remove carbon dioxide from natural gas, localizes key equipment, cuts energy use by 20 percent versus conventional decarbonization, and raises the gas recovery rate by more than 12 percent. The topsides will be commissioned in Qingdao and then shipped to the South China Sea. The jacket is already installed in the Yinggehai basin. In service, the platform will be tied to 12 other platforms, including Dongfang 13-2 and Dongfang 29-1. Beijing News reported the same day that production is planned for 2027. The jacket has eight legs and 12 skirt piles, is 77.2 metres tall, weighs nearly 8,000 tonnes as installed, and was launched 113 kilometres from Dongfang city in Hainan. Installation of the topsides at sea is set for next month.

### 5. After support for Canada's Pacific pipeline, Pathways carbon capture still has no binding commitment (Carbon capture)
**Summary:** The Toronto Star reported on October 4 that, after the Liberal government backed a fast-tracked pipeline, the remaining box in the bargain with Alberta is the Pathways carbon-capture project, which is still not agreed and would not cancel the extra emissions from the new oil. Prime Minister Mark Carney said Thursday in Fort McMurray that Pathways would help cut 16 million tonnes a year, equivalent to taking 90 percent of Alberta's cars off the road, and would create an additional 40,000 jobs and more than $16 billion in GDP. The project, sponsored by the five largest oilsands companies, would move greenhouse gases by pipeline for underground storage. Alberta's own description of the pipeline estimated that the new production needed to fill Pacific Link could raise greenhouse-gas emissions by 15.5 million to 18.7 million tonnes before any Pathways reductions. Janetta McKenzie, director of the oil and gas program at the Pembina Institute, said there is no binding commitment and no real penalty for not building it. The agreement Alberta and the federal government signed with oilsands companies in July requires Pathways to be operating by 2035, two to three years after the pipeline is expected to enter service. When first pitched in 2021, Pathways was designed to sequester 22 million tonnes by 2030. The newer memorandum between Carney and Premier Danielle Smith requires only 6 million tonnes by 2035, plus another 10 million tonnes of reductions by 2045. The Oil Sands Alliance, Ottawa, and the province must produce a detailed construction plan by November 15. Natural Resources Minister Tim Hodgson told the Star that producers are unlikely to sign up for Pathways if they do not expect incremental production.

The same day, Governor Gavin Newsom signed Assembly Bill 40, which requires an environmental impact report before a large-volume bulk coal facility is built. The Trump administration announced a $75 million investment in June. Oakland Bulk and Oversized Terminal is still moving ahead after winning appeals last year. The investor report's "open-air bunkers" contradict earlier developer assurances of covered silos.

### 7. Courts find the EPA's grant termination unlawful, and Arizona and the Hopi may recover $181 million for solar (Solar)
**Summary:** The Arizona Daily Star reported on October 4, citing Cronkite News, that Arizona and the Hopi Tribe could regain about $181 million for solar in low-income communities after two federal judges ruled that the Trump administration unlawfully ended the Solar for All program. The EPA awarded nearly $7 billion in April 2024 under the Inflation Reduction Act. The Trump EPA terminated the program in August 2025, when an audit found only about $71 million had been disbursed. Federal records show Arizona received $42,000 of its $156.1 million grant, and the Hopi Tribe received $421,000 of $25.1 million. On September 18, U.S. District Judge Mary McElroy in Rhode Island ruled that money already promised was obligated and was not covered by the July 2025 law's repeal of unobligated funds. On September 22, U.S. District Judge Tanya Chutkan in Washington, D.C., upheld a challenge by Harris County, Texas, which stood to lose $54 million. Neither ruling directly ordered the grants restored. The EPA said it is reviewing whether to appeal. A May court declaration from the Governor's Office of Resiliency said the state expected the program to help 11,232 moderate- and low-income households save $165 million on electricity and cut 96,000 tons of carbon dioxide a year. The Hopi Utilities Corporation's grant was meant to bring electricity to about 600 homes on the reservation. A 2023 Department of Energy report said 35 percent of homes there lack electricity. Energy manager Fletcher Wilkinson said planning was finished and the tribe was ready to build when the funding stopped.

### 8. Heavy rain in Catalonia kills two people, with one still missing (Extreme weather)
**Summary:** Deutsche Welle reported on October 4 that flooding from torrential rain in Catalonia, in northeastern Spain, had killed two people by Sunday, with at least one person missing. Catalan firefighters said one person died when a wall collapsed at a campsite in Tordera, north of Barcelona, and another drowned in a flooded garage in Montornes del Valles. Rescuers were also searching in Pineda de Mar, about 52 kilometres north of Barcelona. Spain's AEMET weather agency said more than 100 millimetres of rain fell overnight in numerous locations. Civil protection warned people near the Besos River that it could overflow and told them to move to upper floors if water entered. Homes flooded and vehicles were submerged in towns near Barcelona, local rail service was disrupted, flights at Barcelona airport were canceled or delayed, and shops closed early. Regional leader Salvador Illa urged the greatest caution. The storm is linked to the pattern Spain calls a DANA, or cold drop. In October 2024, a related pattern killed 238 people in eastern Spain, mostly in Valencia. A study published in 2026 found that human-caused climate change intensified that rainfall. Deutsche Welle noted that the finding does not automatically apply to this Catalonia flood.
