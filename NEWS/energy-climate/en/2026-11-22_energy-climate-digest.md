## I. Climate Talks & International Cooperation

### 1. COP31 ends in Antalya with no cover deal as fossil-fuel wording and adaptation finance split the room (Policy)
**Summary:** The 31st UN climate conference closed on Sunday in Antalya, Türkiye, without adopting an overarching cover decision after two weeks of talks and two days of overtime. Oil-producing states pressed to drop “transition away from fossil fuels” language and elevate carbon-removal wording, while the EU, Belize, Panama, Vanuatu and others formally objected; adaptation-finance text also lacked concrete figures and a baseline against last year’s COP30 tripling pledge. UN Secretary-General António Guterres called the outcome “deeply disappointing,” stressing there may be no consensus in the room but “no ambiguity in the science”; COP31 President Murat Kurum acknowledged the text was not adopted. Attention now shifts to COP32 in Addis Ababa.

### 2. Parallel outcomes advance: ~70–100 countries join the “35 by 35” electrification pledge as methane and industry initiatives continue (Transition)
**Summary:** Despite the failed cover decision, Türkiye’s signature “35 by 35” goal—raising electricity’s share of final energy use to about 35% by 2035—drew support from roughly 70 to nearly 100 countries as a separate, non-binding pledge; critics note it does not require new power to be clean. E3G and others highlight concurrent moves: more than 20 countries releasing or announcing fossil-fuel transition roadmaps, a methane platform expanding to about 47 countries with roughly $480 million in acceleration funding, plus green-industrialisation and efficient-buildings partnerships. The EU also joined a Global Geothermal Ambition 2050-related pledge launched with IRENA.

## II. Policy & Carbon Markets

### 3. EU and UK finalise ETS linking: mutual allowance recognition targeted from 1 January 2028, paving the way for CBAM exemptions (Carbon market)
**Summary:** At their 20 November bilateral summit, EU and UK leaders announced the conclusion of negotiations to link their emissions trading systems, aiming to apply the deal from 1 January 2028 subject to ratification. UK, EU and already-linked Swiss allowances would be mutually usable for compliance, creating a larger, more liquid carbon market. The UK will expand ETS coverage including aviation and maritime, bringing over 10 million tonnes of additional CO₂ into the system; linking also creates the basis for future mutual Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM) exemptions. The European Commission says the link should cut dual carbon pricing and red tape for cross-Channel firms.

### 4. Spain’s data-centre renewable decree takes effect: ≥10 MW sites must hourly-match ~80% renewables with additionality (Policy)
**Summary:** Royal Decree 1032/2026, approved on 20 November and gazetted on the 21st, took effect on 22 November. Data centres with 10 MW or more of grid-access capacity must, until renewables exceed 90% of Spain’s generation mix, prove hour-by-hour that about 80% of consumption is backed by renewables and meet additionality rules—typically via Spanish self-consumption, storage or renewable PPAs of at least six years, with supplying plants generally commissioned no more than 36 months before the centre starts (with limited PV and other exceptions). Permitted but unconnected projects have six months from 22 November to show compliance or risk losing permits; severe breaches can trigger multimillion-euro fines.

### 5. U.S. Senate passes bipartisan permitting reform: faster energy buildout and data-centre cost responsibility (Policy)
**Summary:** The Senate on Thursday passed the Bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act (BAAJA) 62–33, shortening review timelines for power plants, transmission, pipelines and mines while requiring data centres to pay for the grid capacity they need; the bill now goes to the House. Backers cast it as a response to surging demand and electricity bills; green groups and some Democrats say it weakens NEPA and other safeguards. President Trump still says he will not approve wind turbines, yet the tech-neutral bill’s enforceable agency deadlines lead wind advocates to argue it could limit indefinite project limbo if enacted.

### 6. Karnataka draft lifts renewable purchase obligations to 43.33% by FY2029–30 for utilities and large users (Policy)
**Summary:** Reporting updated on 22 November shows the Karnataka Electricity Regulatory Commission’s draft Procurement of Energy from Renewable Energy Sources Regulations, 2026, would raise renewable purchase obligations for distribution companies and open-access consumers of 1 MW and above from 35.95% in FY2026–27 to 43.33% in FY2029–30 in annual steps. Compliance paths include buying renewable power, renewable energy certificates, or paying a CERC-set buyout price; wind and hydro RPO must come from projects commissioned after 31 March 2024. Non-compliance can trigger proceedings under the Electricity Act.

## III. Energy Security, Clean Power & Storage

### 7. Northern China fully into winter heating: Shendong coal surge, ~80 Xinjiang coal trains/day, over 200 bcm gas secured (Security of supply)
**Summary:** A 22 November Chinese government report says northern regions have fully started winter heating, with coal, gas and clean electricity working in tandem. Residual heat from Haiyang nuclear plant in Shandong serves about 400,000 residents in Yantai and Weihai; 19 mines at Ordos’s Shendong base produced 27 million tonnes since October; Xinjiang coal outbound trains run about 80 a day. Bohai Rim port coal stocks reached 28.03 million tonnes as of 18 November, up 540,000 tonnes year on year; more than 200 billion cubic metres of natural gas has been locked in for the heating season, with storage and pipeline peaking capacity further improved.

## IV. Extreme Weather & Climate System

### 11. Copernicus preliminary data: 20 November ~+2.12°C above pre-industrial—possibly the most anomalously hot day on record (Climate)
**Summary:** Scientific American reported on 22 November that preliminary Copernicus Climate Change Service data put global mean temperature on 20 November about 2.12°C above the pre-industrial average—potentially the largest daily anomaly in the instrumental record—driven by long-term human warming plus a blockbuster El Niño that may be among the strongest in a millennium. Researchers expect more days at or above a 2°C anomaly in coming months; annual means for 2026 and 2027 may both exceed 1.5°C even as the multi-year Paris threshold has not been permanently breached. The record landed the same weekend COP31 talks collapsed.
