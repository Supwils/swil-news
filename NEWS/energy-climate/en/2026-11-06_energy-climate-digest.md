## I. Policy & International Climate Governance

### 1. IEA Renewables 2026: Nearly 4,800 GW of New Capacity by 2031 as Energy Security Takes the Wheel (Policy)
**Summary:** The International Energy Agency on November 6 released Renewables 2026, forecasting almost 4,800 GW of new global renewable power capacity in 2026–2031 — nearly 60% more than in the prior six years. Solar PV is set to deliver more than three-quarters of that growth, followed by onshore wind. After energy-market shocks since the Iran war and the Hormuz crisis, faster build-out is expected across India, Southeast Asia, Africa and the Middle East; for the first time, additions in emerging and developing economies outside China are projected to match those in advanced economies. China remains the largest contributor, but its share is expected to ease from over 60% in 2025 to about 53%. India is emerging as the second-largest growth market, with total renewable capacity more than doubling by 2031. Renewables already supply about one-third of global electricity and are headed toward half; advanced-stage renewable and storage projects waiting for grid connection rose about 60% over the past year to roughly 3,000 GW.

### 3. EU and UK Move to Link Carbon Markets: Mutual ETS Recognition and CBAM Relief Eyed for Nov. 20 Summit (Carbon Market)
**Summary:** Euronews on November 6, citing a draft document, reported that the EU and UK are preparing to reconnect their emissions trading systems, with an announcement expected at the November 20 EU–UK summit in Brussels. The deal would allow mutual recognition of allowances and, subject to respective laws and procedures, exempt qualifying EU and UK goods from each other’s Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanisms. The EU CBAM took effect in January 2026; Britain plans its own system for January 2027. Without a link, manufacturers could face extra border carbon costs despite already paying domestic carbon prices. Supporters see relief for steel, aluminum, cement and fertilizers; critics warn UK carbon prices could rise further toward EU levels. Analysts note remaining differences in sectoral coverage, including transport and buildings, and that linkage would require Britain to keep carbon pricing sufficiently aligned with EU rules.

**Links:**

- [Euronews — EU and UK set to link carbon markets](https://www.euronews.com/2026/11/06/eu-and-uk-set-to-link-carbon-markets-in-major-post-brexit-deal)

**Commentary:** Post-Brexit reconvergence is landing first on carbon pricing — CBAM is forcing market linkage, and industrial-competitiveness fights will follow immediately.

### 4. U.S. Lame-Duck Permitting Reform Under Strain: House Democratic Leader Opposes BAAJA as Monday Senate Vote Looms (United States)
**Summary:** Inside Climate News and The Verge reported on November 6 that the bipartisan Bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act (BAAJA) faces a Monday Senate vote on whether to open debate, but Democratic midterm gains have shaken support. House Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries said Thursday he opposed the bill in its current form; environmental groups argue it weakens NEPA and related statutes, shortens reviews and narrows litigation avenues for both clean and fossil projects. Backers cite modeling that it could cut bills and roughly halve transmission build times; other analyses suggest larger fossil revenue and emissions upsides. Axios separately noted that Democrats barely campaigned on climate this cycle, focusing instead on affordability and electricity prices — a sign of the issue’s falling political priority inside the party.

### 5. China NEA: Push Renewable Hydrogen to 2 Mt by 2030; Q1–Q3 Hydrogen Investment Up 108% (Hydrogen)
**Summary:** At a National Development and Reform Commission briefing on November 6, Ge Ju, deputy director-general of the National Energy Administration’s development planning department, said China will advance hydrogen and green-fuel substitution in the 15th Five-Year Plan period and push renewable hydrogen scale to 2 million tonnes by 2030. Measures include green-power direct-connection and off-grid renewable hydrogen, comprehensive application pilots, and wider use in power, transport, chemicals/metallurgy and large-scale storage. Official figures show hydrogen-sector investment of 28.7 billion yuan in the first three quarters of 2026, up 108% year on year; more than 10 new renewable hydrogen projects started, with 100,000 tonnes of new operating capacity. Cumulative operating renewable hydrogen capacity exceeds 350,000 tonnes a year — over 50% of the global total — with more than 1.2 million tonnes a year under construction.

### 6. CGN Ali CSP First Grid Connection: ~4,300 m Elevation, Called the World’s Highest Grid-Tied CSP Plant (Project)
**Summary:** China General Nuclear on November 6 said the 50 MW concentrating solar power unit at its Ali “zero-carbon” PV–CSP demonstration project in Tibet made its first grid connection, becoming the world’s highest-altitude grid-tied CSP plant and Tibet’s first. The project in Gar County’s Shiquanhe town sits at about 4,300 m average elevation, with total investment of about 2.4 billion yuan and 150 MW total capacity; 100 MW of PV is already fully online. The CSP block includes about 14 hours of molten-salt storage and ~205 MW of molten-salt electric heaters on a “PV + salt heaters + asynchronous interconnection storage” pathway, with both grid-tied and islanded modes. Unit 1 connected today; Unit 2 is planned within the year. Full completion is expected to yield about 270 GWh a year for plateau supply security and heating.

**Links:**

- [China News Service — China builds world’s highest-altitude grid-tied CSP plant](https://www.chinanews.com.cn/cj/2026/11-06/10727382.shtml)

**Commentary:** High-altitude CSP’s value is less the altitude record than converting daytime PV into dispatchable heat — a replicable supply-security path for weak- and off-grid regions.

### 7. Shaanxi Draft TOU Reform: System Operating Fees to Float; Off-Peak Cut of 70% Could Lower Standalone Storage Charging Costs (Power Prices)
**Summary:** Industry coverage on November 6 focused on Shaanxi Development and Reform Commission’s draft notice on optimizing time-of-use pricing, which would fold system operating fees into the TOU float for commercial and industrial users. Public comments close November 13; the pilot would run from January 1, 2027 for one year. Off-peak system fees would fall 70%, with peak and sharp-peak uplifts of 70% and 90%. CNESA put Shaanxi’s November system fee near 0.136 yuan/kWh, among the highest nationally; at that level, a 100 MW/200 MWh standalone storage plant with 300 equivalent cycles a year could save about 5.7 million yuan annually from the off-peak fee cut alone. Shandong and Zhejiang are pursuing similar floats, making provincial rule differences a front-loaded investment variable for storage.

**Links:**

- [Securities Daily — Shaanxi TOU reform may open standalone storage margins](https://news.10jqka.com.cn/20261106/c681532816.shtml)

**Commentary:** Storage returns increasingly hinge on reading provincial pricing circulars — once hardware commoditizes, spread rules become the real permit to build.

### 8. ACT’s Williamsdale Battery Online: 250 MW / 500 MWh Completes Big Canberra Battery Program (Storage)
**Summary:** Eku Energy said the Williamsdale battery energy storage system in Australia’s Capital Territory has entered commercial operation at 250 MW / 500 MWh (about two hours), using Tesla Megapack 2 XL units. It can power roughly one-third of Canberra for two hours at peak, completing the territory’s Big Canberra Battery program. Eku — owned by Macquarie Asset Management and Canadian pension investor BCI — holds a roughly 15-year contract with the ACT for fixed quarterly payments covering system strength and FCAS, plus a revenue swap sharing NEM earnings; the asset is designed for grid-forming operation. Commissioning lands just ahead of the Southern Hemisphere summer peak.

## IV. Climate Risk & Science

### 10. Adaptation Finance Gap Pressures COP31 Agenda: ~$305 bn/Year Needed by 2035; Tripling Still Covers Only ~20–29% (Adaptation)
**Summary:** UNEP’s 2026 Adaptation Gap Report continued to shape pre-COP31 briefings via Carbon Brief, UN News and African negotiators: developing countries need about $305 billion a year by 2035 for adaptation, while international public flows from developed to developing countries reached only about $28.2–31.7 billion in 2024 — a roughly $1 billion rise on 2023. Even meeting the COP30 aspiration to at least triple flows (about $85–120 billion a year) would cover only about 20–29% of needs after inflation. Loss-and-damage fund applications totaling about $2.8 billion far exceeded the initial ~$342 million envelope. France 24 reported African negotiators want the “triple adaptation finance” pledge on the formal COP31 agenda; UN Secretary-General António Guterres warned the shortfall is measured not only in money but in “lives, livelihoods and lost futures.”

### 11. New Research: Dry-Soil–High-Pressure–Heat Feedback May Add ~10% to Mega-Heatwaves; Equatorial Pacific ~3.4°C Above Normal (Science)
**Summary:** New Scientist reported on November 6 that researchers including Kai Kornhuber at IIASA find dry soils, high-pressure systems and extreme heat can reinforce one another once temperatures cross a threshold, making extreme heatwaves about 10% hotter — helping explain mid-latitude extremes that have outrun climate-model expectations. Mid-latitude regions are expected to hit that feedback threshold more often. Same-day monitoring showed equatorial Pacific sea temperatures about 3.4°C above normal — among the highest weekly readings since 1981 — reinforcing El Niño. Parts of the U.S. Northeast saw temperatures roughly 7–10°C above early-November norms as Midwest low pressure pulled Gulf warmth north; Arctic sea-ice extent remained well below the long-term average.
