### 1. IEA’s Birol: COP31 collapse will slow electrification, but not halt soaring power demand (geopolitics)
**Summary:** International Energy Agency Executive Director Fatih Birol said on Nov 26 that the failure of COP31 in Antalya to reach an overarching deal will slow—but not stop—the global shift toward electricity. IEA research underpinned a goal of electricity meeting about 35% of final energy demand by 2035, which drew support from roughly 80 countries under Türkiye’s initiative, before talks collapsed over fossil-fuel language and climate finance. Birol said he was “disappointed” yet argued markets would feel limited impact because enough countries are already accelerating the transition. Forthcoming IEA figures are expected to show global energy demand roughly flat in 2026 amid Middle East-driven fuel price shocks, while global electricity demand still rose about 3%. The UN climate process reconvenes next year in Ethiopia.

### 2. EU and UK finalize ETS link: allowances interchangeable from Jan 1, 2028, paving mutual CBAM relief (carbon market)
**Summary:** EU and UK leaders announced the conclusion of negotiations to link the EU Emissions Trading System and the UK Emissions Trading Scheme, intending the agreement to apply from Jan 1, 2028 subject to ratification and entry into force. From that date, allowances issued in either system can be used for compliance in the other. The link covers electricity, industry, maritime, aviation and greenhouse-gas removals, with dynamic alignment arrangements; the European Commission’s climate directorate said it would create a larger, more liquid carbon market and provide a basis for mutual Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM) exemptions. The UK government estimates about £6.5 billion of UK exports could be exempt from EU CBAM and projects up to £3.8 billion a year in economic gains by 2040. The two systems remain separate but linked; the EU–Switzerland link continues.

### 3. U.S. EPA scraps most power-plant GHG limits as full rescission proposal splits states and health groups (policy)
**Summary:** Reporting on Nov 27 said the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency has partially repealed 2024 carbon standards for fossil fuel-fired power plants and is advancing a proposal to eliminate remaining power-sector greenhouse-gas rules. Administrator Lee Zeldin called it “the largest deregulatory action for the United States power sector in American history.” The proposal’s comment period closed with more than 165,000 submissions: attorneys general from 22 mostly Democratic-leaning states and several cities called it without basis in fact or law, while West Virginia and 24 mostly Republican-leaning states backed reversing prior efforts to reshape the power sector. More than 70 medical and public-health groups opposed the move; NRDC and the Sierra Club said they would sue. EPA said the action covers greenhouse gases only, not other hazardous air pollutants, and estimated roughly $310 billion in savings from the partial repeal.

### 4. Australia opens CIS Tender 12 for up to 5 GW of renewables, first requiring developer ratings (policy)
**Summary:** On Nov 27 the Australian government opened Capacity Investment Scheme (CIS) Tender 12 for about 5 GW of new renewable generation across the National Electricity Market, excluding New South Wales projects, which are steered to NSW Roadmap tenders. For the first time in a NEM generation tender, proponents must have started the Developer Rating Scheme assessment so landholders and communities can see developers’ track records. The round also includes a First Nations set-aside for projects committing to at least 5% First Nations equity or equivalent revenue sharing. Registration closes Jan 22, 2027, bids close Feb 5, and outcomes are expected in June 2027. Officials said the CIS planned tender program is nearing completion and will assess after Tender 12 whether a further 2027 generation round is needed.

### 6. Bangladesh makes solar-equipment recycling mandatory under new installation law (policy)

### 10. Arup: Spain’s capacity market alone won’t make batteries bankable, ~10% of revenues at best (storage)
**Summary:** Engineering consultancy Arup warned on Nov 27 that Spain’s capacity market, launched in June 2026, will not by itself make battery energy storage systems bankable. Renewables supplied 55.5% of Spanish electricity in 2025, yet about 5.4 TWh of potential clean generation could not be integrated, while balancing and adjustment services added a record €15.78/MWh on average to final power prices. Arup estimates the capacity market may cover only about 10% of a battery project’s total income, so developers still need credible stacked revenues from wholesale arbitrage, balancing markets and congestion management. The report maps four ownership and remuneration scenarios to 2036 and notes Spain advanced about 1.9 GW of storage permits in Q3 2026, with some standalone portfolios reaching financial close ahead of the first capacity auctions.

## III. Climate Monitoring & Energy Security

### 12. Global average temperature stays above +2°C for seven straight days—first such stretch on record (climate)
**Summary:** Xinhua reported on Nov 27, citing dpa and the EU’s Copernicus Climate Change Service, that global average temperatures from Nov 18–24 stayed more than 2°C above the 1850–1900 average for the same calendar days—the first seven-day stretch on record, after several other days this month also crossed the mark. Scientists stressed that a week of daily anomalies does not mean the Paris Agreement’s long-term 2°C limit has been breached, though Copernicus strategic lead Samantha Burgess called it “a stark preview of where our climate is headed.” Analysts link the spell to long-term greenhouse-gas warming plus a strong El Niño; the World Meteorological Organization has warned the current event could become among the strongest on record, with impacts lasting into early 2027.
