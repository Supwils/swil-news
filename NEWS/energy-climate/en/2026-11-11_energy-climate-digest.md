## I. Policy, Carbon Markets & International Climate Governance

### 1. COP31 Leaders’ Summit opens: Guterres floats “climate justice compact,” urges taxes on oil majors (COP)
**Summary:** Day 3 of COP31 in Antalya moved into the Leaders’ Summit. UN Secretary-General António Guterres said it is “make or bake time” and proposed a climate justice compact: national plans to accelerate the fossil-fuel exit while protecting workers and communities; stronger early warning and adaptation; affordable climate finance; and cooperation to overcome trade and technology barriers. He contrasted roughly $95 billion in one-quarter profits at the world’s nine largest oil companies with about $600 million reaching the loss-and-damage fund—equivalent to about 14 hours of those profits—calling for windfall taxes and declaring that “fossil fuels must go the way of the dinosaurs” because “the dinosaurs did not have a lobby.” On Zero Waste Day he urged UNEP to develop a global near-zero methane standard for oil and gas; UNEP said more than 5,000 satellite alerts since 2023 have drawn responses in fewer than about one in eight cases. Brazil’s President Lula warned that wars and tariffs are crowding out the climate agenda; inside the talks, adaptation finance, loss and damage, the mitigation work programme, and a just-transition mechanism remain unresolved.

### 2. “35×35” electrification pledge wins endorsements; clean-power wording remains a flashpoint (policy)
**Summary:** At the Nov 11 Leaders’ Summit, a group of countries endorsed the Turkish presidency’s global electrification pledge to raise electricity’s share of final energy use from about 23% to 35% by 2035. The World Resources Institute welcomed the move and urged credible electrification and clean-energy roadmaps with grids, storage, and demand-side electrification. ABC and others report the draft does not require clean or renewable power, saying pathways “will differ according to different starting points”; analysts estimate that meeting the target with clean power only could cut projected warming of at least about 2.6°C under current policies by roughly 0.3°C. Australia continues to back a voluntary push to at least double rooftop-solar households to 300 million by 2030.

**Commentary:** Electrification is necessary but not sufficient—without a clean-power constraint, 35% can become a legal cover for coal- and gas-fired electrification.

### 3. China, Türkiye and Ethiopia launch high-quality carbon-credit initiative; CBGEX and IETA sign MoU (carbon markets)
**Summary:** On Nov 11 in Antalya, China joined COP31 host Türkiye and COP32 host Ethiopia to launch the Initiative for Enhancing International Cooperation on High-Quality Carbon Crediting Markets at the China Pavilion. It calls on governments to strengthen high-integrity governance, on mechanisms and market participants to expand high-quality supply, on users to consume credits responsibly, and on parties to deepen capacity-building, information sharing, and infrastructure interconnection. The platform is voluntary and non-binding; it does not itself authorize Article 6 transfers or require mutual recognition, market/registry linkage, data sharing, or funding. China’s Li Gao said international demand for high-quality credits keeps rising. The same day, IETA and the China Beijing Green Exchange signed an MoU to advance CCER internationalisation and high-integrity practices.

### 4. OGCI side-event report: oil and gas low-carbon investment falls about $6 billion in 2025 (oil & gas transition)
**Summary:** The Oil and Gas Climate Initiative released its 2026 Status Report: Strengthening Delivery on the sidelines of COP31. ### 5. Tsinghua carbon-neutrality tech roadmap: zero-carbon power plus electrification ~71%; ~RMB 180 trillion cumulative investment (China · technology)
**Summary:** On Nov 11 in Beijing, Tsinghua University and dozens of partners released China Carbon Neutrality Technology Roadmap Outlook. The study maps six technology classes and more than 300 key options, estimating more than 12 GtCO₂ of abatement contribution by 2060. Zero-carbon power supply plus end-use electrification account for about 71% of that contribution; zero-carbon heat and sustainable fuels, low-carbon fuel/feedstock substitution, and carbon storage/negative-emissions options together cover about 23% in hard-to-abate areas. Cumulative investment needs for carbon-neutrality technologies through 2060 are put at about RMB 180 trillion. Under an ambitious deployment path, energy and industrial-process CO₂ around 2035 could fall to about 10 Gt (roughly 13% below 2025), with wind-and-solar capacity near 3,960 GW and NEV sales share about 79%. The authors warn nearly half of key technologies remain at pilot/demo stage, risking supply and investment gaps in a 30-year window.

**Commentary:** The report rhymes with Antalya’s electrification narrative—China has made “electrons” the backbone of net zero; the bottleneck is commercialising pilots, not lengthening concept lists.

## II. Clean Power, Storage & Project Updates

### 6. BNEF: AI data centres plan ~181 GW of on-site gas power in the US and Europe (power · compute)
**Summary:** The Guardian on Nov 11 cited BloombergNEF research finding that planned AI data centres across the United States, the UK and mainland Europe propose on-site gas plants totaling about 181 GW—nearly four times Great Britain’s peak electricity demand—because of grid-connection delays. If built, the US share (about 90% of the on-site gas plans) could emit roughly 345 MtCO₂e a year, comparable to about 72.8 million fossil-fuel cars. The “speed to power” push risks locking compute growth into fossil generation even as grids and clean dispatch lag.

**Links:**

- [The Guardian — AI data centres plan huge on-site gas power stations](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/nov/11/ai-datacentres-on-site-gas-fired-power-stations)

**Commentary:** Interconnection queues are harder than climate pledges—if compute does not queue for clean power first, it will rebuild mini fossil empires behind campus fences.

### 9. UK rejects Oxfordshire’s 840 MW Botley West solar over Green Belt, heritage and aviation risks (Europe)
**Summary:** In a decision dated Nov 10 and widely covered on Nov 11, the UK government refused consent for Botley West—one of western Europe’s largest proposed solar farms at about 840 MW across more than 2,000 acres and roughly £800–850 million. Grounds included substantial Green Belt and heritage harm (including Blenheim Palace World Heritage Site), significant landscape and visual effects, unacceptable aviation-safety risks, and inadequate assessment/mitigation for best and most versatile farmland. The developer has six weeks to seek judicial review. Observers call it a rare “no” on the solar NSIP pathway, contrasting with recent gas-field approvals.

## III. Climate Disasters & Scientific Warnings

### 10. State of Wildfires 2025–2026: Iberian burned area about three times larger due to climate change (attribution)
**Summary:** The Met Office, ECMWF and partners launched the State of Wildfires 2025–2026 report during COP31. It finds 2025 set a record for EU burned area; Iberia saw 14 megafires and more than 40,000 evacuations, Spain’s highest burned area since 2002, and Portugal’s largest single wildfire on record. Attribution analysis indicates human-caused climate change made extreme fire weather in northwest Iberia about three times more likely and burned area about three times larger; Canada’s multipliers were about 10× and 9×, Chile about 2×. Under high emissions, Europe-like 2025 fires could burn around one-third more area within 20 years and far more by century’s end; deep cuts could substantially limit that worsening.

### 11. Super El Niño nears its peak as floods and droughts hit multiple regions (climate context)
**Summary:** People’s Daily Online and other Nov 11 briefings say the WMO expects the current super El Niño to peak around year-end; China’s National Climate Center reports eastern Pacific SST anomalies already above 3°C, with a possible peak near 3.2–3.5°C. Central-northern Chile saw century-scale rains while the Atacama received persistent rainfall; Southeast Asia faces cross-border haze and power/public-health alerts, and Papua New Guinea has declared a drought-linked humanitarian emergency. Weekly extreme-weather tallies also note deadly floods in Corsica, Gaza and Iraq, multi-day heavy rain in central Vietnam, and Storm Charles red warnings and urban flooding in Portugal. A strong El Niño on an already warmer baseline is sharpening the urgency of adaptation and loss-and-damage finance inside COP31.
