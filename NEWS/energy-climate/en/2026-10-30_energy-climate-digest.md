### 2. China’s NEA: solar overtakes coal as top capacity source; new-type storage hits 167 GW (China)
**Summary:** At its Oct 30 briefing, China’s National Energy Administration said domestic solar capacity historically surpassed coal in July; by end-September solar stood near 1.31 billion kW, coal’s share of generation fell to about 49.1%, and clean energy supplied over 80% of incremental generation. New-type storage in operation reached about 167 GW / 435 GWh, up roughly 63% year on year. January–September electricity use totaled 8,073.4 TWh (+3.9%), with installed capacity near 4.13 billion kW and a summer peak load of about 1.557 billion kW. Officials said three batches of desert-gobi-wilderness bases have built more than 157 GW, with 14 multi-gigawatt export-base plans approved. Winter supply is expected to balance overall, though Hormuz-linked fuel shipping stress and extreme cold could tighten local peaks; compute-power demand may add over 100 TWh a year in the 15th Five-Year Plan period and reach about 800 TWh by 2030.

### 3. Poland approves first fast-track renewable zones, aiming to cut prep time to 1–2 years (Policy)
**Summary:** Notes from Poland reported that Wielkopolska’s regional assembly this week adopted the country’s first Accelerated Renewable Energy Development Zones (OPRO), covering about 105.4 hectares across Złotów, Lwówek, Rawicz and Słupca with technical solar potential of roughly 66–80 MW. Under the EU’s revised Renewable Energy Directive (RED III), onshore permitting in such zones should take no more than 12 months; the environment ministry estimates project preparation could fall from three-to-four years to one-to-two, with environmental reviews capped at 45 days for projects of at least 150 kW (versus up to about 150 days today). Member states were to designate first zones by 21 February 2026, so Poland is several months late. Coal still supplies about half of Polish power, though renewables rose from about 14.7% of the mix in 2021 to about 29.4% in 2025.

**Links:**

- [Notes from Poland — Poland approves first fast-track renewable energy zones](https://notesfrompoland.com/2026/10/30/poland-approves-first-fast-track-renewable-energy-zones/)

**Commentary:** The first parcels are modest; pre-cleared permitting is the real signal for a coal-heavy system chasing RED III.

### 4. DOE opens ~$237 million Grid SCALED FOA to onshore transformer and grid-component supply (Supply chain)
**Summary:** The U.S. Department of Energy on Oct 30 announced a about $237 million funding opportunity under Grid SCALED to expand domestic manufacturing and supply chains for critical grid components such as distribution and power transformers. DOE estimates about $125 million a year in utility savings and about $50 million a year retained in the U.S. economy versus foreign suppliers. Up to $50 million supports refurbishment and reuse of existing equipment; up to $187 million backs U.S.-sourced alternative materials and subcomponents. It is the first tranche of a Grid Supply Chain Program authorized for up to $375 million. Concept papers are due Nov 20, 2026, and full applications by Feb 8, 2027; further rounds may cover solid-state transformers and common configurations.

### 8. Tamil Nadu adopts 2026 BESS policy with 100% transmission and wheeling charge waivers (Storage)
**Summary:** The New Indian Express reported Oct 30 that Tamil Nadu’s cabinet approved Battery Energy Storage System Policy 2026 for projects of 1 MW and above. Standalone systems may connect to T&D or private networks for peak support and grid services; colocated systems store output from solar, wind-solar hybrid or other RE plants for peak or as-needed discharge. Mercom’s same-day wrap noted India’s Q3 policy and tender focus on grid stability and storage, including Gujarat’s approval of tariffs for about 450 MW / 900 MWh of standalone BESS and a new SECI multi-GWh ESS tender.

### 11. Researchers: October on track for ~1.76°C above preindustrial—hottest October on record (Climate)
**Summary:** Scientific American reported Oct 30 that projections by Andrew Dessler and Zeke Hausfather, based on Copernicus Climate Change Service (C3S) data, put October 2026 global temperatures near 1.76°C above preindustrial levels—above the prior October record of about 1.7°C in 2023—making this month almost certain to be the hottest October on record and lifting odds that 2026 ranks among the hottest years. Hausfather said the record October signals a “Super El Niño” kicking in, with a series of record months likely culminating in a record 2027. The piece noted 2026 has already logged the hottest U.S. September, hottest first eight months and hottest 12-month stretch on record, plus the hottest global ocean temperatures observed.

### 12. Lancet Countdown warns accelerating climate health harms; heat deaths up nearly 36% (Health)
**Summary:** WHO coverage of the 2026 Lancet Countdown on Health and Climate Change, led by University College London with WHO collaboration, finds many climate-related health harms still outrunning the global response a decade after Paris entered into force. Average annual heat-related deaths rose nearly 36% in the years after the agreement versus the prior decade; heat was linked to about 710,000 deaths in 2023 alone, with roughly 71% of that burden attributed to human-caused climate change. Extreme drought affected more than half of the world’s land for at least a month in 2025—rising about 704% faster than a decade earlier—and heat exposure cost about 573 billion potential labour hours. The report also notes record ~$2.1 trillion clean-energy investment in 2025 and calls for treating climate change as a global health emergency, embedding health across mitigation, adaptation and finance.

**Links:**

- [WHO — Lancet Countdown warns climate change is a growing health emergency](https://www.who.int/news/item/28-10-2026-climate-change-is-a-growing-health-emergency--lancet-countdown-warns)
