## I. Policy and Supply

### 1. Sri Lanka's energy minister says renewables may have to exceed 70% after the oil shock (policy)
**Summary:** AFP published an interview on October 25 with Sri Lanka's energy minister, Anura Karunathilaka: the country imports all of its oil and gas, the ministry says the government has at times spent up to 30% of foreign-exchange reserves in a single month on fossil fuels, and the 2030 renewable share of 70% (about 60% now, including about 40% hydropower) will probably have to be exceeded, with 90% to 95% possible in 10 to 15 years even though coal and naphtha cannot yet be fully replaced. Transmission and grid control remain binding: a six-hour nationwide blackout this month may have followed solar output exceeding demand, solar capacity is hoped to reach nearly 4,400 MW by 2029, up 388% from 2024, and the minister will propose ending the rule that lets producers export by day and withdraw the same energy free at night, a cost now borne by the state. Wind potential is estimated at 30 GW, only projects already approved or under construction will proceed at Mannar, and five major transmission lines are planned from next year.

**Commentary:** An importing country is raising its renewable share, and the binding constraints are transmission, midday surplus, and how power taken back at night is priced.

### 2. South Korea plans renewable targets for public agencies that are not utilities (policy)
**Summary:** The Seoul Economic Daily reported on October 25 that the Ministry of Climate, Energy and Environment opened for comment a partial amendment to the enforcement decree of the renewable-energy promotion act, extending capacity targets from next year to public agencies as well as generators, including agencies whose main business is not power, in support of 100 GW by 2030. The change follows the full replacement of the Renewable Portfolio Standard by fixed-price contracts. The old standard required generators of at least 500 MW, including KEPCO and its generation subsidiaries, to build renewable plants or buy certificates. The new targets cover capacity an agency installs itself and idle land it provides to developers, which brings in bodies such as the Korea Expressway Corporation and KORAIL.

**Links:**

- [Seoul Economic Daily — Korea to set renewable targets for public agencies](https://en.sedaily.com/finance/2026/10/25/korea-to-set-renewable-energy-targets-for-public-agencies)

**Commentary:** After the portfolio standard ends, idle land becomes part of the scorecard, so highway and rail agencies are pulled into the 100 GW goal for 2030.

### 3. India's revised transmission compensation: tower bases at 200% of market value (transmission)
**Summary:** Mint on October 25 explained the power ministry's October 6 guidelines for inter-state transmission compensation: where circle rates sit below market value, a local committee prices land from three valuers within 21 days, normalizing the high and low figures to within 20% of the median if needed; tower bases are paid once at 200% of market value, corridors at 30% in rural areas, 45% in municipal planning areas and 60% in municipal corporations, and an appeal within 30 days must still allow work to start. Citing Ember, the paper said transmission constraints cost 300 GWh of clean electricity in the first quarter of 2026, including 34 GWh on March 30, while Central Electricity Authority data show 4,301 circuit kilometres built from April through August of fiscal 2027 against a target of 7,403. The cabinet last month approved Green Energy Corridor phase III, able to evacuate up to 135 GW and including 50 GWh of batteries through fiscal 2033, at 1.86 trillion rupees, including 54,082 crore rupees of central support, about 540.8 billion rupees.

### 5. Williams files the 88-mile Silver Spur gas pipeline with FERC (natural gas)
**Summary:** Planetizen reported on October 25 that Williams last month filed with the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission for Silver Spur, an 88-mile gas line between existing pipes in Idaho and Nevada that, if approved, would carry up to 275 million additional standard cubic feet a day into the Northwest, with completion planned for 2030. It sits inside a larger Rockies Columbia Connector that also includes a nearly 160-mile expansion along the Washington side of the Columbia River and a 137-mile Valley Trail line through Grant County and the Quincy data-center hub; those two segments are not yet filed, the combined buildout could add nearly 1 billion cubic feet a day by late 2030, and the existing Northwest Pipeline system is about 4,000 miles long. Environmental groups say the new electricity demand is coming almost entirely from data centers, and an ECOnorthwest analysis puts data centers at nearly a quarter of Oregon's power use. A Williams spokesperson said on Thursday that the company does not disclose customers and that gas remains critical to reliability as renewables and demand grow.

## II. Renewables, Storage and Manufacturing

### 6. Economic Observer: standalone storage leaves mandatory pairing, and dispatch rates start to set capacity payments (storage)
**Summary:** The Economic Observer reported on October 25 that Document 136, published in February 2025, bars authorities from making storage a precondition for approving, connecting, or exporting power from new renewable projects, so storage can be registered on its own. The China Energy Storage Alliance's October 14 count, described as incomplete, put new novel storage in the first three quarters at 35.30 GW and 102.31 GWh, with grid-side standalone storage at 85.3% of September's new power capacity. Shaanxi's first grid-side list, published in late June, has 15 projects totaling 2.02 million kilowatts and 6.08 million kilowatt-hours; a construction list is not a settlement list, six hours is the baseline for capacity payments, shorter systems are prorated, and new filings are moving toward four and six hours. A provincial dispatcher at China Southern Power Grid said some connected projects have had capacity payments discounted for missing utilization targets, though the share is still low, and integrated stations enter a 96-point schedule with output sampled every five minutes.

**Links:**

- [Economic Observer — After mandatory pairing is loosened, storage has to stand on its own](http://www.eeo.com.cn/2026/1025/1075484.shtml)

**Commentary:** Standalone storage can be permitted on its own, and the capacity payment still depends on the project list, the grid connection, and the dispatch score.

### 11. An unusually large multi-state storm hits eastern Australia, with eight flood warnings in southern Queensland (extreme weather)
**Summary:** The Guardian reported on October 25 that the Bureau of Meteorology issued a severe thunderstorm warning on Sunday afternoon for Sydney, greater Wollongong, and areas including the Blue Mountains and Hawkesbury, after a storm line crossed western New South Wales, with eight flood warnings in southern Queensland and a risk of hail, damaging winds, flash flooding, and outages across southern Queensland, western New South Wales, the Australian Capital Territory, and eastern Victoria. The bureau's long-term record shows Australia has warmed by about 1.5°C since 1910.
