## I. Policy & Carbon Markets

### 1. UN NDC synthesis: current pledges cut emissions only about 6% by 2035 (climate targets)
**Summary:** A UN Climate Change Nationally Determined Contributions Synthesis Report released on October 27 finds that 127 new NDCs recorded between January 1, 2024 and August 31, 2026 cover 154 Parties and about 70% of global greenhouse gas emissions. Under current pledges, global emissions are projected to fall only around 6% below 2019 levels by 2035; full implementation of all current pledges would yield about a 9% cut. The report says collective emission reductions remain substantially off track relative to pathways required by science, while noting more comprehensive NDCs and that 98% of Parties report engaging multiple stakeholder groups in implementation. It was issued with NAP progress and BTR synthesis reports ahead of COP31 in Antalya, Türkiye, November 9–20.

### 2. Trump says he is ending Biden-era oil and gas methane rules as EPA proposes revisions (oil & gas)
**Summary:** At a Lubbock, Texas rally on October 27, President Donald Trump said his administration is “terminating” a Biden-era rule aimed at cutting methane from oil and gas drilling; the U.S. EPA formally proposed revisions to Clean Air Act OOOOb/c oil and gas regulations the same day. EPA says the proposal is expected to save about $45 billion for consumers and businesses and unlock roughly 210 million additional barrels of oil and 1.41 trillion cubic feet of natural gas. Key elements include separate categories and standards for well sites such as marginal wells, rescinding the third-party Super Emitter Program, restoring 95%-efficient flaring or other controls as the best system for associated gas, and easing requirements for covers, closed vent systems and control devices. POLITICO reported on October 28 that a large methane plume was detected near wells close to the rally days earlier.

### 3. Australia proposes firmed clean-power rules for data centres, with possible load shedding (data centres)
**Summary:** Climate Change and Energy Minister Chris Bowen lodged two National Electricity Rules change requests with the Australian Energy Market Commission, discussed publicly on October 28. The first would require qualifying data centres to show demand is firmed through same-region portfolios such as PPAs, cap contracts or tolling agreements, with a high share from verifiably new generation, storage or dependable demand flexibility; the second would streamline connections for large loads co-located with renewables and storage. The AER would audit compliance; financial penalties would be primary, with possible “turn-down” or load shedding until obligations are met. Data-centre use is projected at about 15 TWh in 2029–30 (around 7.8% of NEM operational consumption) and about 34 TWh by 2035–36 (around 13%).

**Links:**

- [The Energy — Bowen sets out new data centre energy rules and how they might be policed](https://theenergy.co/articles/bowen-sets-out-new-data-centre-energy-rules-and-how-they-might-be-policed)

**Commentary:** Tying AI loads to “new + same-region + firmed” power makes curtailment the last line of defense against price spillover.

### 4. U.S. proclamation finds wind turbine imports threaten national security, starts with talks (trade)
**Summary:** A presidential proclamation published in the Federal Register on October 28 concurs with a Commerce Department Section 232 finding that imports of wind turbines and their parts and components threaten to impair U.S. national security. Cited grounds include intermittency and backup fossil/nuclear reliance, cybersecurity risks in foreign inverters, radar interference, and rising import dependence. The proclamation directs the Commerce Secretary and USTR to pursue or continue negotiations with trading partners to adjust such imports and to update the President within 180 days; alternative remedies may follow depending on outcomes. Existing Section 232 tariffs on aluminum, steel and copper are left unchanged.

**Links:**

- [Federal Register — Adjusting imports of wind turbines and their parts and components](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/28/2026-22049/adjusting-imports-of-wind-turbines-and-their-parts-and-components-into-the-united-states)

**Commentary:** Section 232 opens with negotiations, locking a wind-supply-chain security narrative into an executive proclamation.

### 5. EU launches high-level dialogue with oil refining on Middle East shocks and decarbonisation (transition)
**Summary:** The European Commission on October 28 opened its first High-Level Dialogue with the European oil refining industry, co-chaired by Energy and Housing Commissioner Dan Jørgensen and Defence and Space Commissioner Andrius Kubilius, with refiners, emerging fuel producers, engineering and technology firms, associations and NATO. Talks covered near-term Middle East supply disruptions and medium-term competitiveness, fuel-supply resilience, and capacity for crisis and defence demand, plus high energy costs, global competition and investment needs for sustainable biofuels and e-fuels. EU refineries supply about 70% of jet fuel and 85% of diesel consumed in Europe.

### 6. Chongqing lands first cross-compliance-cycle carbon-quota pledge loan; national CEA closes at CNY 95.41 (carbon finance)
**Summary:** Chongqing’s development authority on October 28 highlighted a pilot “cross-compliance-cycle” carbon-quota pledge model that lets banks swap pledged allowances for an enterprise’s newly pre-allocated quotas, so firms can meet year-end surrender while keeping carbon assets financed for multi-year retrofit projects. Chongqing Jianfeng Chemical used the product to advance a low-grade steam waste-heat power unit. The city cites roughly 300 covered emitters and about CNY 6 billion in quota assets that could be unlocked at scale; transformation loans exceeded CNY 9 billion by end-September. Separately, China’s national ETS closed at CNY 95.41/t on October 28, up 0.52% day on day, with about 5.523 million tonnes traded for roughly CNY 518 million.

### 12. Record California high tides keep flooding coasts as warm seas lift king tides (sea level)
**Summary:** Southern California and Bay Area communities on October 28 continued to face record high tides: the San Pedro/Los Angeles Harbor gauge had already reached about 8.02 feet above datum, breaking a January 2005 El Niño record, and Tuesday’s king tide rose further to about 8 feet 2 inches. The National Weather Service attributes much of the excess over pure astronomical tide to thermal expansion from record-warm coastal waters and Kelvin-wave contributions. Newport Beach, Long Beach, Redondo Beach and low-lying Bay Area roads and waterfront businesses flooded, with coastal flood warnings/advisories extended into the weekend. Officials and residents say the combination with record coastal sea temperatures foreshadows more king-tide risk from November through January.
