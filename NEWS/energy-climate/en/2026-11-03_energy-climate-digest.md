## I. Policy & International Climate Governance

### 1. Brazil publishes first global fossil-fuel exit “roadmap,” plus a 2030 deforestation halt pathway (COP)
**Summary:** On November 3, COP30 President André Corrêa do Lago and CEO Ana Toni presented two international roadmaps in Brasília: one on transitioning away from fossil fuels in energy systems in a just, orderly and equitable manner, and another on halting and reversing deforestation and forest degradation by 2030. The texts cite Global Stocktake findings that energy accounts for about 75% of global greenhouse-gas emissions and forest loss about 11%. The fossil roadmap centers on a “Belém Readiness Approach” assessing countries across demand, supply and economy pillars; an open call drew 267 contributions, including 21 from Parties or groups representing 115 Parties. Brazil frames the initiative as a voluntary call to action—not a text to renegotiate at COP31 in Antalya next week—with discussion planned for November 10. Critics including Imperial College’s Joeri Rogelj say the roadmaps are valuable but fall short of clear binding timelines.

### 2. U.S. midterm Election Day: energy affordability dominates ballots as permitting reform splits greens and clean-energy firms (United States)
**Summary:** On November 3 midterm Election Day, a mid-October Pew survey found 74% of U.S. adults worried about gasoline and energy prices—the highest share among issues polled. Candidates in both parties campaigned on household bills; ballot measures restricting data centers proliferated, with Data Center Watch reporting about 32.4% of Democratic candidates backing federal, state or local moratoriums versus roughly 3.4% of Republicans. The Senate bipartisan permitting package (Bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act) remains a year-end hinge: wind, solar and storage trade groups want faster grid and transmission approvals, while some environmental groups split over parallel benefits for fossil projects. Utility Dive and others note that if permitting fails, the next Congress will have few openings for major new energy legislation.

### 3. Global banks’ climate-related financing fees near $3 billion, topping fossil-fuel debt fees for a fifth year (carbon finance)
**Summary:** Bloomberg data show the world’s largest banks have earned roughly $3 billion so far this year arranging climate-related bonds and loans, edging past about $2.9 billion from oil, gas and coal debt deals—the fifth straight year green fees lead fossil fees, reversing 2020 when fossil fees were nearly double. Bank of China, BNP Paribas and Deutsche Bank led green-bond underwriting fees; MUFG, Crédit Agricole and Société Générale led green-loan arranging fees, while Wells Fargo, JPMorgan and Citi remained top fossil debt dealmakers. ## II. Clean Power & Storage

### 4. Tamil Nadu unveils BESS policy: 2.5 GW/10 GWh in three years, mandatory storage for new renewables (storage)
**Summary:** On November 3, Tamil Nadu formally launched a Battery Energy Storage System Policy targeting at least 2.5 GW/10 GWh within three years. New solar and solar-hybrid projects of 1 MW and above—and new wind projects—must install BESS with discharge power of at least 10% of installed capacity and a four-hour duration (e.g., 10 MW/40 MWh for a 100 MW solar plant). Rules apply to projects applying on or after notification, or commissioned from April 1, 2027. Eligible projects may receive a 10-year full waiver of transmission and wheeling charges. New renewable pooling substations must provide standalone BESS equal to at least 10% of sanctioned capacity; existing ones must add 5% by FY28 and another 5% by FY29. A renewable application portal launched the same day to curb “approve-then-stall” behavior.

### 5. Bangladesh solar push: PM eyes 3,000–4,000 MW on the grid before next summer (clean power)
**Summary:** At the opening of Bangladesh Solar Expo-2026 in Dhaka on November 3, Prime Minister Tarique Rahman said the government expects to add 3,000–4,000 MW of solar to the national grid before next summer if plans hold, cutting import dependence. The PM’s adviser said rooftop solar will expand under an OPEX third-party investment model, and noted Rooppur Nuclear Unit-1 entered power start-up on October 29.

### 6. Bulgaria’s operating battery power hits 6.23 GW, among the world’s highest penetrations (storage)
**Summary:** Balkan Green Energy News reported on November 3, citing the Electricity System Operator, that Bulgaria’s operating BESS power reached 6.23 GW by late October—up from about 5.41 GW in mid-August and 3.43 GW before end-May—with more than 19 GWh of energy capacity and average duration above three hours, dominated by four-hour systems. Solar capacity is about 7.3 GW, implying battery power coverage near 85% of PV. From October 1–25, batteries discharged about 18 GWh per day and charged about 19.9 GWh. Relative to system peak, population and economy size, the country ranks among the highest battery penetrations globally.

**Summary:** Carbon Brief’s November 3 state-of-the-climate update finds a roughly 67% chance that 2026 surpasses 2024 as the warmest year across an average of six datasets, with a central estimate around 1.52°C above pre-industrial—effectively tied with 2024—and about a 93% chance of finishing above 1.5°C. ### 11. Study: extreme heat seasons expand asymmetrically into spring and autumn, lengthening health risk windows (climate science)
**Summary:** November coverage by Mongabay and others highlights an AGU Advances paper led by Columbia/NASA researchers: using 1980s local peak extreme-heat months as baseline, over about 45 years extreme dry-heat seasons expanded significantly over ~50% of global land and humid-heat seasons over ~48%, mostly asymmetrically—more post-season “autumn creep” in the western U.S., eastern China, northern Africa and eastern Europe, versus pre-season “spring advance” in western Europe, southern Africa and northwestern India. Authors argue shoulder-season extremes are not explained by simple annual warming layered on historical seasonality; early or late heat often hits before people acclimate or cooling measures are ready. Early-November Mediterranean heat—Spain and Portugal above 30°C, North African sites near or past November records—echoes the pattern.

**Links:**

- [Mongabay — Extreme heat no longer confined to summer](https://news.mongabay.com/2026/11/extreme-heat-no-longer-confined-to-summer-extending-health-risks-study-finds/)
- [AGU Advances — Extreme dry and humid heat seasons changing asymmetrically](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1029/2026AV002516)

**Commentary:** Heat alerts and cooling capacity still designed for “three summer months” will systematically miss spring–autumn mortality peaks.
