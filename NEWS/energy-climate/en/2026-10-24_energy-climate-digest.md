### 2. Southern China’s regional power market leaves trial operation (power market)
**Summary:** Xinhua reported on October 24 that the southern regional power market, covering Guangdong, Guangxi, Yunnan, Guizhou, and Hainan, moved from trial operation to formal operation at a meeting in Guangzhou. Continuous settlement trials had started in June 2025. Coal, hydro, nuclear, wind, and solar clear under one set of rules, and transmission rights on inter-regional lines are allocated by bidding. From January through September, traded electricity was nearly 900 billion kilowatt-hours, more than 70 percent of grid sales. Renewables supplied 40 percent of the increase in sales. The largest single-day clearing exceeded 4 billion kilowatt-hours. Registered participants passed 290,000, and regional renewable utilization stayed above 95 percent. Xinhua said that after formal operation, new storage will take a larger role in the spot and ancillary-service markets. 21st Century Business Herald reported the same day that more than 200 Hong Kong, Macao, and Taiwan companies bought 1.28 million green certificates, that residential and agricultural tariffs stay outside market swings, and that Lao renewables joined for the first time, with cumulative clean-energy trades above 1 billion kilowatt-hours.

### 3. Vietnam tells provinces to adopt household rooftop-solar support by year-end (solar)
**Summary:** pv magazine reported on October 24 that Government Office Dispatch No. 10790/VPCP-CN, dated October 22, orders provincial and municipal people’s committees to introduce support policies by the end of December 2026 for household rooftop solar used on site. The dispatch does not set a nationwide subsidy rate. Prime Minister Le Minh Hung called for faster rooftop PV and battery storage to cut peak demand. The Ministry of Industry and Trade must propose provincial capacity targets and commissioning schedules by October 30, with emphasis on the north. It must also, with the finance and justice ministries, produce policies by December to speed storage, especially rooftop solar plus storage in industrial parks. Vietnam Electricity said the country had 134,410 rooftop systems totaling more than 11.45 GW at the end of August. The ministry said in September that only Bac Ninh and Lai Chau, of 34 provinces and municipalities, had adopted the required household support. Decree No. 243, effective June 26, raised the usual cap on surplus sales from 20 percent to 50 percent.

### 4. India has 308 GW of non-fossil capacity and puts clean-energy investment needs near $220 billion (investment)

Non-fossil sources are more than 54 percent of installed power capacity. The Bharat Renewable Energy Summit runs in New Delhi from November 2 to 5. Work on a Solar Energy Corporation of India listing is underway, with no timetable set.

## II. Green Power and Storage

### 5. Indian official: battery storage rose from about 0.7 GWh to nearly 10 GWh in roughly ten months (storage)
**Summary:** NetZero India on October 24 published an interview with J.V.N. Subramanyam, joint secretary at the Ministry of New and Renewable Energy. He said India’s battery storage rose from about 0.7 GWh to nearly 10 GWh over the past 10 to 11 months, with central and state spending on distribution-level storage aimed at curtailment. He put transmission first: lines typically take about 36 months, while renewable projects can be operating in 12 to 18 months, leaving a temporary gap at hubs such as Khavda that the government expects to narrow within 18 months. Annual renewable additions rose over the past year from about 23 GW to 55 GW, of which solar was about 44 to 45 GW. Module manufacturing capacity grew from about 8 GW at the start of the build-out to nearly 200 GW. Under the PM Surya Ghar scheme, about 6.3 million households have installed rooftop solar, and nearly 2 million have had at least one month with a zero electricity bill.

### 6. Australian batteries supplied more of the evening peak than gas for the first time (storage)
**Summary:** The Australian Energy Market Operator on October 23 released its Quarterly Energy Dynamics report for July 1 to September 30. Renewables supplied a third-quarter record 44.3 percent of National Electricity Market generation. Grid-scale solar rose 30 percent and hydro 15 percent. Energy shifted by grid-scale batteries more than tripled from a year earlier. Between 4 p.m. and 9 p.m., batteries supplied 8 percent of generation and gas 5.1 percent, the first quarter in which batteries exceeded gas in that peak. Average wholesale prices fell 27 percent year on year and 15 percent from the June quarter, to A$63 per megawatt-hour, the lowest September-quarter average since 2021. East-coast gas averaged A$10.77 per gigajoule, 15 percent lower than a year earlier. In Western Australia, real-time prices rose 19 percent to A$120.72 per megawatt-hour, the highest quarterly average on record in that market. RenewEconomy reported on October 24, citing the same report, that economic offloading of grid-scale solar fell 54 percent, or 177 MW on average, to 5.9 percent of available solar, while network curtailment of wind and solar rose to a record average of 281 MW.

## III. Climate and Disasters

### 8. Study estimates 50,391 heat-related excess deaths in 854 European cities this summer (heat)
**Summary:** The London School of Hygiene & Tropical Medicine said on October 23 that research it led with Imperial College London, published in Nature Health, estimates 50,391 heat-related excess deaths (95 percent empirical confidence interval 46,026 to 53,878) from June through August 2026 in 854 cities across 30 European countries. Of those, 32,619, about 65 percent (interval 53 to 73 percent), are attributed to human-caused climate change. The cities hold about 173 million people, roughly 40 percent of the population of the 30 countries. People aged 85 and older account for 22,502 of the deaths. The school’s release called this the highest summer heat-death toll in these cities since 2000, almost double the roughly 29,000 estimated for 2022. It put the UK figure at about 4,500 across 135 urban areas and said about 6,000 of all the deaths were among people under 65. A companion paper estimates more than 430,000 heat-related deaths in these cities over the summers of 2000 to 2025, with the share attributed to human-caused warming rising from about 40 percent in 2000 to more than two-thirds in 2025. The authors describe the figures as modelled estimates, not counts of deaths certified as caused by heat.

Taiwan’s fire department said the death toll remained one, a man swept away by waves at Liuqiu, with 527 people injured in the wind and rain. Taipower said more than 288,000 homes lost electricity at one point and that most had been reconnected. China’s Ministry of Emergency Management sent a team to Zhejiang, warned of strong wind and heavy rain through Sunday, and said 400 rescue workers and 600 pieces of equipment were ready, with more than 41,000 firefighters on duty. In Taizhou, more than 3,800 fishing boats returned to port and 2,084 island visitors were evacuated. Authorities halted 25 offshore construction projects and suspended 18 passenger ferry routes.
