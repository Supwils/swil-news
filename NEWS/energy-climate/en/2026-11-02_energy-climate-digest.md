## I. Policy & Carbon Markets

### 1. China begins first reading of Renewable Energy Law revision: unified power market, offtake duties, and green-certificate fines (Policy)
**Summary:** On November 2, a draft revision of China’s Renewable Energy Law was submitted for a first reading at the 25th session of the 14th NPC Standing Committee. The 10-chapter, 76-article draft would establish a national unified electricity market, refine trading and pricing rules suited to renewables, create a sustainable pricing settlement mechanism, and add provisions on ancillary services, capacity assurance, and transmission/distribution pricing. On the demand side, it pairs mandatory and voluntary minimum renewable-consumption shares with responsibility quotas and a green electricity certificate (GEC) system; entities missing the minimum renewable-power target could face fines of five to 10 times the GEC cost of the shortfall, priced at the average market GEC price in the month before the deadline. Market-traded electricity reached 5,853.6 TWh in January–September, up 18.9% year on year, and more than half of new-energy volumes are already market-priced.

### 3. UK chancellor screens renewables for bill cuts and grid fit, tightening the gate on offshore wind (Policy)
**Summary:** Chancellor John Healey said after the Budget that the next renewables auction will buy only projects that lower bills and can connect without raising consumer costs—coverage framed as a “veto” on offshore wind that fails the test. He argued there is “no sense building power that can’t connect and then paying to switch it off,” while insisting the stance is not a retreat from clean power. Scottish business groups warn the rules could put billions in investment and floating offshore pipelines at risk; UK fact-checkers note “veto” is media language and that a cost-control framework was already in the Budget, without a new statutory blocking power.

### 4. United States: EPA GHG power-plant comment deadline lands as Senate permitting reform modeling drops (United States)
**Summary:** November 2 was the final day for comments on EPA’s supplemental proposal to remove all greenhouse-gas requirements for fossil power plants, part of the repeal of Biden-era standards; the administration has cited potential power-sector savings of as much as about $310 billion. ## II. Oil Shock & Energy Transition

### 5. TotalEnergies Energy Outlook 2026: Hormuz crisis may accelerate transition; oil-demand peak moved to 2035–2040 (Outlook)
**Summary:** TotalEnergies on November 2 released the eighth TotalEnergies Energy Outlook. It argues the 2026 Strait of Hormuz crisis tests supply security yet may also act as a transition “accelerator,” citing faster EV sales in Asia, Latin America and Europe since the second quarter and higher biofuel-blending requirements. Under the Trends scenario, petroleum-product demand plateaus in the 2030s then slowly declines, gas keeps growing before stabilizing in the 2040s, and warming reaches about +2.6–2.8°C by 2100; Momentum yields about +2.3–2.4°C; the Paris-aligned Rupture case about +1.8–1.9°C is judged largely out of reach amid today’s geopolitics. Reuters/OilPrice coverage says the company brought its oil-demand peak window forward to roughly 2035–2040 versus last year.

**Links:**

- [TotalEnergies — Energy Outlook 2026 press release](https://totalenergies.com/newsroom/totalenergies-energy-outlook-2026-517258/?lang=eng)

**Commentary:** The crisis narrative holds “high prices spur EVs” and “fossils still backstop the system” at once—majors advance the peak without pulling warming below 2°C.

### 6. U.S. oil firms clear more than $130 billion in profits during the Iran war as household fuel bills soar (Oil & gas)
**Summary:** A New York Times analysis finds more than 50 large U.S. oil and gas companies earned over $130 billion in profit from April through September 2026—nearly three times the under-$47 billion in the same period of 2025—excluding March, the first war month, and October. Brown University’s Climate Solutions Lab estimates Americans spent an extra about $147 billion on gasoline and diesel since U.S.–Israeli strikes on Iran began February 28, or more than $1,100 per household. Refiners especially gained as Middle East and Russian refining capacity was damaged and fuel supplies tightened; Exxon and Chevron also posted sharply higher third-quarter results. Al Jazeera and others amplified the figures on November 2.

### 8. NEA: coal-power output falls through Q3; coal’s share of generation down to 49.1% by end-September (Transition data)
**Summary:** China’s National Energy Administration said on November 2 that coal-fired generation and its share fell year on year in the third quarter: output was down 4%, 4.6% and 1.9% in July, August and September, with thermal-coal use lower in tandem. Coal’s share of domestic generation first dipped below 50% in the first half and reached 49.1% by end-September; coal’s contribution to incremental electricity demand fell to 4.4%. Officials said coal production recovery and inventories remain in a safe range: Bohai-rim port stocks were 26.92 Mt on October 25, and key power-plant stocks about 210 Mt—roughly 35 days of cover.
