## I. Policy, Carbon Markets & International Cooperation

### 1. UK Ofgem: energy price cap to rise 26% from January 2027, typical dual-fuel bill above £2,166 (Prices)
**Summary:** UK regulator Ofgem announced on Nov 25 that the default tariff cap for 1 January–31 March 2027 will rise about 26%. Using updated typical consumption, the annualised direct-debit dual-fuel bill moves from £1,723 to £2,166; about 20 million households on standard variable tariffs are affected, while roughly 10 million on fixed deals are shielded while contracts last. Officials blamed Middle East conflict-driven gas prices, with the wholesale cost allowance up about 50% quarter-on-quarter; gas bills rise about 33% and electricity about 19%. The government also said about 20 million households will receive a £100 bill credit from January.

**Links:**

- [Ofgem — Energy price cap to rise by 26% from January 2027](https://www.ofgem.gov.uk/press-release/energy-price-cap-rise-26-january-2027)
- [The Guardian — Energy bills in Great Britain to rise 26% in January](https://www.theguardian.com/money/2026/nov/25/energy-bills-in-great-britain-to-rise-ofgem-price-cap)

**Commentary:** Renewables are already muting the electricity hike, but gas still sets the winter bill—affordability remains priced in fossil wholesale markets.

### 2. U.S. House set to vote next week on BAAJA permitting reform: rule vote Tuesday, floor vote midweek (Policy)
**Summary:** The Hill and Axios reported on Nov 25 that House GOP leaders plan to bring the Senate-passed bipartisan energy and infrastructure permitting bill (BAAJA) to the floor under regular order next week, with a rules vote expected Tuesday and a final vote possible Wednesday or Thursday. The measure cleared the Senate 62–33 last week and would go to the White House if the House passes it unchanged. Democrats warn it weakens Clean Water Act and Endangered Species Act protections and eases fossil projects; some Republicans also question transmission provisions. TD Cowen puts roughly a 75% chance of enactment this session, though attendance and Democratic opposition remain wild cards.

**Links:**

### 3. China’s MIIT dual carbon-control plan: industrial CO2 intensity to fall over 17% vs 2025 by 2030 (Policy)
**Summary:** Economic Daily reported on Nov 25 that the Ministry of Industry and Information Technology issued an implementation plan (2026–2030) to build a dual carbon-control system for industry and information technology. By 2030 it targets about 400 product carbon-footprint accounting standards, roughly 500 national zero-carbon factories and zero-carbon computing facilities, and a cut of more than 17% in CO2 emissions per unit of value-added at industrial enterprises above designated size versus 2025, plus an energy-intensity drop of over 10%. The plan spans local assessment, sector controls, corporate management, project evaluation and product footprints; experts flag petrochemicals, clean-tech manufacturing and computing facilities as new pressure points, with electrification and green energy expected to deliver about 25% of cuts.

**Links:**

- [MIIT / Economic Daily — Industry moves toward dual carbon control](https://www.miit.gov.cn/xwfb/mtbd/twbd/art/2026/art_3693938474f745aa865c1d7ed112e24d.html)

**Commentary:** Shifting from energy dual control to carbon dual control finally points the industrial yardstick at “how much is emitted,” not only “how much is burned.”

## II. Renewables, Storage & Nuclear

### 6. Italy’s second MACSE auction awards full 16 GWh of batteries at ~€11,382/MWh-year (Storage)
**Summary:** Energy Storage News reported on Nov 25 that Italian grid operator Terna published results of the second Electricity Storage Capacity Procurement Mechanism (MACSE) auction: all 16 GWh offered for 2029 delivery were awarded across 16 roughly 1 GWh battery projects. The weighted average award was about €11,382/MWh-year, roughly 12% below the prior auction for 2028 delivery and far under the €27,000/MWh-year reserve premium. About 67 GWh of projects qualified and bids totaled ~43 GWh. Unawarded capacity of about 27 GWh must lean more on merchant markets, tolling or the capacity market; derating factors for four-hour batteries in southern zones have also tightened.

**Links:**

- [ESS News — Italy awards 16 GWh of battery storage in second MACSE auction](https://www.ess-news.com/2026/11/25/italy-awards-16-gwh-of-battery-storage-in-second-auction/)

**Commentary:** Europe’s storage capacity market is speaking in prices—falling awards show flexibility assets moving from scarcity premiums to scale competition.

### 7. Ireland’s RESS 6 provisional results: ~1,550 MW onshore renewables, over 1,800 GWh, solar-led (Renewables)
**Summary:** Ireland’s Department of Climate, Energy and the Environment on Nov 25 announced provisional results of the sixth Renewable Electricity Support Scheme (RESS 6) auction: about 1,800 GWh of successful volume, enough for over 450,000 homes, equating to roughly 1,550 MW of onshore capacity (188 MW wind, 1,367 MW solar)—among the largest rounds by successful capacity since RESS 2. Weighted average prices were about €99.35 for wind and €102.42 for solar. Projects must contribute at least €2/MWh to community benefit funds, totaling about €56.82 million over project lives. Minister Darragh O’Brien called solar Ireland’s fastest-growing generation technology while urging stronger wind delivery and grid consenting.

**Links:**

- [gov.ie — Minister O'Brien welcomes results of sixth national auction for onshore wind and solar](https://www.gov.ie/en/department-of-climate-energy-and-the-environment/press-releases/minister-obrien-welcomes-results-of-sixth-national-auction-for-new-onshore-wind-and-solar-energy-projects/)

**Commentary:** Solar has overtaken onshore wind in the auction room—Ireland’s next bottleneck is not ambition, but whether wind projects can clear planning and grid queues.

### 12. Severe El Niño threatens the Amazon: 73% of basin below-average rain in October, fire hotspots up ~82.6% (Climate)
**Summary:** Anadolu Agency reported on Nov 25 that sea-surface temperatures in a key equatorial Pacific region had reached about 2.5°C above average, with NOAA giving roughly a 60% chance the current El Niño could rank among the strongest since 1950. ### 13. MIT Nature study: drained peatlands already contribute ~2.7% of human-caused warming; status quo could nearly double the legacy (Science)
**Summary:** MIT News reported on Nov 25 that researchers combining global drainage data with CO2 and methane models estimate that draining about 0.5% of Earth’s land surface—roughly 15% of peatland area—has contributed about 2.7% of present-day human-caused warming. The Nature study finds that even without further drainage, keeping currently drained peatlands in that state could nearly double their cumulative warming contribution by 2100. Intact peatlands are net cooling carbon stores; drainage for agriculture and forestry breaks that balance.

**Links:**

- [MIT News — Draining peatlands leaves a long legacy of warming](https://news.mit.edu/2026/draining-peatlands-leaves-long-warming-legacy-1125)

**Commentary:** An undercounted page in the climate ledger: stopping drainage only stems the bleeding—rewetting is the real negative-emissions project.

## IV. Fossil Lock-in & Transition Outlook

### 14. U.S. data centers fuel a gas boom: 115+ GW of behind-the-meter gas plants planned; nearly half of PJM queue capacity is gas (Fossil lock-in)
**Summary:** MIT Technology Review analyzed on Nov 25 how AI power demand is driving a U.S. natural-gas resurgence. PJM in August accepted over 200 GW into its queue, nearly half of it gas plants; if built, fossils’ share on PJM could rise from 61% to about 67%. More than 100 behind-the-meter or off-grid gas plants totaling over 115 GW have been proposed, most since early 2025. Meta’s latest sustainability report showed emissions up about 45% year on year and said the company will consider on-site generation when the grid cannot deliver on the needed timeline. Wind, solar-plus-storage and demand flexibility could meet load, but interconnection queues make gas the default speed option.

**Links:**

- [MIT Technology Review — How data centers are driving a US natural-gas boom](https://www.technologyreview.com/2026/11/25/1149836/data-centers-gas-power-plants/)

**Commentary:** The compute race is rewriting fuel choice as “fastest electrons”—digital infrastructure is lengthening the fossil bridge.

### 15. BMI: transition still too slow for climate goals; emissions peak may slip to 2032 as Hormuz shock deepens policy splits (Transition)
**Summary:** Engineering News reported on Nov 25 that Fitch’s BMI unit sees the energy transition shifting from climate-policy leadership to energy security, industrial competitiveness and affordability, fragmenting along policy lines. Fossils are expected to stay dominant through 2034, with emissions peaking around 2032 versus an earlier 2028 forecast. BMI frames the 2026 U.S.–Iran conflict and effective Hormuz disruption as the second major supply crisis in four years: import-dependent markets treat electrification and domestic renewables as security strategy, while exporters and the U.S. double down on hydrocarbons. Green-hydrogen forecasts have been cut; CCS tied to enhanced oil recovery looks more durable. Renewables should still roughly double and displace coal in power, but transport, heavy industry and AI gas demand are extending oil and gas growth curves. IEA’s World Energy Outlook 2026 similarly stresses diversification and electrification after the 2026 Gulf supply shock.

**Links:**

- [Engineering News — Energy transition not scaling up fast enough to meet climate targets](https://www.engineeringnews.co.za/article/energy-transition-not-scaling-up-fast-enough-to-meet-climate-targets-2026-11-25)

**Commentary:** The transition has not stopped, but “security first” is pushing net-zero timetables later—the bridge fuel’s bridge keeps getting longer.
