## I. Policy, Carbon Markets & International Climate Governance

### 1. COP31 finance day: Guterres warns tripling adaptation funds would still cover under 30%; DFIs from UK, Norway and Canada pledge to mobilize $10bn by 2030 (COP · Finance)
**Summary:** On November 13, as COP31 in Antalya closed its first week, climate finance dominated the agenda. UN Secretary-General António Guterres, at his final COP press conference before leaving office, said even tripling adaptation finance for vulnerable countries would meet less than about 30% of needs, and that on current trends annual climate finance for developing countries could fall about $840 billion short of the broader $1.3 trillion goal. The same day, development finance institutions linked to Britain, Norway and Canada announced plans to mobilize about $10 billion for climate projects in emerging markets by 2030. Carbon Brief analysis shows public climate-finance pledges from major donors for coming years are about 70% lower than in 2025, sharpening deadlocks on the finance work programme, adaptation and fossil-fuel texts.

### 2. EU climate chief hints bloc may drop “clean power” language to widen electrification support (COP · Electrification)
**Summary:** On November 13 in Antalya, EU Climate Commissioner Wopke Hoekstra told reporters the world needs “much more radical, much faster” electrification, ideally from solar, wind and nuclear—but that “a certain degree of pragmatism” may be needed to maximize the number of countries signing on. The EU’s original mandate insisted any electrification deal refer explicitly to “clean” power from renewables or nuclear; Hoekstra argued that even “gray” electricity can beat direct fossil combustion because of higher end-use efficiency. The Türkiye–Australia co-presidency’s “35×35” pledge (electricity at about 35% of final energy by 2035) has more than 50 countries as firm supporters or backers per Carbon Brief, though many remain on the fence and Saudi Arabia and Russia are among those said to oppose a hard resolution.

**Links:**

- [POLITICO — EU climate chief hints bloc may drop clean-power push](https://www.politico.eu/article/eu-climate-chief-wopke-hoekstra-hints-bloc-may-drop-clean-power-push-at-cop31/)
- [Carbon Brief — 35×35 electrification support expands](https://www.carbonbrief.org/debriefed-13-november-2026-make-or-bake-electrification-push-climate-finance-down-70)

### 4. IPCC science timetable fight spills into COP31: EU wants reports before 2028 stocktake; Saudi Arabia, China and India lean to 2029 (Science · Governance)
**Summary:** AFP reported on November 13 that the EU vowed in Antalya to “fight” for science after accusing some countries of seeking to undermine the IPCC. The dispute centers on when the Seventh Assessment Report (AR7) should appear: the EU and dozens of nations want it available before the 2028 global stocktake; Saudi Arabia, China and India are among those pushing toward 2029, citing time for developing countries to digest thousands of pages of findings. The IPCC’s October session failed to break the deadlock and was suspended until December 1; AOSIS and the Least Developed Countries group already support a 2028 release. Observers are not optimistic the consensus-bound COP31 can resolve it.

**Links:**

- [France 24 / AFP — Fight over climate science intensifies at COP31](https://www.france24.com/en/live-news/20261113-fight-over-climate-science-intensifies-at-cop31)

**Commentary:** This is not just a calendar fight—it is about whether the next stocktake can use the latest science, and a one-year delay buys another year of political room to postpone cuts.

### 5. ExxonMobil sues EU under Energy Charter Treaty over Net-Zero Industry Act carbon-storage mandates (EU · Litigation)
**Summary:** Euronews reported on November 13, citing a Commission document, that three ExxonMobil entities in Belgium, Luxembourg and the Netherlands have filed an ECT arbitration notice under UNCITRAL rules challenging NZIA carbon-storage infrastructure duties. The law sets an EU objective of at least about 50 million tonnes per year of CO₂ injection capacity by end-2030 and requires authorized oil and gas producers to contribute; producers below about 610,000 tonnes of oil equivalent or under 5% of EU output are exempt, leaving about 44 major producers covered. The companies seek provisional measures to suspend injection obligations, citing lengthy permitting and high costs; the Commission declined to comment on ongoing cases. Online EU injection capacity is about 3.54 Mt, with projected capacity about 27.1 Mt—still well short of the 2030 target.

**Links:**

- [Euronews — ExxonMobil sues EU over rules forcing oil giants into carbon storage](https://www.euronews.com/2026/11/13/exxonmobil-sues-eu-over-rules-forcing-oil-giants-to-invest-in-carbon-storage)

**Commentary:** “Polluter pays” barely entered industrial law before investment arbitration hit back—the ECT again lets fossil capital drag climate duties into court rather than onto the emissions ledger.

## II. Clean Power, Storage & Industry Chains

### 7. NDRC and NEA issue 15th Five-Year Plan new-type energy storage scheme: ~300 GW by 2030, ~3-hour average duration (China · Storage)
**Summary:** On November 13, China’s National Development and Reform Commission and National Energy Administration issued the Implementation Plan for New-Type Energy Storage in the 15th Five-Year Plan period. By 2030, new-type storage should move from scale-up to market-based development, reaching about 300 GW nationally with average duration around three hours; about 50–60 GW would be co-located at renewable bases, and about 140 GW of grid-side independent storage would support peak adequacy. Priorities include fair participation in mid- to long-term and spot markets, allowing “renewable mega-base + storage” packages into interprovincial mid- to long-term trading, refining charge/discharge pricing and capacity-value mechanisms, accelerating frequency-regulation ancillary markets, and advancing lithium upgrades, sodium-ion deployment and compressed-air, flow-battery and other pathways.

### 8. IEA chief: world has entered the “age of electricity”; ~12,000 GW of generation may be added in the next decade (IEA · Electrification)
**Summary:** On November 13 The Guardian published an interview with IEA Executive Director Fatih Birol at COP31. He said the world has left the age of coal, oil and gas dominance and entered the “age of electricity”: about 5,000 GW of generation was added globally in the past decade, and even on a conservative view about 12,000 GW will be added in the next ten years, with electricity demand growing about three times faster than total energy demand. Drivers include AI and data centres, transport electrification (EV sales outside China up about 60% in nine months; EVs may be about one-third of global car sales this year) and explosive air-conditioning growth. Birol argued a clear COP31 signal on ~35% electricity in final energy by 2035 would accelerate investment—but that structural electrification would continue even if the resolution stalls.

**Links:**

- [The Guardian — World is now in the “age of electricity,” IEA chief says](https://www.theguardian.com/environment/2026/nov/13/cop-31-electricity-energy-fossil-fuels-fatih-birol-iea)

**Commentary:** Electrification is already a physical trend; the summit mainly decides whether it arrives expensive or cheap—what is missing is affordable clean kilowatt-hours, not more meters.

### 11. India Power Ministry drafts mandatory storage with solar and domestic-content rules for batteries (India · Policy)
**Summary:** Saur Energy reported on November 13 that India’s Ministry of Power has circulated draft amendments to four competitive-bidding guideline packages (comments due November 25). Storage would shift from optional to mandatory where central government orders and Central Electricity Authority rules require ESS alongside solar; for wind–solar hybrids, storage “shall” rather than “may” be added to the solar component. Batteries and BESS would also need to meet domestic-content norms, though drafts do not set thresholds or duration. Firm and Dispatchable RE and standalone BESS guidelines would likewise embed domestic-content clauses and soften payment-security fund contribution language. Industry is watching transition treatment for already-issued tenders and tariff impacts.

## III. Climate Disasters & Extreme Weather

### 12. Storm Charles batters southern Europe: ~355 mm in 12 hours on Madeira; hundreds of flights cancelled in Lisbon and Madrid (Disaster)
**Summary:** The Guardian reported on November 13 that Storm Charles hit the Iberian Peninsula then tracked northeast into southern France and northern Italy. Funchal airport on Madeira recorded gusts of about 136 km/h and about 355 mm of rain in 12 hours; Lisbon cancelled about 183 flights (roughly a third of daily traffic) and Madrid Barajas about 155. Parts of Cannes saw significant flooding and rockfalls with Riviera road closures; Italy’s weather service issued its highest red rainfall warning from Tuscany into northern areas. The system underlines the real-world pressure behind COP31’s adaptation-finance debates.

### 13. Super El Niño confirmed strengthening; back-to-back atmospheric rivers hit the US Northwest amid king tides (Climate · Extremes)
**Summary:** The Independent and others reported on November 13 that NOAA confirmed a super El Niño that may remain strong to very strong through January–March 2027. The same day, the US Pacific Northwest faced two more atmospheric rivers Friday and Saturday atop earlier rainfall, king tides and higher seas, elevating flash-flood, urban-flood and landslide risk. UC San Diego’s Center for Western Weather and Water Extremes notes atmospheric rivers can stretch about 2,000 miles long and 600 miles wide; about 40 years of FEMA data attribute 90% or more of Western US flood damage to them. Northeast-monsoon rains also brought very heavy rain warnings in southern Thailand and multi-province alerts in Indonesia, showing El Niño and monsoon bands pressing hazards in parallel.
