## I. Regulation & Policy

### 1. U.S. Treasury FinCEN Cuts Off MEXC Global from the U.S. Financial System (Regulation)
**Summary:** The U.S. Treasury’s Financial Crimes Enforcement Network (FinCEN) issued an order on Nov. 6 identifying convertible virtual currency exchanger MEXC Global as of primary money-laundering concern in connection with Russian illicit finance and barring U.S. financial institutions from any transmittal of funds to or from the platform, including crypto. Treasury said that under Operation Economic Outcast, MEXC facilitated at least about $16 billion in flows tied to sanctions evasion, cybercrime and related activity between May 2021 and May 2026—including nearly $1.5 billion linked to Russian sanctions evasion and activity tied to Iran and North Korea. The order rests on section 9714(a) of the Combating Russian Money Laundering Act and takes effect 30 days after Federal Register publication. MEXC said it is operating normally, that user assets remain secure, and that it is reviewing the order and launching an independent AML/sanctions-controls review. CoinDesk and others reported on Nov. 7 that MEXC is among the largest exchanges hit under this statute to date.

### 2. Labor Department’s Final 401(k) Alternatives Rule Reaches White House Review, Edging Crypto Closer to Retirement Plans (Regulation)
**Summary:** The Labor Department’s Employee Benefits Security Administration submitted a final rule titled “Fiduciary Duties in Selecting Designated Investment Alternatives” (RIN 1210-AC38) to the White House Office of Information and Regulatory Affairs on Nov. 5. The rule implements a Trump executive order aiming to clarify how 401(k) fiduciaries may evaluate alternatives such as private equity, real estate and cryptocurrency; the March proposal sketched a process-based safe harbor around six factors—performance, fees, liquidity, valuation, benchmarks and complexity—without requiring plans to offer crypto. The final text and effective date are not yet public. The Block, Bitcoin Magazine and others reported on Nov. 6–7 that OIRA review usually precedes Federal Register publication, but the rule remains pending and is not yet in force.

### 3. Brazil’s Central Bank Restricts Supervised Institutions from Dealing with Unauthorized Crypto Service Providers (Regulation)
**Summary:** Under BCB Resolution No. 589 and related reporting, from Nov. 6 Brazilian Central Bank–supervised financial and payment institutions are generally barred from carrying out or facilitating virtual-asset transactions with service providers that lack authorization, except where the rules expressly allow it. The restriction covers trading, intermediation and custody as well as related FX, payment-account and payment services. First-stage license filings for incumbent providers closed on Oct. 30; those that filed on time may continue existing services while applications are reviewed. Cross-border, some virtual-asset international payments and transfers fall under FX rules, with a roughly $100,000 equivalent cap when the counterparty is not an authorized FX institution. Xinhua Finance and Radar Digital followed the rollout on Nov. 6–7.
