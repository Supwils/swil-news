## I. Regulation & Policy

### 1. CFTC Clears Equity Perpetual Futures; Capital Markets Alliance Sues Same Day (Regulation)
**Summary:** On Nov. 3, 2026, CFTC Chairman Michael Selig said perpetual futures on individual equities (“Equity Perps”) may begin trading on CFTC-designated contract markets that are also SEC notice-registered to trade security futures. The contracts were deemed approved under Commission Regulation 40.3 as security futures under the CFMA and are subject to both CFTC and SEC frameworks. The same day, the Capital Markets Alliance—backed by Cboe, Citadel Securities and others—sued in the U.S. District Court for the Southern District of New York, arguing the products are security-based swaps under exclusive SEC jurisdiction and seeking to vacate the approvals and bar them from taking effect. A CFTC spokesperson called the suit troubling interference with regulated-market innovation.

**Commentary:** Crypto-style perps are formally entering U.S. single-stock risk, but same-day Wall Street litigation means launch timing still hinges on jurisdiction fights and the courts.

### 2. Taiwan FSC Previews Eight VASP Sub-Rule Drafts; Banks May Conduct Four Business Lines (Regulation)
**Summary:** Taiwan’s Financial Supervisory Commission on Nov. 3 previewed eight draft authorizing regulations under the Virtual Asset Services Act, covering VASP licensing, fit-and-proper rules, business/finance oversight, internal controls, outsourcing, anomalous-account controls, financial reporting, and self-regulatory association rules. The regime would shift from AML registration to licensing: minimum paid-in capital of NT$50 million for exchange, transfer, or underwriting businesses; NT$100 million for trading-platform or custody; and NT$200 million for lending. Banks may initially conduct exchange, transfer, custody, and underwriting, with exchange and transfer limited to corporate clients. About 11 existing registered VASPs would have 12 months after the new rules take effect to apply for licenses and meet capital thresholds; comments may be filed within 40 days after formal gazette notice.

### 3. South Korea’s FSC Presses Exchanges to Raise Cold-Wallet Shares After Binance Hack (Regulation)
**Summary:** After Binance’s roughly $6.3 billion theft, South Korea’s Financial Services Commission held an emergency meeting on Nov. 2 with the FSS, Financial Security Institute, DAXA, and major VASPs. Nov. 3 official clarifications and follow-up coverage show regulators immediately urged higher cold-wallet ratios, continuous deposit/withdrawal monitoring, faster reporting of abnormal access, and additional reserves. Current law already requires at least 80% of customer crypto in cold storage; media report officials are weighing a move toward about 99%, though no new binding percentage has been finalized. Industry participants also sought clearer rules on when they may legally suspend withdrawals after incidents at other platforms.

### 4. Brazil Central Bank Resolution 589 to Restrict Deals With Unlicensed VASPs From Nov. 6 (Regulation)
**Summary:** Per Xinhua Finance reporting carried on Nov. 3, Brazil’s central bank is phasing in virtual-asset licensing. Under Resolution 589, from Nov. 6 regulated financial and payment institutions generally may not conduct or facilitate transactions with VASPs lacking a Brazilian Central Bank license, except where rules expressly allow—covering trading, intermediation, custody, and related FX/payment facilitation. The first-stage application deadline for existing providers that must seek licenses already passed on Oct. 30. Earlier Resolutions 519–521 set licensing, operating, and selected cross-border FX requirements.

**Links:**

- [21jingji / Xinhua Finance — Brazil’s central bank redefines crypto-asset rules](https://www.21jingji.com/article/20261103/herald/ff46de6e6a58cc917088d78b11ea2bba.html)

**Commentary:** Pairing licensing with a bank-channel cutoff for unlicensed VASPs pulls Latin America’s largest market deeper into the supervised financial perimeter.

### 12. Open Standard Sets OUSD Fee at 0.1 bp of Payments, Capped at $0.01 (DeFi)
**Summary:** According to internal documents reviewed by CoinDesk on Nov. 3, OpenUSD (OUSD)—the consortium stablecoin associated with Stripe, Visa, Mastercard, Coinbase, and Shopify—will pay governance entity Open Standard a 0.1 basis-point management fee on payment volume, capped at $0.01 per payment (payments of $1,000 or more hit the cap). Unlike USDT/USDC, where issuers keep reserve interest, OUSD distributes that interest to partners after the fee. A related equity earn-in pool measures monthly holdings and flows; partners need at least $50 million to qualify, with a 5% ownership cap including warrants. Visa also released more than 100 pages of technical and user guidance for partners integrating via the Visa Stablecoin Platform.

**Links:**

- [CoinDesk — Stablecoin group behind OUSD sets management fee, offers equity earn-in pool](https://www.coindesk.com/business/2026/11/03/stablecoin-governance-group-behind-ousd-sets-out-management-fee-offers-equity-earning-pool)

**Commentary:** Near-zero payment friction plus reserve-yield sharing shifts stablecoin competition from “who issues” to “who can distribute a payments network and partner equity incentives.”
