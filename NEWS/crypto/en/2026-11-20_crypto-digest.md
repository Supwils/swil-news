## II. Regulation & Policy

### 4. Hong Kong: Virtual Asset Services Amendment Bill Gazetted, Extending Coverage to Dealing, Advice, Management and Custody

**Summary:** The Hong Kong government gazetted the Virtual Asset Services Legislation (Amendment) Bill 2026 on Nov 20. It would amend the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing Ordinance so that businesses providing virtual-asset dealing, advisory, management or custody services in Hong Kong must be licensed by or registered with the SFC; HKMA-supervised banks and stored-value facility licensees would also need SFC registration to offer those services. The bill is scheduled for Legislative Council first reading on Dec 2 and is not yet law.

### 5. Europe: Lagarde Urges Common Tokenization Rules Before Markets Fragment

**Summary:** ECB President Christine Lagarde told the Frankfurt European Banking Congress on Nov 20 that tokenized traditional assets on public blockchains were worth about €23 billion worldwide in March — roughly five times a year earlier — and that the window to set standards is limited. If platforms adopt incompatible rules, they may be unable to settle with one another. The Eurosystem’s Pontes already settles tokenized trades in central bank money, and the European Stability Mechanism settled a roughly €1 billion digitally native bond via Pontes earlier this month; Lagarde stressed that common rules still rest with legislators.

### 6. US: CFTC Chair Says No Special Exemptions for Onchain Derivatives — Register or Partner

**Summary:** CFTC Chairman Michael Selig said platforms such as Hyperliquid that want to offer perpetuals and other derivatives to US retail traders must obtain an exchange license or partner with a registered exchange, with no separate rulebook merely because activity is onchain. He cited crypto-native Backpack’s recent approvals as a path. Firms including Kraken parent Payward are pursuing regulated structures for Hyperliquid-based trading, still subject to approval.

### 7. US: SEC Nominees Hearing Focuses on Tokenization Exemption and Perpetuals Jurisdiction

**Summary:** The Senate Banking Committee held a Nov 19 confirmation hearing for Trump SEC nominees Steven Levine and Kristin Johnson. Johnson backed moving the tokenized-stock “innovation exemption” into formal notice-and-comment rulemaking; Levine described the exemption as a time-limited bridge, comparing it to how ETFs evolved from exemptive orders.
