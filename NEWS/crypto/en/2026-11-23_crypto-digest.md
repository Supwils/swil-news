## II. Regulation & Policy

### 2. Regulation: CFTC Staff Advisory Allows Bitcoin, Ether and Payment Stablecoins Toward Some Financial-Resource Requirements

**Summary:** On Nov 23, the CFTC's Division of Market Oversight and Division of Clearing and Risk issued a staff advisory explaining how swap execution facilities, designated contract markets and derivatives clearing organizations may use crypto assets to meet financial-resources requirements under CFTC Regulations 37.1301, 38.1101(a) and 39.11(a)(2). The advisory says certain crypto assets — including bitcoin, ether and payment stablecoins — may count, subject to haircuts, toward resources covering at least 12 months of operating costs. Because crypto assets are not “cash” or “highly liquid securities” under GAAP, they do not count toward the separate liquidity requirement. The advisory reflects staff views only; it is not a new rule, a no-action position, or necessarily the Commission's view.

**Links:**

- [CFTC — Staff Advisory on Crypto Assets for Financial Resources Requirements](https://www.cftc.gov/PressRoom/PressReleases/9331-26)

**Commentary:** Letting major crypto assets into operating buffers is a modest compliance easing for derivatives venues, while the liquidity line still blocks treating them as cash.

### 3. Regulation: South Korea to Publish Digital Asset Basic Act Draft This Week; AML Freezes and Offshore Transfer Scrutiny Expand

**Summary:** South Korea's Financial Services Commission is preparing to release a government draft of the Digital Asset Basic Act later this week, local reports said — the country's second major crypto statute after the 2024 Virtual Asset User Protection Act. The draft is expected to define digital assets, set issuance and distribution rules, shift crypto businesses from notification toward licensing, and debate a roughly 15%–20% ownership cap for major exchange shareholders; won stablecoins are expected to follow a bank-led consortium model with banks holding more than 50%. The same day, FSC Chair Lee Won-geon said authorities would tighten scrutiny of transfers to high-risk overseas exchanges and private wallets, strengthen registration screening for crypto firms, and seek legal changes to expand phishing-account freezes to other serious financial crimes after more than 11,500 accounts were temporarily frozen from late June through October.

### 4. Regulation: Japan to Ramp Up Crypto Tax Audits After Uncovering About $24.5 Million in Hidden Income

**Summary:** CoinDesk reported on Nov 23 that Tokyo's regional tax bureau uncovered more than ¥3.9 billion (about $24.5 million) in undeclared crypto income across 91 audits in the prior fiscal year and assessed about ¥1.2 billion (about $7.5 million) in additional tax; many targets had filed no returns. On-site audits of individual crypto traders averaged about ¥12.58 million in extra tax per case — nearly three times the bureau's standard on-site income-tax audit average. From 2027, Japan will automatically receive annual trading data from foreign tax authorities under the OECD crypto-asset reporting framework to cross-check offshore activity.

**Summary:** On Nov 23 the U.S. Justice Department announced a civil forfeiture action targeting more than $1.1 billion in cryptocurrency proceeds attributed to North Korean cyber actors in the Oct 29 Binance theft — about $1.17 billion in USDC across 36 addresses on 18 blockchains in the complaint — and separately asked a court to keep the assets restrained.
