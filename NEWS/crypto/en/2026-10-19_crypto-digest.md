## I. Regulation and Policy

### 1. Treasury's GENIUS Act stablecoin rule closes to comments today; the SEC offering regime and a CFTC compute-derivatives request close tomorrow (Regulation)
**Summary:** Federal Register document 2026-16796 (91 FR 53368, RIN 1505-AC95) implements section 3 of the GENIUS Act, and written comments must be received on or before October 19, 2026. That section makes it unlawful for anyone other than a permitted issuer to issue a payment stablecoin in the United States. Knowing participation is punishable by a fine of not more than $1 million per violation, imprisonment of not more than five years, or both. GOV.HOUSE wrote Monday that the Act takes effect on January 18, 2027, 91 days after this comment window, whether or not a final rule is in place. The restriction on digital-asset service providers selling stablecoins from non-permitted issuers to people in the United States begins on July 18, 2028. Inside the same 48 hours, comments on the SEC's Regulation Crypto Assets (Release Nos. 33-11434 and 34-106150, File No. S7-2026-27) are due October 20. The startup exemption caps offerings at $5 million over four years, and the fundraising exemption caps them at $75 million in each 12-month period, alongside an investment-contract safe harbor. A CFTC request for comment on listing compute derivatives (document 2026-17163, 91 FR 54259, RIN 3038-AF77) closes the same day. The Commission preliminarily says compute markets are fragmented, that price formation occurs mainly in opaque bilateral trades, and that compute may not yet show fungibility, standardization, and sufficient liquidity. Its example underlier is the hourly rental price of compute from a B200. The voting summary records that Chairman Selig voted in the affirmative and no commissioner voted in the negative. Cointelegraph reported Monday that the SEC received about 140 comments over roughly 60 days, and that the dockets are moving after Congress failed to pass the CLARITY Act.

### 2. The Hyperliquid Policy Center files five requested changes the day before the SEC deadline (Regulation)
**Summary:** On October 19, 2026, the Hyperliquid Policy Center sent a comment letter to SEC Secretary Vanessa A. Countryman on Regulation Crypto Assets, File No. S7-2026-27. The letter says the group generally supports the proposal and urges prompt adoption, then lists five changes. It asks the Commission to codify the airdrop analysis in final rule text, anchored to a requirement that an investor furnish definable, tangible consideration. It asks for secondary-market transactions and intermediary status to be addressed so assets distributed under the exemptions can trade immediately outside the registrant framework. It asks that the safe harbor be usable without a tacit admission of prior investment-contract or issuer status, and that protection continue while essential managerial efforts are ongoing. It asks that recent Division of Corporation Finance buyback guidance be incorporated, distinguishing programmatic buybacks that follow published rules from discretionary buybacks by a central party. It also asks that federal preemption of state registration and qualification be durable rather than a reward for delay. Coindaro reports that the letter is signed by chief executive Jake Chervinsky and senior counsel Brad Bourque. None of the requests takes effect unless the Commission adopts them.

### 3. The CFTC chair restates a path to retail margined spot crypto without CLARITY, while CTX and CAM comments run to December 14 (Regulation)
**Summary:** Bankless published on October 19 a write-up of an interview with CFTC Chairman Mike Selig. He said that after the Senate failed to advance the CLARITY Act, the Commission can still use rulemaking to give exchanges a federal option for retail customers to trade spot crypto on margin, with leverage, or on a financed basis. Ordinary spot venues are licensed mainly under state money-transmitter regimes, and he said the agency cannot require every crypto asset to trade on a CFTC-registered platform without an act of Congress. The two rule sets he described match an advance notice published on October 13 (document 2026-20888, 91 FR 64811, RIN 3038-AF80): Regulation Crypto Asset Transactions and Regulation Crypto Asset Markets. Comments must be received on or before December 14, 2026. In the interview he also said that even if brokerage, exchange, and clearing sit in one group, a federal framework would have to separate those duties, protect customer funds, and limit conflicts, so the structure does not repeat FTX. The text is still an advance notice, not a permission to offer margin.

**Links:**

### 4. Crypto.com's exchange and Novig sue Missouri, arguing sports event contracts sit under federal commodities law (Litigation)
**Summary:** Unchained reported Monday that two CFTC-regulated exchanges sued in Missouri federal court on October 16 to stop the state from treating sports event contracts as unlicensed betting. Ludlow Exchange, which operates Novig, filed a 42-page complaint in the Eastern District against Attorney General Catherine Hanaway, Missouri Gaming Commission Executive Director Michael A. Leara, and commission Chair Jan Zimmerman. North American Derivatives Exchange, owned by Crypto.com, sued in the Western District, naming Hanaway and members of the gaming commission. In September, Hanaway's office sent cease-and-desist letters to Robinhood, Underdog, Novig, Crypto.com, Kalshi, and Polymarket, telling them to stop offering sports event contracts to Missourians unless they obtain a state gaming license. Novig's complaint says its letter set a deadline of October 17. Novig says the CFTC designated it as a contract market in June, that its event contracts are swaps under the Commodity Exchange Act, and that state gambling law is preempted. As of Monday, no enforcement action against either exchange had been announced, and federal records showed no parallel suits from Kalshi, Polymarket, Robinhood, or Underdog. A related appeal, KalshiEX v. Bird, is at the Eighth Circuit, with briefing set to finish on December 6.

### 7. JPMorgan deploys the dollar deposit token JPM Coin on Ethereum mainnet (Institutions)
**Summary:** Kinexys by J.P. Morgan said on October 19 that JPM Coin (ticker JPMD), its dollar deposit token, is live on Ethereum mainnet for the bank's institutional clients, alongside the deployment on Base that began in November 2025. The bank describes the token as a deposit token, not a cryptocurrency and not a stablecoin. Each unit represents dollars on deposit at JPMorgan, the funds stay inside regulated banking infrastructure, and only vetted counterparties can hold and transfer it. The firm says clients can move money, post collateral, and settle on Ethereum and Base, and can move between cash and the deposit token. Kinexys has processed more than $4 trillion in cumulative transactions since inception, with average daily volume of about $7 billion, calculated from internal volume and value data. Matthew Dawson, co-founder of Ethereum Institutional, said in the announcement that the step follows JPM Coin on Base and tokenized money-market funds from J.P. Morgan Asset Management on Ethereum. Emma Landriault, global head of Kinexys Labs, said issuing first on Base and now on Ethereum gives institutional clients a multi-chain option for moving wholesale money.
