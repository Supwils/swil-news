## I. Security Incidents & Market Shock

### 1. Binance Hot-Wallet Breach Hits ~$6.3B — Largest Crypto Theft on Record; ~$3B Frozen (Security)
**Summary:** Binance confirmed an Oct. 29, 2026 hot-wallet security incident with an estimated impact of about $6.3 billion — the largest crypto theft on record. Co-CEO Richard Teng said the firm will cover all losses with company funds, having reserved more than roughly $6.34 billion, and paused withdrawals while rebuilding withdrawal controls, with a restart targeted in about 40–45 hours. Stablecoin issuers and partners have frozen about $3 billion in related assets, including more than ~$1.57 billion USDT at Tether and more than ~$800 million USDC at Circle. TRM Labs, Elliptic, and Chainalysis attribute the attack with high confidence to North Korea–linked TraderTraitor, citing EIP-7702 and compromised signing infrastructure; the FBI said it is coordinating with partners but has not issued a formal attribution. Binance said cold-storage customer funds were untouched and that investigators found breach points beyond the initial compromised-laptop theory.

## II. Regulation & Policy

### 3. Korea FIU Refers Three Unregistered VASPs and Seeks Access Blocks for Two (Regulation)
**Summary:** On Oct. 30, 2026, South Korea’s Financial Intelligence Unit (FIU) under the Financial Services Commission said the Digital Asset eXchange Alliance (DAXA) referred Aegis Ventures, TetherMax, and SuperTrust for investigation as unregistered virtual-asset operators under the Specialized Financial Information Act. FIU asked the Broadcasting Media and Communications Review Commission to block domestic access to TetherMax and SuperTrust websites and apps (including service termination); Aegis was excluded because it had no separate site or app. Unregistered operation can bring up to five years in prison or a 50 million won fine, including for overseas firms targeting Korean users. FIU said the cumulative tally of unregistered operators reported to investigators is now 47; a June joint probe found average fees of about 1.5%–10%, up to about 62 times the 0.16% average at Korea’s five largest won exchanges.

**Links:**

- [FSC / FIU — Investigation referrals and access-block request](https://www.fsc.go.kr/no010101/88039)
- [Asia Business Daily — Three illegal VASPs referred; two access blocks sought](https://www.asiae.co.kr/en/article/2026103016094883766)

**Commentary:** Pairing criminal referrals with access blocks squeezes both liquidity and onboarding for unregistered platforms, locking in the fee and distribution edge of registered Korean exchanges.

### 4. Japan’s FSA Releases 609-Page Survey of Overseas Financial Systems, Including Crypto and RWA Rules (Regulation)
**Summary:** On Oct. 30, 2026, Japan’s Financial Services Agency published a roughly 609-page commissioned survey of foreign financial systems prepared by Atsumi & Sakai, covering the United States, the United Kingdom, the European Union, Germany, France, Singapore, China, and Hong Kong as a reference for domestic policymaking. Crypto chapters compare exchange, stablecoin, and real-world-asset (RWA) tokenization regimes, including the U.S. GENIUS Act, the EU’s MiCA framework, UK stablecoin proposals, and China’s Feb. 6, 2026 notice that generally bans domestic RWA issuance, trading, and related intermediary/technical services except with formal approval via specified financial infrastructure. The Hong Kong section covers expanded tokenized-securities trading and cross-border order-book integration.

**Links:**

- [TokenPost — Japan’s FSA publishes 609-page overseas systems survey](https://www.tokenpost.com/news/regulation/44736)
- [CoinPost — FSA survey maps overseas crypto and stablecoin rules](https://coinpost.jp/?p=745690)

**Commentary:** The report is not new law, but after Japan’s move of crypto into the Financial Instruments and Exchange Act framework, these overseas benchmarks will shape upcoming implementing rules and ETF/stablecoin timing.

## III. DeFi & Protocols

### 5. Aave Unfreezes All V3/V4 Markets After ~15-Hour Precautionary Pause; Nine Assets Stay at 0% Collateral (DeFi)
**Summary:** After the Binance hot-wallet incident, an exploiter cluster deposited about 60 million USDT on Aave and borrowed about 19,762 WETH (~$44.6 million). Risk managers froze new deposits and borrows across many V3/V4 markets from about 22:44 UTC on Oct. 29 (withdrawals and repayments remained available). Roughly 15 hours later, at about 13:57 UTC on Oct. 30, Aave said all V3 and V4 markets were unfrozen, stressing that smart contracts were unaffected and positions stayed fully collateralized. Risk Stewards temporarily cut Slope2 on major stables and some WETH so full-utilization borrow rates would not exceed about 10% APR, and trimmed borrow caps to roughly 2% above pre-exploit usage — removing about $3.7 billion of unused V3 capacity and about $130 million on V4. USDT, USDC, USDT0, POL, WAVAX, sAVAX, XAUT, PAXG, and OP remain at 0% LTV/collateral factor and cannot back new borrows.

**Links:**

- [Aave Governance — Binance hot-wallet exploit response and unfreeze plan](https://governance.aave.com/t/binance-hot-wallet-exploit-aaves-response/25954/18)
- [TokenPost — Aave reopens deposits and borrowing on V3/V4](https://www.tokenpost.com/news/technology/44859)

**Commentary:** Instant DeFi circuit-breakers after CEX shocks are now table stakes; keeping a zero-collateral keep-list shows the fear is re-leveraging stolen stables, not a protocol bug.
