## I. Regulation & Policy

### 1. CFTC Chair Selig: major exchanges and new entrants are opting into the agency’s crypto rules (Regulation)
**Summary:** CFTC Chairman Michael Selig told CNBC on November 13 that, after the Clarity Act stalled in the Senate in September, major crypto exchanges and new entrants are showing interest in the agency’s proposed federal framework for digital assets. The CFTC issued an advance notice on October 5 seeking comment on retail crypto commodity transactions; Selig said many exchanges already registered for derivatives—including perpetual futures—could add spot trading under a regulated rulebook, while new firms say navigating 50 state regimes is harder than seeking a single CFTC license. He also defended exclusive federal jurisdiction over prediction markets and said the agency is prepared to litigate “up to the Supreme Court” if needed; with Democrats set to control Congress after the midterms, the framework may face sharper scrutiny in 2027.

### 2. DOJ and CFTC sue Arkansas to block state enforcement against Kalshi prediction markets (Regulation)
**Summary:** The CFTC announced on November 13 that, together with the Justice Department, it has sued Arkansas and Attorney General Tim Griffin in the Eastern District of Arkansas, seeking to enjoin state enforcement against Kalshi, a CFTC-designated contract market. The suit follows Arkansas’s November 4 state-court action alleging Kalshi’s event contracts are illegal gambling and seeking civil penalties and injunctive relief. Federal plaintiffs argue the contracts are “swaps” under the Commodity Exchange Act, that DCM trading falls under exclusive CFTC jurisdiction, and that state gambling and consumer-protection laws are preempted. The CFTC said Arkansas is the 11th state it has sued to vindicate those federal interests.

**Links:**

- [CFTC — Lawsuit against Arkansas to defend federal jurisdiction over prediction markets](https://www.cftc.gov/PressRoom/PressReleases/9325-26)
- [The Block — DOJ, CFTC sue Arkansas to block enforcement against Kalshi](https://www.theblock.co/news/regulation/2026-11-13-doj-cftc-sue-arkansas-to-block-state-enforcement-action-against-kalshi-439669)

**Commentary:** Prediction markets have moved from product innovation into a federal–state jurisdiction fight—the outcome will decide whether event contracts can scale under a single national license.

### 4. Dual EU supervision of USDC as a significant e-money token takes effect from November 12 (Regulation)
**Summary:** The European Banking Authority previously classified USDC issued by Circle Internet Financial Europe SAS as the EU’s first “significant” e-money token under MiCA (among 45 notified EMTs at the time). From November 12, 2026, dual supervision is live: the EBA directly oversees own funds, reserve assets, liquidity requirements, and recovery and redemption plans, while France’s ACPR retains governance, qualifying holdings, fit-and-proper assessments, outsourcing, and conflicts of interest. The EBA also signed an MoU with the New York Department of Financial Services to support cross-border stablecoin oversight; officials did not announce changes to holder redemption terms.

**Links:**

- [EBA — USDC classified as first significant e-money token in the EU](https://www.eba.europa.eu/publications-and-media/press-releases/eba-classifies-usdc-first-significant-e-money-token-european-union)
- [NewsBTC — USDC enters dual EU supervision under MiCA](https://www.newsbtc.com/regulation/usdc-eba-dual-supervision-november-12-mica/)

**Commentary:** The “significant EMT” label puts a dollar stablecoin under EU-level reserve and liquidity supervision for the first time—watch stress tests and own-funds add-ons, not the peg itself.
