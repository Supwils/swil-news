## I. Regulation and Policy

### 1. The CFTC's Advance Notice on Retail Crypto Transactions Is Published in the Federal Register, with Comments Due December 14 (Regulation)
**Summary:** The Commodity Futures Trading Commission's advance notice of proposed rulemaking on Regulation Crypto Asset Transactions and Regulation Crypto Asset Markets appeared in the Federal Register on October 13, Volume 91, Number 196 (document 2026-20888, RIN 3038-AF80), at pages 64811-64840. The notice seeks comment on retail commodity transactions in crypto assets, which it calls CTXs, under section 2(c)(2)(D) of the Commodity Exchange Act, and on writing into rules a subcategory of designated contract market built for those transactions, called a crypto asset market. Written comments must be received by December 14, 2026. The CFTC's October 5 press release said the questions cover preventing abuse under a uniform national regime, codifying practices the agency has treated as industry best practice since 2014, and creating a contract-market category purpose-built for CTXs. A footnote, citing the GENIUS Act, says a payment stablecoin issued by a permitted payment stablecoin issuer is outside the Act's definition of a commodity.

### 2. Korea's Financial Intelligence Unit Blocks Websites of 27 Unregistered Exchanges, Including KuCoin, MEXC, and BitMart (Regulation)
**Summary:** CoinDesk reported on October 13 that Korea's Financial Intelligence Unit said Tuesday the Korea Communications Standards Commission had approved access limits on 27 unregistered crypto-exchange websites after reviews on October 6 and 7. The names include KuCoin, BitMart, and MEXC. Sites run from overseas will be blocked, and those run domestically face service termination. Regulators said they can seek a block as soon as they detect unregistered activity, without waiting for a court ruling or evidence that customers have already lost money. The FIU did not say how long the limits will last or set a registration deadline. An exchange that wants to serve Korean customers legally must register with the FIU and meet security and anti-money-laundering requirements. The agency has referred 44 unregistered operators to investigators, only 28 virtual-asset service providers are registered, and it said it will work with the Digital Asset eXchange Alliance to help affected customers withdraw funds. A KuCoin spokesperson said the firm respects applicable laws and that user assets remain safe. CoinDesk also wrote that an estimated $110 billion in crypto left domestic Korean exchanges in 2025.

## IV. Protocols and Infrastructure

### 7. Aptos Turns On an Optional Encrypted Mempool on Mainnet so Validators Cannot Read a Transaction While Ordering It (Protocols)
**Summary:** Aptos said on its site on October 12 that an encrypted mempool is live on mainnet under AIP-144. Encryption is optional per transaction: the address and the fee stay visible, and the contents do not. The client encrypts the payload with a key the validator set generates jointly each epoch. The block leader proposes an order without seeing the function or its arguments. After the block is final, validators decrypt together and then execute. A subset of validators below the chain's two-thirds honest-stake threshold cannot decrypt, so the design adds no trust party beyond the existing consensus assumption. Aptos said batched threshold encryption added about 20 milliseconds of latency per block in 50-node benchmarks against production AptosBFT. Petra is the first wallet to offer the option in the signing flow. The perpetual-futures exchange Decibel will use it to keep orders private until execution. TypeScript SDK 7.0.0 and later supports it, existing Move contracts need no changes, and ZKSecurity has completed an independent security review.

**Links:**

- [Aptos — Encrypted mempool, now on mainnet](https://aptosnetwork.com/currents/encrypted-mempool-mainnet)

**Commentary:** Frontrunning and sandwich attacks lose plaintext in the mempool, while the sender and the fee stay public, so the protection covers the payload and not anonymity.

## V. Security

### 9. Ledger Says an Implant in CryptoBilis Devices Read Recovery Phrases Off the Screen and Sent Them Over a Cellular Network (Security)
**Summary:** CoinDesk reported on October 13, citing an investigation update Ledger posted on X, that an unauthorized chip was implanted in some hardware signers sold by Southeast Asian reseller CryptoBilis. The chip sits inside the enclosure. When the screen shows the 24-word recovery phrase during setup, a microcontroller recognizes and decodes the words and transmits them over LTE using an eSIM. Ledger said the implant did not modify the secure element, the operating system, transactions, or signatures, and described a physical supply-chain attack rather than a breach of its software or internal systems. The company confirmed one affected signer contained an implant and said every confirmed loss in the October 9 incident involved a device sold by CryptoBilis. It has not verified theft from a signer bought elsewhere. Ledger has not confirmed the number of victims or the amount stolen. CoinDesk said onchain researchers estimated losses of up to $93.2 million across more than 300 wallets and that Tether froze $10 million of related USDT. SecurityWeek cited onchain investigator Specter for an estimate above $86 million. Ledger also said European retailer MediaMarktSaturn stopped selling the devices after a customer in Germany reported an implant, and that CryptoBilis has stopped selling Ledger signers. Customers who bought one from that reseller and have not initialized it were told not to initialize it; those who already used one were told to move assets to a new device with a newly generated phrase.
