## II. Regulation & Policy

### 3. Regulation: South Korea's FSC Unveils Digital Asset Basic Act Draft — Bank-Majority Won Stablecoins and a 20% Exchange Stake Cap

**Summary:** On Nov 24, South Korea's Financial Services Commission convened its virtual-asset committee to advance a government bill on digital-asset industry innovation and user protection. Won-pegged stablecoins (styled “digital payment tokens”) would initially be limited to consortia in which banks collectively hold a majority stake, while a single fintech or technology firm could still be the largest individual shareholder. Issuers would need FSC authorization, at least 5 billion won in equity capital, reserves covering 100% of outstanding tokens, no interest payments to holders, and prompt face-value redemption. Platform operators would face a 20% ownership cap (up to 34% with approval), with excess shares stripped of voting rights and about three years of transition for incumbents. The draft also opens a path to lift the domestic ICO ban, and foreign stablecoin issuers would need a local branch and approval before tokens can trade.

### 4. Regulation: SEC Corp Fin Issues Molecule No-Action Relief for Scientific Research Tokens (Facts-Limited)

**Summary:** On Nov 24, the SEC Division of Corporation Finance issued a staff no-action letter on Swiss decentralized-science firm Molecule AG's “Coin-to-Company” model. Based on the facts presented and counsel's opinion, staff said it would not recommend enforcement if LabsCo offers and sells Project Tokens as described without Securities Act Section 5 registration and without Exchange Act Section 12(g) registration as a class of equity securities. The tokens are described as conferring no equity, profit rights, or enforceable financial claims; locking tokens only enables non-binding research-direction votes. The letter addresses staff recommendations only and could change if facts differ; Molecule said its first ecosystem project would use the model within weeks.

**Links:**

## IV. Protocol & Security Buildout

### 7. Protocol: Ethereum Foundation Doubles Protocol Security Team, Calls AI a More Urgent Risk Than Quantum; Advances PQAI Hash-Based Roadmap

**Summary:** In PQAI blog posts published Nov 23–24, Ethereum Foundation researcher Kev Wedderburn said the Foundation has doubled its protocol security team so formal verification can keep pace with a post-quantum, post-AI upgrade. The Foundation now treats AI as a more urgent near-term risk than quantum computing, citing models that already find production bugs faster than human review and are beginning to improve long-standing algorithms. The plan moves validator signatures, user-account signatures, and rollup data commitments to hash-based cryptography; a Nov 24 follow-up recommended Keccak-256 as the hash primitive. The Foundation called the upgrade harder than the Merge; Justin Drake floated a December 2028 target at Devcon, while the EF's internal deadline remains 2029.

## V. Security & Incident Reviews

### 8. Security: Coinkite Publishes Full Coldcard Seed-Generation Postmortem — Five-Year Weak-Entropy Flaw Tied to ~1,800 BTC Thefts

**Summary:** Coinkite published an ~18-page Coldcard seed-generation postmortem on Nov 23 (widely covered Nov 24): from March 29, 2021 through July 30, 2026, affected firmware used MicroPython's Yasmarang software PRNG instead of the STM32 hardware RNG because of a configuration/linkage error, cutting effective entropy to roughly 40 bits (Mk2/Mk3) or 72 bits (Mk4/Mk5/Q). Attackers began regenerating private keys offline on July 29 without remotely compromising devices; on-chain estimates put thefts at about 1,778–1,830 BTC (~$116 million at the time) across roughly 8,600–9,200 addresses. As of Nov 18, Coinkite had about 300 loss-related tickets tied to 242 accounts. Fixed firmware shipped July 31, but weak seeds still require migration to new seeds. A related proposed class action remains uncertified.
