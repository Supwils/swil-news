## I. Regulation & Policy

### 1. UK FCA Settles With HTX; Exchange Exits the British Market (Regulation)
**Summary:** On Oct. 29, 2026, the UK Financial Conduct Authority said it secured High Court undertakings ending its case against HTX (formerly Huobi; defendant Huobi Global S.A.) over alleged unlawful crypto promotions to UK consumers. The FCA said HTX is no longer active in the UK: deposits are unavailable, new UK users cannot register, and the firm must not target or solicit UK consumers via its website, apps, or social media, with undertakings running to Oct. 21, 2031. Settlement terms are confidential and do not constitute an admission of liability. The FCA sued in October 2025 and publicized enforcement in February 2026, ahead of a broader UK crypto regime due in October 2027.

### 2. Bank of England Consults on Tokenised Collateral in Central Clearing (Regulation)
**Summary:** On Oct. 29, 2026, the Bank of England opened a consultation clarifying how central counterparties (CCPs) may accept tokenised forms of eligible collateral under the existing framework. The Bank said current rules are not a barrier if tokenised assets can still be valued, transferred, and realised—including in stress—and that the proposed supervisory statement would clarify how applications are assessed without creating new obligations. Comments close Feb. 12, 2027, as part of a wider BoE–FCA–Treasury agenda on wholesale-market tokenisation.

**Links:**

- [Bank of England — Tokenised collateral consultation](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/news/2026/october/bank-of-england-sets-out-approach-to-tokenised-collateral-in-central-clearing)
- [Cointelegraph — BoE opens consultation on tokenized collateral for clearing](https://cointelegraph.com/news/bank-of-england-opens-consultation-on-tokenized-collateral-for-clearing)

### 4. Crypto Innovation Council Urges SEC to Weigh Onchain Vault Operator Control (Regulation)
**Summary:** TokenPost reported on Oct. 29, 2026 that the Crypto Innovation Council, joined by Bitwise, Galaxy Digital, and Morpho, submitted a roughly 238-page report urging the SEC to weigh onchain vault operators’ actual control over investor assets when applying securities laws—arguing that greater discretion over investments, lending terms, and fund movements strengthens the case for oversight. For vaults that meet the investment-company definition, CCI recommends case-by-case exemptions before broader rules, and clearer guidance on when operators must register as advisers and how custody rules apply when smart contracts manage assets. Whether the SEC will adopt the recommendations is unclear.

**Links:**

- [TokenPost — CCI urges SEC to weigh vault operator control](https://www.tokenpost.com/news/regulation/43719)

**Commentary:** Putting “control” at the center of Howey/Investment Company Act analysis is a necessary compliance step before DeFi vaults can scale to institutions.

### 7. Switzerland’s First Retail Spot Bitcoin ETF Wins Rare FINMA Exemption; SIX Listing Eyed for November (ETF)
**Summary:** CoinDesk reported on Oct. 29, 2026 that BIT Asset Management’s Bitcoin Fund ETF (ticker BBIT)—approved by FINMA on Oct. 20—is Switzerland’s first exchange-traded retail fund investing directly in bitcoin and received a rare exemption from diversification rules allowing a full bitcoin allocation. Trading on SIX Swiss Exchange is expected in November, with no exact date set. Unlike many European bitcoin products structured as debt ETPs, BBIT is a collective investment scheme with bitcoin segregated from the manager’s assets. Public sale is approved only in Switzerland; overseas brokers can access the fund via SIX, but offering shares to clients elsewhere depends on local law. FINMA stressed that bitcoin remains highly risky even via an ETF and that the decision does not automatically clear ether or solana ETFs.

## V. Security & Incidents

### 12. Blockchain Data Provider Allium Discloses Unauthorized Access; Revokes API Keys (Security)
**Summary:** CryptoTimes and crypto.news followed up on Oct. 29 that Allium—whose customers include Visa, Stripe, and Coinbase—detected unauthorized access to certain systems on Oct. 25, restricted access, notified customers, and hired an independent cybersecurity firm. By Oct. 28, core Explorer and APIs were restored, while Assistant and MCP remained unavailable; existing API keys were revoked, with password resets and multifactor authentication recommended, and uploaded-data access temporarily disabled. Datashares to Snowflake, Databricks, and BigQuery were interrupted, with some chain datasets still being verified and backfilled. Allium has not disclosed how the intrusion occurred, who was responsible, or whether customer data was accessed or stolen.
