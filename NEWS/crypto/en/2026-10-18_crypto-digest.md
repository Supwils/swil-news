## I. Regulation and Policy

### 1. Comments on Treasury's GENIUS Act stablecoin issuance rule are due October 19 (Stablecoins)
**Summary:** The Federal Register shows a Treasury notice of proposed rulemaking on payment-stablecoin issuance, offer, and sale under section 3 of the GENIUS Act, docket TREAS-DO-2026-0496, RIN 1505-AC95, 91 FR 53368. Comments must be received on or before October 19, 2026. The statute it implements makes it unlawful for anyone other than a permitted payment stablecoin issuer to issue a payment stablecoin in the United States. Knowing participation is punishable by a fine of not more than $1 million for each violation, imprisonment for not more than five years, or both. The proposal also says a foreign issuer that meets the criteria in section 18(a), including registration with the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency, may issue in the United States. The Act's effective date is expected to be January 18, 2027, eighteen months after enactment on July 18, 2025. The broader offer-and-sale restriction for digital-asset service providers begins on July 18, 2028. As of Sunday the text is still a proposal.

### 3. California's attorney general settles with Fidelity Digital Assets for $500,000 over past crypto withdrawal limits (Compliance)
**Summary:** On October 16, California Attorney General Rob Bonta announced a $500,000 settlement with Fidelity Digital Asset Services, the crypto subsidiary of Fidelity Investments, resolving allegations that past limits on withdrawing certain cryptocurrencies violated the commodity-contract ban in the California Commodity Law. State law prohibits commodity contracts entered mainly for speculation or investment unless the dealer physically delivers the commodity within 28 days of payment. For cryptocurrencies treated as commodities, delivery means the investor can withdraw the asset from the platform. The announcement says the firm was waiting for New York Department of Financial Services permission, which came in April 2025, after which withdrawals were enabled, and that the company disputes any violation. The executed agreement names the successor as Fidelity Digital Assets, National Association. From November 2022 through April 2025, California retail customers could buy and sell but could not transfer crypto directly off the platform, and the feature was rolled out to all California customers by June 2025. The entity converted on December 12, 2025, into a national trust bank regulated by the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency. The agreement requires a $500,000 payment for the attorney general's fees and costs and in settlement of the released claims, without an admission of liability. California customers can now transfer crypto to external wallets. The firm may still restrict transfers for safety and security, and it must give reasonable written notice if it stops offering the feature.

**Links:**

- [California Attorney General — Settlement with Fidelity over crypto withdrawal restrictions](https://oag.ca.gov/news/press-releases/attorney-general-bonta-holds-crypto-platforms-accountable-secures-settlement)
- [Settlement agreement PDF — People of California and Fidelity Digital Assets](https://oag.ca.gov/system/files/attachments/press-docs/fdas-settlement-agreement-fully-executed-10142026.pdf)

**Commentary:** The $500,000 closes the period when California retail customers could buy and sell but not move coins off the platform, and the agreement records that transfers are available now, with a notice duty if that feature is withdrawn.

### 10. Qubic halts its mainnet after 1,404 wallets lose about $1.3 million of QU, blaming weak seeds on legacy Apple devices (Security)
**Summary:** CryptoBriefing reported Sunday that Qubic, a decentralized AI computing network, paused its mainnet on October 18. The attack ran from 23:42 UTC on October 17 to 01:26 UTC on October 18, about 104 minutes, and drained 1,404 user wallets of about 2.94 trillion QU, worth about $1.3 million at the time. Developers said it was not a bug in the network's code. The seeds were weak and predictable, created on legacy Apple devices, Safari, and early iOS applications. Validators stopped the chain at tick 85,371,949. About 87.1% of the drained QU never left the Qubic network and is now immobilized on-chain. The other 12.9% reached MEXC, which froze the receiving account within an hour. Bitget suspended QUBIC deposits and withdrawals. The team shipped client 4.26.4 to fix seed generation and said it secured other funds exposed to the same flaw before they could be taken. The QEARN staking feature was not affected. Compensation sits with the Computor cluster that runs the network, and affected users are being told to file police reports. It is Qubic's second peripheral incident of 2026, after the Vottun Bridge exploit in August.

**Commentary:** The chain is stopped and most of the tokens are still on it. Whether users are repaid depends on a Computor compensation vote and on how MEXC treats the frozen account.
