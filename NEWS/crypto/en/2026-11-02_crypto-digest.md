## I. Regulation & Policy

### 1. SEC Corp Fin Updates Crypto FAQ: Governance Voters vs. “Central Party” (Regulation)
**Summary:** On Nov. 2, 2026, the SEC Division of Corporation Finance updated its FAQs on federal securities laws and crypto assets, adding Questions 1.4–1.6. Staff said participants in a functional crypto system’s broad-based governance generally are not a “group of persons or entities” for the “central party” definition if they are unaffiliated and have no agreement or understanding to act in concert (including via delegation) beyond that governance; even if they are a group, they still need operational, economic, or voting control. The FAQ also explains “overseeing participation” (e.g., unilaterally granting or limiting access, setting upgrade voting, controlling consensus participation, or changing rules code) and distinguishes “functional” from “decentralized.” The answers are staff views only—not Commission rules or statements—and have no legal force.

**Links:**

- [SEC.gov — FAQs on federal securities laws and crypto assets (updated Nov. 2, 2026)](https://www.sec.gov/about/divisions-offices/division-corporation-finance/faqs-crypto-assets)
- [crypto.news — SEC FAQ adds three entries on decentralized governance](https://crypto.news/sec-crypto-faq-decentralized-governance-token-taxonomy/)

**Commentary:** Within a still highly facts-and-circumstances token taxonomy, the FAQ gives projects a citable line that broad governance alone does not equal a central party—without replacing a full securities analysis.

## II. Security Incidents

### 2. Brazil’s Mercado Bitcoin Hit Across ~10 Chains; Losses Estimated at ~$40M–$46.3M (Security)
**Summary:** Mercado Bitcoin, Brazil’s largest crypto exchange, confirmed a Nov. 2 security incident and paused deposits and withdrawals. Security firms and Arkham estimated attackers drained hot wallets across at least 10 blockchains (including Ethereum, Bitcoin, Solana, Tron, Polygon, Arbitrum, Avalanche, BSC, and Stellar), with losses above about $40 million and some tallies near $46.3 million. Reports described ordinary signed transfers rather than a smart-contract exploit; the attacker reportedly held roughly 2,852 ETH, over 100 BTC, about 10,700 SOL, and many other tokens. The exchange said it contained the incident, customers and data were unaffected, and published destination addresses. Tether CEO Paolo Ardoino confirmed roughly $4.5 million in related USDT had been frozen. There is no public evidence linking the breach to Binance’s ~$6.3 billion theft.
