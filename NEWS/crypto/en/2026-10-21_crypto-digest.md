## I. Regulation and Policy

### 1. The OCC publishes a final GENIUS Act rule and gives existing stablecoin rewards no automatic pass (Regulation)
**Summary:** The Office of the Comptroller of the Currency published news release 2026-92 on October 21, 2026, setting out a final rule to implement the GENIUS Act for permitted payment-stablecoin issuers and foreign issuers under its jurisdiction, and for certain custody activities by OCC-supervised entities. The rule covers the regulations the OCC must write under the Act. Bank Secrecy Act, anti-money-laundering, and Office of Foreign Assets Control sanctions requirements will be handled in a separate rulemaking with the Treasury Department. The final rule and the Act take effect on January 18, 2027. The OCC said it will announce separately when it will begin accepting issuer applications and waiver requests. CoinDesk reported that the final text does not presume rewards programs that predated the Act are compliant, and that the agency will apply the statute to the facts of each arrangement. The Act bars payments made solely for holding, using, or retaining a payment stablecoin, so a reward made at least partly for another reason may fall outside the ban. CoinDesk cited PayPal's 4% return on PYUSD, Coinbase One's 3.75% on USDC, and up to 4.5% on USDG for Kraken+ subscribers.

**Links:**

- [CoinDesk — OCC final rule will weigh stablecoin rewards case by case against the Act's ban](https://www.coindesk.com/policy/2026/10/21/occ-s-final-rule-to-start-fresh-in-weighing-stablecoin-rewards-against-genius-act-ban)

**Commentary:** The first federal final rule puts issuance, reserves, and redemption on a January 2027 clock, while exchange rewards still need a case-by-case pass and cannot rely on having existed already.

### 2. France's Treasury and three regulators answer the MiCA review and assign responsibility for DeFi access (Regulation)
**Summary:** On October 21, France's Treasury, the Autorite des Marches Financiers (AMF), the Banque de France, and the Autorite de Controle Prudentiel et de Resolution (ACPR) published a joint response to the European Commission's consultation on revising the Markets in Crypto-Assets Regulation (MiCA). They argue that crypto and traditional-finance regimes should stay legally separate while being better coordinated: a financial instrument remains fully subject to financial-instrument law whatever technology is used to issue or trade it. They support euro e-money tokens and tokenized bank deposits, and they ask for an explicit ban on EU-authorized crypto-asset service providers offering services in stablecoins that do not meet MiCA. For multi-issuance, meaning fungible stablecoins issued both in the Union and in third countries, they propose equivalence assessments, supervisory cooperation, and extra guarantees on reserves and redemption. On DeFi, they want clearly assigned intermediary duties, appropriate due diligence, and protocol certification, plus clearer rules for staking and for crypto lending and borrowing. Cointelegraph quoted the authorities as saying service providers should be liable for certain incidents affecting clients where they actively facilitated access. The Commission must report to EU lawmakers by June 30, 2027, and that report may include amendments.

**Links:**

- [AMF — French authorities publish joint proposals on the MiCA revision](https://www.amf-france.org/en/news-publications/news-releases/amf-news-releases/mica-regulation-revision-french-authorities-publish-their-joint-proposals)
- [Cointelegraph — France proposes liability for crypto platforms that offer DeFi access](https://cointelegraph.com/news/what-happened-in-crypto-today)

**Commentary:** This is advice to the Commission, not an amendment already in force. If it is taken up, licensed platforms that actively connect users to protocols would be the first place liability attaches.

### 3. The UK FCA opens two consultations on DeFi duties and the crypto regulatory perimeter (Regulation)
**Summary:** The UK Financial Conduct Authority opened two consultations on October 21, 2026, both closing on November 18, 2026. Guidance consultation GC26/7 is aimed at regulated firms that provide financial services using decentralized finance and that are subject to operational-resilience rules in SYSC 15A, compliance and financial-crime rules in SYSC 6, and the Money Laundering Regulations. It proposes non-Handbook guidance on how those existing duties apply, and the FCA plans to publish final guidance in the first quarter of 2027. Consultation paper CP26/36 explains new exclusions in the draft Cryptoassets (Miscellaneous Amendments) Regulations 2026, including temporary settlement arrangements, temporary UK qualifying-stablecoin holding arrangements for payment transactions, arrangements for backing assets of such a stablecoin, proprietary trading that is not a service to another person or that is market-making on a qualifying cryptoasset trading platform, and technical services that merely allow access to a third party's service or to a decentralized protocol. The FCA said the new regime is scheduled to take effect on October 25, 2027. Where uncertainty over the technical-services exclusion has materially affected whether an application was complete, the agency will allow extra time on the unfinished arranging-permission points.

**Links:**

- [FCA — GC26/7, decentralized finance guidance consultation](https://www.fca.org.uk/publications/guidance-consultations/gc26-7-decentralised-finance-guidance)
- [FCA — CP26/36, cryptoasset perimeter guidance](https://www.fca.org.uk/publications/consultation-papers/cp26-36-cryptoasset-perimeter-guidance)

**Commentary:** What opened today is guidance on existing duties and a list of exclusions, not a new license category. Whether pure technical access stays outside the perimeter depends on the text after November 18.

### 4. Utah sues Kalshi and two Susquehanna firms, seeking up to about $61.2 million in fees (Litigation)
**Summary:** crypto.news reported on October 21 that Utah Attorney General Derek Brown, in an October 20 complaint, accused prediction-market operator Kalshi and its affiliates of facilitating illegal gambling since September 2024. The case is in Utah's Third District Court and names Susquehanna International Group and Susquehanna Government Products. The state estimates that fees from Utah-linked accounts through October 19 were between $30.6 million and $61.2 million, based on more than three billion wagers and about one to two cents collected per transaction. The complaint also alleges about $5.5 million in losses on individual wagers and says more than 32,000 verified users were in the state as of February. Those figures are the state's estimates and have not been found as damages. Utah seeks to block contracts on sports, elections, politics, culture, and mentions, plus disgorgement and court-approved geolocation limits. Kalshi spokesperson Jacki McGavick said that, as a national derivatives market overseen by the Commodity Futures Trading Commission, Kalshi answers to federal law and that the state case would not affect its existing federal litigation. On August 4, U.S. District Judge Robert Shelby rejected Kalshi's preemption argument and granted summary judgment to Utah. In September the Tenth Circuit denied emergency protection while that appeal proceeds.

**Links:**

- [crypto.news — Utah sues Kalshi, seeking up to about $61.2 million over alleged illegal betting](https://crypto.news/utah-sues-kalshi-61-million-gambling-lawsuit/)

**Commentary:** The state court is being asked for fee recovery and a geofence, while the federal appeal continues. The $61.2 million figure is the top of the complaint's estimate, not a judgment.

## III. DeFi and Protocols

### 6. Solana's Alpenglow switches on mainnet, cutting finality from about 12.8 seconds to about 150 milliseconds (Protocol)
**Summary:** Anza, a core Solana engineering team, announced on October 21 that the Alpenglow consensus upgrade is live on Solana mainnet-beta. At about 14:00 UTC, at slot 459,653,000, validators retired TowerBFT, in place since March 2020, and handed the existing ledger to the new protocol without stopping the chain. Anza said the time for a transaction to become irreversible fell from about 12.8 seconds to about 150 milliseconds. The Solana Foundation page says mainnet activation is in epoch 1064, which started at about 13:20 UTC on October 21. This release ships Votor, the voting protocol. Rotor, which would replace the block-propagation layer, is not yet scheduled. Anza said Rotor, geographically aware leader rotation, and Quantumglow, a research design that would swap elliptic-curve cryptography for hash-based signatures, have no committed dates. Decrypt reported that validators approved SIMD-0236, the proposal that implements the upgrade, with 98% in favor in September 2025.

**Links:**

- [Anza — Alpenglow is live on Solana](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/alpenglow-is-live-on-solana-the-worlds-fastest-finality-on-a-global-network-302915579.html)
- [Solana — Alpenglow upgrade notes](https://solana.com/upgrades/alpenglow)

**Commentary:** What changed today is finality, not execution. The wait for an irreversible confirmation is designed to shrink, while block propagation and post-quantum work still have no dates.

### 7. Zcash developers propose NU7 at block 3,543,405, and the spec had not merged by Wednesday morning (Protocol)
**Summary:** CoinDesk reported Wednesday that Zcash developer Sean Bowe filed a proposal on Tuesday to activate network upgrade NU7 at block 3,543,405, expected around November 5. The height was agreed at a meeting of Zcash Improvement Proposal editors, and node software Zakura's upgrade dashboard lists that planned mainnet point. NU7 would cut the target time between blocks from 75 seconds to 25 seconds, so three confirmations could take about 75 seconds instead of nearly four minutes. It would also temporarily remove 60% of each block's fees from circulation, with payment to miners expected to begin around February 2031, while keeping the existing halving schedule. Sprout, the original privacy system, would shut down, and funds still there at activation would become unspendable. As of 08:00 UTC Wednesday the proposal had two approvals but had not been merged into the deployment specification, which still listed the mainnet height as undecided. Node software must also include the height before the upgrade can take effect. CoinDesk's market report the same day said zcash was up about 11% over 24 hours to about $1,260 and still down about 12% on the month, with open interest up 24% and annualized funding around 10%.

### 9. J.P. Morgan Asset Management's first bitcoin range puts the base case at 10% a year (Institutions)
**Summary:** Unchained on October 21 walked through the 31st edition of J.P. Morgan Asset Management's Long-Term Capital Market Assumptions. Using data as of September 30, 2026, and a 10- to 15-year horizon, the report gives bitcoin a return range for the first time: a 10% annualized compound base case, a bear case of -11.2%, and a bull case of 16.6%, starting from an $83,000 spot price. The authors weight three models by confidence: 65% on bitcoin's four-year supply cycle versus growth in the dollar and global money supply, 30% on treating bitcoin as a complement to gold, and 5% on use of the Bitcoin network. Network use is the only model with a negative base case, at -3.2%. In the same comparison, U.S. large caps are 7.5%, U.S. small caps 7.7%, the U.S. aggregate bond index 5.6%, U.S. high-yield bonds 6.6%, and gold 5.5%. Bitcoin's annualized volatility is listed at 55%. Authors Sheena Shah and Dennis Cristallo wrote that they do not make a case for bitcoin as a portfolio diversifier because its correlations are unstable, and they described it as a speculative asset and a hedge against monetary easing. Bitcoin is not in the firm's main covariance matrices. The report says crypto is worth more than $2.5 trillion and that about a tenth of clients surveyed for J.P. Morgan Private Bank's 2026 Global Family Office Report held crypto.
