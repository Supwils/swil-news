### 2. Comments on the SEC's Regulation Crypto Assets proposal are due October 20 (Offerings)
**Summary:** The SEC's rule page lists October 20, 2026, as the public-comment deadline for proposed Regulation Crypto Assets, file S7-2026-27, release numbers 33-11434 and 34-106150. The proposal was issued on August 18 and published in the Federal Register on August 21. It would create an offering regime for certain investment contracts involving crypto assets, with two exemptions from section 5 of the Securities Act of 1933: up to $5 million over a four-year period, and up to $75 million in any 12-month period. Issuers would make principles-based disclosures and would remain subject to antifraud and antimanipulation provisions. The proposal also includes a conditional safe harbor under which a crypto asset that meets the conditions would not be treated as an investment contract in the definition of "security." As of Saturday the text is still a proposal, not a final rule.

**Links:**

- [SEC — Regulation Crypto Assets proposal](https://www.sec.gov/rules-regulations/2026/08/s7-2026-27)

**Commentary:** The comment window closes next Tuesday, so whether the offering exemptions and the investment-contract safe harbor survive will be visible only after that deadline.

## V. DeFi and Protocols

### 9. Lido outlines Fallback Exits for Lido Lend, so a withdrawal can use borrower collateral when liquidity is short (DeFi)
**Summary:** Lido contributors posted the first feature note for Lido Lend on the governance forum at 14:04 UTC on October 16. Fallback Exits would let a lender cover all or part of a withdrawal with borrower collateral when an isolated market does not have enough of the loan asset, instead of waiting for idle liquidity to return. Collateral would be taken from the riskiest positions first, starting with borrowers closest to the liquidation loan-to-value ratio, and their outstanding debt would be reduced by a corresponding amount. The markets are meant to pair highly correlated assets, such as wETH with wstETH or dollars with Treasury real-world assets, on a modified version of Morpho Blue. The post says technical specifications and market parameters are expected within two weeks, and that the proposed specifications still need governance approval. A mainnet launch has not happened.

**Links:**

- [Lido research forum — Lido Lend: Fallback Exits](https://research.lido.fi/t/unveiling-lido-lend-security-first-lending/11987/11)

**Commentary:** The exit changes from waiting on the pool to taking correlated collateral, so a lender can leave, but not necessarily in the asset originally deposited, and the contracts have not been through a governance vote.

**Summary:** Alex Thorn, Galaxy's head of firmwide research, published a tally on October 16 of roughly $91 million leaving 474 addresses after hardware wallets sold through Southeast Asian reseller CryptoBilis were fitted with a component that read the recovery phrase. Genuine Check still passes because the implant reads the screen from inside the enclosure and sends the phrase out over cellular service through an eSIM. Every confirmed case so far involves a device sold through CryptoBilis.

### 12. Anthropic says Claude traced the NIST curve seeds to an old random-number generator; bitcoin does not use those curves (Cryptography)
**Summary:** Anthropic wrote on October 16 that an internal research model, in under half an hour, traced the seeds of NIST prime curves P-192, P-224, P-256, P-384, and P-521 to the C library generator `lrand48`, initialized with Unix timestamps from May 1997 through April 1998, rather than to a forgotten hashed passphrase. The published P-256 seed is candidate 1,842 of that run. Anthropic said this is not a cryptographic break or a new attack. Being able to reproduce the seeds makes an intentional NSA backdoor much less plausible, and people using the NIST curves do not need to change anything because of the finding. Still unexplained are skipped candidates that would also have produced valid curves, binary curve B-163, and how the base points of P-224, P-256, P-384, and P-521 were chosen. Filippo Valsorda said the bounty was claimed on October 15 and that $36,864 will go to the Internet Security Research Group, which operates Let's Encrypt. CoinDesk noted on Saturday that bitcoin wallets use secp256k1, not this NIST set. A quantum computer that could derive a private key from a public key on the chain would be a separate risk, unrelated to the origin of these seeds, and no such machine exists yet.

**Links:**

- [Anthropic — Claude reveals the origins of the ECDSA curve seeds](https://www.anthropic.com/research/ecdsa-curve-origins)
- [CoinDesk — Claude cracks a 25-year NSA number mystery, and what that means for bitcoin](https://www.coindesk.com/tech/2026/10/17/wow-it-s-a-real-discovery-claude-cracks-a-25-year-nsa-number-mystery)

**Commentary:** The seed story collapses to a reproducible timestamp, which weakens the backdoor theory for the NIST curves; bitcoin's curve choice was already different, and this research does not remove the quantum question.
