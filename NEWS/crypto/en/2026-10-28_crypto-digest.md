## I. Regulation & Policy

### 1. Treasury Secretary Bessent Says Agencies Can Coordinate After CLARITY Stall (Regulation)
**Summary:** U.S. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent told Ripple’s Swell event that the Senate setback for the digital-asset market CLARITY Act was disappointing and that Treasury could convene the SEC and CFTC to close gaps and set guardrails against crypto debanking—without announcing a formal working group or timeline. The bill failed a Sept. 15 procedural vote 49–50, short of the 60 votes needed. Coverage on Oct. 28 noted his Oct. 27 remarks as the main stage opened. Bessent stressed that more than 90% of stablecoins are dollar-denominated; OCC rules implementing the GENIUS Act for payment stablecoins are set to take effect Jan. 18, 2027.

### 2. CFTC Schedules Dec. 15 Roundtable on 24/7 Derivatives Trading (Regulation)
**Summary:** On Oct. 28, 2026, the Commodity Futures Trading Commission said it will host an industry roundtable on Dec. 15 at its Washington headquarters on whether derivatives markets can move to 24/7 trading, building on a summer Request for Comment. Topics include overnight trading, tokenization, infrastructure and staffing, operational readiness, and market integrity; the event will be public and livestreamed on CFTC.gov, with limited in-person capacity. Chairman Selig said the agency will not take a one-size-fits-all approach; agenda and registration details will follow.

**Links:**

- [CFTC — Roundtable on 24/7 trading](https://www.cftc.gov/PressRoom/PressReleases/9318-26)

**Commentary:** The roundtable is a pre-rule hearing step; crypto-linked derivatives may fit continuous hours more readily than some traditional commodity markets.

### 3. Brazil’s Itaú Becomes First Bank to Formalize Crypto Brokerage Under New Rules (Regulation)
**Summary:** CoinDesk reported on Oct. 28, 2026 that Itaú Unibanco, Brazil’s largest private bank, notified the central bank on Tuesday of its crypto trading and custody operations—the first bank to formalize under the new digital-asset framework ahead of an Oct. 30 deadline. Already-authorized banks use a notification path; dedicated VASPs must apply for authorization. Itaú has offered about 15 cryptoassets including BTC, ETH, and USDC since December 2023 and has not disclosed users or volumes. Resolution 520 took effect Feb. 2 with a 270-day window; missing the deadline means stopping crypto services within 30 days.

**Links:**

- [CoinDesk — Brazil’s largest lender formalizes crypto brokerage under new rules](https://www.coindesk.com/policy/2026/10/28/brazil-s-largest-lender-becomes-first-bank-to-formalize-crypto-brokerage-under-new-rules)

**Commentary:** Bank-channel compliance can raise local trust while shrinking room for unauthorized exchanges to partner with licensed banks.

### 4. Circle Urges SEC to Ease Stablecoin Treatment in Fundraising Proposal (Regulation)
**Summary:** Yahoo Finance and others reported on Oct. 28 that Circle disclosed its Oct. 20 comment letter supporting the SEC’s proposed Regulation Crypto Assets while asking for clearer treatment of GENIUS-compliant payment stablecoins—including removing a 2% broker-dealer capital haircut and treating qualifying stablecoins as “cash” under proposed Rule 302(b). The proposal would create exemptions of up to $5 million over four years and up to $75 million per 12 months, plus a conditional investment-contract safe harbor; the comment period closed Oct. 20. Circle shares were reported down about 2% on the day.

**Links:**

- [Yahoo Finance — Circle urges SEC to ease stablecoin rules in crypto fundraising proposal](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/crypto/articles/circle-urges-sec-ease-stablecoin-155100704.html)
- [SEC — Regulation Crypto Assets](https://www.sec.gov/rules-regulations/2026/08/s7-2026-27)

**Commentary:** Aligning stablecoins with GENIUS inside the securities fundraising framework would lower capital friction for brokers holding compliant tokens if adopted.
