## I. Regulation & Policy

### 1. Bitwise and Hyperliquid Press SEC for Clearer Token Classification and Secondary-Market Rules (Regulation)
**Summary:** According to CoinsInsight and related coverage on November 14, Bitwise Asset Management and the Hyperliquid Policy Center submitted comment letters on the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission’s proposed Regulation Crypto Assets. The proposal would create exemptions for qualifying crypto fundraising and a conditional safe harbor for certain tokens tied to investment contracts. Both groups argue that token classification, airdrops and buybacks, and treatment of unregistered intermediaries and onchain protocols in secondary trading remain too unclear. Bitwise asked for a more workable, publicly observable classification standard; Hyperliquid urged a broader safe harbor and direct guidance on onchain trading. The public comment window closes this week; the SEC has not set a final revision timetable.

**Commentary:** The draft is in an industry-correction window—whether the safe harbor covers onchain secondary liquidity will decide if U.S. token markets become tradably compliant or stay stuck in issuance-only paper relief.

### 2. U.S. Treasury Proposes Bank-Style Due Diligence for Payment Stablecoin Issuers (Regulation)
**Summary:** TokenPost reported on November 14 that the U.S. Treasury has proposed due-diligence requirements for payment stablecoin issuers under a licensing framework tied to the GENIUS Act. Permitted issuers would have to examine counterparties and follow existing banking-style diligence standards, while third-party counterparties would also be brought into a federal and state licensing perimeter. The checks are described as similar to U.S. correspondent-bank scrutiny and aim to strengthen accountability, sanctions compliance, and financial stability. The effective date would follow finalization of the proposal, and issuers may need to reassess partnerships and outsourcing arrangements.

**Links:**

- [TokenPost — Treasury proposal would add due diligence rules for stablecoin issuers](https://www.tokenpost.com/news/regulation/57471)

**Commentary:** Stablecoin oversight is expanding from reserves and redemption into who issuers deal with—compliance costs are being pushed closer to bank standards.

### 3. Thailand’s SEC Seeks Comment on Stablecoin Transfer Caps: 5 Million Baht Per Day In and Out, Own Wallets Only (Regulation)
**Summary:** Thailand’s Securities and Exchange Commission issued News No. 253/2026 on November 12 and opened consultation on draft rules for stablecoin transfers through licensed digital-asset operators. Each customer’s inbound and outbound transfers at each operator would be capped separately at 5 million baht per day (about $147,750–$148,000), and transfers would be allowed only between accounts or wallets verified as belonging to the same customer. Operators would need Travel Rule compliance and blockchain analytics screening. Exemptions include customer transfers between two SEC-supervised operators, market-maker liquidity moves, and certain Bank of Thailand–approved business uses; pure peer-to-peer onchain transfers fall outside the draft’s scope. Comments close November 26; the rules are not yet in force. English and Chinese media continued covering the proposal on November 14.

**Links:**

### 5. UK, Singapore, and Thailand Central Banks Demo Synchronized FX Settlement Across Legacy and Blockchain Rails (Regulation / Infrastructure)
**Summary:** The Bank of England and counterparts in Singapore and Thailand said on Friday that Project Meridian FX APAC demonstrated how cross-border FX payments can be coordinated across conventional and blockchain-based settlement systems without requiring identical infrastructure. The experiment tested payment-versus-payment (PvP) conditional settlement and multilateral netting to cut settlement risk and liquidity needs. CoinDesk reported on November 14 that the Bank of England plans a live synchronization interface in 2028, though commercial deployment still needs legal arrangements, regulatory oversight, and broader participation. The work comes as stablecoins increasingly compete as alternative cross-border payment rails.

**Links:**

- [CoinDesk — UK, Singapore, and Thailand central banks test cross-border FX settlement](https://www.coindesk.com/policy/2026/11/14/uk-singapore-and-thailand-central-banks-test-cross-border-fx-settlement-as-stablecoins-gain-ground)

**Commentary:** Central banks are answering the stablecoin narrative with interoperable synchronized settlement—the real test is whether the 2028 interface scales beyond a pilot into wholesale payment rails.

## V. Prediction Markets & Web3

### 10. Meta Internally Tests Takes Prediction-Market App Using “Cred” Points, Not Cash (Prediction Markets)
**Summary:** The Wall Street Journal reported, and The Block followed on November 13–14, that Meta has begun employee testing of a prediction-market app called Takes. Users wager “cred” (credibility) points on event outcomes—gaining points for correct calls and losing them for wrong ones—without using real money or crypto. The project is the former Arena initiative. Takes is not public, and Meta has given no launch timetable or clarity on whether real-money contracts could follow. The test comes as cash-settled platforms such as Kalshi and Polymarket face state gambling lawsuits and federal–state jurisdiction fights.

**Links:**
