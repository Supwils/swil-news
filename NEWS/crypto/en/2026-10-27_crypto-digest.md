## I. Regulation and Charters

### 1. The OCC grants final approval for Ripple National Trust Bank (Charter)
**Summary:** CoinDesk and a Ripple press release said the U.S. Office of the Comptroller of the Currency granted final approval to charter Ripple National Trust Bank (RNTB), which CEO Brad Garlinghouse announced on X late Monday; CoinDesk followed early on October 27, 2026. The bank is authorized to manage segregated reserves backing the RLUSD stablecoin, act as collateral trustee for those reserves, and provide fiduciary digital-asset custody; a national trust charter does not allow deposit-taking or commercial lending. RLUSD will still be issued by Standard Custody & Trust Company, Ripple’s New York Department of Financial Services–regulated unit, while the new bank adds federal oversight of reserves and custody. RLUSD launched in December 2024 and has grown to about $2.5 billion in market value; Ripple applied in July 2025, received conditional approval in December 2025, and converted that to final approval this week. Banking Dive reported the same day that Ripple also said it would broaden institutional custody work with BNY and explore use cases such as tokenized trade finance.

**Links:**

- [CoinDesk — Ripple gets final go-ahead for a U.S. national trust bank](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2026/10/27/ripple-gets-a-final-go-ahead-to-launch-its-own-u-s-national-trust-bank)
- [Ripple — final OCC approval to establish a national trust bank](https://ripple.com/ripple-press/ripple-receives-final-occ-approval-to-establish-national-trust-bank/)

**Commentary:** The federal trust charter puts RLUSD reserves and institutional custody under OCC supervision without changing the New York issuer or turning Ripple into a full-service commercial bank.

### 2. Hong Kong HKD stablecoins: HSBC RedCoin goes retail while Anchorpoint HKDAP builds institutional distribution (Stablecoins)
**Summary:** On October 23, HSBC said its Hong Kong dollar stablecoin HSBC RedCoin (RCHKD) went live on PayMe for more than 3.3 million users, enabling peer-to-peer transfers and payments at more than 100,000 merchants; the product is issued under an HKMA stablecoin licence, fully reserved under the Stablecoins Ordinance, and is not a bank deposit or covered by the deposit protection scheme. A Digital Currency Research Society piece republished by Sina on October 27 put RedCoin circulation at about HK$81.425 million that day, with reserve market value and cash value both about HK$82.425 million; the same licence cohort’s Anchorpoint HKDAP had about HK$6.1069 million in circulation as of October 21, with roughly six licensed distributors including Standard Chartered, Bank of East Asia, HashKey, OSL, and Finloop, covering trade finance, treasury, and tokenized money-fund subscriptions. HSBC said a stablecoin wallet for tokenized investments would later arrive in its wealth app.

## IV. Protocols and DeFi

### 8. Ethereum patches a Glamsterdam flaw that could have credited staking deposits to builders (Protocol)
**Summary:** CoinDesk reported on October 27, 2026, that developers merged a fix for the upcoming Glamsterdam upgrade: when a builder and a validator share a public key, the old deposit rules could have credited ETH intended for new validators to the builder, leaving staking services unable to recover funds. Developers closed the change on October 19 for lack of client support, then added tests and merged it Monday; the new rules sort pending deposits by withdrawal credentials so validator deposits stay with validators even if a builder uses the same key. No losses were reported; client teams must still implement the revision. Glamsterdam already ran on Sepolia, but its October 26 Hoodi testnet launch was postponed, and a mainnet schedule waits on a new Hoodi date.

**Links:**

- [CoinDesk — Ethereum fixes Glamsterdam bug that could have sent staking deposits to builders](https://www.coindesk.com/tech/2026/10/27/ethereum-fixes-glamsterdam-bug-that-could-have-sent-staking-deposits-to-builders)

**Commentary:** The patch is at the pre-mainnet specification layer; delayed testnet timing is not the same as on-chain theft.

## V. Security Incidents

### 10. Keystone says a site breach exposed its device-authentication key and will destroy 18,835 inventory units (Security)
**Summary:** In an October 26 statement covered on October 27, Keystone said an attacker who gained access on October 20 via a third-party monitoring tool on its website obtained customer data and the WebAuth key used to prove devices are genuine; the key is factory-written and cannot be rewritten, so an attacker could in theory build counterfeit wallets that pass the company’s online check. Keystone said recovery phrases, private keys, wallet credentials, and payment data were not involved; it will destroy 18,835 unsold devices carrying the old key, pause website shipments until units with a new key are ready, and said about 25,964 buyer records from 2024–2026—names, contacts, and shipping addresses—were affected. Devices delivered before October 20 that pass verification can be used normally; devices received afterward with the old key should be confirmed by support before use. The company has not reported counterfeit devices or thefts.

**Links:**

- [The Hacker News — Keystone to destroy 18,835 wallets after authentication-key breach](https://thehackernews.com/2026/10/keystone-to-destroy-18835-hardware.html)
- [Cointelegraph — Keystone says hackers also stole the key used to verify wallets](https://cointelegraph.com/news/keystone-breach-deepens-hackers-stole-key-used-to-verify-wallets-as-genuine)

**Commentary:** The risk is supply-chain authenticity, not on-chain private keys; users should treat “official recall” outreach as a likely scam and verify devices by delivery date.
