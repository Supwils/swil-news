## I. Regulation & Policy

### 1. SEC Regulation Crypto Assets comment period closes as Bitwise and Hyperliquid push for clearer token-trading and airdrop rules (Regulation)
**Summary:** The public comment window for the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission’s proposed Regulation Crypto Assets closed on November 10. The proposal would create exemptions for qualifying crypto fundraising and a conditional safe harbor for certain tokens linked to investment contracts. Bitwise Asset Management and the Hyperliquid Policy Center filed comment letters before the deadline: Bitwise urged a clearer, issuer-practical standard—based on publicly available information—for when a crypto asset falls under securities law; Hyperliquid warned that a safe harbor gated on after-the-fact conditions may arrive too late for maturing projects and pressed the SEC to address how unregistered intermediaries and onchain protocols are treated in secondary trading. Separate critics, including a former SEC enforcement official, argued the proposal should be withdrawn entirely. The SEC has not set a timetable for revision, adoption, or withdrawal.

### 2. Coinbase, Kalshi and four peers file amicus brief backing the CFTC on single-stock perpetual futures (Regulation)
**Summary:** Coinbase, Kalshi, Kraken parent Payward, plus DRW Holdings, Jump Trading, and Paradigm Operations asked the U.S. District Court for the Southern District of New York around November 9–10 for leave to file an amicus brief supporting the Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC). The Capital Markets Alliance Association—whose members include Cboe and Citadel Securities—sued to overturn the CFTC’s approvals of perpetual futures tied to individual U.S. stocks; related applications from Coinbase Derivatives, KalshiEx, and Bitnomial were deemed approved on November 2. The six firms argue equity perpetuals fit the Commodity Exchange Act definition of security futures—exchange-traded, centrally cleared, cash-settled—rather than SEC-regulated security-based swaps. A judge earlier denied a TRO but found challengers were likely to succeed on the claim that the CFTC exceeded its authority; the preliminary-injunction motion remains pending.

### 3. UK Lords debate cryptoasset amendments that would carve qualifying stablecoin payments out of some trading rules (Regulation)
**Summary:** The House of Lords Grand Committee on November 10 considered the Financial Services and Markets Act 2000 (Cryptoassets) (Miscellaneous Amendments) Regulations 2026. The government said the instrument does not replace the cryptoasset regime already approved by Parliament; it makes targeted fixes so trading-oriented requirements do not apply disproportionately when UK-issued qualifying stablecoins are used in payment chains. The draft would exclude those stablecoins from certain dealing and arranging activities and exclude temporary holding connected to a payment from cryptoasset safeguarding, while keeping lending and borrowing regulated. It also clarifies the perimeter for issuance and backing-asset arrangements and brings forward commencement language that qualifying stablecoins and their reserves are not collective investment schemes, AIFs, or e-money. The full cryptoasset regime is due to take effect in October 2027; officials said acting now removes barriers before firms must comply.

**Links:**

- [UK Parliament Hansard — FSMA Cryptoassets (Miscellaneous Amendments) Regulations 2026 debate](https://hansard.parliament.uk/Lords/2026-11-10/debates/EED4F98E-D9D5-42D2-BE7E-65C61D867231/FinancialServicesAndMarketsAct2000(Cryptoassets)(MiscellaneousAmendments)Regulations2026)

**Commentary:** By splitting payment stablecoins from crypto trading activities, the UK is clearing a scalable compliance path for payments ahead of the 2027 go-live.

### 11. CertiK urges exchanges to set hard dollar caps on hot wallets, not just cold-storage ratios (Security)
**Summary:** Security firm CertiK told Cointelegraph on November 10 that crypto exchanges should impose hard dollar limits on hot-wallet holdings rather than treating cold-storage ratios as a standalone security score. It recommended per-asset and per-chain caps set to the strictest of three measures: a percentage of customer liabilities, estimated withdrawal needs, and an absolute loss ceiling tied to financial resources and recovery capacity—paired with circuit breakers. The advice follows the Binance hot-wallet breach, after which peers disclosed cold-storage shares (Gate at about 88.26%; Coinbase saying more than 99% of customer assets are offline). CertiK said cold storage is useful but not a complete score, and very high ratios can slow withdrawals and concentrate key-management risk. Cold-storage floors already differ across South Korea, Singapore, Japan and Hong Kong frameworks.

**Links:**

- [Cointelegraph — Crypto exchanges need hard dollar caps on hot wallets, CertiK says](https://cointelegraph.com/news/crypto-exchanges-need-dollar-caps-on-hot-wallets-certik)

**Commentary:** Industry PR is still selling cold-storage percentages; CertiK reframes the real question as absolute online exposure—the loss ceiling that matters in the next systemic theft.
