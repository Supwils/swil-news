## II. Regulation & Policy

### 3. Regulation: UK FCA Signals Authorization Requirements for DeFi Platforms, Citing Binance's ~$6.3 Billion Hack

**Summary:** Per a Nov 26 Financial Times report, the UK Financial Conduct Authority plans to require DeFi platforms to obtain authorization under forthcoming crypto rules — a stricter stance than current U.S. and EU approaches — citing fraud, money-laundering risk, weak consumer protections and the difficulty of freezing stolen funds on decentralized rails. Jane Moore, FCA head of payments and digital assets, said recent hacks underline operational-resilience gaps and that the speed and scale of stolen funds moving through DeFi can frustrate freeze attempts; officials pointed to laundering paths after Binance's Oct 29 ~$6.3 billion security incident. The FCA also intends that UK retail equity orders — including tokenized shares — execute on regulated venues. DeFi guidance consultation GC26/7 closed Nov 18, with final guidance due January 2027; the wider crypto perimeter expands from Oct 25, 2027. Aave Labs' legal chief called the position "highly problematic," while Coinbase adviser George Osborne argued regulated intermediaries should act as gatekeepers.

### 4. Regulation: Swiss Federal Council Approves 57 Jurisdictions for Automatic Crypto Tax-Data Exchange From 2028

**Summary:** At its Nov 25 meeting, Switzerland's Federal Council approved 57 partner jurisdictions of relevance to crypto markets for automatic exchange of information (AEOI) on cryptoassets under the OECD Crypto-Asset Reporting Framework; domestic legal foundations took effect Jan 1, 2026. The list includes all EU member states, the United Kingdom and various G20 countries. In principle, exchanges begin in 2028 only with partners that express interest and meet OECD confidentiality and data-security standards; the Council will run a verification check approved by Parliament on Sept 28, 2026 before any exchange. Switzerland aims to implement CARF on a timetable aligned with other global asset-management centers.

## III. Institutions & Products

### 5. Institutional: Switzerland's First Spot Bitcoin ETF "BBIT" Lists on SIX Under a FINMA-Regulated Fund Wrapper

**Summary:** On Nov 26, SIX Swiss Exchange welcomed Switzerland's first bitcoin ETF — the Bitcoin Fund ETF (BBIT; ISIN CH1593447597) — with Pernet von Ballmoos AG (PvB) as issuer and BIT Asset Management as sponsor and asset manager; trading currencies are CHF and USD, with Flow Traders as market maker. The fund follows a passive strategy tracking the SIX Reference Rate Bitcoin Index, with coins held offline at a Swiss bank and segregated from other assets, under the Swiss Collective Investment Schemes Act (CISA). FINMA had previously approved the first Swiss public fund investing directly in bitcoin. BIT Asset Management CEO Dominik Oggenfuss said investors can access bitcoin through existing securities accounts within Switzerland's collective-investment framework.
