## II. Regulation & Policy

### 3. Regulation: Spain’s Sepblac Can Freeze Crypto Accounts Without Prior Court Approval (Up to Five Business Days)

**Summary:** Spain’s Royal Decree-Law 44/2026 (published around Nov 24–25) adds Article 46 bis to the 2010 AML law: the Executive Service of the Commission for the Prevention of Money Laundering and Monetary Offenses (Sepblac) may — without a prior court order — suspend in-progress transactions for up to 10 business days, or suspend bank, payment and crypto-asset accounts/business relationships for up to five business days, when there are indications of money laundering or terrorist financing. Freezes lift automatically unless prosecutors seek judicial ratification or extension. Previously Sepblac could largely act on foreign FIU requests; it may now move on its own analysis or domestic law-enforcement asks, with liability protection for obliged entities that comply. The measure is slated to take effect around Jan 25, 2027, pending parliamentary validation within about 30 days.

### 4. Regulation: Japan’s FSA Circulates a DeFi Discussion Paper on Front-Ends, AML Touchpoints and Self-Custody Cooling-Off Periods

**Summary:** Japan’s Financial Services Agency (FSA) Digital and Decentralized Finance Study Group circulated a discussion paper around Nov 26 for its third plenary, targeting interim conclusions in early 2027. Core questions include how to identify parties with substantive control over DeFi services; whether DEX front-ends (apps/websites) should fall under financial regulation; whether stablecoin issuers, fiat on/off-ramps and banks should shoulder AML/CFT duties when a clear controller is missing; and whether access to AML-deficient services should be restricted. On self-custody wallets, the paper explores cooling-off periods for transfers from newly opened exchange accounts to external wallets as an anti-fraud buffer. Versus MiCA’s exemption path for fully intermediary-free DeFi, Japan’s framing leans toward gateway and touchpoint regulation.

### 6. Institutional: Brazil’s B3 and Six Financial Firms Plan Real-Pegged Stablecoin Open BRL for Q1 2027

**Summary:** On Nov 27, B3 joined Bradesco, BTG Pactual, Itaú Unibanco, Safra, Santander and XP to develop Open BRL — a Brazilian-real-pegged stablecoin — plus shared rails for transfers and digital-asset settlement, targeting a Q1 2027 launch. Banco B3 will initially issue the token with 1:1 real deposit reserves; B3 will run the tech stack, with a steering committee setting governance, rules and pricing, and other institutions able to join from day one. The project aims to solve rival banks rejecting one another’s private stablecoins and to settle tokenized equities on a platform B3 is building. B3 says Brazil’s central bank has been briefed and the CVM supports the tokenization link; Brazil’s stablecoin flow remains USDT-dominated, with local-currency pegs still a small share of supply.
