## I. Regulation & Policy

### 1. FinCEN and the Fed formally withdraw the proposal to cut the cross-border Travel Rule threshold to $250 (Regulation)
**Summary:** The U.S. Financial Crimes Enforcement Network (FinCEN) and the Federal Reserve Board published a joint withdrawal in the Federal Register on November 9, withdrawing the October 27, 2020 notice of proposed rulemaking. That NPRM would have lowered the recordkeeping and Travel Rule thresholds for funds transfers and transmittals that begin or end outside the United States from $3,000 to $250, and would have clarified that “money” covers convertible virtual currency (CVC) and digital assets with legal tender status. The agencies said they will take no further action on the NPRM; the existing $3,000 threshold and current obligations remain, and any similar future amendments would require a new public-comment process. Nearly 3,000 comments mostly opposed the lower threshold; the agencies cited the age of the proposal and major changes in crypto markets and law, including the GENIUS Act.

**Links:**

- [Federal Register — FinCEN/Fed withdrawal of Travel Rule and recordkeeping NPRM](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/full_text/html/2026/11/09/2026-22809.html)
- [Unchained — FinCEN, Fed withdraw proposal that would have written crypto into Travel Rule definitions](https://unchainedcrypto.com/fincen-fed-withdraw-proposal-that-would-have-written-crypto-into-travel-rule-definitions/)

**Commentary:** Near-term compliance uncertainty eases on the $250 path, but this is not deregulation—the existing Travel Rule and FinCEN’s 2019 crypto guidance still apply.

### 2. China’s central bank halts high-profile Solana promotion onshore, restating that crypto business is illegal financial activity (Regulation)
**Summary:** Financial Times (China), carried by Tonghuashun on November 9, reported that a People’s Bank of China department responded after detecting that major crypto issuer platform Solana (HSDT) had placed ads through onshore entities and planned offline events. The PBOC, together with local financial regulators, police, and market supervisors, stopped the offline events and said it will investigate involved entities and individuals. Officials reiterated China’s long-standing ban on crypto-related business, citing 2021 rules and an eight-agency notice this February: bitcoin, ether, stablecoins and similar assets lack legal-tender status, and onshore crypto activity is illegal financial activity. They said Solana’s token is a virtual currency and that promoting it—while also supporting services for major stablecoins such as USDT—violates the notice; enforcement will keep targeting onshore dealers and domestic affiliates of offshore platforms.

**Commentary:** The signal is unambiguous: marketing crypto to mainland audiences under a “brand/tech” wrapper still draws illegal-finance enforcement.

### 3. New Zealand’s FMA seeks feedback on a regulatory path for NZD payment stablecoins (Regulation)
**Summary:** Odaily and others reported on November 9 that New Zealand’s Financial Markets Authority (FMA) opened consultation on a temporary regulatory path for New Zealand dollar (NZD)–referenced payment stablecoins it would endorse. Stablecoins meeting specified requirements and safeguards would no longer be treated as financial products under the Financial Markets Conduct Act, including NZD backing assets held at registered banks and redeemability at face value. FMA General Counsel Liam Mason said the aim is to test a clearer path that supports innovation while maintaining market confidence; the proposal remains a consultation step alongside broader digital-asset work, with no final decision yet.

### 4. U.S. Treasury’s FinCEN cuts MEXC off from the U.S. financial system over alleged ~$16B in illicit flows (Regulation)
**Summary:** On November 6, FinCEN issued an order under Section 9714 of the Combating Russian Money Laundering Act barring U.S. financial institutions from processing funds transfers involving MEXC Global, including crypto transfers, as part of Operation Economic Outcast. Treasury said the exchange, founded in 2018, facilitated at least about $16 billion in illicit flows tied to Iranian, North Korean, and Russian sanctions evasion and cybercrime between May 2021 and May 2026—including nearly $1.5 billion linked to Russian sanctions evasion—and alleged it still processed activity with sanctioned Garantex/Grinex as recently as June 2026. The order is slated for Federal Register publication around November 10 and takes effect 30 days after publication. MEXC said it is operating normally, user assets remain secure, and it has begun an independent AML/sanctions-controls review.

## III. Institutions & Tokenization

### 7. Coinbase receives a full OCC national trust charter for federally supervised custody (Institutions)
**Summary:** Coinbase announced on November 9 that the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency elevated Coinbase National Trust Company from April’s conditional approval to a full charter. The firm said it can expand institution-grade custody and related transfer services under a federal framework, without taking deposits or making loans, and plans to migrate custody operations from its New York state trust over about three years. Other crypto-native firms such as Ripple already hold full trust status; the GENIUS Act cleared a path for federal trusts in compliant stablecoin issuance. The Independent Community Bankers of America has sued the OCC over crypto trust charters. Coinbase Vice Chairman Ryan VanGrack said the approval puts more of the business behind clear federal oversight.

### 8. Formation Securities notifies the SEC of plans for a Solana tokenized-securities venue (Tokenization)
**Summary:** Formation Securities LLC notified the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission on November 9 that it intends to operate a tokenized securities venue (TSV) on Solana under the SEC’s Innovation Exemption, using permissioned AMM liquidity pools for secondary trading of eligible tokenized NMS stocks, with trading technology licensed from Orca. Its public notice targets an early-2027 launch, with tokenized shares of Exodus Movement (EXOD), Forward Industries (FWDI), and Galaxy Digital (GLXY) expected at open; Superstate and Securitize are expected to supply tokenized shares. The venue will not run an order book or custody user keys, and only KYC-permissioned wallets may participate; the notice stresses the SEC has not passed on the merits of the disclosures. Unlike the OKX-ICE venue’s wave of issuer objections, Formation said it had received none as of the notice date.

## IV. DeFi & Protocols

### 9. Zama spin-off Raycash launches confidential stablecoin payments and private yield on Ethereum (DeFi)
**Summary:** The Block reported on November 9 that Raycash, a stablecoin-payments startup spun out of confidentiality protocol Zama, launched a private beta on Ethereum mainnet—the first retail payment product built on the Zama Protocol. Using fully homomorphic encryption (FHE), users can wrap USDC 1:1 into ERC-7984 confidential tokens, make peer-to-peer payments with encrypted balances and amounts, and earn private yield on idle balances via Morpho inside the app; the app covers gas so users need not hold ETH. Raycash said more than 75,000 people joined its waitlist for staged access. The wallet is described as self-custodial with no freeze/seize at the token layer, though view access can be granted to parties such as accountants. A Raycard for private spending remains in development.
