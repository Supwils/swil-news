## I. Regulation and Policy

### 1. Cheong Wa Dae leans toward a fourth delay of the virtual-asset tax, and says nothing is final (Regulation)
**Summary:** The Asia Business Daily reported on October 25, 2026, citing ruling-party sources, that South Korea's presidential office is leaning toward another deferral of the virtual-asset income tax scheduled for January 1, 2027. If it happens, it would be the fourth postponement since the tax was introduced in 2020. The office says nothing has been decided on its final position and expects a conclusion after talks between the government and the ruling party. The tax deducts 2.5 million won from annual income earned by transferring or lending virtual assets such as bitcoin, then taxes the remainder at 22%, including local income tax. It was first scheduled for 2022 and has already been postponed three times. Hankyoreh the same day quoted a presidential-office official saying the discussion seems to be settling toward a delay, citing younger adults' path to building assets and the expectation that the United States will join the OECD Crypto-Asset Reporting Framework only in 2029. The Asia Business Daily says cross-border CARF exchanges are due to start next year, so a late U.S. start could concentrate the tax on users of domestic exchanges. On October 20, First Vice Minister Kwon Dae-young of the Ministry of Finance and Economy met exchanges and experts, restating that income should be taxed where it is earned and saying tax infrastructure and a market regulatory framework must be built together. Democratic Party lawmaker Park Min-gyu has introduced an Income Tax Act amendment that would effectively push implementation to 2029, and the People Power Party also supports another delay. Democratic Party leader Kim Min-seok said the question should be settled within the year. The Asia Business Daily also notes that, on the current schedule, the first filing and payment would fall in May 2028, just after the April 2028 general election.

**Commentary:** This is a presidential-office leaning and a legislative window, and the January 1, 2027 start remains the date in current law until the National Assembly changes it.

### 2. The Bank of Israel rejects using crypto to bypass sanctions, and more than 100 economists sign a Sunday letter (Regulation)
**Summary:** Reuters reported on October 25, 2026, that Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu told Israel's Channel 10 on Friday that sanctions on banks would require a "bypass economy." Asked whether he meant cryptocurrency, he said "Very true, very true," and named blockchain as another possibility. The Bank of Israel issued a rare direct rebuke, saying crypto cannot serve as a route around sanctions and that Israel's economy should preserve relations with its main trading partners and focus on preventing sanctions and their economic damage. Ynet reported that officials in the economic ministries said they knew of no such preparation team. On Channel 14 on Saturday, Netanyahu said he hears the objections, thanks the central bank for its advice, and will do what he thinks is right. Reuters said that on Sunday more than 100 economists signed a letter, including former Bank of Israel officials, former governor Jacob Frenkel, and several Nobel laureates. The letter says the path would raise unemployment, cut foreign investment, and collapse Israel's economic standing, and that living standards in countries that suffered similar sanctions, including South Africa, Iran, and Russia, fell sharply. It calls the proposal a puzzling solution accepted in criminal organizations. Reuters also notes that Netanyahu faces re-election this week. Britain, France, and Canada said last month they would block imports from Israeli settlements in the West Bank.

### 3. ECB's Cipollone calls the digital euro a payments-sovereignty tool, with merchant pilot applications due October 27 (CBDC)
**Summary:** Cointelegraph reported on October 25, 2026, that ECB Executive Board member Piero Cipollone, in a Milano Finanza interview published by the central bank on Saturday, said the digital euro could give Europe sovereignty over payments infrastructure that now depends on outside third parties. He said Europe has learned it cannot rely only on third parties for essential services such as energy and defense, and that the same is true for payments. Sovereignty, he said, does not mean shutting third parties out; it means having the power to choose and knowing Europe can also rely on its own capacity for an essential service. The ECB's pilot page says that if the digital-euro regulation is adopted in 2026, the Eurosystem aims to be ready for a potential first issuance during 2029. A 12-month pilot is planned to start in the second half of 2027 to test a beta version in real situations, including in-shop and person-to-person payments. Participants are 36 licensed euro-area payment service providers, selected merchants, and staff of the ECB and 19 national central banks. Cointelegraph says seven of the 36 market operators are from Italy. The ECB page says preparing the pilot does not decide issuance, which waits on the regulation. Cipollone told Milano Finanza he hopes the regulation is adopted by the end of the year so the Governing Council can take a formal issuance decision. The ECB page sets October 27, 2026, as the deadline for e-commerce and mobile-commerce merchants to apply for the pilot.

**Links:**

- [Cointelegraph — ECB official sees the digital euro as a tool to protect EU sovereignty](https://cointelegraph.com/news/ecb-official-sees-digital-euro-tool-to-protect-eu-sovereignty)
- [European Central Bank — digital euro pilot and merchant applications](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/euro/digital_euro/pilot/html/index.en.html)

**Commentary:** Sunday's news is a pilot timetable and a sovereignty argument; issuance still depends on legislation in Brussels, and October 27 closes merchant applications, not the issuance decision.

### 4. Issuer objections to the OKX-ICE tokenized-stock venue rise to 22 (Regulation)
**Summary:** Decrypt reported on October 25, 2026, that OKXICE TSV's public notice dated October 23 added six issuer objections: Wells Fargo, Cisco, Marvell, Arm, Moderna, and Intuit. That takes the total to 22, up from 16 on October 19 and nine on October 15. Earlier names include Microsoft, Alphabet, Goldman Sachs, Verizon, Broadcom, and UnitedHealth. The notices do not explain why the companies objected or what an objection does to a listing. OKXICE is a 50-50 joint venture of Intercontinental Exchange, owner of the New York Stock Exchange, and OKX's U.S. holding company. It plans to let approved users trade tokenized U.S. stocks around the clock through Uniswap v4 liquidity pools on OKX's X Layer, settled in stablecoins such as USDC, under the SEC innovation exemption that Decrypt says the agency unveiled in September after the Clarity Act stalled in the Senate. The latest notice says that, beyond pausing when the underlying stock is halted, the venue does not use circuit breakers, has no policy on maximal extractable value, allows administrators to upgrade smart contracts without advance notice to users, and has had only an internal audit of the contract that controls who can trade. It warns that broker-dealers face unsettled questions on trade reporting and best execution, which could limit participation and thin liquidity. OKXICE's notice lists all 22 objecting issuers.
