### 1. China's cyberspace commission issues the informatization plan and targets a national blockchain network by 2030 (Policy)
**Summary:** The Central Cyberspace Affairs Commission issued the 15th Five-Year Plan for National Informatization on October 23, 2026. The plan says that by 2030 China should make major progress in informatization, complete a next-generation communications network, start commercial 6G at an appropriate time, and largely complete a national blockchain network. A project column calls for a central backbone, linked regional hubs, and shared services such as distributed digital identity, with uses in shipping and cargo trade, the low-carbon transition, government services, and taxation. Regional tasks name blockchain for the Beijing-Tianjin-Hebei airport hubs and smart logistics, trade and finance in the Yangtze River Delta, faster blockchain applications in the Guangdong-Hong Kong-Macao Greater Bay Area, and scaled use in commerce, manufacturing, and agriculture in the Chengdu-Chongqing region. The indicator table sets expected internet penetration at 85 percent in 2030, from 80.1 percent in 2025, and 5G base stations, including 5G-Advanced, at 50 per 10,000 people, from 34. Xinhua's English report the same day confirms that the commission issued the document and that the 2030 goals include largely completing the national blockchain network.

### 2. MAS says the Malaysian and Thai central banks have joined BLOOM for cross-border digital-money settlement (Regulation)
**Summary:** The Monetary Authority of Singapore announced on October 23, 2026, that Bank Negara Malaysia and the Bank of Thailand have joined BLOOM (Borderless, Liquid, Open, Online, Multi-currency) as policymaker members. The three central banks will work with industry in their jurisdictions on how digital money is accepted, exchanged, and settled across borders, including tokenized deposits and regulated stablecoins. Newly listed industry and multilateral participants include Airwallex, Mastercard, Maybank, Monee, Nium, Ripple, ShopeePay, Visa, and the World Bank Group. The work has three tracks: acceptance and settlement across jurisdictions, interoperability among digital-money arrangements, and programmable controls such as identity checks, sanctions screening, and transaction controls. MAS Deputy Managing Director Leong Sing Chiong said multiple forms of digital money will operate across institutions and markets. Bank of Thailand Deputy Governor Daranee Saeju said the work will inform regulatory approaches to tokenized money, especially in cross-border payments and wholesale settlement. The MAS BLOOM page carries a published date of October 23, 2026, and lists the three central banks as policymaker members.

### 3. The Bank of Korea says it will draft a forex-law amendment this year defining stablecoins as payment instruments (Regulation)
**Summary:** The Block reported Friday, citing local outlet Digital Asset, that South Korea's government and the Bank of Korea plan to revise the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act this year so stablecoins are regulated as payment instruments, alongside a monitoring framework for virtual assets. The central bank said it is coordinating with the government on rules for cross-border digital-asset transactions. It noted that crypto transfers can move across borders outside traditional financial institutions, and that mixers and unhosted wallets increase anonymity. The report ties the statement to last year's National Assembly audit, when lawmakers asked for large-value payment systems to run until 8 p.m. and for measures against money-laundering risk from global crypto exchanges. The Bank of Korea also said it will keep consulting ministries to limit regulatory arbitrage while the Digital Asset Basic Act takes shape. That act is still being drafted and is meant to cover exchange licensing and stablecoin issuance. Financial Services Commission Chairman Lee Eog-weon said at a hearing on Thursday that consultations were nearing completion and that the government would introduce the bill as soon as possible. The Block's account is a report of the central bank's statement. The amendment text has not been published.

**Links:**

- [The Block — South Korea to amend its forex law and regulate stablecoins as payment instruments](https://www.theblock.co/news/regulation/2026-10-23-south-korea-stablecoins-payment-instruments-424028)

**Commentary:** A payment-instrument definition in the forex law would put stablecoins into cross-border monitoring first, while issuance licenses still wait on the basic-act text.

## III. Protocols and Upgrades

### 7. Ethereum postpones the Glamsterdam activation on Hoodi that had been set for October 26 (Protocol)
**Summary:** CoinDesk reported Friday that Ethereum developers called off the Glamsterdam upgrade on the Hoodi test network. Activation had been set for October 26 at 17:42:48 UTC. The delay was decided at Thursday's All Core Devs Execution meeting because new gas accounting could change how contracts already on the network handle failed transactions, letting an error that should have reversed the whole transaction be recorded permanently. The focus is EIP-8037 and EIP-8038. ChainSecurity published an analysis on October 13 calculating that creating a new storage slot could require 110,020 gas when a separate allowance is exhausted, compared with 22,100 under the old rules. CoinDesk said ChainSecurity has demonstrated the mechanisms but has not publicly established that a major production protocol is exploitable. Sepolia completed its activation on October 6. A planned increase of Hoodi's gas limit to 200 million was postponed with the fork. No replacement date has been announced, and the mainnet activation date remains unconfirmed. Cointelegraph reported that ChainSecurity identified 13 real-world contract examples that could be affected and is coordinating disclosure with the projects, and that developers had been considering early December for mainnet, with the schedule to be set after a successful Hoodi test.

**Links:**

- [CoinDesk — Ethereum delays the Glamsterdam test after security researchers flag risk to existing contracts](https://www.coindesk.com/tech/2026/10/23/ethereum-delays-glamsterdam-test-after-security-devs-flag-risk-to-existing-contracts)
- [Cointelegraph — Ethereum delays the Glamsterdam Hoodi testnet fork over gas security risks](https://cointelegraph.com/news/ethereum-delays-glamsterdam-testnet-fork-gas-security-risks)

### 11. CoinDCX denies that active wallets were hacked and attributes about $16 million of outflows to addresses taken in July 2025 (Security)
**Summary:** CoinDesk reported Friday that CoinDCX, India's largest crypto exchange, denied that its active wallets were compromised. On-chain observers flagged about $16 million of outflows on Wednesday from addresses linked to the exchange, in USDT and Tron TRX, sent to a single Tron address. The largest transaction was 15,819,002 USDT, and that address then split the funds. A spokesperson told CoinDesk that the addresses were created in January 2025, were compromised in the July 2025 hack, and have not been used by CoinDCX since. The exchange said current central and user addresses were not affected and that no funds from active wallets were taken. CoinDCX was exploited for about $44 million in July 2025. Chief executive Sumit Gupta wrote on X that the July 2025 attackers appear to be reusing the old addresses. Investigator ZachXBT disputed that account, saying deposit address TQKJQ was still used by a liquidity provider for 10 months after the July 2025 incident and swept user deposits and received gas as recently as April and May 2026 into the exchange's then-current public hot wallet. CoinDCX acknowledged that some users kept depositing to deprecated addresses after being told not to, that the sweep of those deposits failed for a period, and that it absorbed the operational losses and credited affected users. Trading continues as usual.
