## I. Regulation and Policy

### 1. After Clarity Stalls in the Senate, Executives See Onchain Experiments While Lawyers Say Spot Markets Still Lack a Statute (Regulation)
**Summary:** CoinDesk reported on October 11 that the Digital Asset Market Clarity Act's failure to advance in the Senate has moved the next phase of U.S. crypto regulation to the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) and the Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC). The SEC's five-year Innovation Exemption, introduced September 17, lets qualifying venues trade tokenized U.S. stocks through blockchain liquidity pools while permanent rules are written. On October 5 the CFTC asked for feedback on rules for leveraged retail crypto trading and a new registration category for crypto markets. The SEC's October 1 custody proposal would let state trust companies safeguard client crypto and, under certain conditions, let advisers and funds hold it themselves. Ordinary unleveraged spot trading still lacks comprehensive federal oversight beyond the CFTC's anti-fraud and anti-manipulation powers, the gap Clarity was meant to close. Bitwise chief investment officer Matt Hougan said the agency path is more favorable in the short term than legislation that would need years of follow-up rules, and that Clarity's failure preserves a regulatory moat for Coinbase, Kraken, and other incumbents. Lev Breydo, an assistant professor at William & Mary Law School, said only Congress can deliver a comprehensive framework with statutory durability.

**Commentary:** Tokenized stocks and custody can proceed under agency rules, while the federal supervisor for unleveraged spot trading and the durability of those rules remain open until Congress acts.

### 2. Lawyers Say the CFTC's CTX and CAM Plan Is Clever and May Not Survive Review after Loper Bright (Regulation)
**Summary:** CryptoBriefing on October 11 cited lawyer Renato Mariotti, who called the Commodity Futures Trading Commission's plan to police leveraged crypto trading under existing Commodity Exchange Act authority a clever reading that does not need a new statute from Congress, and warned that courts may reject it after the Supreme Court's 2024 decision in Loper Bright reduced deference to agency interpretations. On October 5 the CFTC published an advance notice of proposed rulemaking introducing Regulation CTX and Regulation CAM. CTX targets leveraged and margined retail crypto-asset transactions. CAM would create a subcategory for crypto-asset markets and a voluntary federal registration path. Venues registered under CAM would have to provide proof of reserves and maintain anti-manipulation controls. The notice sets no numerical leverage cap; staff would review a leveraged offering before it reaches customers. Unleveraged spot trading would remain under state money-transmitter licenses. Comments are expected within 60 days of publication. A Senate procedural vote on the Clarity Act failed 49-50 on September 15, after which Chairman Michael Selig pushed the rulemaking.

**Links:**

- [CryptoBriefing — CFTC's new crypto market plan faces scrutiny after Loper Bright](https://cryptobriefing.com/cftc-crypto-plan-loper-bright-scrutiny/)

**Commentary:** Leveraged venues have a draft federal registration path, and a court challenge after a final rule could leave early compliance spending attached to a framework that does not survive.

### 3. ESMA Tells Licensed Firms to Wind Down Non-MiCA Stablecoin Exposure, with an Outer Date around January 8, 2027 (Stablecoins)
**Summary:** On October 8 the European Securities and Markets Authority published an opinion telling national supervisors to ensure that crypto-asset service providers authorized under the Markets in Crypto-Assets Regulation stop maintaining, introducing, or facilitating EU client access to non-compliant asset-referenced tokens and e-money tokens. The expectation covers trading platforms, exchange, order execution, transfers, custody, and portfolio management. Where legacy exposures remain, national authorities should require remediation as soon as possible and no later than three months after publication. Any continued service should be limited to liquidation, conversion, withdrawal, transfer, or safekeeping, and should stay time-limited, risk-based, and closely supervised. ForexCrunch wrote on October 11 that the outer date is about January 8, 2027, that the opinion names no token or issuer, and that new purchases, promotion, and continued market availability are prohibited. Warnings and client acknowledgements do not replace issuer-level authorization on reserves, redemption, and governance. The opinion guides national authorities, which carry out implementation. Full MiCA rules for crypto-asset service providers have applied since July 1, 2026.

### 4. Prediction Markets Head toward the Supreme Court as the CFTC Keeps Event Contracts inside the Swap Definition (Regulation)
**Summary:** CoinDesk wrote on October 11 that parties filed amicus briefs last week urging the U.S. Supreme Court to take a case on whether sports-based prediction-market contracts are swaps. Former Senator Chris Dodd, former CFTC and SEC chair Gary Gensler, the National Football League, 39 states and the District of Columbia, and 145 tribal nations were among the filers. Most briefs asked the Court to side with the states and treat at least sports-related prediction markets as gambling regulated at the state level. Appellate courts are split: two have ruled that prediction markets touching sports are gambling products, and one has ruled that they are a CFTC matter. On Friday the CFTC proposed an interim final rule and a separate proposal. Casino-style gambling falls outside its definition of a swap, while event contracts remain inside it, and the agency wants sports, politics, culture, and weather formally included as event contracts counted as swaps. Kalshi received an extension to respond, with a deadline of November 9, 2026. The Supreme Court has not said whether it will hear the case.

## III. Tokenized Assets

### 7. The IMF Says More Than Half of Tokenized U.S. Stock Trades Happen Outside Regular Hours, at about 1.5 Times the Volatility (Institutions)
**Summary:** CoinDesk on October 11 described a chapter of the International Monetary Fund's Global Financial Stability Report, Scaling Tokenization: New efficiencies and new vulnerabilities. The study looks at the five most actively traded tokenized U.S. equities, including Tesla, Nvidia, and Alphabet, plus measures such as the Nasdaq 100, across centralized and decentralized venues. More than half of trading took place outside regular U.S. market hours, and about 80% of trades were for less than one share. Within five minutes of the U.S. open, more than 85% of the overnight move in tokenized shares showed up in the conventional shares. The IMF estimated the tokenized real-world-asset market at about $65 billion as of July 31, with tokenized equities about $2.3 billion of that, against a 2025 global equity market capitalization of just under $160 trillion cited from the Securities Industry and Financial Markets Association. Tokenized stocks were about 1.5 times as volatile as the equivalent shares on traditional venues and significantly less liquid. The report says automated margin calls and liquidations, collateral moving between platforms, and 24-hour trading could make a shock harder to contain, and that legal rules on ownership, liquidity safeguards, links between systems, and settlement need to be in place before the market grows much larger.

## IV. Protocols and Security

### 8. The XRP Ledger Discloses a Payment-Engine Overflow That Could Have Minted about 18 Trillion XRP in One Transaction (Security)
**Summary:** The Block reported on October 11 that an integer overflow in the XRP Ledger payment engine, present since 2015, summed a payment that consumed many order-book offers with unchecked 64-bit addition. A large enough total wrapped around to a small number, while the engine still credited each offer owner in full and charged the buyer only the wrapped total. The official XRPL disclosure says investigators found no evidence the issue was exploited on any public network. The attack needed hundreds of offers priced in a way no ordinary trader would use, could not be triggered by accident, and cost a few hundred XRP in account and offer reserves plus ordinary fees. Veria Labs co-founder Cayden Liao wrote on X on Saturday that an AI agent found the bug and built a working exploit, that one transaction could have created about 18 trillion XRP, about 184 times the 100 billion supply cap, and that Ripple paid the bug-bounty program's maximum of $250,000. The report was filed through the bounty on September 22. RippleX reproduced it the same day, raised it to critical, and shipped the fix in xrpld 3.4.1 on September 25, effective as soon as a server upgraded. More than 80% of validators on the default Unique Node List were running that version the same day. The project said this was the first time in more than ten years of the amendment system that a transaction-processing change deliberately skipped the normal activation process. The Batch wrapper fix and Batch itself activated on mainnet on October 9, and older servers are now amendment-blocked.
