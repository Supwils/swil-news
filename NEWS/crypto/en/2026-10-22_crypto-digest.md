## I. Regulation and Policy

### 1. Ireland's central bank announces a €3,252,618 Coinbase Ireland penalty, pending High Court confirmation (Regulation)
**Summary:** On October 22, 2026, the Central Bank of Ireland announced a €3,252,618 fine against Coinbase Ireland Limited for breaches of safeguarding and outsourcing duties under the Electronic Money Regulations 2011 between November 1, 2020, and August 19, 2025. The settlement was reached on October 21. The Central Bank determined that a reprimand and a penalty of €4,646,597 were warranted, then applied the 30% discount available in an undisputed-facts settlement, producing the published amount. Coinbase Ireland admitted the contraventions and accepted the sanctions, which take effect only after the High Court confirms them. The Central Bank said the firm relied too heavily on the U.S. Coinbase group to run safeguarding controls, lacked enough local skill to oversee customer funds, allowed its designated safeguarding account to be underfunded for long periods, and lacked adequate escalation and reporting of material safeguarding changes. Coinbase Ireland is an e-money institution: customers exchange fiat for e-money it issues, then use that e-money to buy crypto on the group's trading platform.

### 2. South Korea's FSC chair says the Digital Asset Basic Act is near completion while the 2027 crypto tax remains in play (Regulation)
**Summary:** The Block, citing local media, reported that Financial Services Commission Chairman Lee Eog-weon told a parliamentary hearing on Thursday that government consultations on the Digital Asset Basic Act were in their final stages and that the government would introduce it as soon as possible. The bill is meant to set a comprehensive crypto framework, including exchange licensing, stablecoin issuance rules, and clearer legal definitions. A crypto tax targeted for January 1, 2027, has already been delayed three times over fairness and infrastructure. Kim Min-seok, leader of the ruling Democratic Party of Korea, said Thursday that he is reviewing the inputs and that he had called a day earlier for a thorough final review. The National Tax Service is still proceeding on its existing schedule and plans to finish a draft digital-asset tax notice by October 30, with a preliminary review and public notice in November. A May petition to repeal the tax and an August petition to defer it for two years each crossed the 50,000-signature threshold for legislative review.

### 4. Starknet's poll gives 93.52% of voting power to research an independent post-quantum layer 1, with any migration still requiring another vote (Protocol)
**Summary:** The Starknet Foundation said on its governance forum on October 22 that a seven-day Snapshot poll closed that day, with 93.52% of voting power favoring research into becoming an independent, post-quantum layer 1. The Foundation wrote that the poll authorizes StarkWare and the Foundation to start research and does not approve leaving Ethereum; formal approval would be sought after research and design. The Foundation said STRK remains the native token, no new token will be issued, and Ethereum bridges to Starknet stay open whichever path is chosen.

### 5. Ethereum researchers file a decoupled-consensus draft aimed at cutting finality from about 16 minutes to about four (Protocol)
**Summary:** CoinDesk reported Thursday that Ben Edgington, who coordinates Ethereum's fast-finality research, filed a draft on Wednesday with Francesco D'Amato, Roberto Saltini, Yann Vonlanthen, and Mikhail Kalinin. The initial target is to cut the wait for a transaction to be treated as final from about 16 minutes to roughly four. The draft is unfinished and has no activation date. Citing Ethereum Foundation research on idle capital, CoinDesk said a cross-chain service advancing $1 million an hour would have about $267,000 tied up during a 16-minute wait, falling to about $67,000 at four minutes. Circle's fast USDC route off Ethereum currently charges 0.01%, or $1 per $10,000, its standard route has no Circle transfer fee, and fast transfers waiting on final settlement are capped. Today one validator vote both helps pick the next block and helps lock older blocks, so finality can move only as fast as that rotation; the draft splits the two jobs. As of Thursday, voting timings, penalties, and parts of the security analysis were still open. Edgington wrote that many aspects remain to be decided and that the immediate aim is to obtain a proposal number.

**Links:**

- [CoinDesk — Ethereum's four-minute settlement plan could lower the cost of moving crypto](https://www.coindesk.com/tech/2026/10/22/ethereum-s-four-minute-settlement-plan-could-lower-the-cost-of-moving-crypto)

### 6. Uniswap v4 aggregator hooks go live on Ethereum mainnet, Base, and Robinhood Chain (DeFi)
**Summary:** On October 22, Uniswap said aggregator hooks are live in the Uniswap web app and API on Ethereum mainnet, Base, and Robinhood Chain. Each hook wraps an external liquidity source as a v4 pool. The router pulls fresh quotes every block and settles a route across Uniswap pools and outside sources in one transaction, with nothing new to approve. Seven sources are available at launch, covering more than 90% of DEX activity on these chains. Swaps routed through outside sources pay protocol fees, which contribute to the UNI burn. Uniswap said the protocol has processed more than $4.5 trillion in volume. The Block, citing Uniswap Labs, said the first external sources include Fluid, Curve, and PancakeSwap, with more chains planned. The first aggregator hook went live on Tempo in March 2026, and Tempo is available only in the web app.

### 10. A proposed Ontario class action alleges Coldcard firmware flaws and more than $140 million in losses; Coinkite declines to comment (Litigation)
**Summary:** Bitcoin.com News reported and updated on October 22 that plaintiff Amir Reza Naderi filed a proposed class action in the Ontario Superior Court of Justice on September 11, 2026, against Toronto-based Coinkite Inc. and co-founders Rodolfo Novak and Peter D. Gray. The claim says a March 2021 firmware build error made affected Coldcard devices use MicroPython's Yasmarang pseudorandom generator instead of the hardware random-number generator, so recovery phrases could be predicted. It says exploitation began on July 30, 2026, with about 594 bitcoin, worth $38 million, moved in the first 25 minutes, and estimated losses thereafter above $140 million. The plaintiff says he lost 1.22940052 bitcoin, about $79,764, on August 6, and that engineer James O'Beirne warned the company in May 2025. The proposed class covers personal-use buyers of Mk2, Mk3, Mk4, Mk5, and Q devices. The allegations have not been proved in court, and no damages have been awarded. Coinkite told Bitcoin.com News the same day: "We can't comment on pending litigation." It said it expects to publish a full postmortem within days covering the cause, remediation, and independent verification. The company disclosed the flaw on July 30 and shipped fixed firmware the next day, but an update does not change a phrase already generated.

The requested relief includes a declaration that the profits are unconstitutional, disclosure of other foreign crypto emoluments, and return of profits found illegal. CNBC said Trump's 2025 annual disclosure reported more than $1.4 billion in crypto income, nearly $800 million of it tied to World Liberty, and that a family-affiliated entity owns about 38% of the company.
