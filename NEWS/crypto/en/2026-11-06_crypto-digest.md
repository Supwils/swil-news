## I. Regulation & Policy

### 1. FinCEN and the Fed Withdraw Plan to Cut Cross-Border Transfer Threshold to $250 (Regulation)
**Summary:** The U.S. Treasury’s Financial Crimes Enforcement Network (FinCEN) and the Federal Reserve Board filed a joint withdrawal notice on Nov. 6, abandoning a 2020 proposal that would have lowered the recordkeeping and Travel Rule threshold for funds transfers beginning or ending outside the United States from $3,000 to $250 and clarified coverage of convertible virtual currency and certain digital assets. The agencies said they “will not take further action” on the NPRM; the withdrawal is scheduled to take effect upon Federal Register publication on Nov. 9, leaving the existing $3,000 threshold in place. They cited the proposal’s age, intervening crypto-market and GENIUS Act developments, and the need for fresh public comment before any similar amendments; nearly 3,000 comments largely opposed lower-threshold compliance costs and expanded personal-data retention.

### 2. House Democrats Float SEC-Led Rewrite of CLARITY-Style Market Structure (Regulation)
**Summary:** After Democrats took both chambers in the Nov. 3 midterms, Nov. 6 reporting said House Financial Services Democratic staff are discussing a next-Congress crypto market-structure draft that would expand SEC oversight of crypto markets, narrow the CFTC’s role, and build on Rep. Maxine Waters’ Crypto Investor and Consumer Bill of Rights—including custody segregation, SIPC-like coverage, monthly proof-of-reserves attestations, and limits on platforms trading against customers; formal bill text has not been released. The House-passed CLARITY framework had given the CFTC primary oversight of spot digital-commodity markets; unless revived in the lame-duck session, the industry faces a fresh start when the new Congress convenes in January 2027. Some Republicans have dismissed the effort as a “messaging bill,” and any statute would still need Senate votes and could face a presidential veto.

### 4. Greece Approves Crypto Tax Framework: 10% on Gains, 15% on Staking and DeFi Income (Regulation)
**Summary:** Greece’s parliament approved the country’s first comprehensive crypto tax framework, with rules set to take effect Jan. 1, 2027. Individuals will pay 10% on annual net crypto capital gains once they exceed €500—taxing the full gain, not only the excess—and using crypto to buy goods or services can also trigger tax; staking, crypto lending, and DeFi liquidity returns are treated as interest income taxed at 15%. Annual crypto losses above €500 can be carried forward five years against future crypto gains, and the law provides a 12-month window to declare past gains without late-filing penalties or interest if conditions are met, with tax due within 60 days of filing. The Finance Ministry says crypto-to-crypto swaps should not be taxable, but parliament’s Scientific Service warned the bill’s “for consideration” wording could cover such trades.

### 5. Nigeria’s Central Bank Introduces Stablecoin Licensing Rules and Bans Algorithmic Versions (Regulation)
**Summary:** The Block reported on Nov. 6 that Central Bank of Nigeria rules took effect Nov. 5, covering virtual-asset activity conducted in Nigeria, directed at Nigerian users, or affecting the country’s payments, financial, or FX systems—even without a local physical presence. The framework creates separate licensing categories for naira-referenced and foreign-currency-referenced stablecoins, requires written approval for each token’s classification, reserves, name, ticker, and supported chains, and limits foreign-currency stablecoins offered to Nigerians to Class 2 tokens issued by a Nigerian-incorporated Foreign Currency Issuer Licence holder. Issuers must meet AML/CFT, CDD, sanctions screening, monitoring, and Travel Rule duties; algorithmic stablecoins that rely primarily on market dynamics rather than eligible reserves are prohibited. Existing operators have 180 days to apply; the CBN oversees payment-related activity while the SEC retains securities jurisdiction.

## IV. Protocols & Infrastructure

### 8. Zcash Activates NU7 on Mainnet: Target Block Time Falls to 25 Seconds and Fee Reserve Starts (Protocols)
**Summary:** Zcash’s major NU7 network upgrade activated on mainnet at block height 3,543,405 (around Nov. 5–6 UTC), The Block and CoinDesk confirmed on Nov. 6. Target block interval drops from 75 seconds to 25 seconds, with subsidies scaled so daily issuance, the 21 million ZEC cap, and the halving schedule stay essentially unchanged. The Network Sustainability Mechanism removes 60% of each block’s transaction fees from circulation into a public reserve (miners receive 40%), with gradual reissuance via block rewards expected to begin around 2031. Version 4 transactions are disallowed, leaving about 12,224 ZEC in the legacy Sprout shielded pool unspendable; node operators had to upgrade to stay on the consensus chain. Spec estimates capacity for simple private payments could rise above about six per second while cutting worst-case wallet scan download burden.
