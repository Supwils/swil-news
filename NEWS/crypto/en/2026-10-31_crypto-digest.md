## I. Security Incidents & Withdrawal Restart

### 1. Binance Says ~90% of Stolen Assets Replenished, Top Tokens Fully Backed; Withdrawals Targeted for Sunday (Security)
**Summary:** On Oct. 31, 2026, Binance said it had restored backing for about 90% of the roughly $6.3 billion in token assets stolen from its hot wallets on Oct. 29, using its Secure Asset Fund for Users (SAFU), part of its futures insurance fund, and corporate treasury. Holdings of BTC, ETH, USDT, BNB, SOL, USDC, XRP, USD1, RLUSD, and ZEC are now fully backed; the remaining ~10% in less-liquid tokens will be bought via markets and OTC until coverage reaches 100%. Binance reiterated that it will absorb the losses and that user balances are intact, with the next milestone a withdrawal restart on Sunday, Nov. 1, subject to security checks; reserve snapshots and cryptographic proof are also in progress. Partners had already frozen about $3 billion in related assets. TRM Labs and others still link the attack with high confidence to North Korea–linked TraderTraitor; President Trump said “we don’t know” if North Korea was behind it and that the government is “looking at that.”

### 2. Coinbase, Kraken and Peers Harden Defenses; Industry Calls for Real-Time Sharing and Freezes (Security)
**Summary:** Reuters and others reported that after Binance’s ~$6.3 billion theft, exchanges moved quickly to reinforce their own systems: Coinbase said it adjusted offline key-storage systems “solely out of an abundance of caution,” while Kraken said it was monitoring industry incidents for lessons applicable to its defenses. Executives also warned that as regulators push deeper integration of digital assets into mainstream finance, exchange security gaps carry rising systemic cost.

## III. Regulation & Policy

### 4. Taiwan’s Central Bank and FSC Warn After Binance Hack; FSC to Tighten VASP Custodian Rules (Regulation)
**Summary:** Per Commercial Times coverage on Oct. 31, Taiwan’s central bank officials said Binance is an overseas platform not registered as a domestic compliant venue, that investors using such platforms often leave assets in platform custody, and that they should choose channels carefully; officials also said moved funds have largely been frozen and that Binance has pledged to cover losses. The Financial Supervisory Commission (FSC) said its principles are “customer rights first” and “disclose information quickly,” and that draft secondary rules under the virtual-asset services law will tighten custodian capital, business guarantee deposits, and custody-size requirements, with a public draft targeted before year-end after talks with VASP and banking associations.

### 5. Brazil’s Crypto Authorisation Deadline Passes Without Extension; Bank Channel Ban From Nov. 6 (Regulation)
**Summary:** The Central Bank of Brazil (BCB) set Oct. 30, 2026 as the deadline for virtual-asset service providers already operating in Brazil to file for authorisation; checks on Oct. 31 found no extension. The next key date is Nov. 6, after which banks, payment institutions, and other BCB-authorised firms may no longer carry out or enable virtual-asset operations with providers that are “not authorised to operate in the country,” covering trading, intermediation, custody, FX, and payment accounts except where the resolution expressly allows. Firms that filed on time may keep existing services while applications are reviewed but may not add new service types; non-filers must stop virtual-asset services within about 30 days of the deadline. The BCB has not yet published application counts or a transition list.

**Links:**

- [VASP Tracker — Brazil crypto authorisation deadline passes without extension](https://vasptracker.com/en/news/2026-10-31-brazil-crypto-authorisation-deadline-passes-without-extension/)

**Commentary:** Pairing an application cutoff with a bank “off-ramp ban” will quickly sort compliant from non-compliant VASPs’ fiat access and may lift Latin America’s compliance premium in stages.

### 6. Spain’s CNMV Echoes ESMA: Non-MiCA Stablecoins Should Not Circulate in the EU (Regulation)
**Summary:** Víctor Rodríguez, CNMV director general for policy and international affairs, said Spain “cannot accept” non-MiCA-compliant stablecoins in the EU, aligning with ESMA’s Oct. 8 opinion. ESMA expects national authorities to supervise crypto-asset services involving asset-referenced tokens (ARTs) and e-money tokens (EMTs) whose issuers lack MiCA authorisation, covering trading platforms, exchange, order execution, custody, advice, transfers, and portfolio management; preexisting exposures should generally be remediated within three months of the opinion, with only time-limited, supervised wind-down activities allowed. Local coverage has focused on unauthorised tokens such as USDT as affected examples.
