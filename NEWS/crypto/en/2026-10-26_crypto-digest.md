## I. Regulation and Policy

### 1. Consumer groups and California's regulator object to the SEC crypto fundraising exemptions (Regulation)
**Summary:** American Banker reported on October 26, 2026, that the comment period on the SEC proposal "Regulation Crypto Assets" closed on October 20, and that the California Department of Financial Protection and Innovation, Better Markets, and Americans for Financial Reform Education Fund criticized the two offering exemptions for weakening investor protection and questioned both the SEC's authority and whether agency rules can last like an act of Congress. The draft allows a one-time offering of up to $5 million over four years, or up to $75 million in each 12-month period with ongoing reporting for the larger exemption, plus a conditional safe harbor under which a qualifying crypto asset would no longer be treated as an investment contract, and it limits separate state registration of offerings that qualify for the federal exemptions. Better Markets asked the SEC to withdraw the proposal and cited a 2025 FBI internet-crime report in which cryptocurrency complaints accounted for the highest reported losses, more than $11 billion across 181,565 complaints, while Coinbase's letter asked the SEC to raise the startup exemption from $5 million to $20 million over four years and to remove the $75 million second-tier cap for issuers that provide audited financial statements.

### 2. The CFTC chair restates a voluntary federal registration path for exchanges, with comments due December 14 (Regulation)
**Summary:** crypto.news reported on October 26, 2026, that Chairman Mike Selig wrote on X, alongside a Bloomberg TV interview, that crypto markets deserve the protections long applied to U.S. derivatives markets and that, while Congress has yet to legislate, the CFTC is advancing a voluntary federal registration pathway for crypto exchanges. The pathway matches the October 5 advance notice covering Regulation Crypto Asset Transactions (CTX) and Regulation Crypto Asset Markets (CAM), which relies on section 2(c)(2)(D) of the Commodity Exchange Act and concerns retail crypto commodity transactions offered with leverage, margin, or financing; the notice does not put a final rule into effect. crypto.news says the notice appeared in the Federal Register on October 13, with comments due December 14, 2026, and reports the agency's preliminary view that an offer of leverage can bring transactions into the framework even when the customer does not actually borrow, while a spot-only exchange that makes no covered leverage offer can remain state-licensed, subject to anti-fraud and anti-manipulation authority.

**Links:**

- [crypto.news — CFTC advances voluntary crypto-exchange registration as Congress stalls](https://crypto.news/cftc-voluntary-crypto-exchange-registration/)
- [Bloomingbit — Selig says the CFTC is pursuing voluntary federal registration for crypto exchanges](https://en.bloomingbit.io/feed/news/122966)

**Commentary:** Monday adds the chair's public statement. Registration remains a voluntary consultation open through December 14, and state-licensed spot trading is not converted into a mandatory federal license.

### 3. The OCC encourages community banks to apply as payment-stablecoin issuers (Stablecoins)
**Summary:** CoinDesk reported on October 26, 2026, that the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency on Monday urged community banks to enter the stablecoin market, including by applying directly or through subsidiaries to become permitted payment-stablecoin issuers under the GENIUS Act, and said the opening complements its Community Bank Comeback initiatives. Bulletin 2026-51, dated October 21, says that issuing a payment stablecoin in the United States generally requires permission from January 18, 2027, that issuing without permission can be a criminal offense, and that an issuer with a substantially complete application pending by that date may request a waiver in writing for up to 12 months from that date. The bulletin also says the OCC will start accepting applications after the Office of Management and Budget approves the related information collection and will announce the opening in a press release.

## IV. Security

### 10. A third-party module bug at Bloom moves about 9,408 SOL (Security)
**Summary:** Bitcoin.com and TokenPost reported on October 26, 2026, that Bloom, a trading bot covering Solana and EVM networks, said the day's attack used a remote-code-execution flaw in a third-party module and moved about 9,408 SOL from 49 wallets belonging to nine users, with Bloom's investigation valuing the loss at about $947,000 and earlier on-chain estimates near $954,000.
