### 4. US: CFTC Seeks Comment on Retail Crypto Framework Until Dec 14

**Summary:** The CFTC's Oct 13 advance notice of proposed rulemaking would apply Section 2(c)(2)(D) of the Commodity Exchange Act to leveraged, margined or financed retail crypto transactions, and floats a new "crypto asset market" (CAM) category of contract market, generally requiring FCM intermediation and DCO clearing. It asserts the federal framework would displace conflicting state licensing; the notice contains no rule text and comments are due Dec 14, 2026.

**Links:**

- [Mayer Brown — CFTC Previews a Federal Framework for Some Retail Crypto Asset Transactions](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2026/11/cftc-previews-a-federal-framework-for-some-retail-crypto-asset-transactions)

### 5. US: FinCEN Names MEXC a Primary Money-Laundering Concern

**Summary:** On Nov 6, FinCEN used Section 9714 of the Combating Russian Money Laundering Act to designate MEXC Global a primary money-laundering concern, barring covered US financial institutions from transmitting funds, including convertible virtual currency, to or from it. FinCEN linked about $16.7 billion in transactions to illicit activity from May 2021 to May 2026, and the measure takes effect 30 days after Federal Register publication. The same day FinCEN and the Fed withdrew a 2020 proposal to cut the Travel Rule threshold from $3,000 to $250.

**Links:**

- [Lowenstein Sandler — Crypto Brief, November 12, 2026](https://www.lowenstein.com/news-insights/newsletters/crypto-brief-november-12-2026)

**Commentary:** Targeted action against offshore venues is intensifying while the Travel Rule threshold stays put: precision strikes, not broader reach.

### 7. Russia: Central Bank Allows Organized Crypto Trading on Exchanges From Nov 22

**Summary:** A Bank of Russia ordinance registered by the Ministry of Justice lets exchanges begin organized crypto trading from Nov 22, 2026, provided the central bank first approves their trading rules and they onboard intermediaries such as brokers, trust managers and central counterparties. The rules cover organized venues, not over-the-counter deals.
