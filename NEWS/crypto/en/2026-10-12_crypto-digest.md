## I. Regulation and Policy

### 1. The Financial Stability Board Sends the G20 a Note on Cross-Border Stablecoin Vulnerabilities, with Crisis Plans Still Untested (Regulation)
**Summary:** On October 12 the Financial Stability Board published "Global stablecoin arrangements: Cross-border vulnerabilities and financial stability monitoring," together with a letter from Chair Andrew Bailey to G20 finance ministers and central bank governors ahead of their October 15 meeting. The note says global stablecoins operate across borders and sectors, so a single jurisdiction may be unable to assess or address the vulnerabilities on its own, and crisis-management and insolvency arrangements remain untested. As of mid-2026 the stablecoin market was about $300 billion, with 99 percent by value pegged to the U.S. dollar. CoinDesk, citing the report, said affiliated firms can issue the same stablecoin and share liability while holding reserves in different jurisdictions; if holders can redeem with any issuer in the group, demand can pile onto one entity while the assets sit elsewhere. Stablecoins trade around the clock, while markets for the assets backing them have set hours, so a token can lose its peg in a run if redemptions cannot keep up with selling. The recommendations focus on cross-border information sharing, monitoring, and planning for distress, and they are not legally binding.

**Commentary:** Thursday's G20 meeting receives a monitoring note with no new lawmaking power, and the mismatch between multi-issuer redemptions and scattered reserves is now written down as a cross-border risk.

### 2. TD Cowen Says the CFTC Crypto Framework May Not Be Final Until Early 2028 (Regulation)
**Summary:** The Block reported on October 12 that Jaret Seiberg, managing director at TD Cowen's Washington Research Group, said in a Monday note that the Commodity Futures Trading Commission's advance notice of proposed rulemaking from a week earlier is a positive step for crypto platforms, and that the agency is unlikely to finish the rulemaking before early 2028, leaving a risk the rule is never finalized. He estimated a proposal no sooner than late in the first quarter of 2027 or early in the second, with a final rule possibly slipping to early 2028. The note said the draft would require proof-of-reserves audits and give the CFTC authority over state-licensed platforms, while skirting outright jurisdiction over spot transactions. After the Senate failed to pass legislation that would have given the CFTC broad spot authority, the Securities and Exchange Commission and the CFTC have filled the gap with agency rules, including an innovation exemption, without the durability a statute would provide. Seiberg said speeding the process would raise the chance a judge blocks it on procedural grounds, and that legal challenges could extend past 2028 even if the agency is careful.

### 3. Bank of Korea Governor Rules Out a General-Purpose CBDC and Backs a Bank-Led Won Stablecoin (Regulation)
**Summary:** The Asia Business Daily reported on October 12 that Bank of Korea Governor Hyun Song Shin, at the National Assembly Strategy and Finance Committee's audit of the central bank, said a general-purpose central bank digital currency for individuals has not been considered since 2023 and will absolutely not be pursued. Project Hangang uses a wholesale digital currency that moves only between the central bank and banks, while banks and individuals use deposit tokens that function like existing deposits. On privately issued won stablecoins he said the Bank is basically in favor, and that the approach would be a consortium led by private banks in line with the plan agreed with the government. Asked about side effects such as large capital outflows, he said it would depend on scale; most stablecoins in circulation are dollar-denominated, so how widely a won stablecoin would be used remains uncertain, and the Bank will work with the authorities on potential risks. The remarks announced no new licensing rule.

**Commentary:** Korea has placed a retail CBDC outside current policy, and the issuance path for a won stablecoin still points to a bank-led consortium while the statute remains unfinished.

### 4. The Bank of Russia Adds Sberbank to the Crypto Exchange Register, with a December 1 Product Target (Regulation)
**Summary:** crypto.news on October 12, citing Interfax, reported that the Bank of Russia has added Sberbank to the register of firms authorized to exchange digital currencies. Interfax put the exchange register at nine organizations, including Sberbank, VTB, and T-Invest Lab. The digital depository register contains five organizations, including VTB and Atomyze, and Sberbank entered that register earlier this month. The bank plans to introduce its first products on December 1 through interfaces customers already use, rather than a separate service. Registered firms must follow transaction-execution and recordkeeping rules from the day they enter the registers; institutions admitted under transitional provisions have until September 1, 2027, to meet the remaining requirements. The U.S. Treasury imposed full blocking sanctions on Sberbank in April 2022 under Executive Order 14024, and OFAC says those obligations apply to digital-currency transactions as well as fiat.

**Links:**

- [crypto.news — Sberbank wins Russia's approval for crypto exchange services](https://crypto.news/sberbank-wins-russia-approval-crypto-exchange-services/)

**Commentary:** Russia's largest bank is now on a supervised exchange register, and U.S. persons' crypto dealings with it remain inside the existing blocking sanctions.

### 7. Standard Chartered's Dubai Branch Starts Custody for RLUSD on the XRP Ledger; the LMAX Collateral Link Is Not Live (Institutions)
**Summary:** Decrypt reported on October 12 that Standard Chartered said on Monday its Dubai International Financial Centre branch now offers institutional custody for Ripple USD (RLUSD) issued on the XRP Ledger. The branch is regulated by the Dubai Financial Services Authority. The bank also joined a planned integration with LMAX Group that would let clients post RLUSD as collateral on agreed products through collateral mirroring: the tokens stay at the bank, and LMAX receives a mirrored record of their value. That leg is not live. It remains subject to final documentation, testing, and regulatory approvals, and the bank gave no date. Decrypt put RLUSD's market cap at about $2.45 billion, under 1 percent of a stablecoin market it described at about $306 billion. Standard Chartered has offered crypto custody in Dubai since 2024, and Dubai's regulator cleared RLUSD in June 2025.

## IV. Security

### 8. Analysts Trace as Much as About $94.5 Million Tied to CryptoBilis, and Tether Freezes $10 Million in USDT (Security)
**Summary:** CoinDesk reported on October 12 that blockchain analytics firm Bitquery traced about $93.2 million linked to Ledger devices sold by reseller CryptoBilis, saying attackers took mostly USDT and TRON tokens from 315 wallets across six chains. Security research platform YFarmX reported a higher estimate, about $94.5 million from 521 addresses across seven chains. Both figures come from public chain data. Ledger has not confirmed the total and did not say whether it is considering reimbursement. Bitquery said Tether froze $10 million in USDT tied to the theft, and that about $81 million could still be traced onchain as of October 11, with some funds moving through Tornado Cash and Zcash. Ledger said Sunday that the investigation continues, that confirmed unexpected drains were tied to devices sold by CryptoBilis, and that the affected volume was limited; it has confirmed an unauthorized hardware implant in at least one device. BleepingComputer quoted Ledger's advice that unused devices should not be set up and that assets on a device already in use should move to a newly generated recovery phrase. Genuine Check cannot detect an added implant when the original secure element is intact. The case is separate from an April phishing campaign that used a fake Ledger Live app and drained at least $9.5 million.
