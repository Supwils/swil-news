## I. Regulation & Policy

### 1. Hong Kong Passes Crypto-Asset Reporting Framework Bill Aligned With OECD CARF (Regulation)
**Summary:** The Hong Kong SAR Government on Nov. 4 welcomed the Legislative Council’s passage of the Inland Revenue (Amendment) (Crypto-Asset Reporting Framework and Amended Common Reporting Standard) Bill 2026, implementing the OECD Crypto-Asset Reporting Framework (CARF) and the latest Common Reporting Standard (CRS) amendments. Secretary for Financial Services and the Treasury Christopher Hui said aligning with automatic exchange-of-information standards is vital to Hong Kong’s standing as an international financial centre and should reinforce confidence among crypto-asset service providers and digital-asset users. CARF takes effect from 2027, when covered providers must collect specified data for Inland Revenue Department exchanges with partner jurisdictions from 2028; amended CRS takes effect in 2028, with exchanges under the new standard from 2029. The IRD will issue guidance and has formed a dedicated support team.

### 3. South Korea’s Ruling Party Formally Proposes Delaying the Crypto Income Tax (Regulation)
**Summary:** South Korea’s Democratic Party supreme council on Nov. 4 formally proposed that the government postpone the virtual-asset income tax due to take effect Jan. 1, 2027. Party leader Kim Min-seok said lawmakers want the Digital Asset Basic Act enacted first to set an overall framework and give firms and investors an adaptation period. Party members on the finance committee had agreed Nov. 3 to recommend a delay, with about two years (toward 2029) the leading option, citing fuller OECD CARF overseas tax-information exchange around 2029, alignment with pending digital-asset legislation, and the need to test National Tax Service–exchange reporting systems. Current law would tax annual crypto gains above 2.5 million won at about 22% including local tax; another postponement would be the fourth and still requires an Income Tax Act amendment. The finance minister had previously supported starting on schedule.

### 4. SEC Staff Treats Canton Coin as a Digital Commodity and Grants Minting No-Action Relief (Regulation)
**Summary:** In a Nov. 3 response, the SEC Division of Corporation Finance said it views Canton Coin as a “digital commodity” under the Commission’s March 2026 interpretive release: value derives from programmatic operation of the Canton Network and supply-demand dynamics rather than essential managerial efforts of others, and the network has no “central party.” Based on the facts presented, staff will not recommend enforcement action if Super Validators, Validators, and application providers mint Canton Coin programmatically as described without registration under Securities Act Section 5 or Exchange Act Section 12(g). Canton Network’s Nov. 4 release called it the first such staff relief since the interpretive release and the August Regulation Crypto Assets proposal. The letter stresses it is an enforcement position on the submitted facts only, not a legal conclusion, and different facts could yield a different outcome.

**Summary:** Gnosis and the EEZ team on Nov. 4 at Devcon in Mumbai launched Rollup X, the Ethereum Economic Zone’s first public test network. ## V. Security Incidents

### 10. Binance Shareholder Demands Answers After ~$6.3B Hot-Wallet Breach (Security)
**Summary:** Crypto Briefing reported on Nov. 4 that a Binance shareholder publicly demanded the exchange explain the attack path, accept responsibility, and harden security after the roughly $6.3 billion hot-wallet theft. The incident around Oct. 29 moved funds across about 10 blockchains in roughly 20 minutes; early findings pointed to a compromised DevOps engineer laptop, with later analysis describing a multi-month campaign involving social engineering and malware. The FBI attributed the attack to North Korea–linked TraderTraitor. Binance’s co-CEOs pledged to cover losses with corporate funds and SAFU, set aside more than about $6.34 billion, said roughly $2.85–$3 billion was frozen, and phased withdrawals back from Nov. 1. Shareholder focus now shifts to whether a detailed incident report and proof of reserves withstand external scrutiny.
