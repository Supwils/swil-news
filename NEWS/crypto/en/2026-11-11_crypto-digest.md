### 1. OKX–ICE venture OKXICE sets Nov. 23 launch for tokenized securities under SEC innovation exemption (Regulation)
**Summary:** On November 11, OKX and Intercontinental Exchange (ICE)—parent of the NYSE—said their joint venture OKXICE will launch a Tokenized Securities Venue (TSV) on November 23 under the SEC’s five-year innovation exemption introduced in September, offering roughly 24/7 trading to U.S. users who complete CeDeFi Wallet onboarding. The firms said about 70 assets are confirmed so far, including tokenized shares tied to issuers such as Coinbase, Circle, and Robinhood, plus about 59 ETFs. Reporting noted that around 67 companies—including Meta, Home Depot, Netflix, Tesla, and Costco—had objected to unaffiliated third-party tokenization of their stock as of early November filings; Nasdaq has also warned that trading across disconnected venues could fragment liquidity. OKXICE’s public notice describes a permissioned Uniswap v4 AMM design on X Layer, pairing tokenized NMS stocks with payment stablecoins, without registering as a traditional exchange.

### 2. CFTC warns Kalshi of possible enforcement over event-contract filings (Regulation)
**Summary:** Politico, CNBC, Reuters and others reported on November 10–11 that the Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC) privately warned prediction-market platform Kalshi that enforcement charges could follow a months-long probe into whether it properly notified the agency about new event contracts, including dozens of products still in development. Kalshi spokesperson Elisabeth Diana said the notice concerns “a procedural error involving the submission of a handful of contracts, many of which weren’t live,” and that the firm is addressing it; CNBC separately reported that weather-related contracts were flagged. Self-certification lets a CFTC-regulated exchange list products as soon as the next day after certifying compliance. The CFTC is also reported to be investigating Polymarket. ### 3. SEC grants accelerated approval for Nasdaq ISE rules on digital-commodity ETP options (Regulation)
**Summary:** On November 10 the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission granted accelerated approval to Nasdaq ISE rule filing SR-ISE-2026-42 (Release No. 34-106863), aligning ISE’s options listing criteria with Nasdaq’s revised generic standards for commodity-based trusts. The change replaces “crypto asset” language with “digital commodity” and allows up to about 15% of a qualifying trust’s NAV in digital commodities that lack a comprehensive surveillance-sharing agreement (measured on a gross notional basis for derivatives). Each underlying digital commodity must still meet an average daily global market-value test of at least about $700 million over the prior 12 months. The order creates an eligibility framework; it does not itself launch a specific options ticker or guarantee which funds will qualify.

- [BlockBriefing — SEC approves Nasdaq ISE rules for crypto ETF options](https://blockbriefing.net/sec-nasdaq-ise-crypto-etf-options-approval-november-2026/)

### 4. FSB urges Türkiye to build a stablecoin regime; Taiwan FSC targets Q1 2027 for Virtual Asset Service Act (Regulation)
**Summary:** On November 11 the Financial Stability Board published a peer review of Türkiye, praising progress in bringing most crypto-asset activity under registration, custody segregation, and a domestic ban on crypto payments, while urging a dedicated stablecoin regime, stronger enforcement, financial-stability monitoring, and cross-border information sharing to align with the FSB’s global crypto framework. The same day, Taiwan Financial Supervisory Commission deputy director Huang Hou-ming said at a Virtual Asset Forum that the Virtual Asset Service Act is expected to take effect in the first quarter of 2027, moving the market into a “fourth stage” of comprehensive oversight covering VASPs, finance/operations, cybersecurity, and stablecoins. Multiple implementing rules have been pre-announced, and the law is designed to leave administrative room for innovations such as crypto lending.

### 10. Pendle opens four fixed-yield USDe markets with terms up to 12 months (DeFi)
**Summary:** Pendle opened four fixed-yield USDe markets on Ethereum around November 11, with maturities of three, six, nine, and 12 months. Buyers can purchase discounted PT-USDe and redeem one-for-one into USDe at maturity, locking in a yield set by the purchase discount. Each pool starts with a $50 million cap that can be raised once filled; caps apply per pool rather than across all four. Ethena said the markets will not include Ethena-token incentives and that it will receive half of the fees from third-party-operated Ethena pools on Pendle that would otherwise go to Pendle’s treasury. The launch expands term-structure products for yield-bearing synthetic dollars even as aggregate stablecoin supply contracts.

**Links:**

- [TokenPost — Pendle opens four fixed-yield USDe markets with terms up to 12 months](https://www.tokenpost.com/news/investing/54108)

**Commentary:** In a month when dollars are leaving crypto, clear-maturity yield pools are still competing for the remaining stablecoins—structured yield demand has not collapsed with supply.
