## I. Regulation and Policy

### 1. Comments close today on the SEC's Regulation Crypto Assets, leaving the offering exemptions and investment-contract safe harbor as a proposal (Regulation)
**Summary:** The SEC's rule page shows that public comments on Regulation Crypto Assets, File No. S7-2026-27, Release Nos. 33-11434 and 34-106150 (RIN 3235-AN38), are due October 20, 2026. The proposal would create two exemptions from section 5 of the Securities Act of 1933: offerings of up to $5 million over four years, and offerings of up to $75 million in each 12-month period. Issuers would make principles-based disclosures and would remain subject to antifraud and antimanipulation provisions. The larger exemption would also require financial statements and ongoing reporting. A conditional safe harbor would treat a crypto asset that meets its conditions as not subject to an investment contract for purposes of the definitions of "security" in the Securities Act and the Exchange Act. The Federal Register published the proposal on August 21, 2026. Closing the comment window does not put the rule into effect. The Commission still has to review the comments and vote on any final text.

### 2. The CFTC compute-derivatives request closes the same day, and the Hyperliquid Policy Center asks for GPU and inference perpetuals (Regulation)
**Summary:** Federal Register document 2026-17163 (91 FR 54259, RIN 3038-AF77) requires comments on listing compute derivatives to be received on or before October 20, 2026. It is a request for comment, not a proposed rule. crypto.news reported Tuesday that the Hyperliquid Policy Center wrote to the CFTC on October 20, in a letter signed by chief executive Jake Chervinsky and senior counsel Brad Bourque, asking the agency to allow U.S. futures and perpetual contracts tied to GPU rental prices and the cost of running AI models. The letter says rented computing capacity is a commodity under the Commodity Exchange Act. It argues that perpetuals can hedge round-the-clock rental and inference bills, while training runs or purchases with a known end date fit dated futures better. The group also asks the Commission to allow competing settlement benchmarks and to handle thin cash markets with position limits, funding-rate caps, and published index rules, rather than delaying listings because cash-market transparency is incomplete. crypto.news says the letter cites Silicon Data figures putting Nvidia H100 marketplace rental rates at about $2.50 to $3.19 per GPU-hour in the second half of 2024, versus about $1.92 to $2.00 in the second half of 2025, and says NYMEX and ICE Futures U.S. had proposed cash-settled compute futures that had not begun trading when the letter was written. The filing is a policy recommendation. It is not an approval, and it is not a Hyperliquid product launch.

### 3. The Blockchain Association files a 26-page letter on GENIUS Act section 3, asking Treasury to narrow service-provider and knowing-participation rules (Regulation)
**Summary:** The Block reported on October 20 that the Blockchain Association filed a 26-page comment letter on Monday on the Treasury proposal implementing section 3 of the GENIUS Act. That section governs who may issue, offer, and sell payment stablecoins in the United States. The association said it supports the Treasury's aim of having stablecoins issued by regulated firms with adequate reserves, but it asked for a clearer definition of digital-asset service providers: Congress wrote the definition for businesses serving customers, and it excluded self-custody software, validators, and firms trading only their own assets. On "knowing participation" in unlawful issuance, the association said liability should require actual knowledge and substantial assistance in coordination with the issuer, and that ordinary secondary-market trading should not count as participation. For foreign issuers, it opposed open-ended due diligence by service providers and recommended reliance on regulators' public prohibition lists. It also asked the Treasury to replace a "reasonably believes" test for whether a counterparty is in the United States with a standard based on actual knowledge and information gathered in the ordinary course of business. The Block said the comment period closed Monday and that the federal regulations portal showed 123 submissions. The Act is expected to take effect in January 2027. None of the requests binds anyone unless it is written into a final rule.

**Links:**

- [The Block — Blockchain Association asks Treasury for clearer DeFi and foreign-issuer rules](https://www.theblock.co/news/regulation/2026-10-20-blockchain-association-clearer-rules-genius-act-421092)

**Commentary:** The comment period is already closed. Whether self-custody software and secondary trading stay outside criminal participation depends on how Treasury rewrites the final text.

### 4. France's National Assembly passes an amendment 38-37 that would end tax deferral on swaps into stablecoins from 2027 (Regulation)
**Summary:** CoinDesk reported Tuesday that France's National Assembly adopted amendment I-3898 to the 2027 finance bill on October 16 by 38 votes to 37, with 25 abstentions. From January 1, 2027, the text would remove tax deferral when an investor receives an e-money token as defined by the European Union's Markets in Crypto-Assets regulation. Under current rules, exchanges of crypto assets without an extra cash payment generally defer tax. Taxable gains on private investments face a default rate of 31.4%, and investors may instead elect the progressive income-tax scale. After the change, moving into a qualifying stablecoin could create a taxable gain even if the funds are not withdrawn in fiat to a bank account. Public Accounts Minister David Amiel asked lawmakers to withdraw the amendment and said he would oppose it otherwise, arguing that stablecoins must first be defined and that reallocations inside a portfolio must be traceable. Finance committee rapporteur general Philippe Juvin favored keeping the deferral because of volatility. The text would also replace a portfolio-wide capital-gains calculation with the acquisition cost of the assets sold, using a weighted average for units of the same crypto asset bought at different prices. The National Assembly site lists the amendment as adopted. It still has to clear the rest of the budget process and can be amended or removed before it becomes law.

**Links:**

- [CoinDesk — France's parliament votes to hit crypto-to-stablecoin trades with a 31% levy](https://www.coindesk.com/policy/2026/10/20/france-s-parliament-approves-vote-to-hit-crypto-to-stablecoin-trades-with-31-levy)
- [French National Assembly — Finance bill 2027 amendment I-3898](https://www.assemblee-nationale.fr/dyn/17/amendements/3210A/AN/3898.pdf)

**Commentary:** A one-vote margin advanced a budget amendment, not a tax that is already in force. If it survives, a fiat withdrawal would no longer be required before a stablecoin position can trigger tax.

### 5. Taiwan's FSC publishes a virtual-asset travel rule, requiring extra identity data above NT$30,000 on domestic transfers (Regulation)
**Summary:** On October 20, 2026, Taiwan's Financial Supervisory Commission amended and issued the regulations on anti-money laundering and countering the financing of terrorism for enterprises or persons providing virtual-asset services, implementing the Financial Action Task Force travel rule. Virtual-asset service providers must apply the rule regardless of amount. When a transfer exceeds NT$30,000, a natural person must also provide date of birth and residential address, and a legal person must also provide an official identification number and the address of its place of registration. A receiving provider, for transfers above NT$30,000, must match the beneficiary information supplied by the sender against its own customer records. CNA quoted Securities and Futures Bureau deputy director-general Huang Chung-hao at a regular briefing: the rule applies immediately to transfers between domestic platforms, and cross-border transfers are expected by the end of 2027. Eleven firms have completed anti-money-laundering registration. Eight of them handle transfers, and three have not yet opened; if they later offer transfers, they must comply. A customer moving virtual assets from one domestic provider to another must supply the information required by the rule, and the sending firm may execute the transfer only after passing that information to the receiver.

### 8. FINMA approves Switzerland's first retail ETF that holds bitcoin directly, and BIT announces ticker BBIT with a November SIX listing expected (Institutions)
**Summary:** On October 20, 2026, the Swiss Financial Market Supervisory Authority FINMA said it had approved, for the first time, an exchange-traded retail fund that invests directly in bitcoin. Under Swiss law the fund is an "other fund for alternative investments" with a specific risk. FINMA said bitcoin's long trading history and much higher volume than other cryptocurrencies justified an exemption from the usual risk-diversification rules, and that other providers have also submitted or announced bitcoin ETF applications. In 2021 FINMA authorized a fund for qualified investors that invests mainly in a basket of cryptocurrencies. Supervised firms must mitigate operational and custody risks under guidance published on January 12, 2026. Point-of-sale conduct rules under the Financial Services Act apply, and fund documents must explain the risks. FINMA also said bitcoin market risk remains very high even through an approved ETF, and that invested capital can lose a significant part of its value. The regulator's release does not name the issuer or the ticker. BIT Asset Management announced the same day that FINMA had approved its Bitcoin Fund ETF (ticker BBIT), called it Switzerland's first and currently Europe's only spot bitcoin ETF, and said trading on SIX Swiss Exchange is expected in November. The company said most bitcoin products in Europe are exchange-traded products that carry issuer risk, while BBIT is a collective investment scheme whose assets are segregated from the manager. The Block reported that a FINMA spokesperson declined to identify the approved fund or its issuer.

## IV. Security

### 9. NGRAVE confirms its LIQUID push channel was hijacked and traces about $2.56 million; Bitquery puts about $2.16 million on Hyperliquid (Security)
**Summary:** NGRAVE published an incident report on October 19. At about 21:30 UTC on October 17, attackers hijacked a third-party provider that sends LIQUID push notifications and delivered a fake security alert saying a portfolio had been synced to a Pixel 9 in Moscow. The link led to a copy of NGRAVE's website that asked for a 24-word recovery phrase or a Perfect Key. Wallets of people who entered either credential were emptied within minutes. The company said the LIQUID code and the private keys on ZERO devices were not breached, and that the earliest sign of access to the notification provider was October 11. It cut off the first key at 21:40 UTC and shut down all notification sending at 22:45 UTC. The last theft it has traced was at 03:51 UTC on October 18. At October 17 prices, the company has traced about $2.56 million from about 70 addresses on four networks, and it calls that a minimum: about $1.77 million in bitcoin, about $454,000 of XRP in 54 deposits to ChangeNOW, about $316,000 on Ethereum that was sold and then moved as 130.47 ETH through LI.FI, and about $18,000 on Stellar. CryptoSlate reported Tuesday, citing Bitquery, that about $2.65 million involved 84 wallets, that about $2.16 million reached 39 Hyperliquid accounts, and that those accounts bought about 3,593 XMR1 tokens, which track the price of Monero. As of Bitquery's recorded check on October 19, the tokens had not been sold or withdrawn and were worth about $2.12 million. The two traces do not match on amount or address count.
