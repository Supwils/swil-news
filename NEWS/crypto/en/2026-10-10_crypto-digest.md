## I. Regulation and Policy

### 1. France's Finance Committee Backs a Stablecoin-Swap Tax and a Crypto Exit Tax, Then Rejects the Budget's Revenue Section (Regulation)
**Summary:** Decrypt reported on October 10 that the French National Assembly's Finance Committee adopted crypto tax amendments this week. From January 1, 2027, swapping crypto into MiCA electronic-money tokens, the category covering most stablecoins tied to a single currency, would count as a taxable sale, with the gain measured against the original cost. A second amendment would extend the exit tax to crypto when a household's combined holdings, including assets kept with custodians, exceed €800,000 and the taxpayer was a French tax resident for at least six of the previous 10 years, for moves from January 1, 2027. The threshold matches the one already used for shares. Nicolas Sansu of the left-wing GDR group and 16 co-signers filed the stablecoin text. France currently taxes crypto when it is sold for regular money or spent, so a bitcoin-to-stablecoin swap is not taxed. The amendment names no separate rate and defers to the flat tax, which stands at 31.4% from January 1 after the 2026 social-security financing law raised the social-charge portion from 17.2% to 18.6%. Crypto-to-crypto swaps with no cash component would not count as sales for the exit tax, but a taxpayer would have to declare all crypto on the moving date, including assets abroad and in self-custody. A further amendment from Daniel Labaronne, also adopted in committee, would let investors carry crypto losses forward for 10 years. On Friday the committee rejected the budget's entire revenue section by 31 votes to 3, so the full Assembly starts from the government's original text. The amendments do not carry over and would have to be tabled again for the floor debate that opens October 13, with a vote set for October 20. Neither measure is law.

### 4. Bitcoin Has Already Logged 10 Three-Sigma Days in 2026, More Than in All of 2018 (Market)
**Summary:** A CoinDesk count published October 10 found 10 days in 2026 when bitcoin moved at least three times its prior 30-day realized volatility, more than the eight such days in all of 2018, when bitcoin fell 73%. Annualized volatility is about 46% this year, against about 84% in 2018, and the three-sigma moves have averaged about 7%, down from about 10% eight years ago. Since 2024, bitcoin's annualized volatility has been about 47%, similar to Nvidia, but bitcoin has logged 26 three-sigma days in that span, against eight for Nvidia, 16 for the S&P 500, and 12 for gold. Nicolas Quatravaux, head of EMEA at Paradigm, said the average day is calmer while shocks from macro news, leverage, and positioning remain. Deribit chief executive Luuk Strijers said standard value-at-risk does not capture the full tail, which is why the industry has been moving toward expected shortfall. Alexander Blume, chief executive of Two Prime, said call overwriting is a crowded trade and that short covering amplifies rallies. On September 21, bitcoin's latest three-sigma jump, Paradigm facilitated a record $6.7 billion in options trades. Quatravaux said he had not heard of a desk taking a bad hit that day, and that these days are not going away.

## V. Security and Enforcement

### 8. A Jury Convicts a Maryland Man of Computer Fraud and Money Laundering over the Uranium Finance Hacks (Enforcement)
**Summary:** WTOP reported on October 10 that Jonathan Spalletta, 36, of Rockville, Maryland, was found guilty this week of computer fraud and money laundering after a six-day trial in New York. Prosecutors said he hacked the decentralized exchange Uranium Finance twice in April 2021. The first exploit withdrew $1.4 million. The second used an error to pull $53.3 million from liquidity pools, and the exchange shut down for lack of funds. Evidence included a message in which he said he "did a crypto heist" and that "Crypto is all fake internet money anyway." Court documents said law enforcement seized about $31 million in cryptocurrency taken from Uranium in February 2025. Computer fraud carries a maximum of 10 years in prison, and money laundering a maximum of 20. Decrypt wrote on October 8 that federal prosecutors announced the verdict on Wednesday and that U.S. District Judge Jed S. Rakoff presided. Decrypt dated the first hack to April 8, 2021, and the second to April 28, and said the funds moved through a series of transactions including Tornado Cash. The sentence is for the judge to set.
