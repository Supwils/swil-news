### 1. A Manhattan federal judge rejects Gemini and the CFTC's joint bid to vacate a $5 million settlement (Litigation)
**Summary:** Reuters reported on October 16 that U.S. District Judge Alvin Hellerstein ruled on Thursday that the Commodity Futures Trading Commission and Gemini Trust Co. had not met the standard to set aside their settlement. The CFTC sued Gemini in 2022 over allegedly false or misleading statements during the review of a proposed bitcoin futures contract. The January 2025 settlement, reached shortly before trial, imposed a $5 million penalty. In May the agency and the company jointly asked to wipe out the settlement and the court order, saying the 2022 complaint would not have been filed under current enforcement priorities and that the CFTC had "resorted to inappropriate tactics." Gemini agreed not to seek a refund if the deal were vacated. The judge wrote that Gemini had litigated for more than three years and then settled voluntarily, and that the agency's current view does not require vacatur. CoinDesk reported that the order barring Gemini from misleading the CFTC or publicly denying the findings remains in place.

### 2. Singapore's MAS stablecoin consultation closes on October 16, with a proposed interest ban and a new issuance licence (Stablecoins)
**Summary:** Monetary Authority of Singapore consultation P015-2026 asked for written comments by October 16, 2026, on writing the single-currency stablecoin framework into the Payment Services Act 2019. The draft adds a stablecoin-issuance licence: a firm without it may not hold itself out as issuing an MAS-regulated stablecoin. Issuers would have to keep reserve assets at least equal to the par value of coins in circulation and meet redemption requests in the pegged currency within MAS-prescribed timeframes. They generally could not carry on other regulated activities. The paper would also bar interest, a return, or any other benefit attributable to holding an MAS-regulated stablecoin, so the token is treated as a payment instrument rather than a savings product. On a minimum share of reserves in cash or bank deposits, MAS cites ranges used or planned in the UK and EU — about 5% to 30% for non-systemic coins and 40% to 60% for systemic ones — and asks for views rather than setting its own threshold. MAS also proposes a power to designate a stablecoin as systemic whether it is issued in Singapore or abroad.

**Links:**

- [MAS — Consultation on Payment Services Act amendments for stablecoin regulation](https://www.mas.gov.sg/publications/consultations/2026/consultation-on-proposed-amendments-to-the-payment-services-act-for-stablecoin-regulation)
- [MAS consultation paper P015-2026 (PDF)](https://www.mas.gov.sg/-/media/mas-media-library/publications/consultations/pd/2026/sept-2026_consultation-paper-on-proposed-amendments-to-ps-act-for-stablecoins-regulation/consultation-paper-on-proposed-amendments-to-ps-act-for-stablecoins-regulation_vfinal.pdf)

**Commentary:** The comment window closes today, while subsidiary legislation and any cash-reserve floor are still open, so issuers are aligning to a licence boundary and an interest ban rather than to a finished rulebook.

### 8. MSCI's non-operating-company rule takes effect at the November 30 close, and Strategy falls 4.2% (Institutions)
**Summary:** At 8:48 p.m. GMT on October 15, MSCI said it will treat non-operating companies as ineligible for the MSCI Global Investable Market Indexes, implementing the August 3, 2026 consultation with minor modifications. The change is part of the November 2026 index review and takes effect as of the close of November 30, 2026. An issuer is screened first for substantial operating assets; those that fail are then tested on five financial ratios and are ineligible if flagged on four or more. Current constituents are removed only if they fail in each of their two most recent annual filing periods. The final list of affected names is due on November 11. Interim measures from October 10, 2025, covering companies whose digital-asset holdings were 50% or more of total assets, will be discontinued with that review. Channel NewsAsia, citing Reuters, said Strategy and Japan's Metaplanet are on the list of potential deletions and SharpLink is among names on a watchlist. Strategy shares fell 4.2% on Friday, its market value was about $64.3 billion, and the company warned last year that an exclusion would result in $2.8 billion of its stock being liquidated.

## IV. DeFi and Protocols

### 11. Lido proposes Fallback Exits for Lido Lend, letting lenders exit into borrower collateral when liquidity runs short (DeFi)
**Summary:** Lido contributor Izzy posted on the governance forum at 14:04 UTC on October 16 describing Fallback Exits for the proposed Lido Lend market. When loan-asset liquidity is insufficient, a lender could complete a withdrawal by drawing on eligible borrower collateral. The design is for isolated markets in highly correlated assets, with examples including wETH against wstETH and dollars against Treasury real-world assets. If the mechanism is used, the outstanding debt of the borrowers whose collateral is taken is reduced by a matching amount. Subject to governance approval of the specification, collateral would be taken from the riskiest positions first, those closest to the liquidation loan-to-value ratio. Technical specifications and market parameters are expected within two weeks, and a mainnet launch still requires the Lido DAO to accept the protocol. Lido Lend was first described on October 7 as a modified fork of Morpho Blue, with a target of this quarter if governance passes.

**Links:**

- [Lido research forum — Lido Lend: Fallback Exits](https://research.lido.fi/t/unveiling-lido-lend-security-first-lending/11987)

**Commentary:** The exit becomes correlated collateral instead of a wait for the pool to refill, so the lender does not receive the asset originally deposited, and the contracts are still awaiting a published spec and audits.
