## I. Regulation and Policy

### 1. Australia's ASIC opens consultation on digital-asset platform standards ahead of an April 8, 2027 start date (Regulation)
**Summary:** On October 15, the Australian Securities and Investments Commission released Consultation Paper 392 on guidance and standards for digital asset platforms and tokenised custody platforms under the Corporations Amendment (Digital Assets Framework) Act 2026. The package covers draft compliance guidance, asset-holding standards, trading and settlement standards, and financial requirements for licensees. Comments are due at 5 p.m. AEDT on November 27, 2026, and ASIC's roadmap puts final guidance and instruments in March 2027. The law starts on April 8, 2027. Firms that will be a digital asset platform or tokenised custody platform must apply for or vary an Australian financial services licence by October 7, 2027, and may keep operating while the application is reviewed. Commissioner Alan Kirkland said the principle is "same activity, same risk, same regulatory outcome." ASIC has recorded more than 140 applications to provide digital-asset financial services, including more than 110 received during the INFO 225 no-action period from October 2025 to September 30, 2026.

### 2. England's Court of Appeal sets aside a flawed Bitcoin delivery order, letting an exchange customer rely on contractual rights (Litigation)
**Summary:** Herbert Smith Freehills Kramer wrote on October 15 that the Court of Appeal granted Kyrrex Ltd's application to set aside the relevant paragraphs of a summary judgment a fraud victim, Jones, had obtained on a flawed tracing report. In September 2022 the court held that Huobi was a constructive trustee of 89.6 bitcoin Jones had sent to a fraudulent platform and ordered delivery up. Huobi paid from a different wallet and reimbursed itself by debiting Kyrrex's account. The bitcoin was worth nearly 1.7 million pounds at the rate in the original order and more than 5 million pounds by October 2024. Lord Justice Nugee, with whom Lord Justice Peter Jackson and Lord Justice Foxton agreed, held that being "directly affected" under CPR 40.9 can rest on contractual rights, without a proprietary interest in the bitcoin. The court set aside the relevant paragraphs but did not order immediate repayment, and said ordering costs by a transfer of cryptocurrency lacked clear precedent: English law treats crypto as property, and "it would be a very significant step to treat them as money."

**Links:**

- [Herbert Smith Freehills Kramer — Court of Appeal sets aside a Bitcoin delivery order in a flawed tracing case](https://www.hsfkramer.com/notes/litigation/2026-10/court-of-appeal-sets-aside-order-for-delivery-up-of-bitcoin-on-application-of-third-party-in-flawed-crypto-tracing-case)

**Commentary:** A customer debited to satisfy an order against a named wallet can challenge that order on contractual rights, so a bad tracing report no longer locks the delivery in place by default.

## IV. Stablecoins and Payments

### 6. Mastercard and BVNK launch Global Money Platform, putting fiat and stablecoins on one API (Payments)
**Summary:** Mastercard and BVNK announced on October 15 the Mastercard Global Money Platform, connecting Mastercard's payments capabilities with BVNK's stablecoin infrastructure through a single API. They called it the first major product integration since Mastercard's acquisition of BVNK closed in August 2026. Banks, fintechs, and enterprises can embed multi-currency accounts that hold fiat and stablecoins, real-time conversion, stablecoin-linked cards, and local bank payouts through Mastercard Move, or use the platform for their own treasury, collections, and settlement. Nuvei, Marqeta, Ontop, Bolt Group, and Almond FinTech are among the first to integrate. Mastercard Move reaches more than 17 billion endpoints, with coverage expected to expand to more than 40 currencies across 200-plus countries and territories in 2027. Stablecoin balances can be spent at hundreds of millions of merchant locations, and card issuing is available in 130-plus countries. The platform is available now through BVNK, with stated future support for agentic commerce and regulated forms of money including tokenized deposits.

### 8. Fireblocks launches Liquidity Market so payment firms can compare OTC stablecoin quotes side by side (Infrastructure)
**Summary:** Fireblocks on October 15 launched Liquidity Market, a single screen where payment companies and other businesses can request, compare, and execute quotes from multiple over-the-counter desks while keeping each desk's existing credit and settlement terms. Design partners are MoneyGram, OpenPayd, Reap, TapTapSend, and Zepz. B2C2 and Zodia Markets are live at launch, with Cumberland, FalconX, GSR, Hercle, Nonco, and Wintermute to follow. Fireblocks said OTC and trading firms moved nearly $50 billion in stablecoins to payment companies, fintechs, and banks across its network over the prior 12 months. Fireblocks takes no spread, does not take custody at conversion, and does not sit in the flow of funds. Settlement is direct between the customer and the liquidity provider. The launch covers major stablecoins against the U.S. dollar, euro, pound, and other G10 currencies, and connecting an account or executing an order requires quorum approval. The company also said its platform has secured $16 trillion in digital-asset transactions.

**Links:**

- [PR Newswire — Fireblocks launches Liquidity Market for stablecoin payments](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/fireblocks-launches-liquidity-market-bringing-streaming-otc-pricing-into-stablecoin-payments-302909484.html)

**Commentary:** Quote comparison now sits inside the existing policy engine, but execution and settlement stay between the customer and the desk, so Fireblocks is not the counterparty on those stablecoin trades.

## V. DeFi and Protocols

### 9. Arbitrum opens a stablecoin growth program, paying 200,000 ARB to each of the first 10 teams to reach $10 million of USDG (DeFi)
**Summary:** Arbitrum's blog on October 15 opened applications for a stablecoin growth program. Eligible protocols must have at least $1 million in stablecoin balances at application, verified on DefiLlama, a live product, and audited contracts. They must make Global Dollar (USDG) their primary stablecoin on Arbitrum and move at least $1 million of total value locked into USDG. A 2 million ARB prize pool pays 200,000 ARB to each of the first 10 teams to reach $10 million of USDG total value locked, measured as a 30-day average, on top of performance-based incentives and possible marketing, technical support, and access to DRIP incentives. USDG is issued by Paxos Digital Singapore Pte. Ltd., supervised by the Monetary Authority of Singapore, and by Paxos Issuance Europe Oy, supervised by Finland's FIN-FSA and compliant with MiCA. Participation requires approval by the Arbitrum Foundation and is subject to the program terms.

**Links:**

- [Arbitrum blog — Introducing the Stablecoin Growth Program](https://blog.arbitrum.io/stablecoin-growth-program/)

**Commentary:** The 2 million ARB pool pays protocols that already hold $1 million in stablecoins and then grow that balance in USDG, so an empty new pool does not qualify for the prize.
