### 2. French Assembly Rejects Budget Revenue Section, Shelving Stablecoin Tax and Wallet-Reporting Amendments (Regulation)
**Summary:** France's National Assembly late on Nov. 4 rejected the revenue section of the 2027 state budget by 458–0 (with two abstentions), dropping previously adopted crypto amendments from the text sent to the Senate. Cointelegraph and others reported on Nov. 5 that the measures include taxing crypto-to-stablecoin conversions, requiring disclosure of self-custodied holdings of at least €100,000, and raising penalties for crypto platforms that fail to supply tax information from €10,000 to €50,000. The government will send the Senate the original budget text without those amendments; Senate debate is scheduled to begin Nov. 20, and the proposals could still be reintroduced.

### 3. South Korea Formally Moves to Delay Crypto Income Tax Another Two Years to 2029 (Regulation)
**Summary:** Finance Minister Lee Hyoung-il said on Nov. 5 in Seoul that the government will work with the National Assembly to postpone the virtual-asset income tax due in January 2027 by two years to 2029; if approved, it would be the fourth delay since the tax was legislated in 2020. Officials cited fuller OECD CARF information exchange with the United States around 2029, concern that taxing earlier could disadvantage the domestic market versus overseas venues, and the need to align with a proposed Digital Asset Basic Act. Current law would tax annual net gains above 2.5 million won at about 22% including local tax; the government also wants up to three years of loss carryforward and a public-private working group on staking and other tax treatments, with legislative amendments targeted for the 2027 session and detailed guidance in the first half of 2028.

- [The Korea Herald — Korea seeks another delay to crypto tax until 2029](https://www.koreaherald.com/article/10927485)

### 4. Ukraine's Central Bank Opposes Current Crypto Legalization Bill Over Monetary Sovereignty (Regulation)
**Summary:** The National Bank of Ukraine (NBU) said on Nov. 5 it will not support draft law No. 10225-d in its current form and wants revisions before a final vote. The NBU says it backs crypto regulation aligned with the EU's MiCA framework and IMF recommendations, but argues the final draft fails key safeguards: foreign-issued tokens unsupervised in their home jurisdictions could act as e-money and threaten monetary sovereignty and financial stability, and leaving the number of trading-platform operators in the first five years to the securities regulator's discretion departs from an earlier compromise to competitively select three operators, raising concentration and corruption risks. The bank said it received the final text late Tuesday with little time to review. The updated bill would set a transitional tax as low as about 5% on existing crypto assets through end-2028 and exempt crypto-to-crypto trades, with the NSSMC as primary market regulator.

**Links:**

- [CoinDesk — Ukraine's central bank opposes crypto bill citing monetary sovereignty](https://www.coindesk.com/business/2026/11/05/ukraine-s-central-bank-opposes-crypto-bill-citing-monetary-sovereignty-concerns)

**Commentary:** A central-bank veto pulls legalization back into money and market-access governance, adding fresh uncertainty to Eastern Europe's crypto statute fight.

### 9. Base Mainnet Activates Denim Upgrade, Cutting Native Block Times to About 200ms (Infrastructure)
**Summary:** Coinbase-incubated Ethereum L2 Base activated its fourth major upgrade, Denim, on mainnet around 18:00 UTC on Nov. 5 (after Sepolia on Oct. 21). Official docs and CryptoBriefing said block production moves from one canonical block about every two seconds to about 200ms—roughly five complete canonical blocks per second—replacing Flashblocks preconfirmations with canonical block streams. Denim also adds BaseTime millisecond on-chain timing and RPC timestamp fields, and tightens B20 transfer and compliance policies (including reverting transfers to a token's own contract address). Apps relying on Flashblocks or the `pending` tag need to migrate to canonical interfaces such as `newHeads`.

**Links:**

- [CryptoBriefing — Base activates Denim, cutting block times roughly tenfold](https://cryptobriefing.com/base-denim-upgrade-cuts-block-times/)
- [Base Docs — Denim upgrade overview](https://docs.base.org/upgrades/denim/overview)

**Commentary:** L2s are making trading- and payments-grade latency the default block cadence, pushing product design from preconfirmation hacks toward canonical finality UX.
