## I. Regulation and Policy

### 1. The Federal Register publishes the OCC's final GENIUS Act rule, effective January 18, 2027 (Regulation)
**Summary:** The Office of the Comptroller of the Currency's final rule implementing the GENIUS Act appeared in the Federal Register on October 23, 2026, as document 2026-21706, with an effective date of January 18, 2027, the same day as the statute. The OCC said it received about 328 comments. The rule removes the proposed rebuttable presumption that would have treated certain interest or yield payments through affiliates and related third parties as violations, and says the statutory text will be applied to the facts of each arrangement. The OCC is not publishing a list of permitted or prohibited reward programs, is not treating rewards that predate the GENIUS Act as compliant, and is not adding a de minimis exemption. For the two-business-day redemption standard, a business day is any day other than a Saturday, Sunday, or federal holiday. Bank Secrecy Act, anti-money-laundering, and sanctions requirements are left to a separate rulemaking with the Treasury Department. Reserve-asset provisions are more flexible than the proposal while keeping liquidity, diversification, and concentration requirements. The OCC said it will announce separately when it begins accepting issuer applications.

- [Federal Register — OCC final rule on payment stablecoins under the GENIUS Act](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/23/2026-21706/implementing-the-guiding-and-establishing-national-innovation-for-us-stablecoins-act-for-the)
- [Office of the Comptroller of the Currency — final rule implementing the GENIUS Act](https://occ.gov/news-issuances/news-releases/2026/nr-occ-2026-92.html)

**Commentary:** The rule now has a publication date and an effective date, and exchange or issuer reward programs still face case-by-case review without a listed safe harbor.

### 3. An Argentine federal judge dismisses the criminal inquiry into Milei and the LIBRA token (Litigation)
**Summary:** Al Jazeera reported on October 24, 2026, that federal judge Marcelo Martínez de Giorgi issued a ruling on Friday ending the criminal investigation of President Javier Milei and his sister Karina Milei over the collapse of the LIBRA token. The ruling says the reported events do not constitute a criminal offence, and that investor losses and the rise and fall in price were not enough to establish fraud. The judge wrote that Milei's office could have given his post greater impact, and that this does not itself amount to a fraudulent scheme. Milei recommended the memecoin on X in February 2025, later deleted the post, and denied any link. The Rio Times dates the ruling to October 23, 2026, describes it as about 60 pages, and says it dismisses 11 people under article 336, subsection 3, of the criminal procedure code, at the request of prosecutor Eduardo Taiano, including U.S. businessman Hayden Davis, who described himself as a launch adviser. Complaints say the token rose from about $0.01 to nearly $5 before crashing, that a few wallets took out about $100 million, and that more than 40,000 buyers lost money. Al Jazeera says Davis has acknowledged holding close to $100 million after the crash and denies wrongdoing. A post from the presidential office said the inquiry proved the president's innocence. The dismissal can be appealed and was not tested at trial.

## III. DeFi and Stablecoins

### 7. Circle Gateway adds cirBTC and EURC, targeting cross-chain access in under 500 milliseconds after finality (Stablecoin)
**Summary:** Circle said on its website on October 23, 2026, that Gateway now supports Circle Wrapped Bitcoin, cirBTC, and the euro stablecoin EURC alongside USDC. Each asset has its own unified balance, and Circle's target is cross-chain access in under 500 milliseconds after a deposit is finalized, with an explicit note that times and fees are not guaranteed. cirBTC is supported on Arc and Ethereum. EURC is supported on Arc, Avalanche, Base, Ethereum, Solana, and World Chain. Circle said it can mint natively on destination chains because it issues both assets, without relying on a third party's inventory. Gateway had processed more than $10 billion in volume as of October 23, a figure Circle attributes to internal data. Gateway fees for cirBTC and EURC are waived on Arc at launch and are 1 basis point off Arc, while USDC stays at 0.5 basis point. Nanopayments powered by Gateway now cover both assets on the networks where Gateway supports them: payments are aggregated off chain and settled on chain in batches, so individual payments do not add a separate gas charge, while the batch still does. Gateway is provided by Circle Technology Services, which describes it as non-custodial software that has not been reviewed or approved by the New York Department of Financial Services or any other regulator.

**Links:**

- [Circle — Gateway now supports cirBTC and EURC](https://www.circle.com/blog/circle-gateway-now-supports-cirbtc-and-eurc)

**Commentary:** The unified balance cuts the inventory a user has to pre-position on each chain, and minting and redemption stay inside Circle's own issuance and custody setup.
