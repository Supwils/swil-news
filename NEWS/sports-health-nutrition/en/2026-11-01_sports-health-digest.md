## I. Nutrition & Diet

### 1. American Journal of Clinical Nutrition RCT: In gestational diabetes, a higher-complex-carbohydrate/lower-fat diet cut postprandial free fatty acids by about 20% vs conventional low-carb/high-fat (Nutrition)
**Summary:** Teri L. Hernandez and colleagues ran a parallel RCT (NCT02244814) in diet-controlled adults with gestational diabetes mellitus (GDM), randomizing after diagnosis at ~28–30 weeks to an isocaloric CHOICE diet (~60% complex carbohydrate, 25% fat) or a conventional LC/CONV diet (~40% carbohydrate, 45% fat), with all meals provided for ~7.2 weeks. The American Journal of Clinical Nutrition published the breakfast-meal secondary metabolic analysis. About 23 participants per arm completed analysis: at 36–37 weeks, postprandial FFA AUC was ~20% higher on LC/CONV than CHOICE (P=0.016); CHOICE had ~12% higher glucose AUC yet remained clinically acceptable. Weight gain, birth weight, and newborn adiposity were similar. Authors argue lower dietary fat with higher-quality carbohydrate may jointly blunt TG/FFA exposure. The sample is modest and endpoints are postprandial metabolic measures, so this does not by itself rewrite all GDM guidance.

**Links:**

- [American Journal of Clinical Nutrition — Higher-complex-carbohydrate vs conventional low-carb diet and postprandial FFA in GDM](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ajcnut.2026.101525)
- [AJCN full text — CHOICE diet postprandial lipids and glucose](https://ajcn.nutrition.org/article/S0002-9165(26)00334-5/fulltext)

**Commentary:** GDM eating plans need not default to “lower carb at all costs”—under clinical monitoring, more complex carbohydrate and less fat may improve lipid fuel exposure; do not add sugar freely or stop glucose checks on your own.

### 2. Food & Function crossover RCT: 60 g/day unsalted skin-roasted peanuts for 16 weeks improved insulin-related cerebral blood-flow responses in memory regions of older adults (Nutrition)
**Summary:** Lucia Kerkhof, Peter J. Joris and colleagues at Maastricht University enrolled 31 healthy adults aged 60–75 in a single-blind randomized crossover trial (NCT05724654): 60 g/day unsalted skin-roasted peanuts versus a no-peanut control for 16 weeks each, separated by an 8-week washout. ScienceDaily covered the Food & Function paper on November 1. After intranasal insulin, ASL-MRI showed a right-hemisphere cluster (lingual gyrus, hippocampus, parahippocampal gyrus, posterior cingulate and related memory areas) with a peanut-versus-control CBF response difference of about −5.4 mL/100 g tissue/min (P=0.044). Intrahepatic lipid and circulating glucose, lipid, and inflammation markers were largely unchanged. Authors suggest this may help explain prior memory gains with the same protocol; the small sample and imaging-only endpoint do not prove dementia prevention.

**Links:**

- [Food & Function — Skin-roasted peanuts and regional brain insulin responsiveness in older adults](https://doi.org/10.1039/d6fo03208g)
- [ScienceDaily — November 1 coverage of daily peanuts and brain insulin responses](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/10/261030003001.htm)

**Commentary:** Swapping ultraprocessed snacks for about two servings of unsalted skin-on peanuts is reasonable for many people—but this is not an Alzheimer drug, and allergy or calorie load still matter.

### 3. University of Eastern Finland cross-sectional study: Higher animal protein linked to slightly lower total testosterone in middle-aged men; plant protein showed the opposite pattern (Evidence check)
**Summary:** Miika Wynne-Ellis and colleagues analyzed 4-day food records and blood total testosterone and SHBG in 2,546 Finnish men aged 42–60 from the Kuopio Ischaemic Heart Disease Risk Factor Study. Published in the International Journal of Food Sciences and Nutrition and amplified by the university and media through November 1, multivariable models found total testosterone about 1.2 nmol/L lower in the highest versus lowest animal-protein quartile and about 1.1 nmol/L higher in the highest versus lowest plant-protein quartile. Dairy protein tracked with lower testosterone/SHBG; meat protein mainly with lower SHBG. Authors stress the differences are small, the design is cross-sectional, and the data cannot show that changing protein sources will alter hormones or health outcomes.

**Links:**

- [International Journal of Food Sciences and Nutrition — Dietary proteins, testosterone, and SHBG in middle-aged men](https://doi.org/10.1080/09637486.2026.2731397)
- [University of Eastern Finland — Animal and plant proteins and testosterone associations](https://www.uef.fi/en/article/animal-and-plant-proteins-show-different-associations-with-testosterone-levels-in-men)

**Commentary:** Do not quit meat to “boost testosterone” on this signal alone—the effect size is modest and non-causal; keep protein choices anchored in overall diet quality, energy needs, and recovery.

## II. Guidelines & Consensus

### 4. ACC Food and Health Scientific Statement spotlight: Heart and CKM health hinge on overall dietary pattern, not isolated nutrients (Guidelines)
**Summary:** In its November 1 “New in Clinical Guidance” feature, the American College of Cardiology again highlighted the 2026 JACC Scientific Statement Food and Health: Nutrition for Prevention and Management of Cardiovascular Disease, led by Frank B. Hu and colleagues. The statement synthesizes RCT, cohort, and mechanistic evidence and urges a shift from nutrient-centric advice to whole-food dietary patterns: prioritize minimally processed plant foods with healthy fats and quality proteins; limit refined starches, added sugars, sodium, unhealthy fats, and poor-quality ultraprocessed foods; prefer water and unsweetened beverages; and do not start alcohol for supposed heart benefit. It also stresses food security across availability, affordability, accessibility, and utilization, plus integrating Food Is Medicine and clinical nutrition counseling—and keeping protein, fiber, and micronutrients adequate during GLP-1 therapy.

**Links:**

- [JACC — 2026 ACC Scientific Statement on food and cardiovascular health](https://www.jacc.org/doi/10.1016/j.jacc.2026.08.025)
- [ACC — November 1 clinical guidance note: overall diet quality is key](https://www.acc.org/latest-in-cardiology/articles/2026/11/01/01/new-in-clinical-guidance-overall-diet-quality)

**Commentary:** Day-to-day choices should obsess less over one macronutrient percentage and more over a plant-forward, less ultraprocessed plate—medication-assisted weight loss does not replace diet quality.

## III. Exercise & Training Science

### 5. BMC Sports Science, Medicine and Rehabilitation meta-analysis: HIIT work intervals ≥2 minutes and variable intensity accumulate more time near VO₂max (Training)
**Summary:** Searching through September 2025, investigators pooled 86 studies and 239 HIIT protocols reporting acute time at or near ≥80%/90%/95% VO₂max. The BMC Sports Science, Medicine and Rehabilitation review was featured in a November 1 training-research roundup. Work intervals ≥2 minutes produced more time ≥90% VO₂max than ≤30-second or 30-second-to-<2-minute formats (~555 s vs ~336/322 s on average), with the relationship persisting after adjusting for total work; variable-intensity work intervals beat even-paced formats (SMD ~0.80), while active versus passive recovery did not differ. Of 239 protocols, about 133 reached ≥5 minutes near VO₂max and about 48 reached ≥10 minutes; interval length and total duration explained only ~7% of between-protocol variance. Acute stimulus time is not proof of long-term performance gains.

**Links:**

- [BMC Sports Science, Medicine and Rehabilitation — Time near VO₂max during HIIT: systematic review and meta-analysis](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13102-026-01766-x)
- [Daniel Rowland — Research roundup for 26 October–1 November 2026](https://dwrowland.substack.com/p/research-5-studies-worth-knowing-0fb)

**Commentary:** If the session goal is more time in the high aerobic zone, try longer intervals such as 3–5×2–3 minutes with small within-rep surges; short intervals still have a role—do not assume “more fragmented is always better.”

### 6. Frontiers in Public Health meta-analysis of 13 trials: Exercise-based injury prevention in female team sports cut overall injury risk ~35%, with clearer benefit when adherence was ≥75% (Sports medicine)
**Summary:** A systematic review of 13 unique RCTs in female team-sport athletes (about 13,675 participants) quantified exercise-based injury prevention programmes (IPPs) for overall, lower-limb, and site-specific injuries. Published in Frontiers in Public Health, IPPs reduced overall injury risk by about 35% (RR 0.65, 95% CI 0.53–0.79) and lower-limb injuries similarly (RR ~0.67); ACL injury RR was ~0.62 and ankle injury RR ~0.59. Exploratory subgroups suggested larger effects with 4–5 sessions/week, 21–30 minutes/session, or 51–120 minutes/week of preventive training; trials with ≥75% adherence showed RR ~0.55, versus little benefit when adherence was lower (RR ~0.93). Certainty for the primary overall-injury outcome was low, so dose subgroups should be read cautiously.

**Links:**

- [Frontiers in Public Health — Injury prevention programmes in female team sports: effects, dose, and adherence](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/public-health/articles/10.3389/fpubh.2026.1950998/full)

**Commentary:** For women’s soccer, basketball, and similar teams, making neuromuscular warm-ups frequent and actually completed beats collecting trendy drills on paper—adherence is the effect modifier that matters.

## IV. Public Health & Disease Prevention

### 7. International Journal of Cancer UK Biobank cohort: Higher CKM stage tracked with higher cancer incidence and mortality; a favorable lifestyle partly offset the risk (Public health)
**Summary:** Ranran Qie and colleagues studied 227,330 cancer-free UK Biobank participants, staging cardiovascular-kidney-metabolic (CKM) syndrome by American Heart Association criteria and scoring lifestyle from smoking, alcohol, physical activity, and diet. International Journal of Cancer published the analysis on November 1, 2026. Over a median 11.5–13.7 years, 24,916 incident cancers and 7,791 deaths occurred. Risks rose with advancing CKM stage (P trend <0.001); stage 4 HRs were about 1.23 for incidence and 1.58 for mortality. Across stages 0–4, a favorable versus unfavorable lifestyle was linked to roughly 14%–24% lower cancer incidence and 22%–40% lower cancer mortality. Jointly, stage 4 plus unfavorable lifestyle carried HRs of about 1.54 (incidence) and 2.43 (mortality). Observational design and self-reported behaviors limit causal inference.

**Links:**

- [International Journal of Cancer — CKM stage, healthy lifestyles, and cancer incidence and mortality](https://doi.org/10.1002/ijc.70586)

**Commentary:** Existing metabolic or cardiorenal disease is not a reason to abandon cancer-prevention habits—tobacco control, activity, and better diet may still shift risk, especially at earlier CKM stages.

### 8. JACC: Advances UK Biobank analysis: Across CKM stages, high physical activity and high diet quality were each—and jointly—linked to lower mortality and CVD (Public health)
**Summary:** Among 339,074 adults with CKM stages 0–4 (51.3% women; mean age 56.4 years), high physical activity was defined as ≥150 min/week of walking or moderate-to-vigorous activity (self-report and accelerometry) and diet quality was tertiled. In the November 2026 JACC: Advances paper, high activity was associated with HRs of about 0.76 for all-cause mortality, 0.74 for composite CVD mortality, and 0.90 for incident composite CVD; high diet quality corresponded to about 0.81, 0.81, and 0.87. Activity associations were nonlinear, plateauing near ~2,500 MET-min/week. Combined high activity and high diet quality yielded HRs of about 0.64, 0.61, and 0.83, with stronger joint associations at more advanced CKM stages. Findings remain observational.

**Links:**

- [JACC: Advances — Physical activity and diet quality with mortality and CVD in CKM syndrome](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2772963X2600760X)

**Commentary:** Diet-only or exercise-only changes each show a signal, but doing both tracks with the strongest associations—people with CKM burden need integrated behavior change, not a single supplement or gadget bet.

## V. Sleep & Mental Health

### 9. BMC Public Health dual-cohort study: Frequent restless sleep linked to ~22% higher stroke risk; multidimensional sleep scores also favor quality over hours alone (Sleep)
**Summary:** Complementary analyses included 11,574 CHARLS participants and 485,014 UK Biobank participants. CHARLS examined nighttime sleep duration, napping, and restless sleep versus incident stroke, with hypertension and heart disease tested as mediators; UK Biobank built a 0–5 sleep score from duration, chronotype, insomnia, snoring, and daytime sleepiness. The paper went online October 27 and was widely covered in Chinese media on November 1. In CHARLS, restless sleep most or all of the time versus rare/none had stroke HR ~1.22 (95% CI 1.00–1.48), with estimated mediation of ~19.5% via hypertension and ~17.0% via heart disease. In UK Biobank, each 1-point worse sleep score tracked with ischemic-stroke HR ~1.07; versus a healthy pattern, moderate and poor patterns had HRs ~1.10 and ~1.29. Self-reported sleep and residual confounding limit causality.

**Links:**

- [BMC Public Health — Sleep health and stroke risk in CHARLS and UK Biobank](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12889-026-30082-9)
- [Economic Observer — November 1 briefing on restless sleep and stroke risk](http://www.eeo.com.cn/2026/1101/1081122.shtml)

**Commentary:** Judge sleep by more than nightly hours—frequent light arousals, snoring, and daytime sleepiness deserve attention, especially if blood pressure or heart disease is already in the picture.

### 10. BMC Public Health Gansu worker survey: Sleep quality and disturbance, more than duration, linked work stress to depressive symptoms (Sleep / Mental health)
**Summary:** Using the 2025 Worker Health Survey in Gansu Province (n=9,736 employed adults), investigators applied Bayesian mediation to the path from work stress through sleep problems to depressive symptoms. Covered by Psychreg on November 1 and published in BMC Public Health, overall sleep problems accounted for about 27.8% of the total statistical association. In parallel mediation, sleep quality contributed ~50.7%, sleep disturbance ~37.2%, duration ~9.2%, and latency ~2.9%. Sleep duration showed a U-shaped link with depressive symptoms, lowest near eight hours. Cross-sectional design, single-item sleep dimensions, and a one-province sample cannot establish temporal causality.

**Links:**

- [BMC Public Health — Differential sleep dimensions linking work stress to depressive symptoms](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12889-026-30085-6)
- [Psychreg — November 1: Sleep quality outweighs sleep length in the stress–depression link](https://www.psychreg.org/poor-sleep-quality-more-than-short-sleep-links-work-stress-depression-chinese-workers/)

**Commentary:** Workplace mental-health programs that only push “more hours in bed” may miss the mark—reducing nighttime disruption and helping people mentally detach from work better matches this pathway.

## VI. Supplements & Evidence Boundaries

### 11. Journal of Functional Foods 12-week double-blind RCT: B. lactis IDCC 4301 lowered DEXA total body fat by about 1.86 kg versus placebo in overweight adults (Supplements)
**Summary:** A single-center Korean double-blind, placebo-controlled trial randomized adults with BMI 25.0–29.9 kg/m² to Bifidobacterium lactis IDCC 4301 at 1×10¹⁰ CFU/day or placebo for 12 weeks; the per-protocol set included 97 participants. In the November 2026 Journal of Functional Foods report, DEXA total fat mass changed by about −735 g with the probiotic versus +1,126 g with placebo (treatment contrast ≈ −1.86 kg; P<0.0001). Regional DEXA, BIA, and waist measures were generally directionally consistent; diet and activity changes did not differ between groups, and no product-related serious adverse events were reported. Single-center, 12-week, strain- and population-specific results do not generalize to all “probiotics for weight loss” and do not replace diet and training.

**Links:**

- [Journal of Functional Foods — B. lactis IDCC 4301 and body fat in overweight adults](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jff.2026.107527)

**Commentary:** Treat this as a moderate, strain-specific body-fat signal—not a universal fat-loss shortcut; if you try a product, keep an energy deficit and resistance training primary and check the exact strain name.
