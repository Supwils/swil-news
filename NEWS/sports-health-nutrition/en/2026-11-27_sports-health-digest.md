## I. Nutrition & Diet

### 1. UK sugar-rationing natural experiment: early-life low-sugar exposure linked to lower adult cancer risk—but causal claims are contested (Nutrition / Evidence Check)
**Summary:** A PNAS analysis of 64,761 UK Biobank adults born 1951–1956 used the September 1953 end of UK sugar rationing as a quasi-experiment. Cohorts whose first ~1,000 days overlapped rationing showed lower later incidence of liver/intrahepatic bile duct, rectal, lung, prostate, and breast cancers (reported HRs ~0.31–0.64, i.e., ~36%–69% lower), longer leukocyte telomeres (~2.2 fewer years of biological aging), and healthier, lower-sugar diets decades later. A companion critique argues sugar intake did not jump as a clean step function in 1953, infant sugar exposure assumptions are questionable, and derationing also changed calories and other foods—so a causal “sugar intake” interpretation is not well supported; the finding is better read as a long-term association under a rationing regime.

**Links:**

- [PNAS — Early-life sugar restriction, cancer and aging](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2610287123)
- [PMC — Reassessing the causal interpretation](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13751645/)

**Commentary:** Limiting added sugar for young children remains sensible; reading this as proof that “less infant sugar prevents cancer” overclaims a policy-era association.

### 2. Scandinavian birth cohorts: higher childhood fruit and fish intake linked to lower early-adult Crohn’s risk (Nutrition)
**Summary:** In Gut, pooled data from Norwegian, Danish, and Swedish birth cohorts followed ~111,999 people for >1.5 million person-years and identified 653 IBD cases by 2024. Highest vs lowest tertile fruit intake at ages 7–8 was associated with lower Crohn’s disease risk (adjusted HR ~0.66); high adolescent fruit intake showed aHR ~0.57 for Crohn’s; high childhood fish intake showed aHR ~0.65 for Crohn’s. High childhood fruit intake was also linked to higher ulcerative colitis risk (aHR ~1.54), which authors flagged as possibly chance amid multiple comparisons; high adolescent salty-snack intake had a borderline higher IBD risk (aHR ~1.32). Observational design cannot prove causation.

**Links:**

- [Gut — Childhood food groups and IBD risk](https://gut.bmj.com/content/early/2026/11/19/gutjnl-2026-339600)
- [BMJ Group — Salty snacks and IBD risk summary](https://bmjgroup.com/high-teen-salty-snack-intake-linked-to-heightened-inflammatory-bowel-disease-risk/)

**Commentary:** Stocking fruit and fish while cutting chip-style salty snacks is a low-harm household move that aligns with the IBD-risk signal.

### 3. Controlled-feeding crossover RCT: whole-wheat bread cut postprandial glucose excursions ~27% in adults with prediabetes (Nutrition)
**Summary:** A Frontiers in Nutrition Ohio State randomized crossover controlled-feeding trial enrolled 39 adults with overweight/obesity and fasting glucose 100–125 mg/dL. Two 2-week diets differed only in bread (whole wheat vs refined white), with a 2-week washout; all meals were provided. Versus white bread, whole wheat reduced oral glucose tolerance test postprandial glucose excursion by ~27% (p=0.04), independent of body weight, inflammation, carbonyl stress, and fecal SCFAs. Overall microbiome diversity was unchanged, but selected taxa shifted directionally; baseline Lachnoclostridium and Lachnospiraceae NK4A136 moderated response. The trial was short and small and did not show diabetes prevention.

**Links:**

- [Frontiers in Nutrition — Whole wheat bread and postprandial glucose](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/nutrition/articles/10.3389/fnut.2026.1907908/full)
- [Ohio State News — Gut microbiome and whole-grain benefits](https://news.osu.edu/gut-microbiome-may-shape-blood-sugar-benefits-of-whole-grains/)

**Commentary:** For prediabetes, swapping true whole-wheat bread is a cheaper first step than waiting on a “precision microbiome” report; check your own meter/CGM response.

### 4. Systematic review: partial plant-for-animal protein swaps may improve lipids and weight; B12 and bone findings mixed (Nutrition)
**Summary:** A Nutrition Reviews systematic review searched through 5 Dec 2024 and included 4 RCTs (7 papers; mean duration ~9.5 weeks; n=36–136). Highest plant-protein share groups showed lower LDL-C (2 RCTs), total cholesterol (1 RCT), and body weight (2 RCTs) versus higher animal-protein patterns; one trial found worse vitamin B12 status in a 70% plant-protein arm; bone-turnover markers were inconsistent. One trial linked meat reduction to lower estimated greenhouse-gas emissions. Risk of bias was often “some concerns,” and the evidence base remains narrow.

**Links:**

- [Nutrition Reviews — Partial plant-for-animal protein replacement](https://academic.oup.com/nutritionreviews/advance-article/doi/10.1093/nutrit/nuag058/8662103)
- [Ebiotrade — Chinese abstract of the plant-protein review](https://n.ebiotrade.com/newsf/2026-11/202611270250220522.htm)

**Commentary:** Default toward less red meat and more legumes/nuts for cardiometabolic and planetary gains—and monitor/supplement B12 on long-term plant-forward diets.

## II. Exercise & Training Science

### 5. 8-week RCT: in type 2 diabetes, aerobic-then-strength lowered mean/nocturnal glucose; strength-first stabilized variability (Training)
**Summary:** An iScience randomized trial assigned 56 community-dwelling adults with type 2 diabetes (~mean age 62.5; previously inactive) to aerobic-then-resistance (AR) or resistance-then-aerobic (RA), 30 min each, 3×/week for 8 weeks. Only AR showed significant within-group drops in mean glucose and HbA1c (~−0.3%); at week 8 AR beat RA on exercise/recovery and nocturnal glucose but raised glucose CV, while RA kept fluctuations smaller. Fasting insulin fell in both groups among non–insulin-treated participants. Diet was not tightly controlled and prior trials are mixed; authors call for larger confirmation.

**Links:**

- [iScience — Exercise sequence and glycemic control in T2D](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.isci.2026.118041)
- [PMC — Full text of the randomized trial](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13755579/)

**Commentary:** First hit weekly aerobic-plus-strength volume; if you have a CGM, tweak order for mean/night glucose versus smoother swings—don’t treat sequence as destiny.

### 6. RCT meta-analysis: neuromuscular training may cut athletes’ lower-limb injury risk; optimal dose still uncertain (Training / Injury Prevention)
**Summary:** A Sports Medicine - Open systematic review and meta-analysis of 27 RCTs (27,663 participants) compared neuromuscular training (NMT) with controls for lower-limb injuries. NMT was associated with lower injury risk (RR ≈ 0.72, 95% CI 0.64–0.82), attenuated after trim-and-fill; structured and multidimensional programmes both showed favourable pooled estimates. Exploratory subgroups favoured ~2–3 sessions/week, ~16–25 min/session, and 1.5–12 month programmes, but authors stress these are study-level associations, not a proven optimal dose; overall certainty was very low. Football and basketball subgroups also looked favourable.

**Links:**

- [PMC — NMT modalities, dose and lower-limb injury](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13782306/)

**Commentary:** Make a 15–25 minute warm-up with strength, landing control, and balance the default start of practice—more useful than chasing an exact “optimal minute.”

## III. Public Health & Disease Prevention

### 7. WHO: 2025 measles deaths hit a historic low, but immunization gaps threaten elimination (Public Health)
**Summary:** On 27 Nov 2026 WHO reported ~72,000 measles deaths worldwide in 2025—about 91% lower than in 2000—with vaccination estimated to have saved >60 million lives since 2000. Nearly 10 million people were still infected in 2025; cases in the Americas rose ~8-fold since 2000. Global first-dose coverage was ~84% and second-dose ~77%, versus ~69% and ~57% in low-income countries; >29 million children were un- or under-vaccinated, and 59 countries had large/disruptive outbreaks, including 11 that had previously eliminated measles. The Director-General stressed elimination is not a finish line and requires two doses for every child.

**Links:**

- [WHO — Historic low in measles deaths, immunization gaps](https://www.who.int/news/item/27-11-2026-historic-low-in-measles-deaths--but-immunization-gaps-threaten-progress)

**Commentary:** Check children’s and your own MMR records—personal coverage locks in progress better than celebrating a global death-rate headline.

### 8. 14 European cohorts: air pollution, scarce greenspace, and impervious surfaces jointly linked to ischemic heart disease (Public Health)
**Summary:** The Lancet Regional Health – Europe pooled 14 cohorts across 11 countries (>29 million people; 464,386 incident IHD cases). In administrative cohorts, higher PM₂.₅, NO₂, lack of vegetation (MSAVI), greater distance to greenspace, and more impervious surface were each associated with higher IHD incidence; one-quartile increments in air pollution, built environment, and combined domains raised incidence by ~2.5%, ~2.9%, and ~3.8%. Single-exposure associations were often non-significant in traditional adult cohorts, though joint-exposure directions aligned. Authors (including Karolinska) argue meeting air limits alone may not suffice—urban planning should also protect greenspace and curb hardscape.

**Links:**

- [PMC — External exposome and IHD in Europe](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13782602/)
- [Karolinska Institutet — Air pollution and green space](https://news.ki.se/air-pollution-and-lack-of-green-spaces-linked-to-increased-risk-of-heart-disease)

**Commentary:** Prefer greener, lower-traffic outdoor routes; treat nearby greenspace access as a heart-health variable alongside PM metrics when choosing where to live or walk.

### 9. Huantai child cohort: sustained higher Life’s Essential 8 cardiovascular health linked to less subclinical damage and fatty liver risk (Public Health)
**Summary:** Shandong University’s Bo Xi team announced on 27 Nov 2026 two longitudinal analyses from the Huantai Childhood Cardiovascular Health Cohort. One JAMA Network Open study followed 1,090 children aged 6–11 at baseline through ~2023: highest vs lowest cumulative cardiovascular-health quartiles had lower risks of left-ventricular hypertrophy, geometric remodeling, and elevated carotid intima-media thickness; persistent-high trajectories beat persistent-low ones, while “start high then decline” lost protective advantage. A second eGastroenterology analysis in 875 children linked higher cumulative/persistent cardiovascular health to lower 2023 incident hepatic steatosis. Associations are observational; effect sizes await the full papers.

**Links:**

- [Shandong University News — Childhood CVH trajectories and subclinical damage](https://www.view.sdu.edu.cn/info/1021/215384.htm)

**Commentary:** Pediatric cardiometabolic prevention is about year-after-year sleep, activity, weight, and blood-pressure habits—not a one-time checkup high score.

## IV. Sleep & Mental Health

### 11. ~23,000 wearable users: less regular sleep timing tracked with worse depression, anxiety, and loneliness (Sleep)
**Summary:** A Scientific Reports TemPredict cross-sectional analysis of 22,894 wearable users (mean age ~46) derived Sleep Regularity Index (SRI, 0–100) from ~154 days of ring data on average. Mean SRI was ~74.93 with severe depression vs ~83.74 within normal limits; ~78.94 vs ~83.67 for severe vs normal anxiety; elevated loneliness also tracked lower SRI. Each 5-point SRI drop raised odds of worse symptom categories, persisting after age, sex, and sleep-duration adjustment. Findings are correlational with self-reported symptoms and a North America/Europe–skewed sample; UCSF authors say intervention trials must test whether regularizing schedules improves mental health.

**Links:**

- [Scientific Reports — Sleep regularity and mental health](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-77545-y)
- [UCSF — Erratic sleep schedule and mental health](https://www.ucsf.edu/news/2026/11/432996/could-erratic-sleep-schedule-be-harming-your-mental-health)

**Commentary:** Beyond sleep duration, keep bedtime/wake-time drift near ~1 hour—it may be as actionable a mental-health lever as total hours.

## V. Evidence & Clarification

### 12. FDA-linked systematic review: oral N-acetylcysteine (NAC) shows a reasonable expectation of safety as a dietary supplement (Supplements)
**Summary:** In Toxicological Sciences, FDA dietary-supplement reviewers screened 149 studies and retained 41 high-quality ones on adverse effects of ingested NAC. One ulcerative-colitis trial at 800 mg/day for 16 weeks found more nausea; remaining high-quality studies reported no matching AE increase at doses up to ~9,000 mg/day for up to ~3 years. Clinical doses resembled labeled servings of marketed “supplement” products. Authors conclude a reasonable expectation of safety for NAC in a dietary-supplement exposure context; U.S. statutory classification given drug approval remains a regulatory question, and the review is not an efficacy endorsement.

**Links:**

- [Toxicological Sciences — Safety assessment of ingested NAC](https://academic.oup.com/toxsci/advance-article/doi/10.1093/toxsci/kfag149/9051707)

**Commentary:** “Reasonably safe” ≠ “everyone should take it”—skip NAC without a clear indication; if you use it, watch dose and GI tolerance.
