## I. Nutrition & Diet

### 1. UK Biobank, 340,000 adults: the "wine advantage" at moderate drinking remains an observational signal (Nutrition)

**Summary:** A UK Biobank cohort analysis presented at the American College of Cardiology meeting covered 340,924 adults with over 13 years of average follow-up, grouped by grams of pure alcohol. Compared with never/occasional drinkers, heavy drinkers had 24% higher all-cause and 36% higher cancer mortality; moderate wine drinkers had 21% lower cardiovascular death risk, while low-level beer, cider or spirits drinkers had 9% higher. The study is observational and adjusted for age, socioeconomic status and lifestyle factors.

**Links:**

- [ScienceDaily — Wine, Beer, or Spirits? Massive Study Reveals a Surprising Health Divide](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/11/261116015104.htm)

**Commentary:** This is a conference report with no peer-reviewed paper cited, and wine drinkers tend to have healthier lifestyles anyway, so it is no reason to start drinking wine; the harm of heavy drinking is the most consistent part of the evidence.

### 2. Randomized double-blind trial: resistant starch plus psyllium fiber shifts gut bacteria in IBS (Nutrition)

**Summary:** Edith Cowan University researchers published a randomized, double-blind controlled trial in the European Journal of Nutrition in people with IBS on a low-FODMAP diet. The supplement (resistant starch plus psyllium husk) changed gut bacteria substantially in a short period and reduced gut-related anxiety without worsening pain, bloating, diarrhea or constipation. Sample size and duration were not given in secondary coverage.

**Links:**

- [ScienceDaily — A New Fiber Supplement Could Help Millions Suffering from IBS](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/11/261112230450.htm)
- [Edith Cowan University — Press release](https://www.ecu.edu.au/newsroom/articles/research/gut-friendly-fibre-formula-gives-hope-to-ibs-sufferers-ecu-study-finds)

**Commentary:** Low-FODMAP diets often cut fiber intake, so a fiber blend that does not worsen symptoms is of interest, but the sample size is unknown and it is a specific product; people with IBS should consult a clinician or dietitian before changing fiber.

### 3. 2,500 Finnish men: animal protein linked to lower testosterone, plant protein the opposite (Nutrition)

**Summary:** The University of Eastern Finland ran a cross-sectional analysis of 2,546 men aged 42–60 from the Kuopio Ischaemic Heart Disease Risk Factor Study (diet recorded 1984–1989). The highest animal protein group had 1.2 nmol/l lower total testosterone than the lowest, while the highest plant protein group had 1.1 nmol/l higher; dairy protein was linked to lower total testosterone and SHBG. It was published in the International Journal of Food Sciences and Nutrition.

**Links:**

- [ScienceDaily — Animal Vs. Plant Protein Reveals a Surprising Testosterone Divide](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/11/261111005845.htm)

**Commentary:** A cross-sectional design cannot show causation, the differences are small with unknown clinical meaning, and the diet data are about 40 years old, so this is not a basis for changing your protein sources.

### 4. Mathematical model: vitamin C may reduce formation of carcinogenic nitrosation compounds in the stomach (Evidence pending)

**Summary:** A University of Waterloo team published a modeling study in the Journal of Theoretical Biology simulating how nitrates and nitrites move and react through the salivary glands, stomach, small intestine and plasma. The simulations suggest vitamin C taken with nitrate/nitrite-containing foods may interfere with formation of cancer-linked compounds, with a moderate reduction possible after meals involving processed meats such as bacon and salami.

**Links:**

- [ScienceDaily — Vitamin C Could Help Block Cancer-linked Chemicals in Your Stomach](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/11/261114202236.htm)

**Commentary:** This is a simulation, not a human trial, so it does not mean processed meat plus vitamin C is safe; the practical advice remains to eat less processed meat and more fruit and vegetables.

## II. Evidence & Clarification

### 5. Yale review in Nature Medicine: exercise plus healthy diet lowers epigenetic age, common anti-aging supplements show no clear effect (Evidence)

**Summary:** Yale researchers analyzed 51 anti-aging intervention studies in Nature Medicine, using over 110 DNA methylation biomarkers including 16 epigenetic clocks. Regular exercise combined with a healthy diet (Mediterranean, low-fat or low-carb) consistently lowered epigenetic age; metformin, semaglutide and anti-TNF therapies showed the largest reductions; over-the-counter supplements and some procedures showed no measurable reduction. (Yale's release dates from August; ScienceDaily republished it on November 13.)

**Links:**

- [ScienceDaily — Yale Scientists Reveal What Really Slows Aging and What Doesn't](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/11/261112230452.htm)
- [Yale News — Can you slow aging process?](https://news.yale.edu/2026/08/21/can-you-slow-aging-process-study-reveals-which-interventions-might-help)

**Commentary:** A lower biological-age marker does not equal longer life or less disease, as the authors themselves note; the actionable takeaway is to spend your effort on exercise and diet rather than anti-aging supplements.

### 6. Energy drinks and stimulant products: cardiac arrest mentioned about 11 times more often in FDA adverse-event reports (Evidence)

**Summary:** A preliminary study at the American Heart Association Scientific Sessions analyzed 28,345 reports (854 involving caffeine or stimulant products) from an FDA database of food and supplement adverse events, 2017–2025, for people aged 13+. In stimulant-product reports, cardiac arrest was mentioned about 11 times more often, heart attack about 4.5 times and ventricular arrhythmias or palpitations about 4 times; around 45% of cardiovascular reports came from ages 18–34.

**Links:**

- [ScienceDaily — Energy Drinks Linked to 11 Times More Reports of Cardiac Arrest](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/11/261110042543.htm)
- [AHA Newsroom — Energy drinks, stimulant products linked to cardiac arrest, heart attack in young adults](https://newsroom.heart.org/news/energy-drinks-stimulant-products-linked-to-cardiac-arrest-heart-attack-in-young-adults)

**Commentary:** Voluntary reports cannot give true risk or prove causation, but people with heart disease, a family history or palpitations should avoid stacking high-dose caffeine and stimulant products.

## III. Sleep & Mental Health

### 7. About 23,000 wearable users: irregular sleep linked to more severe depression, anxiety and loneliness (Sleep)

**Summary:** UCSF and colleagues published in Scientific Reports on November 12 an analysis of about 23,000 TemPredict participants (average age 46, roughly 154 days of smart-ring sleep data each). The Sleep Regularity Index (SRI, 0–100) averaged about 84 for minimal/no depression versus about 75 for severe depression, and about 84 for normal anxiety versus about 79 for severe anxiety. The authors estimate SRI 84 equals bed and wake times shifting about an hour day to day, and SRI 75 about 1.5 hours. It is observational, direction of causation is unclear, the sample is mostly Oura users, and the study had industry funding.

**Links:**

- [News-Medical — Irregular sleep schedules linked to increased anxiety and depression](https://www.news-medical.net/news/20261112/Irregular-sleep-schedules-linked-to-increased-anxiety-and-depression.aspx)

**Commentary:** Keeping a regular schedule deserves priority over merely hitting a sleep duration, and holding bed and wake times within about an hour is a realistic goal, though mood problems can also disrupt sleep, so the link may run both ways.

## IV. Public Health & Disease Prevention

### 8. WHO's first global guidelines on child and adolescent obesity: start with structured diet, activity and behavior programs (Public health)

**Summary:** WHO issued its first global guidelines on child and adolescent obesity on October 7 (not today's news, but still the most important recent guideline). WHO says prevalence in this age group has quadrupled since 1990, with about 170 million 5–19-year-olds living with obesity in 2024. The guidelines strongly recommend structured diet, physical activity and behavior-change programs; per Reuters-based coverage, weight-loss drugs may be considered for ages 10–19 only after supervised interventions fail. Outlets differ on the exact wording for ages 0–9 on drugs and surgery, so check WHO's own text.

**Links:**

- [PAHO — WHO issues first global guidelines on child and adolescent obesity](https://www.paho.org/en/news/8-10-2026-who-issues-first-global-guidelines-child-and-adolescent-obesity)
- [TimesLive/Reuters — Try diet and exercise before weight-loss drugs, WHO tells parents](https://www.timeslive.co.za/news/world/2026-10-07-try-diet-and-exercise-before-weight-loss-drugs-who-tells-parents/)

**Commentary:** For parents, family-level diet, activity and mental-health support remain the first step, with medication as a fallback rather than a first choice.
