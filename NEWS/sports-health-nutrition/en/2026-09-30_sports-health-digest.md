## I. Nutrition & Diet

### 1. Nature: During hospitalization for stem-cell transplant, sweets plus broad-spectrum antibiotics tracked with lower microbiome diversity and Enterococcus expansion (Nutrition)
**Summary:** Jonathan U. Peled, Jonas Schluter, and colleagues published an observational analysis with mouse validation in *Nature* on Sep 30, 2026. Staff recorded 9,419 meals from 173 inpatients with blood cancers undergoing allogeneic hematopoietic-cell transplantation; 158 patients had analyzable longitudinal stool microbiomes (1,009 samples). A Bayesian model found no clear link between sweets and beverages alone and fecal alpha-diversity, but during broad-spectrum antibiotic exposure in the two days before sampling, each additional about 100 g (dehydrated weight) of sweets predicted a further 24.1% lower mean alpha-diversity (95% CI 7.7%–38.1%). Recoded as macronutrients, the sugar-by-antibiotic interaction was about a 20.6% further decrease per 100 g (2%–35.6%). Higher-resolution models pointed to formulated drinks (sports drinks and nutrition shakes) and added sugars rather than sugars occurring naturally in foods. In mice, sucrose increased and prolonged antibiotic-driven Enterococcus expansion. Among patients above the median sugar intake, each extra day of broad-spectrum antibiotics carried a mortality HR of 1.12 (1.01–1.23); the association was not significant below the median. The authors say independent cohorts are still needed and that the finding should not be generalized to every outpatient antibiotic course.

**Links:**

- [Nature — Sugar-rich foods and antibiotic-related microbiome disruption](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-11077-3)

**Commentary:** During broad-spectrum antibiotics, especially in hospital, swap sugary drinks and sweet nutrition shakes for water and regular meals; transplant nutrition support stays with the clinical team, and a short oral course in otherwise healthy people is not a reason to stop eating.

### 2. American College of Cardiology: Heart health tracks overall diet quality, and GLP-1 drugs do not replace a nutrient-dense pattern (Guideline)
**Summary:** On Sep 30, 2026, the American College of Cardiology released the scientific statement *Food and Health: Nutrition for Prevention and Management of Cardiovascular Disease*, published in *JACC* (DOI: 10.1016/j.jacc.2026.08.025; Frank B. Hu and colleagues). It reviews macronutrients, micronutrients, dietary patterns, ultra-processed foods, and emerging nutrition interventions, and concludes that a sustainable overall diet quality matters more for cardiovascular and cardiovascular-kidney-metabolic health than focusing on a single nutrient or food. It recommends minimally processed plant foods, healthy fats, and higher-quality proteins, while limiting refined starches, added sugars, sodium, unhealthy fats, and highly processed foods of poor nutritional quality. Water and other unsweetened drinks should come first, sugar-sweetened beverages should be limited, and alcohol should generally be limited or avoided. The statement treats food security across availability, affordability, accessibility, and utilization, and asks clinics to combine dietary counseling, medical nutrition therapy, and food policy. On GLP-1 receptor agonists, it notes substantial weight loss and cardiovascular benefit, with a nutrient-rich diet still required during treatment.

**Links:**

- [American College of Cardiology — Overall diet quality and heart health](https://www.acc.org/latest-in-cardiology/journal-scans/2026/09/29/12/22/acc-scientific-statement-overall-diet-quality)
- [JACC — DOI 10.1016/j.jacc.2026.08.025](https://www.jacc.org/doi/10.1016/j.jacc.2026.08.025)

**Commentary:** For heart health, look at the week of eating: minimally processed vegetables, whole grains, legumes, nuts, fish, and plant oils, with sugary drinks later; people using a GLP-1 still need enough protein and fiber, because the drug does not replace the meal.

## II. Exercise & Training Science

### 3. JNCI: Twelve months of remote exercise improved attention more than health coaching alone in breast cancer survivors, and epigenetic aging was slower (Training)
**Summary:** A randomized trial led by Sheri J. Hartman at the University of California San Diego was published on Sep 30, 2026, in the *Journal of the National Cancer Institute* (cognition paper DOI: 10.1093/jnci/djag295; epigenetic companion DOI: 10.1093/jnci/djag296; ClinicalTrials.gov NCT04049695). Among 253 breast cancer survivors who had finished active treatment, were inactive, and reported cognitive difficulties, one group received remote exercise coaching and the other received health coaching without exercise guidance. The exercise arm used wearables and individualized coaching toward 150 minutes a week of moderate-to-vigorous activity. Both groups improved on cognitive measures, but the two primary outcomes—processing speed and self-reported cognition—did not differ significantly. Attention improved more in the exercise group at 6 and 12 months. Memory was better at 6 months only; the gap was gone at 12 months. Executive function did not differ. A companion analysis of blood from 124 participants found epigenetic ages about 8.7 years older than chronological age at baseline in both groups. Over 12 months, epigenetic aging markers advanced nearly 10 months in the health-coaching group and about one month in the exercise group. GrimAge2 (P = 0.02) and DNAmFitAge (P = 0.002) both showed slower aging with exercise, and faster aging tracked with less attention gain and worse self-reported cognition.

**Links:**

- [UC San Diego — Exercise, attention, and epigenetic aging after breast cancer](https://today.ucsd.edu/story/exercise-program-supports-better-attention-and-slows-biological-aging-in-breast-cancer-survivors)
- [JNCI — DOI 10.1093/jnci/djag295](https://doi.org/10.1093/jnci/djag295)

**Commentary:** Survivors who notice poorer attention and currently move very little can use 150 weekly minutes of moderate-or-harder activity as a practical target; processing speed and self-reported cognition, the primary endpoints, did not separate, so exercise does not replace a clinical workup for cancer-related cognitive change.

## III. Sports Medicine & Injury Prevention

### 4. Annals of Internal Medicine: Four months of supervised exercise improved symptoms after ACL reconstruction in young adults, without a cartilage-thickness penalty (Sports medicine)
**Summary:** Adam G. Culvenor and colleagues at La Trobe University published the SUPER-Knee randomized trial in *Annals of Internal Medicine* (DOI: 10.7326/ANNALS-26-02460). The university release is dated Sep 29, 2026, and the American College of Physicians release Sep 28. The trial enrolled 184 adults aged 18–40 who still had knee problems 9–36 months after anterior cruciate ligament reconstruction. They were assigned either to four months of physiotherapist-supervised progressive strengthening and neuromuscular exercise plus education, or to a single education session with self-directed exercise. The primary outcome was four-month change in the composite Knee injury and Osteoarthritis Outcome Score. At four months the supervised group had larger gains in knee symptoms, quality of life, and thigh strength, and higher rates of perceived recovery. By 12 months the gap had narrowed. Tibiofemoral and patellofemoral cartilage thickness did not differ between groups. The investigators conclude that supervised exercise is safe for young adults with persistent symptoms after reconstruction and that they did not see harm to knee cartilage.

**Links:**

- [La Trobe University — Exercise after ACL reconstruction](https://www.latrobe.edu.au/news/articles/2026/release/exercise-averts-younger-onset-knee-osteoarthritis)
- [Annals of Internal Medicine — DOI 10.7326/ANNALS-26-02460](https://doi.org/10.7326/ANNALS-26-02460)

**Commentary:** If the knee is still painful or unstable one to three years after ligament reconstruction, a few months of progressive strength and neuromuscular work with a physiotherapist is a reasonable next step; the advantage shrinks by 12 months, so early supervision matters more than a short burst, and this trial does not show that osteoarthritis has been prevented.

## IV. Public Health & Disease Prevention

### 5. NPR: U.S. measles confirmations exceed 3,600, and travel protection is still two doses of MMR (Public health)
**Summary:** NPR reported on Sep 30, 2026, that the U.S. Centers for Disease Control and Prevention listed more than 3,600 confirmed measles cases as of Sep 24, and two deaths as of Sep 28, with almost all cases among unvaccinated people. Experts quoted in the piece said two doses of measles-mumps-rubella (MMR) vaccine are considered protective, and that a clinician can judge a catch-up dose when records are unclear. The California Department of Public Health notified the public after an infected, unvaccinated passenger traveled by train and bus across states and through several California counties. The first childhood dose is usually around 12 months; for infants aged 6–11 months traveling internationally, CDC advice is an early dose. NPR also cited CDC figures that kindergarten coverage last school year fell to 92.4%, below the roughly 95% level associated with measles herd immunity. People with severe immunocompromise who could not be vaccinated depend on coverage around them.

**Links:**

- [NPR — Measles exposure, travel, and MMR](https://www.npr.org/2026/09/30/nx-s1-5978940/traveling-measles-exposure-vaccines-transportation)

**Commentary:** Before a train trip, a game, or international travel, confirm two MMR doses; infants aged 6–11 months can receive an early dose for international travel under CDC guidance, and missing records are a reason to ask a clinician before an exposure notice arrives.

## V. Sleep & Mental Health

### 6. Beijing Huilongguan Hospital: In 6,670 adolescents, social isolation connected more strongly to insomnia symptoms than most other childhood adversities (Sleep)
**Summary:** Lei-Lei Wang, Jing-Xu Chen, and colleagues at Beijing Huilongguan Hospital, Capital Medical University, published a cross-sectional network analysis in *Nature and Science of Sleep* on Sep 30, 2026. An online convenience sample of junior- and senior-high students in Shandong Province was collected in May 2022: 7,794 responses yielded 6,670 analyzable adolescents aged 12–18, using an adverse childhood experiences questionnaire and the Insomnia Severity Index. Prevalence was 47.6% for any adverse childhood experience and 20.6% for insomnia symptoms, with a cumulative dose pattern. In logistic regression, physical abuse (OR 1.41), emotional neglect (OR 1.32), social isolation or exclusion (OR 2.52), and witnessing community violence (OR 1.57) were significantly associated with insomnia symptoms. In the network, family crime had the highest strength centrality (z = 2.084) and isolation or exclusion the highest betweenness (z = 3.594). The strongest cross-domain edges were sleep dissatisfaction with isolation (edge weight 0.081) and difficulty falling asleep with isolation (0.077). The authors state that a single-province cross-section cannot support causation or national prevalence.

**Links:**

- [Nature and Science of Sleep — Adverse childhood experiences and adolescent insomnia](https://www.dovepress.com/network-analysis-of-symptom-level-interrelationships-between-adverse-c-peer-reviewed-fulltext-article-NSS)

**Commentary:** When a teenager has lasting trouble falling asleep, ask about isolation or exclusion as well as bedtime; the data are a 2022 convenience sample from Shandong, useful as a prompt for families and schools, not as a national rate.

### 7. Scientific Reports: Two weeks with a weighted blanket improved anxiety, sleep efficiency, and skin-picking in adults with Prader-Willi syndrome (Sleep)
**Summary:** A randomized crossover trial in *Scientific Reports* on Sep 30, 2026, enrolled 40 adults with genetically confirmed Prader-Willi syndrome. Each person used a weighted proprioceptive blanket and a visually identical placebo blanket for 14 days, separated by a 4-week washout. The weighted blanket was associated with improvements in anxiety, hyperphagia, and self-injurious skin-picking (P < 0.05). Actigraphy showed higher sleep efficiency, fewer nocturnal awakenings, less daytime somnolence, a lower share of sedentary time, a higher share of moderate activity, and increases in fat-free mass and skeletal muscle. No adverse events were reported. The authors frame it as a non-drug adjunct in multidisciplinary care of this rare neurodevelopmental condition; it was not tested in ordinary insomnia.

**Links:**

- [Scientific Reports — Weighted blankets in Prader–Willi syndrome](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-73427-5)

**Commentary:** This is a two-week crossover in adults with a rare condition, so it is not a reason for people with ordinary insomnia to buy a weighted blanket; anyone with sleep apnea, trouble regulating temperature, or limited mobility should ask a clinician before adding heavier bedding.

## VI. Evidence & Clarification

### 8. West China Hospital meta-analysis: Overnight sleep architecture in idiopathic nightmares did not differ from healthy controls (Evidence clarification)
**Summary:** Jiaxue Huang, Ye Zhang, Rong Ren, and colleagues at West China Hospital, Sichuan University, published a systematic review and meta-analysis in *Nature and Science of Sleep* on Sep 30, 2026 (PROSPERO CRD42024582896). Searches through December 2025 found 14 case-control studies that excluded post-traumatic stress disorder, depression, and anxiety disorders; seven studies, with 112 people with nightmares and 112 controls, entered the quantitative synthesis. Under a random-effects model there were no significant group differences in total sleep time, wake after sleep onset, sleep efficiency, sleep latency, REM latency, REM efficiency, or the percentages of N1, N2, N3, and REM sleep (all P > 0.05). Dropping studies without an adaptation night, or without a clear exclusion of sleep apnea and periodic limb movements, did not change that result. Micro-architecture (cyclic alternating pattern, power spectra) and autonomic measures were too sparse to pool. The authors say routine sleep architecture does not explain idiopathic nightmares and that micro-architecture deserves study, while stressing how limited the data are.

**Links:**

- [Nature and Science of Sleep — Polysomnography in idiopathic nightmares](https://www.dovepress.com/polysomnographic-sleep-in-idiopathic-nightmares-a-systematic-review-an-peer-reviewed-fulltext-article-NSS)

**Commentary:** When distressing dreams impair the day, a polysomnogram with normal sleep stages does not clear the problem; address dream content and daytime impairment, and treat this 112-pair meta-analysis as a limit on what an overnight study can show, not as a new diagnostic rule.
