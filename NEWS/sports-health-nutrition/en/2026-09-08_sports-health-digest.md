## I. Nutrition & Diet

### 1. UK Biobank cohort: Higher healthy plant-based diet–lifestyle scores tied to lower obesity-related cancer incidence and mortality and all-cause death (Nutrition)
**Summary:** *Frontiers in Nutrition* (Sep 8, 2026; DOI: 10.3389/fnut.2026.1922232) analyzed about 93,763 UK Biobank participants with a complete-case primary analysis, using an hPDI-Lifestyle score that combines healthy plant-based diet quality (hPDI), physical activity, smoking status, and sleep duration. Over median follow-up of roughly 16.3 / 15.0 / 15.1 years, investigators recorded 2,258 incident obesity-related cancers, 1,095 obesity-related cancer deaths, and 6,215 all-cause deaths. Versus the lowest tertile, the highest tertile had HRs of about 0.82 (0.74–0.92) for incidence, 0.66 (0.56–0.77) for cancer-specific mortality, and 0.64 (0.60–0.68) for all-cause mortality; each 1-SD higher score was linked to about 8%, 18%, and 22% lower risks, respectively. The incidence association was not statistically significant in women in sex-stratified analyses. Observational design; confirmation in broader populations and trials is still needed.

**Links:**

- [Frontiers — hPDI-Lifestyle and obesity-related cancer](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/nutrition/articles/10.3389/fnut.2026.1922232/full)
- [DOI: 10.3389/fnut.2026.1922232](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnut.2026.1922232)

**Commentary:** Treat cancer-relevant lifestyle as a bundle—minimally processed plant foods, movement, not smoking, and roughly 7–9 hours of sleep—rather than chasing one “superfood.”

### 2. 12-week RCT (RyeWeight2): Wholegrain rye does not beat refined wheat for weight loss under calorie restriction, but lowers CRP and shifts microbiota and butyrate (Nutrition)
**Summary:** *Clinical Nutrition* (DOI: 10.1016/j.clnu.2026.106618; Chinese media coverage dated Sep 8, 2026) randomized 255 adults with overweight or obesity 1:1 to 12-week hypocaloric diets substituting habitual cereals with wholegrain rye or refined wheat; 229 completed. Weight loss was about 3.2 kg (rye) versus 2.9 kg (wheat) with no significant between-group difference (p=0.32). The rye arm reduced CRP by about 17% (p=0.03), raised plasma acetate and butyrate (p=0.003), and reduced several taxa previously linked to adverse outcomes. Baseline HOMA-IR and CRP correlated with fat-mass change in the wheat arm, suggesting people with higher insulin resistance or inflammation may gain more metabolic upside from rye. Baseline microbiota did not predict 12-week weight loss. Trial ID: NCT04203758.

**Links:**

- [DOI: 10.1016/j.clnu.2026.106618](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.clnu.2026.106618)
- [Family Doctor — Wholegrain rye, inflammation, and microbiota](https://www.familydoctor.cn/news/quan-guwu-heimai-gaibian-changdaoxijun-jiangdi-feipang-shiyan-zhongde-yanzheng-367284.html)

**Commentary:** Do not expect rye to deliver extra kilos lost versus wheat on a calorie-restricted plan; do consider it when the goal is lower inflammation and a more favorable gut-metabolite profile.

### 3. Shanghai Preconception Cohort: Higher parental periconception vitamin B12 and RBC folate linked to lower predicted birth-defect prevalence (Nutrition)
**Summary:** *Annals of Internal Medicine* (around Aug 31, 2026; DOI: 10.7326/ANNALS-25-05243; Fudan Children’s Hospital SPCC team) assessed parental red-blood-cell folate and vitamin B12 in relation to offspring birth defects. Analyses included about 3,032 preconception couples and about 17,765 postconception mothers; roughly 98% of cases were non–neural-tube defects. Higher preconception maternal and paternal B12 tracked with lower predicted defect prevalence (maternal association continuous in the observed range; paternal association plateaued at mid-high levels). Higher early-pregnancy maternal RBC folate also associated with lower predicted prevalence in a nonlinear pattern that leveled after a threshold. Observational associations; they do not prove that supplements alone replace comprehensive preconception care.

**Links:**

- [ACP Journals — DOI: 10.7326/ANNALS-25-05243](https://www.acpjournals.org/doi/10.7326/ANNALS-25-05243)
- [Fudan News — SPCC folate and B12 study](https://newsroom.fudan.edu.cn/news/2026/0903/c1892a150276/page.psp)

**Commentary:** Preconception nutrition is a couple’s project—both partners should address diet and indicated supplementation (especially B12 in at-risk groups) before pregnancy, not only after a positive test.

### 4. Two-cohort study: Human milk oligosaccharides (notably 2′FL and 6′SL) associate with a lighter infant gut antibiotic-resistance gene load (Nutrition / Public Health)
**Summary:** *Cell Reports Medicine* (2026; DOI: 10.1016/j.xcrm.2026.103007; CSIC/IATA) combined shotgun metagenomics and HMO profiling in a Spanish cohort of about 57 one-month-old infants and 50 mothers, with validation in about 199 exclusively breastfed infants from the Dutch Lifelines NEXT cohort. Exclusive breastfeeding associated with lower abundance and diversity of antimicrobial-resistance genes; milk from secretor (FUT2) mothers had higher total HMO content (median about 12.19 vs 8.24 mg/mL). 2′-fucosyllactose (2′FL) and 6′-sialyllactose (6′SL) correlated inversely with specific resistance-gene classes. Delivery mode also shaped resistome diversity. Correlational design; commercial HMO products should not be equated with whole human milk.

**Links:**

- [DOI: 10.1016/j.xcrm.2026.103007](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.xcrm.2026.103007)
- [IATA-CSIC — Milk oligosaccharides and infant resistome](https://iata.csic.es/los-oligosacaridos-de-la-leche-materna-limitan-la-aparicion-de-genes-de-resistencia-a-antibioticos-en-bebes/)

**Commentary:** When feasible, supporting exclusive breastfeeding remains one of the few early-life lifestyle levers against a heavier infant gut resistome—do not treat over-the-counter HMO blends as automatic substitutes.

## II. Exercise & Training Science

### 5. Mechanistic review: Exercise-induced sublethal muscle mitochondrial stress may drive systemic mitochondrial quality-control adaptations via myokines, metabolites, and vesicles (Training)
**Summary:** A *Sports Medicine and Health Science* review (Chinese coverage Sep 5, 2026) frames contractile fibers’ sublethal mitochondrial stress as activating ISRmt and UPRmt, which shape release of myokines, metabolites, extracellular vesicles, and mitochondrial material. Circulating signals reach liver, heart, brain, and adipose tissue and converge on mitochondrial quality control (MQC); liver may amplify muscle cues into systemic FGF21 and GDF15. Exercise-derived ROS dose-dependently balances protective versus damaging signals; epigenetic marks may convert transient exercise cues into longer-lasting adaptation. Authors stress that much mechanistic evidence comes from non-exercise systems and that direct causal chains in physiological exercise, plus intensity–signal dose–response, still need clarification.

**Links:**

- [Ebiotrade — Exercise mitochondrial stress and systemic adaptation](https://www.ebiotrade.com/newsf/2026-9/202609050013489852.htm)
- [SciOpen — Sports Medicine and Health Science](https://www.sciopen.com/journal/2097-6291)

**Commentary:** Whole-body benefits look more like recoverable mitohormesis than “harder is always better”—favor sustainable, recoverable training loads and avoid casually blotting out adaptive ROS signals with megadose antioxidants.

## III. Public Health & Disease Prevention

### 6. China CDC issues Technical Guidelines for Seasonal Influenza Vaccination (2026–2027): vaccinate from 6 months of age and prioritize eight groups (Guidelines)
**Summary:** China CDC recently released the 2026–2027 influenza vaccination technical guidelines. All persons aged ≥6 months without contraindications are advised to vaccinate, with priority for eight groups: health-care workers; adults ≥60 years; people with chronic disease; residents and staff of long-term care and similar congregate settings; children 6 months–5 years; household contacts and caregivers of infants <6 months; people in childcare, schools, and custodial settings; and pregnant people. Licensed products highlighted for the season include trivalent inactivated (IIV3) and trivalent live attenuated (LAIV3) vaccines. Vaccination is ideally completed in September–October; doses may still be given throughout the season if missed. Protective antibody levels typically develop about 2–4 weeks after vaccination.

**Links:**

- [Guangming Online — China CDC flu vaccine guideline coverage](https://politics.gmw.cn/2026-09/07/content_38987622.htm)

### 7. CDC posts interim clinical considerations for 2026–2027 seasonal influenza vaccines; most people still best served by September–October vaccination (Guidelines)
**Summary:** On Sep 1, 2026, CDC updated *Interim Clinical Considerations for the Use of Seasonal Influenza Vaccines*, stating that seasonal influenza recommendations from the July 2025 immunization schedule remain in effect for 2026–2027. For most people needing one dose, vaccination is ideally offered in September or October. Vaccination in July–August is generally not preferred for most groups—especially older adults—because of possible late-season waning, though earlier vaccination can be considered when later access is unlikely. The document is aimed at clinicians and public-health officials and reflects ACIP/CDC guidance plus FDA licensure.

**Links:**

### 8. GBD 2023 in *Lancet Public Health*: About 2.71 billion people exposed to second-hand smoke in 2023, with roughly 1.66 million attributable deaths (Public Health)
**Summary:** *The Lancet Public Health* (online Sep 7, 2026; DOI: 10.1016/S2468-2667(26)00168-4) estimates that in 2023 about 2.71 billion people were exposed to second-hand smoke, including about 767 million children aged 0–14, accounting for about 1.66 million (1.33–2.07) deaths and about 44.8 million DALYs. Age-standardized exposure prevalence fell about 22% since 1990, yet absolute numbers exposed stayed roughly stable and rose sharply in sub-Saharan Africa and North Africa and the Middle East. Ischemic heart disease led adult attributable burden; lower respiratory infections predominated in children. The analysis reiterates that no level of second-hand smoke exposure is safe.

**Links:**

## IV. Sleep & Mental Health

### 9. University RCT: LMS-based self-guided resilience program improves distress among completers, but adherence is low and ITT effects are null (Mental Health)
**Summary:** *Scientific Reports* (Sep 2, 2026; DOI: 10.1038/s41598-026-67088-7) randomized 216 university students to a learning-management-system self-guided online resilience intervention or wait-list control, with pre, post, and follow-up assessments. Per-protocol analyses (about n=150) found post-test improvements in mental distress and self-compassion and better acceptance at follow-up; self-reported resilience and most resilience factors did not show clear advantages. Adherence was low, and intention-to-treat analyses were non-significant. Satisfaction was high. The tool may help under high-adherence conditions; reach hinges on persistence more than content alone.

**Links:**

- [Nature Scientific Reports — Online resilience RCT](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-67088-7)
- [DOI: 10.1038/s41598-026-67088-7](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-67088-7)

**Commentary:** Campus mental-health digital tools need built-in accountability (check-ins, peers, or counselors); otherwise promising content evaporates in low completion rates.

### 10. Animal study: Sleep disruption worsens allergic airway inflammation via sympathetic–macrophage mtDNA–cGAS–STING signaling (Sleep)
**Summary:** Work linked to *Journal of Neuroinflammation* (Chinese coverage Sep 4, 2026) combined ovalbumin allergic airway inflammation with sleep disruption in animals. Sleep disruption increased airway hyperresponsiveness, peribronchial inflammation, and mucus metaplasia, with higher pulmonary norepinephrine and tyrosine-hydroxylase signals. Lung macrophages showed mitochondrial injury, cytosolic mtDNA accumulation, and cGAS–STING activation. Chemical sympathectomy reduced hyperresponsiveness and STING activation; in vitro norepinephrine amplified LPS-driven inflammation, attenuable by adrenergic blockade or STING inhibition. Animal and cell mechanisms; not a license for human STING-targeted self-treatment.

**Links:**

- [Ebiotrade — Sleep disruption and airway inflammation](https://www.ebiotrade.com/newsf/2026-9/20260903205651003.htm)
- [Springer Medicine — Sleep disruption and airway inflammation](https://www.springermedicine.com/sleep-disruption-exacerbates-allergic-airway-inflammation-throug/53211152)

**Commentary:** For people with allergic airway disease, sleep hygiene deserves a place beside allergen control—but this remains a mechanistic warning, not a new drug recipe.

### 11. Rodent study: REM prefrontal high-frequency oscillation chains organize cortical–hippocampal reactivation differently from NREM (Sleep)
**Summary:** *eLife* (Sep 7, 2026) recorded hippocampal CA1 and prefrontal cortex in rodents during spatial-memory consolidation and reported theta-modulated prefrontal high-frequency oscillation (HFO) chains in REM that coincide with higher CA1–PFC theta coherence and windows of enhanced hippocampal–cortical communication. REM HFO chains sparsely and over longer timescales organize behavior-related prefrontal reactivation, distinct from widespread burst reactivation during NREM ripples, and relate to CA1 theta-phase preference shifts between behavior and REM. A cortical network model incorporating acetylcholine effects can reproduce REM versus NREM patterns. Animal electrophysiology; it does not justify human “REM booster” supplements.

**Links:**

- [eLife — REM prefrontal HFO chains](https://elifesciences.org/articles/110795)

**Commentary:** The practical takeaway is still to protect consolidated nighttime sleep architecture—including REM—rather than relying on fragmented catch-up naps as a full substitute.

## V. Evidence & Clarification / Supplements

### 12. Contaminant screen of commercial plant protein isolates: PFAS detected in all five matrices and dominate estimated non-carcinogenic cumulative risk (Evidence Clarification)
**Summary:** *NFS Journal* (DOI: 10.1016/j.nfs.2026.100277; *Food Safety Magazine* Sep 4, 2026) measured PFAS, mycotoxins, neonicotinoid pesticides, PAHs, and the processing contaminant 5-HMF in commercial protein isolates from soy, rice, pea, hemp, and pumpkin. PFAS were detected in all samples (reported total concentrations on the order of about 724–3010 ng/g) and drove most of the hazard-index estimate based on health-based guidance values; rice protein showed a high hazard quotient. Mycotoxins and other residues were widely detected though often below EU limits. Small sample size and conservative exposure assumptions make this a screening-level alert, not individualized quantitative risk assessment.

**Links:**

- [DOI: 10.1016/j.nfs.2026.100277](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.nfs.2026.100277)
- [Food Safety Magazine — PFAS in plant-based protein supplements](https://www.food-safety.com/articles/11793-study-finds-pfas-in-all-tested-commercially-available-plant-based-protein-supplements)

**Commentary:** Protein powder is not automatically “clean”: prefer whole-food protein first, and if you use powders, choose brands with third-party contaminant testing and avoid treating them as unlimited meal replacements.
