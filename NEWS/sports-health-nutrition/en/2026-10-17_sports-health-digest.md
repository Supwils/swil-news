## I. Nutrition and Diet

### 1. PLOS Medicine: In 461,682 U.S. adults followed for a median of 18.8 years, total ultra-processed food was not linked to colorectal cancer, while processed meat and whole grains pointed in opposite directions (Nutrition)
**Summary:** Leila Abar, Caitlin P. O'Connell, Erikka Loftfield, and colleagues published a prospective cohort in PLOS Medicine on October 15. The NIH-AARP Diet and Health Study included 461,682 adults who were aged 50–71 and cancer-free at enrollment in 1995–96 (274,269 men and 187,413 women). Food-frequency items were classified with Nova. Follow-up through 2018, median 18.8 years, found 10,075 colorectal adenocarcinomas. Median energy-adjusted ultra-processed intake was 293 g per 1,000 kcal per day, about 43% of energy. The hazard ratio for the highest versus lowest quintile was 0.97 (95% CI 0.91–1.03; P for trend = 0.55). The abstract says whole grain, fiber, dairy, and calcium from ultra-processed sources were associated with lower incidence, and meat from those sources with higher incidence. In an October 17 commentary, the authors wrote that replacing 10% of ultra-processed weight with the same weight of minimally processed food was linked to a 2% lower incidence. The exposure is a single baseline diet questionnaire.

**Links:**

- [PLOS Medicine — Food processing and colorectal cancer](https://journals.plos.org/plosmedicine/article?id=10.1371/journal.pmed.1005251)
- [The Conversation — Authors on the 10% substitution result](https://theconversation.com/replacing-just-10-of-ultra-processed-foods-with-whole-foods-linked-to-drop-in-bowel-cancer-rate-294469)

**Commentary:** For colorectal cancer, act on the food: keep processed meats such as hot dogs down, and keep whole grains, fiber, and dairy on the plate; the single label "ultra-processed" did not track higher incidence in this large cohort.

### 2. JAMA Network Open: Among 8,783 middle-aged Canadians without cardiovascular disease, the top quartile of ultra-processed intake had an odds ratio of 1.40 for covert vascular brain injury (Nutrition)
**Summary:** Russell J. de Souza, Sonia S. Anand, and colleagues published a cross-sectional study online in JAMA Network Open on October 15 (doi: 10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2026.39118). It included 8,783 adults aged 35–69 without cardiovascular disease in the Canadian Alliance for Healthy Hearts and Healthy Minds and PURE-MIND (mean age 57.7 years; 56.6% women). Ultra-processed intake came from food-frequency questionnaires as servings per 2,000 kcal. MRI defined vascular brain injury as covert brain infarcts or a high burden of white-matter hyperintensities. Versus the lowest quartile, the highest had body fat 1.7 percentage points higher (95% CI 1.2–2.2) and visceral fat volume 5.7 mL greater (3.2–8.3), with no significant difference in hepatic fat fraction. The adjusted odds ratio for vascular brain injury was 1.40 (1.10–1.77; P for trend = 0.009). Montreal Cognitive Assessment and Digit Symbol Substitution scores were not associated. The authors say the brain finding was sensitive to how ultra-processed food was defined, and one-time measurement cannot set the time order.

**Links:**

- [JAMA Network Open — Ultra-processed food, adiposity, and vascular brain injury](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2855351)

**Commentary:** Middle-aged adults who cut sugary drinks, ready snacks, and convenience meals are looking at a link with lower body fat and one MRI signal of vessel injury; this study did not show worse cognitive scores, and it cannot promise that one fewer snack prevents a stroke.

### 3. PLOS ONE: In a cross-section of 1,020 adults in Chennai, a higher genetic risk score lined up with higher total and LDL cholesterol when diet quality was low (Nutrition)
**Summary:** Manahil M. Bineid, Dhanasekaran Bodhini, Vimal Karani Vimaleswaran, and colleagues published a cross-sectional analysis in PLOS ONE on October 14. The Chennai Urban Rural Epidemiology Study contributed 1,020 adults aged 25–80 (452 men and 568 women). The genetic risk score used TCF7L2 rs7903146, FTO rs8050136, and FTO rs1588413. Diet quality used an Indian score built from eight food groups. A high score was associated with higher fasting glucose (P = 0.005) and glycated hemoglobin (P = 0.006). After correction for multiple testing, the score interacted with diet quality for total cholesterol (P for interaction = 0.003) and LDL cholesterol (P = 0.005). At the low-quality end, people with a high score had higher total and LDL cholesterol than those with a low score (P < 0.001 for each comparison). A University of Reading note dated October 15 describes the low-quality pattern as less fruit, vegetables, beans, and lentils. The design cannot show that changing diet lowers cholesterol.

**Links:**

- [PLOS ONE — Diet quality and genetic cholesterol risk in Asian Indian adults](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0357182)
- [University of Reading — Diet pattern and the cholesterol gene interaction](https://www.reading.ac.uk/news/2026/Research-News/Indian-diet-shift-exposes-hidden-genetic-cholesterol-risk)

**Commentary:** Adults eating in a South Asian pattern can put fruit, vegetables, and pulses on the plate before ordering a cholesterol gene test; this cross-section only shows that a higher genetic score and higher cholesterol sat together when diet quality was low.

### 4. CCTV.com: A clinical nutrition specialist compressed chronic-disease eating advice into "two mores and three lesses," with no new trial effect size (Nutrition)
**Summary:** On October 17, CCTV.com published remarks from Liu Yinghua, chair of the clinical nutrition and health branch of the Chinese Health Management Association and chief physician in the nutrition department at the First Medical Center of Chinese PLA General Hospital. The piece calls consistent daily meals a way to prevent chronic disease and says that adjusting the pattern can lower the risk of central obesity and serious cardiovascular events, without citing a new trial or an effect size. "Two mores" means more whole grains, vegetables, lower-glycemic fruit, lower-fat nuts, milk, and eggs, plus freshwater fish such as crucian carp and sea bass, sweet potato, taro, lotus root, tofu, soy milk, shiitake, and wood ear. "Three lesses" means less saturated fat from fatty meat and organ meats, pairing white rice and white noodles with coarse grains, and less fried food, pastries, and sugar-sweetened drinks. This is an expert mnemonic, not a new dietary guideline.

**Links:**

- [CCTV.com — The two-mores, three-lesses eating pattern](https://health.cnr.cn/jkgdxw/20261017/t20261017_527844644.shtml)

**Commentary:** One meal today can swap part of the white rice for a whole grain and add tofu or a freshwater fish; the mnemonic restates an existing direction, and the article does not show that this pattern cuts cardiovascular events by a stated percent.

## II. Exercise and Training Science

### 5. Nature Communications: In 187 adults with obesity, an 8-hour eating window and 12 weeks of supervised exercise did not lower liver fat more than Mediterranean-diet education (Training)
**Summary:** Antonio Clavero-Jimeno, Alba Camacho-Cardenosa, Jonatan R. Ruiz, and colleagues published a parallel-group randomized trial in Nature Communications on October 14. In Granada, Spain, 187 adults with obesity, half of them women, were randomized and all were kept in the intention-to-treat analysis: usual care 48, time-restricted eating 46, exercise 47, and both 46. Usual care was education on a Mediterranean diet and physical activity. Time-restricted eating used an 8-hour daily window. The primary outcome was MRI intrahepatic fat fraction at 12 weeks. Versus usual care, the differences were −0.4 percentage points for time-restricted eating (95% CI −2.0 to 1.2; P = 0.93), −0.8 for exercise (−2.4 to 0.8; P = 0.54), and −1.1 for the combination (−2.7 to 0.5; P = 0.28), with no differences among the intervention arms. Outcome assessors and statisticians were blinded; intervention staff were not. Registration NCT05897073.

**Links:**

- [Nature Communications — TEMPUS: time-restricted eating, exercise, and liver fat](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-78625-3)

**Commentary:** Adults with obesity who already receive Mediterranean-diet and activity education should not expect an added 8-hour eating window to clear extra liver fat within 12 weeks; the intervals include no difference, so the trial also does not prove these methods useless.

### 6. Lancet Regional Health – Americas: Among 96 adults with early-onset type 2 diabetes, a low-energy diet plus supervised exercise produced 54% remission at 24 weeks versus 4% with usual care (Training)
**Summary:** Kaberi Dasgupta, Normand G. Boulé, Tom Yates, and colleagues report an open-label, blinded-endpoint randomized trial in The Lancet Regional Health – Americas; ScienceAlert summarized it on October 16. Three centers in England and Canada randomized 96 adults aged 18–45 with early-onset type 2 diabetes and obesity who were not on insulin (mean age 38 years; BMI 35.2) between September 24, 2021, and June 6, 2025. The intervention was 800–900 kcal a day in weeks 1–12, with twice-weekly supervised aerobic plus resistance work and one independent session, then a weight-maintenance diet and independent exercise in weeks 13–24. Remission at 24 weeks (HbA1c below 6.5% and no glucose-lowering drug for at least 12 weeks) was 27/50 (54%) versus 2/46 (4%), a 50-point difference (95% CI 33%–64%) and an adjusted odds ratio of 21.6 (6.2–114.5). Missing outcomes were counted as no remission. The between-group HbA1c difference was −0.7 percentage points (−1.1 to −0.3). The paper reports a 16.5% intervention effect on fat mass and no between-group change in fat-free mass. Adverse events occurred in 43/50 versus 6/46, mostly early constipation, headache, dizziness, and fatigue, with no serious adverse events. Registration ISRCTN15487120.

**Links:**

- [Lancet Regional Health – Americas — RESET for Remission](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lanam/article/PIIS2667-193X(26)00294-2/fulltext)
- [ScienceAlert — October 16 account of the trial](https://www.sciencealert.com/this-diet-and-exercise-combination-put-early-onset-diabetes-into-remission-in-half-of-participants)

**Commentary:** Adults under 45 with type 2 diabetes who are not yet on insulin can ask a clinician about this supervised low-energy diet plus aerobic and resistance work; 800–900 kcal is not a self-started plan, early side effects were common, and the odds-ratio interval is wide.

A Franklin County death remained under review against a definition that requires death within 30 days of onset, clinical evidence, a positive laboratory test, and no unrelated cause. ## IV. Sleep and Mental Health

### 9. Applied Physiology, Nutrition, and Metabolism: Among 59 completers, four weeks of time-restricted ketogenic eating lowered weight and raised Pittsburgh sleep scores (Sleep)
**Summary:** Zongze Wu, Zhaowei Kong, Mingzhu Hu, Qingde Shi, and Jinlei Nie published a randomized trial online on October 13 in Applied Physiology, Nutrition, and Metabolism. Sixty-eight young adults with overweight or obesity were assigned to control, ketogenic diet, ketogenic diet plus sprint intervals, or ketogenic diet plus moderate continuous training, 17 per arm; 59 finished 4 weeks. The ketogenic arms were time-restricted and not energy-restricted. Every ketogenic arm lost 5.0%–6.0% of body weight (d = −2.5 to −1.9). Relative peak oxygen uptake rose 13.0% with sprint intervals (d = 1.1) and 7.8% with moderate continuous training (d = 0.6). Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index scores rose 12.2%–29.8% in every ketogenic arm (d = 0.3–1.0); a higher score means worse sleep. Daytime dysfunction fell in the two exercise arms and rose 66.7% with diet alone. Greater weight loss tracked a larger rise in the sleep index (r = −0.5; P < 0.01). Adherence was 82.4%–94.1%.

**Links:**

- [Applied Physiology, Nutrition, and Metabolism — Time-restricted ketogenic diet, exercise, and sleep](https://cdnsciencepub.com/doi/10.1139/apnm-2026-0138)

**Commentary:** Young adults using a ketogenic pattern for a month of faster weight loss should expect sleep scores to worsen, and adding intervals or steady cardio did not pull that primary sleep outcome back; four weeks is not evidence of long-term sleep safety.

**Summary:** On October 16 the U.S. Food and Drug Administration posted a company announcement from Krasnyi Oktyabr Inc. of Brooklyn, New York, dated October 9. The recall covers Starfish Seafood Premium Cold Smoked Norwegian Salmon Sliced, 227 g / 8 oz, vacuum packed, labeled frozen, origin Latvia. Identifiers are UPC 4751048940099, best before 27.11.2027, lot D080-148. Distribution was New York, Florida, Georgia, New Jersey, Pennsylvania, and Ohio. The company is a wholesaler, not the manufacturer. Buyers are told not to eat the product and to return it for a full refund.
