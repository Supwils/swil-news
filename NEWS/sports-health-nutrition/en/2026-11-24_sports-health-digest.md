## I. Nutrition & Diet

### 1. MESA cohort: each extra daily ultraprocessed-food serving tied to ~5% higher heart-failure risk, mainly HFpEF (Nutrition)
**Summary:** In *JACC: Heart Failure*, investigators analyzed 6,531 MESA adults aged 45–84 without baseline CVD, classifying ultraprocessed food (UPF) intake with the Nova system and relating it to incident heart failure (HF). Each additional daily UPF serving was associated with ~5% higher HF risk (HR 1.052, 95% CI 1.002–1.103). The signal was driven mainly by HF with preserved ejection fraction (HFpEF; HR 1.096, 95% CI 1.016–1.183); participants in the upper 80% of UPF intake had more than double the HFpEF risk of the lowest 20% (HR 2.234). Mediation analyses suggested waist-to-height ratio accounted for ~51% of the total-HF association and ~49% of the HFpEF association. Observational design cannot prove causation.

**Links:**

- [JACC: Heart Failure — UPF and incident HF in MESA](https://www.jacc.org/doi/10.1016/j.jchf.2026.103571)
- [ACC — Ultraprocessed food intake and HF risk](https://www.acc.org/Latest-in-Cardiology/Journal-Scans/2026/11/24/14/01/Ultraprocessed-Food-Intake)

**Commentary:** Cutting back on packaged snacks, sugary drinks, and heavily processed meats is not only a weight tactic—it may also trim a central-adiposity pathway linked to HFpEF risk.

### 2. RCT: adding <9-hour time-restricted eating to calorie restriction spared radius BMD in postmenopausal women (Nutrition)
**Summary:** A 24-week *American Journal of Clinical Nutrition* RCT randomized 49 postmenopausal women (BMI ≥25; ages 50–79) to calorie restriction (~−500 kcal/day) alone or TRE (eating window <9 hours) plus CR. Weight loss was similar (~−6.8 kg). Hip BMD fell similarly in both groups, but TRE+CR attenuated radius BMD loss versus CR alone (~+0.009 vs −0.020 g/cm²; P=0.001) and reduced visceral fat more. Gut-microbiota beta diversity shifted in the TRE+CR arm, and microbial guild-ratio changes correlated with radius BMD change. The trial was small and short; no comparable hip protection was seen.

**Links:**

- [AJCN — TRE + CR, bone and gut microbiota](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ajcnut.2026.101637)

**Commentary:** Midlife weight loss should track bone, not only the scale—if you use TRE, pair it with resistance training, protein/calcium/vitamin D habits, and fall-prevention work for the hip rather than assuming a shorter eating window protects every site.

### 3. Multicenter RCT: 8-hour TRE did not beat a Mediterranean-diet education program for liver fat (Nutrition/Evidence check)
**Summary:** A 12-week Spanish multicenter RCT in *JHEP Reports* assigned adults with overweight/obesity to usual care (UC: Mediterranean diet–based education) or UC plus early, late, or self-selected 8-hour TRE (~47–52 per arm). Hepatic fat fraction (HFF) fell within all three TRE arms, but between-group differences versus UC were nonsignificant for HFF, liver markers, and fecal microbiota. Larger HFF drops occurred with baseline MASLD or ≥5% weight loss (mean differences ~−2.7% and −2.6%). More TRE participants hit ≥5% weight loss (~41%–44% vs 16% in UC). Authors conclude eating-window timing itself added little beyond weight-loss–linked effects.

**Links:**

- [JHEP Reports — TRE, liver fat and microbiota](https://www.jhep-reports.eu/article/S2589-5559(26)00227-2/fulltext)
- [HealthDay — TRE does not top Mediterranean program for liver](https://www.healthday.com/healthpro-news/liver-health/time-restricted-eating-does-not-top-mediterranean-diet-based-program-for-liver-health)

**Commentary:** Do not treat “early window only” as liver dogma—choose a sustainable plan that actually delivers clinically meaningful weight loss.

### 4. UK Biobank: one shared plant-forward 8-food core works across polygenic-risk strata for multimorbidity transitions (Nutrition)
**Summary:** An *AJCN* prospective analysis of ~330,549 UK Biobank participants used target-disease polygenic risk scores (PRS) to test diet-quality effect modification across 253 source→target disease transitions, then derived a low-burden sDiet-8 (fresh whole fruit, vegetables, whole grains, legumes/soy, nuts/seeds, fish, plant oils, less processed meat). High PRS plus poor diet showed higher transition risk; for dyslipidemia→type 2 diabetes, high-PRS poor vs ideal diet HRs were ~2.02 vs 1.83, with alternative Mediterranean food-score 10-year risks ~95.8 vs 79.5 per 1,000. In 22 of 24 continuous associations, higher sDiet-8 tracked lower risk point estimates. Observational design and prior-day diet recalls limit inference.

**Links:**

- [AJCN — Polygenic risk and shared healthy-food core](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ajcnut.2026.101512)
- [ebiotrade — Polygenic risk and healthy-food core](https://n.ebiotrade.com/newsf/2026-11/20261124084036337.htm)

**Commentary:** Use genetic risk to decide who needs earlier, denser diet counseling—not to invent a wholly different food list; nail the same plant-forward core first.

## II. Exercise & Training Science

### 5. COPD 12-week cardio-plus-strength program: impulse oscillometry shows peripheral-airway gains that spirometry largely misses (Training/Clinical)
**Summary:** A Lund University–led nonrandomized intervention with Uppsala collaborators, reported in *Respiratory Research* (institutional release Nov 24, 2026), followed 62 people with stable mild-to-severe COPD through 12 weeks of thrice-weekly individualized cardio and strength training (two supervised group sessions, one home session). Within-person IOS measures of peripheral airway function improved clearly, while standard spirometry changes were small, absent, or hard to detect; 6-minute walk distance rose ~28 m and 1-minute sit-to-stand reps rose ~2.5. Symptom gains were largest among those with worse baseline symptoms. Mechanisms remain under study; nonrandomized design limits causal strength.

**Links:**

- [Lund University — Exercise improves COPD peripheral airways](https://www.medicine.lu.se/article/exercise-led-measurable-improvement-copd-affected-lungs-something-standard-spirometry-failed-detect)
- [Respiratory Research — 12-week exercise and peripheral airway function](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12931-026-03933-7)

**Commentary:** Do not judge pulmonary rehab by FEV1 alone—after clinical clearance, keep cardio-plus-strength work going to break the “breathlessness → inactivity → worse fitness” loop.

### 6. Crossover RCT: ~1 minute of light evening stair-stepping cut the post–glucose-drink spike by about 40% in healthy adults (Training)
**Summary:** An *Experimental Physiology* randomized crossover trial in 30 nondiabetic adults (~age 29) compared evening seated rest after a 75 g oral glucose load with ~1 minute of self-paced light stair-stepping ~57 minutes later. The stair bout lowered the baseline-to-60-minute glucose rise (~2.5 vs 4.3 mmol/L; P<0.001) and reduced AUC/iAUC; perceived exertion was light. Findings are acute, lab-based after sugar water—not mixed meals—and do not establish benefits for diabetes care or long-term HbA1c.

**Links:**

- [PMC — One-minute stair-stepping and evening glycaemia](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13238582/)
- [Washington Post — One minute of activity and glucose spikes](https://www.washingtonpost.com/wellness/2026/11/20/just-one-minute-this-activity-may-cut-blood-sugar-spikes-by-40-percent/)

**Commentary:** A post-meal one-minute stair bout is a low-friction way to blunt acute glucose peaks—useful as a habit add-on, not a substitute for regular training or diabetes therapy.

### 7. Large meta-analysis: chronic stretching shows no clear sports-injury prevention effect (Evidence check)
**Summary:** A *Sports Medicine* systematic review and meta-analysis (~Nov 21, 2026) screened >300,000 records and included 19 studies (>9,000 participants; >80% male; ages ~14–59), with 15 in the meta-analysis. Versus all comparators, stretching was not associated with lower injury odds (OR 0.945, 95% CI 0.828–1.078); null findings held versus passive or active controls and for muscle, tendon, and ligament/joint-capsule outcomes. Most interventions were 4–24 weeks of static unassisted stretching; RCT bias risk was often high and GRADE certainty mostly very low. No study assessed acute pre-session stretching for injury risk.

**Links:**

- [Sports Medicine — Stretching for injury prevention](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-026-02559-5)
- [ebiotrade — Stretching injury-prevention meta-analysis](https://n.ebiotrade.com/newsf/2026-11/20261122202553430.htm)

**Commentary:** Spend warm-up minutes on strength, balance, and sport-specific neuromuscular work; keep stretching for mobility or comfort, not as your main injury-prevention pill.

## III. Public Health & Disease Prevention

### 8. NEJM Evidence: U.S. hepatitis B birth-dose coverage fell from ~80% to ~66% (Public health)
**Summary:** A Truveta EHR analysis of mother–infant dyads published in *NEJM Evidence* Public Health Alerts (Nov 24, 2026) covered 1,338,961 infants born January 2018–June 2026. Hepatitis B vaccination within one day of birth was ~76.4% in 2018, averaged ~80.0% in 2020–2022, then declined from 2023 to ~65.6% in 2026. Among infants of HBsAg-positive mothers, birth-dose coverage fell from ~97%–98% in 2020–2025 to ~93.9% in the first half of 2026. The drop began before the December 2025 ACIP shift away from a universal birth-dose recommendation; prenatal screening gaps still leave some newborns without a known maternal status safety net.

**Links:**

- [Truveta — Hepatitis B birth-dose decline](https://www.truveta.com/blog/news/hepatitis-b-vaccine-nejm-evidence/)
- [CIDRAP/NEJM Evidence PDF — Birth dose 2018–June 2026](https://www.cidrap.umn.edu/sites/default/files/page-files/PHA-Hepatitis%20B%20birth%20dose112426.pdf)

**Commentary:** Expectant parents and obstetric teams should explicitly confirm prenatal HBV screening and the birth dose—perinatal transmission risk and later liver-disease stakes make this a high-leverage safety net.

### 9. NIH ECHO: prenatal DINCH plasticizer exposure linked to smaller babies (Public health)
**Summary:** NIH Research Matters (Nov 24, 2026) highlighted an NYU Grossman–led *Environment International* analysis in the ECHO cohort of >5,500 mother–child pairs. DINCH metabolites were detectable in roughly half to two-thirds of pregnant participants. A 10-fold higher level of certain metabolites was linked to nearly an ounce lower birth weight and about a tenth of an inch shorter birth length, with stronger associations for third-trimester exposure. Female infants more often weighed less; male infants more often were shorter. No link to preterm birth was found. Observational design cannot prove causation.

**Links:**

- [NIH — Chemical in plastics linked to smaller babies](https://www.nih.gov/news-events/nih-research-matters/chemical-plastics-linked-smaller-babies)
- [Environment International — Prenatal DINCH and birth outcomes](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.envint.2026.110548)

**Commentary:** “Phthalate-free” packaging is not automatically risk-free—during pregnancy, minimize unnecessary plastic food contact and heating plastics as part of fetal-growth protection.

### 10. Chinese consensus (2026): Alzheimer risk-factor management stresses modifiable targets (Guidelines)
**Summary:** The Alzheimer Disease Precision Prevention, Screening, Diagnosis and Treatment Alliance’s *Expert Consensus on Management of High-Risk Factors for Alzheimer’s Disease (2026)* appears in the *Chinese Journal of Neuromedicine*, with professional platforms highlighting it on Nov 24, 2026. It separates nonmodifiable factors (age, sex, genetics) from modifiable ones (education, anxiety/depression, social engagement, diet, exercise, weight, smoking/alcohol, sleep, vascular risks, sensory and oral health, and more), referencing the framing that ~40%–45% of dementia may be preventable or delayable via risk-factor control. Recommendations favor lifelong learning and social contact, Mediterranean/MIND-style eating, ≥3 weekly moderate-or-higher intensity exercise sessions, and structured care for mood and cardiometabolic risks. Much evidence is observational or from limited trials and should be read by recommendation grade.

**Links:**

- [Chinese Journal of Neuromedicine — Consensus entry](https://rs.yiigle.com/cmaid/1668655)
- [BrainMed — Consensus reprint](https://www.brainmed.com/info/detail?id=56904)

**Commentary:** Brain-health prevention largely overlaps cardiovascular prevention—treat exercise, sleep, social ties, blood-pressure/glucose control, and a Mediterranean-style plate as midlife prescriptions, not late-life afterthoughts.

## IV. Sleep & Mental Health

### 11. UK Biobank: poor sleep patterns linked to accelerated “organ aging” across lung, liver, muscle-bone, immune, metabolic, and brain systems (Sleep)
**Summary:** *GeroScience* (Nov 24, 2026) analyzed 247,697 UK Biobank adults free of major chronic disease at baseline (mean age ~55.2 years). Self-reported sleep duration, chronotype, insomnia, snoring, and daytime sleepiness formed healthy/intermediate/poor sleep patterns; machine learning estimated ages for eight organ systems. Poor versus healthy sleep related to higher organ age gaps for pulmonary, hepatic, renal, musculoskeletal, immune, metabolic, and brain systems, with accelerated-aging ORs including musculoskeletal ~1.708, metabolic ~1.659, brain ~1.592, immune ~1.428, hepatic ~1.382, and pulmonary ~1.251, plus multi-system (≥2) accelerated aging OR ~1.463. Cross-sectional design, self-reported sleep, and model-derived organ ages cannot establish causality.

**Links:**

- [GeroScience — Sleep patterns and organ aging](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11357-026-02633-w)

**Commentary:** Treat sleep quality as multi-organ maintenance—regular schedules, insomnia/apnea screening, and less daytime sleepiness belong in the same annual check-in as blood pressure and glucose.

### 12. Nordic cohorts: depression polygenic liability shows the most consistent links to cardiometabolic disease (Mental health/Metabolism)
**Summary:** A *Nature Mental Health* multi-cohort observational study of 355,717 genotyped participants from Sweden, Norway, and Estonia tested polygenic scores (PGS) for six psychiatric disorders against ten clinically diagnosed cardiometabolic diseases (CMDs). Major depressive disorder PGS showed the most consistent CMD associations and remained relatively robust after mutual adjustment for other psychiatric PGS plus BMI, smoking, and education. ADHD, anxiety, and PTSD associations attenuated substantially after adjusting for depression PGS. Schizophrenia PGS (and, more weakly, bipolar PGS) associated with lower CMD risk. Authors identify depression liability as a key shared genetic source of mental–metabolic overlap; European-ancestry GWAS and Nordic cohorts limit generalization.

**Links:**

- [Nature Mental Health — Depression liability and CMDs](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44220-026-00749-8)
- [ebiotrade — Depression genetic liability and CMDs](https://n.ebiotrade.com/newsf/2026-11/20261124001015997.htm)

**Commentary:** If depression runs in your history or family, put blood pressure, lipids, glucose, and waist size on the same checklist as mental-health care—mood treatment and metabolic risk management should run in parallel.
