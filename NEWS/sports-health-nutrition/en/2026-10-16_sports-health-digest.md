## I. Nutrition and Diet

### 1. American Journal of Clinical Nutrition: In a crossover trial of 23 adults with overweight or obesity, 2 grams of plant sterols a day did not lower LDL cholesterol (Nutrition)
**Summary:** Leonardo Musto, Angela Dura, Juan Francisco Merino-Torres, Amparo Alegria, and Guadalupe Garcia-Llatas published a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled crossover trial online in the American Journal of Clinical Nutrition on October 16 (doi: 10.1016/j.ajcnut.2026.101588). All 23 participants completed two 8-week phases separated by a 6-week washout. Mean age was 51.7 years, 18 were women (78%), BMI was 36.4 ± 4.2, baseline LDL cholesterol was 125.0 ± 26.1 mg/dL, and class II and class III obesity accounted for 44% and 26%. Each person took a powder with 2 grams of plant sterols, or a matched placebo, before the main meal. The change in LDL cholesterol was +0.1 mg/dL (95% CI −7.1 to 7.3) on plant sterols and +3.9 mg/dL (−7.6 to 15.4) on placebo (P = 0.632). Other lipids, high-sensitivity C-reactive protein, and glucose-regulation measures also did not differ between treatments, and body-composition, sarcopenia, and lifestyle contrasts did not survive correction for multiple testing. The trial was powered only for LDL cholesterol (NCT06481020).

**Links:**

- [AJCN — Plant sterols and cardiometabolic measures in obesity](https://ajcn.nutrition.org/article/S0002-9165(26)00397-7/fulltext)

**Commentary:** Adults with obesity and mildly high LDL cholesterol should not treat 2 grams a day of plant sterols as a substitute for lipid-lowering medicine; this 23-person trial saw no LDL drop, and it was powered only for that primary outcome.

### 2. British Journal of Nutrition: Among 16,666 Korean adults aged 40 and older, the highest overall dietary-diversity group had a lower rate of new type 2 diabetes (Nutrition)
**Summary:** Min-Ji Kim, Hye Won Woo, Min-Ho Shin, and colleagues published a prospective cohort online in the British Journal of Nutrition on October 15. The Korean Genome and Epidemiology Study–CVD Association Study followed 16,666 adults aged at least 40 without diabetes at baseline (6,162 men and 10,504 women) for 96,609 person-years, a median of 5.96 years, with 953 incident cases. For the highest versus lowest cumulative dietary-diversity score, the incidence rate ratio was 0.56 in men (95% CI 0.40–0.79; P for linear trend = 0.0004) and 0.73 in women (0.54–0.98; P for trend = 0.0325); the sex interaction P was 0.308. Within food groups, the top versus bottom quartile of milk and dairy diversity had IRRs of 0.69 (0.53–0.90) in men and 0.70 (0.54–0.92) in women; fruit diversity in men was 0.70 (0.52–0.94), and vegetables, seaweed, and mushrooms in women were 0.73 (0.55–0.97). The linear trend for that vegetable group in women was not significant. Adjusting for the amount eaten in the same food group attenuated the fruit association in men. The exposure came from repeated food-frequency questionnaires.

**Links:**

- [British Journal of Nutrition — Dietary diversity and type 2 diabetes](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/british-journal-of-nutrition/article/dietary-diversity-and-type-2-diabetes-risk-evidence-from-a-prospective-korean-cohort-study/BA5048600D073EEB3981686AF734D652)

**Commentary:** Adults over 40 can widen the variety of dairy foods, fruit, and vegetables, but the overall diversity score was the more stable signal; the fruit association in men weakened after the amount eaten was taken into account, so variety does not replace quantity.

## II. Exercise and Training Science

### 4. Frontiers in Physiology: Across 25 randomized trials, exercise training lowered liver enzymes and BMI in adults with fatty liver disease, while liver fat itself stayed uncertain (Training)
**Summary:** Sai Long and Yao Li published a systematic review and meta-analysis in Frontiers in Physiology on October 16 (doi: 10.3389/fphys.2026.1976073). Searches through June 2026 included 25 randomized trials in metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease, or the older nonalcoholic fatty liver label. Programs ranged from aerobic, resistance, and high-intensity interval training to yoga and walking, lasting 6 weeks to 12 months. Alanine aminotransferase, from 15 trials and 504 people, fell by a mean difference of −7.33 U/L (95% CI −10.71 to −3.95), with moderate GRADE certainty, although the prediction interval crossed zero. Aspartate aminotransferase, from 13 trials and 439 people, was −5.52 U/L (−10.24 to −0.79), with very low certainty. BMI, from 17 trials and 564 people, was −1.54 kg/m² (−2.28 to −0.80), moderate certainty. Peak or maximal oxygen uptake rose by 4.08 mL/kg/min (1.00 to 7.16), low certainty. Pooled effects for liver fat, HOMA-IR, and HbA1c were not significant, all at very low certainty. Trials of 12 weeks or less showed a larger ALT drop, which the authors describe as an observational between-study subgroup, not evidence that shorter programs are physiologically better.

**Links:**

- [Frontiers in Physiology — Exercise, liver enzymes, BMI, and fitness in fatty liver disease](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/physiology/articles/10.3389/fphys.2026.1976073/full)

**Commentary:** Adults with fatty liver disease can use regular exercise as a way to lower transaminases and BMI, where certainty is moderate; this review did not show a reliable drop in liver fat or HbA1c, so a lower enzyme is not proof that the fat has cleared.

### 5. Sports Medicine: In 1,177 people, training over several weeks improved the metabolic cost of walking, and the stroke estimate was not significant (Training)
**Summary:** Tibor Hortobágyi and colleagues published a systematic review and robust-variance meta-analysis online in Sports Medicine on October 14. Twenty-seven randomized trials, 36 intervention arms, and 54 outcomes covered 1,177 people: 703 healthy adults and 474 patients with multiple sclerosis, Parkinson disease, or stroke, with stroke contributing 9 studies. Across training types, the metabolic cost of walking improved (g = 0.40, P = 0.001). The healthy-adult estimate was g = 0.49 (P = 0.001); the stroke estimate was g = 0.24 (P = 0.065). After adjustment for training characteristics, population moderated the effect (P = 0.043), and a higher study-level mean BMI was associated with a smaller effect (β = −0.20, P = 0.040). Training type, duration, intensity, frequency, and session length did not significantly moderate the result, and the authors note that the meta-regression may have been underpowered. Heterogeneity was I² below 62.5%, and risk of bias was moderate. Included trials lasted at least 3 weeks.

**Links:**

- [Sports Medicine — Exercise training and the metabolic cost of walking](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40279-026-02553-x)

**Commentary:** Healthy adults who train for several weeks can spend less energy covering the same walking distance, and aerobic, resistance, and other modes were not separated here; the stroke result was still unstable, and studies with higher BMI saw smaller effects, so no single class schedule is established.

### 7. American Heart Association: On World Restart a Heart Day, a collapsed teen or adult who is not breathing normally should prompt a call for help and hard, fast chest compressions (Public health)
**Summary:** The American Heart Association issued its World Restart a Heart Day action note on October 16. Citing its CPR perceptions survey, the association says 2 in 3 people still mistakenly believe that only someone with special training should perform CPR. Drawing on the 2025 heart-disease and stroke statistics, it says more than 350,000 cardiac arrests occur outside hospitals in the United States each year and about 90% of those people do not survive. Survival depends on how quickly people nearby recognize the emergency, call for help, start compressions, and use an automated external defibrillator. Volunteer president Manesh R. Patel said parents, neighbors, and coworkers are often first on the scene. The instruction is specific: if a teen or adult suddenly collapses and is not breathing normally, call emergency services and push hard and fast in the center of the chest until help arrives. The association also says research shows children as young as 9 can learn CPR, and it urges schools, workplaces, and sports facilities to keep a written cardiac-emergency response plan. This is a public call to action, not a new resuscitation trial.

**Links:**

- [American Heart Association — Four actions for World Restart a Heart Day](https://newsroom.heart.org/news/4-ways-to-take-action-on-world-restart-a-heart-day)

**Commentary:** The practical step is narrow: if a teen or adult collapses and is not breathing normally, call emergency services and push hard and fast in the center of the chest, without waiting to decide whether you count as a professional.

## IV. Sleep and Mental Health

### 8. BMC Psychiatry: In 15 adults with ADHD who already responded well to stimulants, one week on medication did not make polysomnographic sleep worse (Sleep)
**Summary:** Kevin Mammeri, Sebastien Weibel, and colleagues published a randomized crossover trial in BMC Psychiatry on October 16. Thirty-six adults with ADHD who had already been identified as good responders to methylphenidate or lisdexamfetamine were screened, including an adaptation night; 15 then entered the crossover. In random order they spent one week on stimulant medication and one week off it, followed by polysomnography at a sleep center, plus questionnaires, sleep diaries, and actigraphy. Compared with no medication, stimulants reduced self-reported fatigue and improved subjective sleep quality, with fewer transitions from deeper to lighter sleep and fewer arousals during deep sleep. Peak frequencies of fast sleep spindles and slow oscillations increased, and the spindle change was associated with more dream experience. The authors describe a stabilizing effect on the sleep-wake cycle and say it remains untested whether that comes from better daytime attention feeding a homeostatic loop. The sample was a small group of people who already responded well, not newly diagnosed adults who had never taken a stimulant.

**Links:**

- [BMC Psychiatry — Stimulants and sleep in adults with ADHD](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12888-026-08759-7)

**Commentary:** Adults with ADHD who already respond well to a stimulant need not stop it on the assumption that stimulants always ruin sleep; this 15-person crossover is also not a reason to prescribe a stimulant for insomnia in someone without ADHD.

## V. Evidence and Clarification

### 9. American Journal of Clinical Nutrition: Across 185 countries, a 10-point higher planetary-health diet index lined up with a 1.32% lower dementia DALY rate a decade later, and Mediterranean and DASH scores did not (Evidence)
**Summary:** Yuan Sheng, Kai Di, Haiyan Chi, Changling Li, Baogeng Huai, and Deshan Liu published a country-level longitudinal ecological analysis online in the American Journal of Clinical Nutrition on October 16 (doi: 10.1016/j.ajcnut.2026.101589). Global Dietary Database estimates for 185 countries and territories at seven time points from 1990 to 2018 were linked to Global Burden of Disease dementia figures and life-cycle environmental footprints. Two-way fixed-effects models looked at dementia burden lagged by 10 years. A 10-point higher modified Planetary Health Diet Index was associated with a 1.32% lower dementia disability-adjusted life-year rate a decade later (95% CI −2.27 to −0.37; q = 0.029), but not with dementia prevalence. Mediterranean and DASH scores were not associated with dementia burden. The Alternative Healthy Eating Index estimate was −1.14%, with q = 0.086 after correction, so it did not clear the threshold. Higher scores on the planetary index were also associated with lower land use (−11.39%) and greenhouse-gas emissions (−8.16%), while all four patterns were associated with greater water scarcity. The unit of analysis was the country, not the person.

**Links:**

- [AJCN — Dietary patterns, dementia burden, and environmental footprints](https://ajcn.nutrition.org/article/S0002-9165(26)00398-9/abstract)

**Commentary:** This is not a personal prescription that switching to a planetary-health diet will prevent dementia; the association is between country averages, prevalence did not move with it, and Mediterranean and DASH scores showed no dementia-burden association in the same data.
