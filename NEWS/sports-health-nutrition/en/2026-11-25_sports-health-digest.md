## I. Nutrition & Diet

### 1. Crossover RCT: whole-wheat bread cut postprandial glucose rise ~27% in adults with prediabetes, with microbiome-linked response variation (Nutrition)
**Summary:** A controlled-feeding randomized crossover trial in *Frontiers in Nutrition*, led by Ohio State University, enrolled 39 adults with prediabetes (mean BMI ~34.7). Fully controlled diets differed only by bread—whole wheat versus refined white—for 2 weeks each, separated by a 2-week washout. Versus the white-bread phase, whole wheat lowered OGTT postprandial Δglucose AUC₀₋₁₈₀ by ~27% (~4,092 vs 5,623 mg/dL·min; p=0.04), independent of body weight, inflammation, carbonyl stress, and fecal SCFAs; fasting glucose/insulin improved over time without a treatment effect. Alpha/beta diversity were unchanged, but whole wheat raised relative abundances of Agathobacter, Agathobaculum, and Lachnospiraceae NK4A136 group; baseline Lachnoclostridium and Lachnospiraceae NK4A136 predicted diet-dependent glycemic benefit. Moderate sample size, short intervention; microbial links are exploratory.

**Links:**

- [Frontiers in Nutrition — Whole wheat bread and microbiome-linked glycaemia](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/nutrition/articles/10.3389/fnut.2026.1907908/full)
- [Ohio State News — Gut microbiome and whole-grain benefits](https://news.osu.edu/gut-microbiome-may-shape-blood-sugar-benefits-of-whole-grains/)

**Commentary:** Swapping refined white bread for whole wheat remains a high-leverage move in prediabetes—and if whole grains barely move your glucose, do not dismiss the food class before discussing more personalized nutrition assessment with a clinician.

### 2. Three Nordic cohorts: higher childhood fruit and fish intake linked to lower later Crohn's disease risk (Nutrition)
**Summary:** *Gut* pooled three Scandinavian birth cohorts (n=111,999) with food-group questionnaires at ages 7–8 and 11–15, followed to roughly ages 20–25 (1,541,247 person-years; 653 IBD events). High versus low childhood fruit intake was associated with lower Crohn's disease risk (aHR 0.66, 95% CI 0.47–0.94) but higher ulcerative colitis risk (aHR 1.54, 95% CI 1.07–2.20); high childhood fish intake was also linked to lower Crohn's risk (aHR 0.65, 95% CI 0.44–0.97). Higher adolescent fruit intake tracked lower Crohn's risk (aHR 0.57); salty-snack associations with IBD weakened after fuller adjustment. Observational design cannot prove causation.

**Links:**

- [Gut — Childhood food groups and IBD risk](https://gut.bmj.com/content/early/2026/11/19/gutjnl-2026-339600)
- [University of Gothenburg — Diet in youth and IBD](https://www.gu.se/en/news/diet-in-youth-linked-to-inflammatory-bowel-disease)

**Commentary:** Keep prioritizing fruit and fish on kids' plates as a sensible long-horizon gut-health investment—and do not turn "eat more fruit" into a one-size IBD-prevention slogan given the divergent ulcerative colitis signal.

### 3. China expert consensus on dietary nutrition and weight management: control total intake, optimize structure, prioritize quality (Guidelines)
**Summary:** Xinhua reported release of the *Expert Consensus on Dietary Nutrition and Weight Management*, led by the China Nutrition and Health Food Association with nutrition and clinical experts, at the 2026 Special Food Conference. The consensus frames weight management across the life course around "control totals, optimize structure, emphasize quality," covering fat loss in overweight/obesity, weight maintenance, and healthy weight gain in underweight/sarcopenia; it centers dietary intervention, body-composition goals, individualized eating patterns, and avoiding crash diets or fad chasing; supervised use of weight-management nutrition foods is allowed, with long-term maintenance and anti-regain transition plans after goals are met. This is an industry-association expert consensus, not a binding national guideline.

**Links:**

- [Xinhua — Expert consensus on dietary nutrition and weight management](https://www.news.cn/food/20261125/f1a19e16137e4ffdae4d85ffbbfb0f05/c.html)

**Commentary:** Shift the target from "lose kilos on the scale" to "lose fat, keep muscle, and sustain the plan"—closer to this consensus than chasing the next viral diet.

### 4. ISPOR systematic review: oral nutrition supplements in older adults often show short-term benefits and favorable economics; long-term value unclear (Nutrition/Public health)
**Summary:** An ISPOR Nutrition Economics Special Interest Group report in *Value in Health* (news release Nov 25, 2026) reviewed 12 health-economic evaluations (2014–2024) of oral nutrition supplements (ONS) in older adults (mean ages mostly 63–88; largely high-income settings). Most horizons were ≤6 months; seven studies reported per-patient savings of about $127–$1,988 (2024 USD), mainly from lower healthcare use, while five reported cost increases of about $356–$672 driven by intervention costs. Nine studies favored main health/utilization outcomes; three were mixed. Authors generally concluded cost-effectiveness or cost-saving, but eight trials embedded ONS in multicomponent programs, long-term value remains uncertain, and industry funding was common.

**Links:**

- [ISPOR — ONS economic review news](https://www.ispor.org/heor-resources/news-top/news/2026/11/25/oral-nutrition-supplement-interventions-show-short-term-benefits-in-patient-outcomes-at-low-cost-for-older-adults)
- [Value in Health — Systematic review of ONS in older adults](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jval.2026.06.023)

**Commentary:** For older adults at malnutrition risk or across care transitions, discuss medically supervised ONS with a clinician or dietitian—this is not a green light for everyone to add a protein shake.

## II. Exercise & Training Science

### 5. Nonrandomized pilot: adults over 55 who self-selected low-to-moderate resistance and impact training matched most gains of high-intensity training (Training)
**Summary:** A University of Western Australia nonrandomized pilot in *Translational Exercise Biomedicine* (EurekAlert Nov 25, 2026) enrolled 40 community-dwelling adults over 55 (33 women, 7 men). After 4 weeks of low-to-moderate familiarization, participants self-selected high-intensity resistance and impact training (HIRIT) or continued low-to-moderate (L-MIRIT) for a total 12 weeks, twice weekly and supervised, with total work matched. Twenty-six entered adherence-based analysis; dropouts had higher baseline fat mass and lower relative leg-press strength. Both groups improved chest- and leg-press 1RM; only relative leg-press strength differed between groups. Balance-related measures improved in both. Authors note that equating total work often shrinks the apparent advantage of high intensity; small, nonrandomized, high attrition.

**Links:**

- [EurekAlert — Older adults and self-selected training intensity](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/1153413)

**Commentary:** Midlife strength work should prioritize adherence and sufficient total work—if high intensity is intolerable, lower the load and keep showing up rather than quitting.

### 6. Nature Communications: hepatokine AGP2 mediates exercise-driven bone anabolism via liver–bone crosstalk (Training/Mechanism)
**Summary:** Teams including Chongqing Medical University reported in *Nature Communications* (Nov 25, 2026) that exercise raises circulating hepatokine AGP2 in mice and humans. Hepatocyte-specific Agp2 knockout blunted exercise-induced bone formation, while hepatic AGP2 overexpression limited bone loss after ovariectomy. Mechanistically, exercise-induced lactate promoted hepatic Agp2 via histone H3K14 lactylation; secreted AGP2 bound Wnt co-receptor LRP5, activated Wnt/β-catenin, supported osteoblasts, and suppressed osteoclasts. Recombinant AGP2 also mitigated bone loss in mouse osteoporosis models. Human data mainly reflect circulating changes; therapeutic extrapolation remains preclinical.

**Links:**

- [Nature Communications — Hepatokine AGP2 and exercise-induced bone anabolism](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-79582-7)

**Commentary:** Bone protection is not only about calcium pills—regular metabolically stressful exercise may support bone via liver–bone signaling; AGP2 drugs are not a consumer option yet.

### 7. Nine-month trial analysis: fitness gains tied to fewer "adverse responder" HbA1c phenotypes in type 2 diabetes (Training/Clinical)
**Summary:** A *Scientific Reports* secondary analysis of a 9-month randomized exercise trial included 188 people with type 2 diabetes (HbA1c ≥6.5%). Exercise-arm residual HbA1c change classified adverse responders (ΔHbA1c >0.10%), non-responders (±0.09%), and responders (≤−0.10%) at ~38.8%, 16.5%, and 44.5%. Improvements in weight, BMI, waist, and fat measures raised odds of responder status versus control; increases in treadmill time or estimated METs raised responder odds ~1.93- and ~4.50-fold, and only fitness gains distinguished responders from adverse responders. Associations are observational, not proof of causation.

**Links:**

- [Scientific Reports — Fitness change and T2D exercise responder phenotypes](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-81529-3)

**Commentary:** Do not judge a diabetes exercise plan by HbA1c alone—if A1c is flat short-term, check walking tolerance or estimated METs; rising fitness may still mean you are benefiting.

## III. Sleep & Cognitive Health

### 8. Two cohorts: midlife short sleep plus obesity linked to ~1.5–1.6× higher dementia risk 10–23 years later (Sleep)
**Summary:** *Nature Progress Brain Health* (Nov 25, 2026) analyzed Whitehall II (n=4,769) and Finland's FPS cohort (n=23,215). Versus people with neither short sleep nor obesity, those with both had higher later dementia risk after excluding cases in the first 10 years (Whitehall II HR 1.61, 95% CI 1.14–2.27; FPS HR 1.50, 95% CI 1.03–2.18; pooled ~1.56) and lower cognition throughout follow-up; short sleep alone was not significantly associated with dementia. Baseline plasma proteins related to neuroaxonal injury, inflammation, and metabolism differed but did not explain the dementia link. Self-reported sleep and observational design limit causal inference.

**Links:**

- [Nature Progress Brain Health — Short sleep, obesity and dementia](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44559-026-00004-w)

**Commentary:** Treat midlife short sleep and excess weight as a joint risk package—fixing only sleep or only weight may miss the highest-risk group.

### 9. AI framework estimates OSA severity from ~10-minute physiologic snapshots; AUROC >0.91 across five cohorts (Sleep/Clinical tools)
**Summary:** USC-led researchers described LRIA (Long-Range Interdependencies Aware) analysis in *Nature Communications*: fractional-dynamics coupling features from ~10-minute polysomnography segments feed a deep-learning model that detects obstructive sleep apnea and grades severity. Validation across five heterogeneous cohorts (>35,000 participants) yielded AUROCs mostly above 0.91 and ~8%–18% gains versus comparison models. Authors stress retrospective analysis, no replacement for clinician judgment or gold-standard testing, and the need for prospective trials; sex-stratified analyses were not performed. MedicalXpress covered the work on Nov 25, 2026.

**Links:**

- [Nature Communications — LRIA for OSA prediction](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-76274-0)
- [MedicalXpress — 10-minute AI sleep apnea estimate](https://medicalxpress.com/news/2026-11-ai-framework-rapidly-apnea-severity.html)

**Commentary:** Snoring and daytime sleepiness still warrant formal sleep evaluation—tools like this are for faster triage, not home self-diagnosis that replaces care.

## IV. Public Health & Disease Prevention

### 10. WHO conditionally recommends single-dose oral zoliflodacin as an additional gonorrhoea option; ceftriaxone remains first line (Public health)
**Summary:** On Nov 25, 2026, WHO issued interim guidance conditionally recommending a single 3 g oral dose of zoliflodacin as an additional option for uncomplicated urogenital gonorrhoea in adolescents and adults. It does not cover pharyngeal or rectal infection or severe disease and is not recommended in pregnancy on current evidence. Intramuscular ceftriaxone 1 g remains first line; gepotidacin was not generally recommended but may be considered when first-line therapy cannot be used. The 2026 EGASP report from 10 countries detected ceftriaxone/cefixime resistance in four and azithromycin resistance in eight, with peak country-level resistant-isolate proportions around 16% (ceftriaxone/azithromycin) and 47% (cefixime).

**Links:**

- [WHO — Oral zoliflodacin for gonorrhoea](https://www.who.int/news/item/25-11-2026-who-recommends-new-oral-treatment-option-for-gonorrhoea-and-reports-new-data-on-antimicrobial-resistance)

**Commentary:** After possible exposure, seek timely testing and clinician-directed therapy—do not stockpile new oral agents; complete treatment, partner management, and follow-up matter more than the newest drug name.

### 11. NEJM Evidence: 112 U.S. pregnancy measles cases and 13 congenital measles cases in 2025–2026; ~98% lacked documented pre-pregnancy MMR (Public health)
**Summary:** A preliminary CDC analysis in *NEJM Evidence* counted 112 confirmed measles cases in pregnancy across 16 U.S. jurisdictions from Jan 1, 2025, to Sep 28, 2026 (~96% linked to large outbreaks) and 13 congenital measles cases; 26 women had non–labor-related hospitalization and 11 had pneumonia. Almost all (98%; 110/112) had no pre-pregnancy MMR or unknown vaccination status. A Nov 25, 2026 Johns Hopkins Public Health On Call discussion stressed that MMR is not indicated during pregnancy; protection relies on pre-pregnancy vaccination and high community coverage, with immunoglobulin considered after exposure. Data remain preliminary with incomplete follow-up.

**Links:**

- [NEJM Evidence — Measles in pregnancy and congenital measles](https://evidence.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/EVIDpha2600416)
- [Johns Hopkins Bloomberg School — Measles in pregnant women and newborns](https://publichealth.jhu.edu/2026/amid-loss-of-us-elimination-status-new-concerns-about-congenital-measles)

**Commentary:** Confirm MMR immunity before pregnancy—the cheapest lever for mother and newborn; during pregnancy, rely on household vaccination and exposure control, not live-virus catch-up shots.

## V. Food Safety & Evidence Check

### 12. Modeling study: most store cinnamon was below New York spice lead action levels, but high daily use plus other lead sources can raise toddler exposure (Food safety)
**Summary:** University at Buffalo researchers in *Journal of Xenobiotics* tested 20 ground cinnamon products; 19 had detectable lead (LOD 0.04 μg/g), one >1 μg/g and two >0.6 μg/g, with most below New York's current spice action level. IEUBK modeling for ages 3–5 across intakes (~0.24–3.8 g/day), concentrations, and absorption rates showed higher estimated blood lead with heavier use; servings needed to exceed FDA's interim dietary lead reference (2.2 μg/day) ranged from about one-eighth teaspoon to ~two teaspoons, with worse estimates when soil/water/air lead were included. Late-November institutional coverage stressed moderation and reducing other lead sources rather than discarding all cinnamon.

**Links:**

- [Journal of Xenobiotics — Lead in cinnamon and child BLLs](https://doi.org/10.3390/jox16070216)
- [University at Buffalo — Cinnamon lead and kids](https://www.buffalo.edu/news/releases/2026/11/public-health-cinnamon-lead-study.html)

**Commentary:** No need to dump the spice jar—but skip stacking cinnamon-heavy sweets for toddlers daily, and prioritize paint chips, plumbing, and unverified spice sources over a seasonal pinch.
