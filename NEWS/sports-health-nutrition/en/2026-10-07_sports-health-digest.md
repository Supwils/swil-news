## I. Nutrition & Diet

### 1. Frontiers in Microbiology: after 84 days, a high-fiber diet had lower adjusted HbA1c and BMI than metformin alone in newly diagnosed type 2 diabetes with overweight (Nutrition)
**Summary:** GUT2DF, by Yan Li, Yalei Liu, Yiqi Chen, Huijuan Yuan, Chenhong Zhang, and colleagues, appeared in Frontiers in Microbiology on October 2, 2026, and was reported by News-Medical on October 7. At Henan Provincial People's Hospital, this single-center, open-label, head-to-head trial randomized 48 adults aged 18–70 with newly diagnosed type 2 diabetes (HbA1c from 6.5% to below 9.0%) and a body mass index of at least 24 to 84 days of a high-fiber diet without glucose-lowering drugs (formulas including inulin and resistant dextrin; 36 assigned) or to metformin alone (12 assigned); 46 completed. At day 84, baseline-adjusted mean HbA1c was 6.32% (95% CI 6.11–6.53) with fiber and 6.74% (6.39–7.09) with metformin (p = 0.042); BMI was 28.42 versus 30.22 kg/m² (p < 0.001). The fiber group had already shifted microbiota by day 28, with enrichment of Bifidobacterium and Lactobacillus genomes and depletion of Bilophila wadsworthia; short-chain fatty acid concentrations did not differ between groups at matched time points, while 25 of 34 people in the fiber group increased fecal acetate and those people had larger HbA1c drops at days 28 and 56. The registry number is ChiCTR-INR-17011752. The authors note that the metformin arm is small, so between-group estimates are imprecise, and that GLP-1 was not measured.

**Links:**

- [Frontiers in Microbiology — High-fiber diet versus metformin](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/microbiology/articles/10.3389/fmicb.2026.1972983/full)
- [News-Medical — October 7 trial report](https://www.news-medical.net/news/20261007/High-fiber-diet-beats-metformin-for-blood-sugar-and-weight-in-trial.aspx)

**Commentary:** Adults with newly diagnosed type 2 diabetes and overweight who are not yet on medication can discuss a soluble-plus-insoluble fiber pattern they can actually keep; an open-label trial with 12 people on metformin does not make 6.32% versus 6.74% a reason to stop the drug on one's own.

### 2. European Journal of Nutrition: in Finnish adults, part of the planetary-health-diet link with a smaller waist ran through fatty-acid unsaturation and acetate (Nutrition)
**Summary:** Tiina Suikki, Satu Männistö, and colleagues at the Finnish Institute for Health and Welfare published an observational mediation analysis in the European Journal of Nutrition on October 7. The main sample pooled FinHealth 2017 and DILGOM 2007/2014 (9,225 adults, 54% women, mean age 54); an independent replication pooled FINRISK 2012 and Health 2000/2011 (10,242 adults). Diet came from a food-frequency questionnaire scored as a Finnish-adapted Planetary Health Diet (0–13 points), 158 serum metabolites were quantified by NMR, and weight and waist were measured at baseline and over follow-up of up to about seven years. In both sexes, seven metabolites partly mediated the inverse association between the score and waist circumference: acetate (7.6% of the association in men and 3.1% in women), degree of fatty-acid unsaturation (26.0%/23.1%), and the ratios to total fatty acids of polyunsaturated fat (35.6%/46.5%), linoleic acid (31.4%/49.9%), saturated fat (12.1%/13.0%), DHA (5.5%/9.6%), and omega-3 fat (4.0%/9.8%). This is statistical mediation in cohorts, not a randomized test of the diet.

**Links:**

- [European Journal of Nutrition — Planetary health diet, serum metabolites, and weight](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00394-026-04115-3)

**Commentary:** For waist circumference, raising whole grains and fiber while shifting fat toward unsaturated sources is the practical reading of this score; the mediation percentages describe part of a statistical association, not a promised change in centimeters.

### 3. Nature Communications: neonatal overfeeding in mice brought earlier sarcopenia and metabolic disease, and fecal transplant carried the muscle phenotype (Nutrition)
**Summary:** Nature Communications published a mouse study on October 7. Neonatal overfeeding is described as a developmental insult that accelerated age-related sarcopenia in both sexes, shortened lifespan, and worsened late-life muscle atrophy and metabolic dysfunction. Overfeeding disrupted goblet-cell development, left a persistent mucus-barrier defect, and reduced Akkermansia muciniphila colonization during aging. Fecal microbiota transplantation transferred the sarcopenic phenotype; loss of A. muciniphila reduced acetate and impaired muscle mitochondria at least partly through suppressed GPR43 signaling. Restoring A. muciniphila or supplementing acetate improved mitochondrial homeostasis and muscle performance in aged mice. The abstract does not give a human infant-feeding dose.

**Links:**

- [Nature Communications — Neonatal overfeeding and age-related sarcopenia](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-78216-2)

**Commentary:** Excess feeding in infancy is a research clue to later muscle health that, for now, stops at mice; pediatric feeding guidance still stands, and this mechanism paper is not a reason to give children acetate or a probiotic.

## II. Exercise & Training Science

### 4. Archives of Public Health: Korean adults with adequate health literacy had 1.31-fold odds of meeting both aerobic and muscle-strengthening guidelines (Training)
**Summary:** Eun Jung Bae and Heesook Son published a cross-sectional analysis in Archives of Public Health on October 7, using 5,384 adults aged 19 or older in the 2023 Korea National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey. Combined adherence meant meeting both the aerobic and the muscle-strengthening parts of World Health Organization guidance. Health literacy was a 10-item scale, split into adequate and inadequate. Overall, 18.7% met both guidelines and 61.9% had adequate literacy. After adjustment for sociodemographic, health, behavioral, and psychological factors, adequate literacy was associated with combined adherence at an odds ratio of 1.31 (95% CI 1.09–1.58). One survey cannot show that teaching literacy causes people to start training. The ethics exemption number is P01-202508-01-037.

**Links:**

- [Archives of Public Health — Health literacy and combined activity guidelines](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13690-026-02087-8)

**Commentary:** People who already do cardio and still skip strength work can check whether they know what weekly muscle-strengthening actually requires; in this Korean sample fewer than one in five met both parts, and understanding the guideline was only one correlate.

### 5. International Journal of Behavioral Nutrition and Physical Activity: across 29 momentary-assessment studies, friend presence tracked with activity more consistently than family presence (Training)
**Summary:** Tyler Prochnow, Yunlin Zhou, Parinika Joshi, and Veronika Croan at Texas A&M University published a systematic review on October 7 in the International Journal of Behavioral Nutrition and Physical Activity. Searches of PubMed, PsycINFO, Web of Science, and SPORTDiscus/CINAHL returned 3,731 records; 76 full texts were reviewed and 29 studies were included if they used ecological momentary assessment to link social context with physical activity. Social support was positively associated with activity. Peer and friend presence was more consistently associated with activity than family presence. Within-person and between-person effects often diverged: in some samples people were more active in moments when they were alone, yet people who spent more time alone overall were less active on average. Diverse social ties and better interaction quality looked promising, while weight-management-focused support and social barriers did not show a facilitative link. Quality was rated with the NHLBI tool for observational studies. Funding included K01HL169818.

**Links:**

- [International Journal of Behavioral Nutrition and Physical Activity — Social context and physical activity](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12966-026-01988-9)

**Commentary:** Scheduling movement with a friend who will actually move fits these 29 studies better than relying only on family reminders; being busier in a lone moment and less active across a lonely week means the useful window is the week, not a single hour.

## III. Sleep & Mental Health

### 6. Environment & Health: one night at 30°C left college students about 53 minutes shorter on sleep and 27.80% higher on state anxiety (Sleep)
**Summary:** A randomized crossover trial by Jintao Liu, Cunrui Huang, Yuefang Li, Yarui Fan, and Li Ling was posted in Environment & Health on September 29, 2026, and reported by News-Medical on October 7. In July–August 2025, 41 healthy college students aged 18–25 each completed eight hours of daytime heat at 32°C, nighttime heat at 30°C, and a 26°C thermoneutral control, with humidity held at 65% and at least a week between the heat phases. Versus control, state-anxiety scores were 24.71% higher after daytime heat (95% CI 13.32%–36.11%) and 27.80% higher after nighttime heat (17.32%–38.29%). Nighttime sleep duration was 275.98 ± 91.21 minutes versus 329.00 ± 70.59 minutes (P = 0.004), and sleep-quality scores were 66.46 ± 11.55 versus 74.80 ± 8.66 (P < 0.001). Nighttime heat also came with interleukin-1β up 7.74%, malondialdehyde up 34.71%, cortisol up 8.46%, and BDNF down 18.13%; malondialdehyde and cortisol mediated 14.42% and 6.88% of the nighttime heat–anxiety association. Daytime heat mainly tracked with higher malondialdehyde and oxidized LDL. The sample was young and healthy, and the exposure was a single night.

**Links:**

- [Environment & Health — Daytime and nighttime heat and anxiety](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/envhealth.6c00392)
- [News-Medical — October 7 trial report](https://www.news-medical.net/news/20261007/A-hot-night-changed-sleep-anxiety-and-stress-markers.aspx)

**Commentary:** On a hot night, getting the bedroom closer to 26°C is the direct lever before explaining mood with an anxiety score; this was one night in 41 college students and does not extend to a multi-day heat wave or to people who already sleep poorly.

### 7. Scientific Reports: in a Chinese midlife and older cohort, loneliness plus restless sleep had an odds ratio of 1.53 for incident possible or confirmed sarcopenia (Sleep)
**Summary:** Tian Wang and Yongwei Song at Luoyang Orthopedic Hospital of Henan Province, with Rongxing Shi at China-Japan Friendship Hospital and colleagues, published a prospective analysis in Scientific Reports on October 7. Using CHARLS waves from 2011 and 2015, the unweighted complete-case sample free of possible or confirmed sarcopenia at baseline was 6,614 people. Loneliness and restless sleep were each one item from the CES-D-10. During follow-up, 1,067 people developed the composite outcome. In the most adjusted logistic model, the odds ratio was 1.38 for loneliness (95% CI 1.18–1.62) and 1.20 for restless sleep (1.03–1.38). Versus neither symptom, loneliness only was 1.32 (1.02–1.71), restless sleep only was 1.13 (0.95–1.34), and both symptoms were 1.53 (1.26–1.87). A modified Poisson model gave a risk ratio of 1.34 (1.16–1.54) for both symptoms, and multiplicative interaction was not supported. The authors describe the findings as observational. CHARLS ethics numbers are IRB00001052-11015 and IRB00001052-11014.

**Links:**

- [Scientific Reports — Loneliness, restless sleep, and incident sarcopenia](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-73578-5)

**Commentary:** When a midlife or older visit already covers strength and gait, it is worth asking whether loneliness and restless sleep are both present; two single-item symptoms are a risk marker, not a sarcopenia test, and they do not show that fixing sleep preserves muscle.

## IV. Public Health & Disease Prevention

### 8. World Health Organization: first guidelines on child and adolescent obesity put diet, activity, and sleep at the base of care (Guideline)
**Summary:** On October 7 the World Health Organization released its first guideline on the integrated management of obesity in children and its first guideline for adolescents, and held a launch the same day. The news release says that in 2024, 170 million children and adolescents aged 5–19 were living with obesity, 70 million of them aged 5–9 and 100 million aged 10–19, and that obesity prevalence in this age group rose from 2% in 1990 to 8%. For children and adolescents who already have obesity, WHO strongly recommends structured dietary, physical-activity, and behaviour-change interventions, alone or inside a multimodal programme. Digital tools are conditionally recommended, with a parent or caregiver supervising. Medicines, bariatric surgery, and weight-loss devices are not recommended at ages 0–9. For ages 10–19, approved medicines may be considered only after a supervised multimodal lifestyle programme has not reached the intended results, and surgery only under strict conditions for severe obesity. Care is meant to improve health, functioning, and well-being, and to address anxiety, depression, and stigma together with weight. The launch page separately says an estimated 400 million children and adolescents aged 5–19 were overweight in 2024, including 170 million with obesity.

**Links:**

- [WHO — October 7 news release on child and adolescent obesity guidelines](https://www.who.int/news/item/07-10-2026-who-issues-first-global-guidelines-on-child-and-adolescent-obesity)
- [WHO — Guideline on obesity in adolescents](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240123878)

**Commentary:** Families of a child or adolescent with obesity can start with a pediatric or family clinician on eating, activity, and sleep that a caregiver can support; medicines and surgery enter the guideline only at specified ages and after a lifestyle programme.

### 9. World Health Organization: a 2026–2035 roadmap on hypertensive disorders of pregnancy targets the gap in blood-pressure care and medicines (Public health)
**Summary:** In the October 7 media briefing, the WHO Director-General said WHO and partners were launching a global roadmap on hypertensive disorders of pregnancy, including pre-eclampsia. The publication is titled Global roadmap for hypertensive disorders of pregnancy from 2026 to 2035 and beyond. The briefing says hypertensive disorders account for about 16% of maternal deaths, about 42,000 deaths, and more than 500,000 stillbirths and newborn deaths. In many countries women still lack reliable blood-pressure measurement, testing, and essential medicines, and weak referral, shortages of trained workers, and limited emergency obstetric care delay treatment. The roadmap is aimed at the innovation gap between the scale of the problem and the availability of medicines to prevent or treat pre-eclampsia, and at coordinated investment so proven interventions and later innovations reach the women and babies who need them. WHO's existing fact sheet still lists calcium supplementation in pregnancy where dietary calcium is low, low-dose aspirin for women at high risk, and magnesium sulfate to prevent eclampsia.

**Links:**

- [WHO — Director-General media briefing, 7 October 2026](https://www.who.int/news-room/speeches/item/who-director-general-s-opening-remarks-at-the-media-briefing---7-october-2026)
- [WHO — Global roadmap on hypertensive disorders of pregnancy](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240125483)

**Commentary:** After 20 weeks of pregnancy, each visit should include a blood-pressure reading, and headache, visual change, or upper abdominal pain belongs with obstetric care the same day; calcium, aspirin, and magnesium sulfate remain clinical decisions, and the roadmap is about access rather than self-directed dosing.

## V. Sports Medicine

### 10. Scientific Reports: in a U.S. claims cohort, tobacco-use disorder tracked with meniscus tears and ACL injuries (Sports medicine)
**Summary:** Ernesto Joubran, Craig Dent, Leah Ilyaev, Halford Warlick, Andrew Ardeljan, and Vincent Gallicchio published a retrospective matched cohort in Scientific Reports on October 7. From a Humana claims database of more than 8 million patients in 2007–2017, they matched 3,310,668 patients with and without tobacco-use disorder 1:1 on age and comorbidities. Meniscus-tear incidence was 4.25% among tobacco users and 3.97% among matches (OR 1.07, 95% CI 1.06–1.09); ACL injury was 0.48% versus 0.42% (OR 1.15, 1.11–1.19). Among people with a meniscal injury, the odds ratio for later surgical repair was 1.18 (1.07–1.31, p = 0.001); for ACL reconstruction it was 1.07 (0.99–1.16, p = 0.09). Postoperative length of stay was also longer in tobacco users; the abstract does not give the number of days. The authors limit the claims design to association. The work had no specific funding.

**Links:**

- [Scientific Reports — Tobacco use and meniscus and ACL injury](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-66328-0)

**Commentary:** People who still use tobacco and who cut or jump can put quitting inside the same conversation as knee-injury risk; the absolute gaps are about 0.28 extra meniscus tears and 0.06 extra ACL injuries per 100 people, from claims associations in 2007–2017.
