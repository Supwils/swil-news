## I. Nutrition & Diet

### 1. UK Biobank: Closer EAT-Lancet “planetary health” eating linked to ~15%–17% lower type 2 diabetes risk (nutrition/metabolism)
**Summary:** A UK Biobank cohort analysis in *Frontiers in Nutrition*, covered by Yahoo Health on November 14, 2026, included 155,413 participants without baseline type 2 diabetes who had dietary recalls and biological-aging markers. Adherence to a plant-forward EAT-Lancet pattern (limiting added sugar and red/processed meat) was scored with Stubbendorff and Knuppel indices over a median ~11.6 years of follow-up. In fully adjusted models, the highest versus lowest quartile had HRs of about 0.85 (Stubbendorff) and 0.83 (Knuppel)—roughly 15%–17% lower relative T2D risk. Exploratory mediation suggested biological-age acceleration statistically accounted for ~33%–44% of the diet–diabetes association. Observational design cannot prove causation; the cohort is mostly white UK adults, limiting generalizability.

**Links:**

- [Frontiers in Nutrition — EAT-Lancet diet, biological aging, and type 2 diabetes](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/nutrition/articles/10.3389/fnut.2026.1951780/full)
- [Yahoo Health — Plant-forward eating and diabetes risk (November 14)](https://health.yahoo.com/conditions/endocrine/diabetes/articles/155k-person-study-found-plant-063300176.html)

**Commentary:** Practical takeaway: more vegetables, fruits, whole grains, legumes, and nuts, and less red/processed meat and added sugar—do not equate “planetary diet” with mandatory veganism or treat an association as proven prevention.

### 2. CLHLS 10-year multistate analysis: Higher dietary diversity linked to lower intensity of later robust-to-prefrail transitions (nutrition/aging)
**Summary:** Investigators from Guangzhou University of Chinese Medicine and colleagues analyzed Chinese Longitudinal Healthy Longevity Survey (CLHLS) waves 2008–2018 in *Frontiers in Nutrition*, with eBioTrade coverage on November 14, 2026. The primary sample comprised 22,804 person-periods from 13,427 older adults; a 0–18 dietary diversity score (DDS) covered nine food groups including fruit, vegetables, legumes, meat, fish, and eggs. In previous-wave models, each 2-point higher DDS associated with lower robust-to-prefrail transition intensity (IR 0.892, 95% CI 0.835–0.953); interval-start DDS in the same 9,464 intervals was near null (IR 0.982). Model-standardized age-65 contrasts of DDS 11 versus 7 suggested about +0.45 robust years, while differences in total and non-frail years had uncertainty intervals that could include zero. Observational results do not prove that changing diet prevents frailty.

**Links:**

- [Frontiers in Nutrition — Dietary diversity and frailty transitions (doi:10.3389/fnut.2026.2006988)](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/nutrition/articles/10.3389/fnut.2026.2006988/full)
- [eBioTrade — CLHLS dietary diversity and frailty (November 14)](https://www.ebiotrade.com/newsf/2026-11/20261114000105770.htm)

**Commentary:** For older adults, aiming for several real food groups daily is more actionable than chasing a single “anti-frailty” supplement—diversity is a habit metric, not a precision prescription.

### 3. Mouse study: Western high-fat diet can imprint chromatin “memory” in intestinal stem cells that reacts more strongly on re-exposure (nutrition/mechanism)
**Summary:** An Arizona State University team reports in *Stem Cell Reports* that mice on a Western-style high-fat diet for about six months gained weight, expanded and accelerated intestinal stem cells, and—when genetically predisposed—grew more and larger tumors. Chromatin accessibility was broadly remodeled; after return to a lower-fat diet, cell behavior normalized but roughly 18% of differential accessible regions persisted at four weeks. Re-exposure to high fat produced faster, stronger stem-cell responses and heavier tumor burden than first-time exposure. Changes required PPARδ/α nuclear receptors but not necessarily their classic metabolic transcriptional targets. This is animal epigenetic-mechanism evidence—not a quantified human colon-cancer risk from dietary fat.

**Links:**

- [PMC — Diet-induced chromatin states and intestinal stem-cell memory](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12934732/)
- [ISSCR — High-fat diet and colon-cancer risk mechanism brief](https://www.isscr.org/isscr-news/high-fat-diet-may-increase-colon-cancer-risk-by-changing-intestinal-stem-cells)

**Commentary:** For everyday eating, limit chronic Western ultra-processed high-fat patterns; do not translate mouse chromatin “memory” into an extreme fat-elimination fad.

## II. Exercise & Training Science

### 4. Annals of Internal Medicine SUPER-Knee: After ACL reconstruction with lingering symptoms, 4 months of supervised exercise plus education improves outcomes without MRI cartilage worsening (training/sports medicine)
**Summary:** La Trobe University’s SUPER-Knee RCT randomized 184 adults aged 18–40 with persistent knee problems 9–36 months after ACL reconstruction to a 4-month twice-weekly physiotherapist-supervised progressive strength and neuromuscular program with education (SUPER) or a single education session plus self-directed resources; results appear in *Annals of Internal Medicine*, with university and ScienceDaily coverage on November 14, 2026. At 4 months, KOOS₄ improved more with SUPER (~+14.1 vs ~+9.4; adjusted mean difference ~4.8, 95% CI 1.4–8.2); patient-rated pain “success” was ~73% vs ~40%, with greater thigh-strength gains. Cartilage thickness/composition on MRI did not differ between groups at 4 or 12 months; between-group symptom gaps narrowed by 12 months. Participants were unblinded; long-term osteoarthritis prevention remains unproven.

**Links:**

- [Annals of Internal Medicine — SUPER-Knee randomized trial](https://www.acpjournals.org/doi/10.7326/ANNALS-26-02460)
- [La Trobe — Exercise and younger-onset knee osteoarthritis risk narrative (release)](https://www.latrobe.edu.au/news/articles/2026/release/exercise-averts-younger-onset-knee-osteoarthritis)
- [ScienceDaily — Exercise after ACL surgery and early osteoarthritis (November 14)](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/11/261112025538.htm)

**Commentary:** If knee symptoms persist past the usual post-ACL rehab window, prioritize supervised progressive loading rather than long-term rest driven by fear of “wearing out” cartilage.

### 5. Meta-analysis: For patellofemoral pain, “train outside the knee” is not on average better than knee-focused exercise (training/rehab)
**Summary:** Beijing Sport University investigators systematically reviewed and meta-analyzed randomized trials comparing non-knee-focused training (hip/core/distal emphasis) with knee-focused training for patellofemoral pain; the paper in *Frontiers in Medicine* was covered by Scienmag/Bioengineer around November 14, 2026. Thirteen trials (877 participants) were included; 11 entered quantitative synthesis. At end of the randomized contrast, pooled pain mean difference was ~0.02 on a 0–10 scale (95% CI −0.65 to 0.68; I²≈83%; very-low certainty); function effect size was g≈0.04 (95% CI −0.05 to 0.13). Authors stress that near-zero averages do not prove equivalence and that long-term outcomes were not assessed.

**Links:**

- [Frontiers in Medicine — Non-knee-focused vs knee-focused exercise for patellofemoral pain](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/medicine/articles/10.3389/fmed.2026.1954976/full)
- [Scienmag — No average advantage for non-knee focus (around November 14)](https://scienmag.com/exercise-outside-knee-shows-similar-results-to-knee-work-for-patellofemoral-pain/)

**Commentary:** Do not fetishize “glutes only” or “quads only” slogans for kneecap pain—dose, supervision, and movement quality usually matter more than the anatomical marketing label.

## III. Public Health & Disease Prevention

### 6. WHO issues first guidelines on type 1 diabetes in adults, including low-resource and humanitarian settings (guidelines/public health)
**Summary:** On World Diabetes Day 2026 (November 14), WHO released its first guidelines on diagnosing and managing type 1 diabetes in adults, with companion guidance on insulin use across settings. About 9.5 million people lived with type 1 diabetes globally in 2025, with ~500,000 new diagnoses that year; adult-onset cases are rising. WHO recommends multiple daily insulin injections with doses adjusted to blood glucose; human insulin is an appropriate, generally lower-cost option, with analogues usable when needed. Regular monitoring is advised (clinic HbA1c; home fingerstick or continuous glucose monitoring; urine glucose when blood testing is unavailable). Guidance supports the 2030 target of universal affordable insulin and self-monitoring access.

**Links:**

- [WHO — First guidelines on type 1 diabetes for adults (November 14)](https://www.who.int/news/item/14-11-2026-who-issues-first-guidelines-on-type-1-diabetes-for-adults)
- [WHO — Insulin use for type 1 diabetes in adults, including emergency and humanitarian settings](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240126244)

**Commentary:** For patients and primary systems, the core is a workable path to insulin plus glucose checks plus dose adjustment—not defaulting first to the newest pump or costliest analogue.

## IV. Sleep & Mental Health

### 8. TemPredict cohort: Lower wearable-derived sleep regularity tracks with worse depression, anxiety, and loneliness symptoms (sleep/mental health)
**Summary:** UCSF and UC San Diego investigators analyzed wearable sleep data from 22,894 TemPredict adults (mean ~154 days each), computed a Sleep Regularity Index (SRI), and compared it with monthly self-reported depression, anxiety, and loneliness; the paper published November 12, 2026, in *Scientific Reports*, with MedicalXpress and other coverage on November 14. In longer-window analyses, mean SRI was ~84 with few/no depression symptoms versus ~75 with severe depression and ~79 with severe anxiety; elevated loneliness also aligned with less regular schedules. Each 5-point SRI decrease associated with higher odds of worse symptom categories (media summaries cite roughly +46% for severe depression and +29% for severe anxiety), persisting after adjustment for age, sex, and average sleep duration. Findings concern symptom scores, not clinician diagnoses; causality may be bidirectional, and no trial yet shows that regularizing sleep treats depression.

**Links:**

- [Scientific Reports — Wearable sleep regularity and mental-health symptoms](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-77545-y)
- [MedicalXpress — Erratic sleep and mental health (coverage)](https://medicalxpress.com/news/2026-11-erratic-mental-health.html)

**Commentary:** Beyond “hours of sleep,” stabilize bedtime and wake time; for heavy mood symptoms, schedule regularity is an adjunct—not a substitute for clinical care.

## V. Evidence & Clarifications

### 9. PrEggNut (NEJM): High maternal egg and peanut intake in pregnancy and lactation did not cut infant food allergy at age 1 (evidence/allergy)
**Summary:** Australia’s multicenter PrEggNut randomized trial assigned 2,137 pregnant women whose unborn child had ≥2 biologic relatives with medically diagnosed allergic disease 1:1 to a high egg–peanut diet (≥6 eggs and 60 peanuts/week) or a standard diet (≤3 eggs and ≤30 peanuts/week) from before 23 weeks’ gestation through ~4 months of lactation; results in the *New England Journal of Medicine* continue to be discussed by Physicians Weekly and others. IgE-mediated egg or peanut allergy at age 1 was 7.8% (high) versus 8.4% (standard) (RR 0.93, 95% CI 0.69–1.26, P=0.65), with similar safety. Conclusion: high versus low maternal egg/peanut intake did not significantly reduce infant allergy risk by one year.

**Links:**

- [NEJM — Trial of maternal high egg and peanut diet to reduce infant allergy](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2605659)
- [Physicians Weekly — High maternal intake failed to reduce infant allergy](https://www.physiciansweekly.com/post/high-maternal-egg-peanut-intake-fails-to-reduce-infant-allergy-risk)

**Commentary:** High-allergy-risk families should not treat “eat lots of peanut in pregnancy” as prevention; follow infant complementary-feeding guidance for timely allergen introduction instead of maternal mega-dosing.

### 10. ACS Omega: Retrospective screen of 2014–2017 fish-oil supplements finds organohalogen coexposure; six products could exceed current dl-PCB intake at label doses (evidence/supplement safety)
**Summary:** Screening of 47 omega-3 fish-oil supplements purchased in Brazil and abroad in 2014–2017, published online November 12, 2026, in *ACS Omega*, quantified PCBs (including dioxin-like congeners), PBDEs, natural methoxylated PBDEs (MeO-BDEs), and total mercury. MeO-BDEs were found in every product and typically dominated the organobromine burden. Reassessed under current WHO TEFs and the 2026 EFSA tolerable intake for dioxins/dl-PCBs (0.6 pg TEQ kg⁻¹ week⁻¹), a quantified-only lower-bound screen flagged six products that could exceed current dl-PCB tolerable intake at label doses (up to ~19-fold), though signals often rested on a single congener and need confirmatory analysis. Authors state the data describe a historical market, make no claim about products sold today, and note that all products bearing contaminant-absence statements still contained quantifiable contaminants (statement scopes were incompletely recorded).

**Links:**

- [ACS Omega — Regulated and unregulated organohalogens in omega-3 fish-oil supplements](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/acsomega.6c10079)

**Commentary:** Prefer fish oils with third-party contaminant testing and batch traceability over “purity” packaging claims; historical exceedances do not prove today’s shelf is uniformly unsafe, but supplements are not risk-free foods.

### 11. Viral claim that water gargling cuts respiratory infections 36%: recycled 2005 Japanese RCT with limited replication (evidence clarification)
**Summary:** Outlets including the *New York Post* on November 14, 2026, recirculated the claim that gargling with plain water three times daily cuts respiratory-infection risk by about 36%. That figure comes from a 2002–2003 winter RCT of 387 healthy Japanese adults published in the *American Journal of Preventive Medicine*: water gargling versus usual care had an incidence rate ratio of about 0.64; povidone-iodine gargling was not significantly better. Later reviews note the finding has not been reliably replicated in other populations, and some student trials found no significant reduction in clinical or lab-confirmed upper respiratory infection. This is seasonal media recycling of old evidence—not a 2026 definitive discovery.

**Links:**

- [NY Post — Gargling and infection-risk coverage (November 14)](https://nypost.com/2026/11/14/health/could-easy-daily-habit-slash-risk-of-respiratory-infection-by-36/)
- [American Journal of Preventive Medicine — Gargling to prevent URTIs (2005)](https://www.ajpmonline.org/article/S0749-3797(05)00258-8/abstract)

**Commentary:** Low-cost water gargling is harmless to try as hygiene, but do not brand it a cold-prevention miracle—handwashing, vaccination, and rest remain the firmer evidence base.
