## I. Nutrition & Diet

### 1. Swedish national surveys: UPF calorie share is classification-sensitive, yet higher intake tracks poorer diet quality (Nutrition)
**Summary:** University of Gothenburg and the Swedish Food Agency analyzed three national dietary surveys (about 1,797 adults, 3,099 adolescents, and 1,820 young children) in *Nutrition Journal*; institutional and health media followed recently. Under the primary Nova scenario, ultra-processed foods supplied about 45% of adults’ energy, 52% of adolescents’, and 46% of young children’s; excluding ambiguous items cut estimates to roughly 23%–29%. Higher UPF share was linked to less favorable diet-quality indicators; in adults, each 10 energy-percent increment corresponded to about 0.61% higher BMI and a higher overweight/obesity prevalence ratio, with no robust links in younger groups. Observational design and Nova boundary sensitivity mean processing itself is not proven to cause weight gain.

**Links:**

- [Nutrition Journal — UPF intake, diet quality, and weight status in Sweden](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12937-026-01408-5)
- [University of Gothenburg — news release](https://www.gu.se/en/news/poorer-diet-with-a-higher-proportion-of-ultra-processed-food)

**Commentary:** Cut packaged snacks, sugary drinks, and processed meats on nutrition quality first—do not treat a single UPF percentage as destiny, because the number can nearly halve when classification rules tighten.

### 2. Three national cohorts: plant-protein density shows the most consistent inverse link with incident sarcopenic obesity (Nutrition / Aging)
**Summary:** A *Frontiers in Nutrition* analysis of CHARLS (n≈6,395), ELSA (n≈4,880), and HRS (n≈3,900) midlife/older adults was detailed by Chinese science media on Nov 7, 2026. Among participants free of sarcopenic obesity (SO) at baseline, about 10.1%, 7.1%, and 7.0% developed SO during follow-up. Fully adjusted models showed a pooled total-protein HR≈0.87, but the most consistent inverse association was for plant protein (pooled HR≈0.86, I²≈3%), with no independent animal-protein signal; the plant-protein link was largely confined to people with central obesity. Isocaloric replacement of 5% energy from animal with plant protein yielded a pooled HR≈0.66. Observational residual confounding and reverse causation (baseline SO predicted later protein decline) mean this is not a prescription to drop all animal foods.

**Links:**

- [Ebiotrade — plant protein and sarcopenic obesity across three cohorts](https://www.ebiotrade.com/newsf/2026-11/20261107000106945.htm)

**Commentary:** Adults with larger waistlines can make beans, whole grains, and nuts a daily protein base alongside resistance training—do not expect a powder alone to fix sarcopenic obesity risk.

### 3. *Chinese Medical Journal*: higher dietary lecithin intake associated with lower odds of mild cognitive impairment in older adults (Nutrition / Cognition)
**Summary:** A cross-sectional study from Capital Medical University, Qingdao University, and collaborators enrolled 1,917 adults aged ≥60 years across three Chinese cities, splitting dietary lecithin at the questionnaire median (~88 mg/day); Health Chosun covered the findings on Nov 7, 2026, with the full paper in *Chinese Medical Journal*/PMC. About 53.4% met mild cognitive impairment (MCI) criteria; high versus low lecithin intake had an MCI odds ratio ≈0.80 (95% CI 0.64–0.99) and ~0.5-point higher MoCA scores. A small lipidomics subsample suggested lower total phosphatidylcholine in MCI. Cross-sectional FFQ recall bias and no supplement arm mean causality and dementia prevention are unproven.

**Links:**

- [PMC — dietary lecithin, MCI, and cognitive performance](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13626688/)
- [Health Chosun — Nov 7 report](https://health.chosun.com/english/site/data/html_dir/2026/11/07/2026110700736.html)

**Commentary:** Prefer eggs and soy foods for lecithin before buying pills; cognitive concerns still deserve checks on hearing, sleep, blood pressure, glucose, and social engagement.

## II. Exercise & Training Science

### 4. *BMJ Open Sport & Exercise Medicine* RCT: isometric wall-squats lower blood pressure, with a maintenance-dose threshold (Training / Blood pressure)
**Summary:** One hundred sedentary adults with normal to high-normal blood pressure completed 4 weeks of home-based isometric wall-squats three times weekly (four ≈2-minute holds with seated rest), cutting systolic BP by about 9.5 mmHg on average, then were randomized to 3×, 2×, 1×, or 0× weekly sessions or control for another 4 weeks; Prevention and other outlets summarized the work on Nov 7, 2026. At week 8, 1–2 sessions/week partly maintained systolic and mean arterial pressure reductions (~−6.6 to −7.0 mmHg), while 3×/week produced the largest systolic drop (~−13.7 mmHg) and better preservation of vascular/autonomic markers; stopping training reversed benefits toward control. The maintenance window was only 4 weeks, and results apply mainly to normal/high-normal BP, not established hypertension without medical clearance.

**Links:**

- [BMJ Open Sport & Exercise Medicine — isometric training frequency and BP maintenance](https://bmjopensem.bmj.com/content/12/3/e003172)
- [Prevention — Nov 7 coverage](https://www.prevention.com/fitness/a74313065/isometric-exercise-lowers-blood-pressure-study/)

**Commentary:** If cleared to exercise and BP runs high-normal, a few weekly wall-sits can complement aerobic/resistance work—cutting frequency may slow regression, but roughly three sessions/week best holds the gain.

### 5. *Scientific Reports* meta-analysis: eccentric work protects soccer hamstrings; plyometrics better drive sprint/power (Training / Injury prevention)
**Summary:** A systematic review and meta-analysis of 54 RCTs (search through 2026) compared eccentric, plyometric, or combined training versus control in soccer players aged ≥13 years. Eccentric (e.g., Nordic) training cut hamstring-injury incidence by about 70% (4 trials; RR≈0.29, 95% CI 0.17–0.48), whereas plyometric bounding did not show comparable protection; jump and sprint outcomes improved overall, but subgroup analyses suggested eccentric work alone did not reliably improve sprint, while plyometric and combined programs favored speed/power and eccentric training raised eccentric knee-flexor strength. Heterogeneity and small subgroups warrant progressive loading and coaching.

**Links:**

- [Scientific Reports — eccentric vs plyometric training for soccer hamstring health](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-79351-y)

**Commentary:** Recreational sprint/soccer athletes: use Nordic-style eccentrics to lower injury risk and plyometrics to get faster—the jobs differ, and combining often beats picking only one.

### 6. *European Geriatric Medicine* network meta-analysis: combined exercise provisionally ranks first for older adults’ sleep (Training / Sleep)
**Summary:** Wen and colleagues pooled 32 RCTs (2,658 older adults) comparing exercise types for sleep quality. Network SUCRA rankings placed combined exercise at about 74.8%, aerobic 65.4%, resistance 64.4%, mind–body 49.7%, and dance 46.1%. Subgroups suggested session length, weekly frequency, intervention period, intensity, and participant traits can reshuffle ranks. Authors call the conclusions provisional given limited studies. DOI 10.1007/s41999-026-01586-9.

**Links:**

- [European Geriatric Medicine — exercise regimens and sleep quality in older adults (DOI)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s41999-026-01586-9)

**Commentary:** Older adults with poor sleep should not cling to a single cardio habit—pairing walking/cycling with resistance within tolerance better matches today’s evidence map.

## III. Public Health & Disease Prevention

### 7. AHA 2026 / *NEJM*: pelacarsen sharply lowers Lp(a) but does not cut secondary-prevention MACE (Public health / Lipids)
**Summary:** The phase 3 Lp(a)HORIZON double-blind RCT enrolled 8,323 adults with established cardiovascular disease and Lp(a) ≥70 mg/dL across 797 sites in 42 countries, randomizing monthly subcutaneous pelacarsen 80 mg or placebo for a median ~4.7 years. Results were presented at AHA Scientific Sessions 2026 on Nov 7, 2026, and published simultaneously in *NEJM*. Lp(a) fell by about 78% at 12 months, yet the primary composite (CV death, nonfatal MI/stroke, or urgent revascularization) occurred in about 12.3% versus 12.1% (HR≈1.02; no significant benefit), including in higher baseline Lp(a) subgroups. Most participants were already on intensive statin and BP therapy. One drug in secondary prevention neither invalidates Lp(a) as a risk marker nor speaks for other Lp(a)-lowering agents or primary prevention.

**Links:**

- [American Heart Association — Lp(a) HORIZON news release](https://newsroom.heart.org/news/lp-a-horizon-trial-lp-a-fell-by-78-but-cardiovascular-outcomes-unexpectedly-neutral)
- [Cleveland Clinic — trial briefing](https://newsroom.clevelandclinic.org/2026/11/07/cleveland-clinic-trial-shows-novel-lipoproteina-drug-did-not-reduce-major-cardiovascular-events-after-nearly-5-years)
- [NEJM — pelacarsen cardiovascular outcomes (DOI)](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2610159)

**Commentary:** Still measure Lp(a) for risk stratification, but do not equate biomarker drops with fewer events—LDL control, blood pressure, smoking cessation, and an exercise prescription remain the hard cards in hand.

### 8. *British Journal of Cancer* three-arm RCT: text reminders roughly triple bowel-screening uptake among London non-responders (Public health / Screening)
**Summary:** Adults in London who had not completed fecal immunochemical testing within 18 weeks of invitation were randomized (intention-to-treat n=3,129) to usual care, automated texts at weeks 19/21/23/25, or a week-19 text plus telephone patient navigation. Completion by 26 weeks was about 2.0% (usual care), 6.5% (texts), and 7.5% (text+navigation); both interventions beat usual care, with no overall ITT difference between texts and navigation, though per-protocol navigation was higher and more effective in men. University coverage estimated London-wide texts could raise absolute uptake among non-responders by about 4.5–5.5 percentage points. Regional delivery and privacy constraints limit direct export.

**Links:**

- [British Journal of Cancer — text reminders vs patient navigation for bowel screening](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41416-026-03645-1)
- [University of Surrey — news release](https://www.surrey.ac.uk/news/simple-text-reminders-could-help-close-londons-bowel-cancer-screening-gap)

**Commentary:** If a screening kit is sitting unreturned, put a calendar reminder to mail it; systems should scale low-cost texts first and add phone navigation for hard-to-reach men.

## IV. Sleep & Mental Health

### 9. *BMC Psychiatry* CHARLS analysis: childhood adversity links to later depressive symptoms; concurrent sleep duration moderates the association (Sleep / Mental health)
**Summary:** Among 9,118 CHARLS adults aged ≥45 years, each one-point rise in cumulative childhood adversity raised 2020 depressive-symptom odds by about 1.24 after covariate adjustment including baseline depression; nighttime sleep <6 hours versus normal sleep had OR≈2.02 for depressive symptoms. Sleep duration measured in 2020 moderated the adversity–depression link (interaction OR≈0.97, p=0.002)—associations were weaker at longer sleep—whereas prospective interaction using 2013 sleep was nonsignificant. Observational data cannot prove that “sleeping more erases trauma,” nor replace clinical care for insomnia or depression.

**Links:**

- [BMC Psychiatry — childhood adversity, sleep duration, and depressive symptoms](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12888-026-08801-8)

**Commentary:** Adults with early adversity and low mood can treat stable nighttime sleep (avoid chronic <6 hours) as one modifiable mental-health lever while seeking professional support—not as a solo cure.

## V. Evidence & Clarifications

### 10. *Military Medicine*: only about 10% of dietary supplements on two Army installation shelves carried DoD-vetted third-party seals (Evidence / Supplements)
**Summary:** A quality-improvement survey of 1,209 unique dietary supplements sold in 2024 at Fort Drum and Camp Buehring found proprietary blends in about 21%, >200% Daily Value for at least one vitamin/mineral in 38%, >200 mg caffeine per serving in 5%, and multiple stimulants in 7%; only 120 products (10%) carried Operation Supplement Safety–vetted third-party certification seals, with no labeled prohibited substances identified. Findings cannot be generalized to every military retail site but challenge the assumption that base-store products are fully vetted.

**Links:**

- [Military Medicine — safety assessment of supplements on two Army installations](https://academic.oup.com/milmed/article/191/9-10/e2197/8423061)

**Commentary:** Check independent certification seals and full labels before buying performance supplements—do not relax scrutiny just because a product is sold on base.

### 11. *JACC: Advances* WAHA RCT: two years of walnuts raised total IgG ~6%—not proof of “boosted immunity” (Evidence clarification)
**Summary:** In the Spain–US WAHA trial, adults aged 63–79 years were randomized to a walnut-enriched diet (~15% of energy, about 30–60 g/day) or a walnut-free control diet for 2 years; immunoglobulins were available for 474 participants. Pooled analyses showed about 6% higher total IgG with walnuts (95% CI 0%–12%), ~11% in the US center, and no significant effect in Spain; total IgM, MDA-LDL, and anti–MDA-LDL antibodies showed no significant intervention effects. US participants already had ~25% lower baseline IgG/IgM than Spanish peers. No infection rates or vaccine-response endpoints were reported.

**Links:**

- [JACC: Advances — WAHA walnuts, immunoglobulins, and anti–MDA-LDL antibodies](https://www.jacc.org/doi/10.1016/j.jacadv.2026.103288)

**Commentary:** Walnuts remain a heart-friendly snack, but a few percent higher IgG is not evidence you will catch fewer colds—without clinical infection outcomes, do not market them as immune boosters.
