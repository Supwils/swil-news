## I. Nutrition & Diet

### 1. Korean HEXA cohort: more frequent high-sugar snacks linked to higher breast-cancer risk; fruit sugar is not (Nutrition / Cancer)
**Summary:** Seoul National University’s Daehee Kang and Wonshik Han, with Woo-Kyoung Shin of Mokwon University and colleagues, reported a Health Examinees-Gem (HEXA-G) prospective analysis in *European Journal of Nutrition*; Health Chosun followed on Nov 8, 2026. About 92,000 women aged 40–69 were followed for a mean ~9.2 years, with 713 incident breast cancers. Eating high-sugar foods more than twice weekly versus less than once monthly was associated with HR≈1.28 (95% CI 1.02–1.61); ice cream more than once weekly versus never had HR≈1.26; sugary cereals also showed associations. Fruit supplied about 43% of dietary sugar yet was not linked to breast-cancer risk. Observational residual confounding means ice cream is not proven to cause cancer.

**Links:**

- [European Journal of Nutrition — high-sugar foods and breast cancer (HEXA)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00394-026-04134-0)
- [Health Chosun — Nov 8 report](https://health.chosun.com/english/site/data/html_dir/2026/11/08/2026110803279.html)

**Commentary:** Cut ice cream, sugary cereals, and sweet drinks first—do not equate fruit sugar with empty snack sugar; this calibrates risk, it does not convict a single food.

### 2. *JAMA Network Open*: children’s ultraprocessed intake tracks dorsal-striatal food-cue responses and later adiposity gain (Nutrition / Pediatrics)
**Summary:** Kathleen Page’s team at USC Keck’s BrainChild cohort prospectively studied 46 children of healthy weight (mean age ~8.7 years); USC and Health Chosun covered the work recently. Multiple 24-hour recalls scored UPF energy share under NOVA; baseline food-cue fMRI and one-year adiposity follow-up showed higher UPF share associated with stronger dorsal-striatal BOLD responses (β≈0.34), which in turn tracked larger one-year BMI and body-fat gains; waist circumference also rose with UPF share. Adjusting for dorsal-striatal responsivity attenuated UPF–BMI and UPF–body-fat links by about 31% and 44%. Small observational sample cannot prove that UPFs “rewire” brains or directly cause obesity.

**Links:**

- [JAMA Network Open — UPF, striatal responses, and childhood adiposity](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2855779)
- [USC Keck — news release](https://keck.usc.edu/news/children-who-eat-more-ultra-processed-foods-show-stronger-brain-responses-to-appetizing-foods/)

**Commentary:** Swap packaged snacks for less-processed staples in the school-age years before waiting for weight gain—treat the neural–diet link as a signal, not a verdict.

### 3. *Nature Communications* TEMPUS RCT: 8-hour time-restricted eating ± supervised exercise did not beat usual care for liver fat (Nutrition / Training)
**Summary:** University of Granada investigators randomized 187 adults with obesity (~50% women) to usual care (Mediterranean diet and activity education), 8-hour time-restricted eating (TRE), supervised exercise, or TRE+exercise for 12 weeks; the primary endpoint was MRI intrahepatic fat fraction (IHFF). Versus usual care, IHFF differences for TRE, exercise, and both were nonsignificant (about −0.4% to −1.1%). Eating-window arms lost ~2.2 kg more weight; the combined arm improved body fat and fitness more clearly, with exploratory signals for greater IHFF drop in men and higher MASLD remission. Between-group differences were gone at one year. A negative primary endpoint means secondary wins need cautious reading.

**Links:**

- [Nature Communications — TEMPUS: TRE, exercise, and hepatic fat](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-78625-3)
- [Universidad de Granada — news release](https://www.ugr.es/en/about/news/fasting-and-exercise-promote-fat-loss-obesity-ugr)

**Commentary:** TRE can help with weight management, but do not cast it as a fatty-liver specialty drug—diet quality, calories, and sustainable activity still come first, and short-term losses may not stick a year later.

### 4. Free-living CGM secondary analysis: prior-meal sugar and long gaps raise next-meal glucose; protein goes the other way (Nutrition / Glycemia)
**Summary:** A National University of Singapore eTRIP secondary analysis (detailed by Ebiotrade on Nov 8, 2026; published in *Nutrients*) included 55 overweight Southeast Asian adults (BMI ≥23) with Dexcom G6 and phone diet logs yielding 342 same-day consecutive eating pairs. Participant fixed-effects models found that each within-person SD rise in prior-meal total sugar energy share linked to ~29.6% higher next-meal 0–120 min positive iAUC; a ~1.9-hour longer inter-meal interval linked to ~29.1% higher iAUC; higher prior-meal protein energy share linked to ~13.9% lower iAUC. Observational gaps in diet/metabolic data and sensitivity of the protein estimate to further adjustment block causal prescriptions.

**Links:**

- [Ebiotrade — free-living second-meal effect with CGM](https://www.ebiotrade.com/newsf/2026-11/20261108081908747.htm)

**Commentary:** For post-meal glucose worry, try a less-sweet prior meal, avoid very long gaps, and include enough protein at meals before layering on complicated intermittent rules.

## II. Exercise & Training Science

### 5. *BMC Geriatrics* RCT: variable dual-task training boosts lower-limb strength immediately; cognitive and mobility gains arrive later (Training / Aging)
**Summary:** Fifty community-dwelling adults aged ≥65 (including mild cognitive impairment) were randomized to intervention (n=30) or waitlist control (n=20); intervention was a highly variable cognitive–motor dual-task program twice weekly for 60 minutes over 12 weeks. At post-test, Five Times Sit-to-Stand improved versus control (Group×Time p=0.005, SMD≈0.54) and held at three-month follow-up. Timed Up and Go, Flanker reaction time, and forward Digit Span mostly improved within the intervention arm only at follow-up (SMDs ~0.28–0.62), without significant between-group effects right after training. Modest sample and waitlist design limit generalization.

**Links:**

- [BMC Geriatrics — immediate and delayed adaptations to variable dual-task training](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12877-026-08667-1)
- [Scienmag — Nov 8 coverage](https://scienmag.com/variable-dual-task-training-sparks-immediate-physical-and-delayed-cognitive-gains/)

**Commentary:** Older adults doing “walk-and-talk/calculate” work should not judge success only at program end—leg strength may rise first, while attention and short-term memory often consolidate months later.

### 6. *Frontiers in Sports and Active Living*: snatch pulls are not a substitute for dedicated core work (Training / EMG)
**Summary:** Taiwanese investigators Wu et al. compared surface EMG of the rectus abdominis and lumbar erector spinae in 20 resistance-trained female university students during barbell snatch pulls across graded loads, inverted sit-ups, and horizontal hyperextensions (DOI 10.3389/fspor.2026.1927838; science media covered Nov 8). Snatch pulls drove load-dependent erector excitation but much lower rectus abdominis activity than inverted sit-ups; equivalence tests (±10% MVC) failed to show that snatch pulls can replace specialized core drills at any load. Superficial muscles only, acute single reps, and a female student sample limit extrapolation to elite male lifters or deep stabilizers.

**Links:**

- [Frontiers — sEMG of snatch pulls vs specialized core work (DOI)](https://doi.org/10.3389/fspor.2026.1927838)
- [Scienmag — EMG comparison](https://scienmag.com/study-compares-core-muscle-electromyography-in-snatch-pulls-and-specialized-exercises/)

**Commentary:** Heavy pulls train the posterior chain well, but do not treat “snatch pull only” as a full core program—pair compound pulls with anterior-focused work instead of choosing one.

## III. Public Health & Disease Prevention

### 7. AHA Scientific Sessions: ~1 month of bedroom HEPA filtration blunted glycemic swings in prediabetes (Public health / Metabolism)
**Summary:** NYU Langone Health reported at the American Heart Association 2026 Scientific Sessions (Nov 8) that more than 150 adults with prediabetes and no known atherosclerosis were randomized to real or sham portable bedroom air cleaners for about a month; real filters cut fine particles by ~36%. With continuous glucose monitoring, daily glucose variability fell ~8%, and post-breakfast glucose incremental area and peak fell about 17% and 13%. Authors call this early human evidence that may not apply to established diabetes or other pollution settings; longer prevention of progression is unproven.

**Links:**

- [PR Newswire / NYU Langone — bedroom air cleaning and prediabetes glucose](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/cleaner-bedroom-air-may-help-manage-blood-sugar-in-prediabetes-302932132.html)

**Commentary:** Diet and exercise remain the prediabetes backbone; if bedroom particles run high, a proper HEPA unit is a low-cost environmental add-on—not a substitute for meds or weight plans.

### 8. UK Biobank: under ~42 minutes of bright daytime light linked to higher dementia risk (Public health / Brain health)
**Summary:** A *General Psychiatry* analysis of 87,577 dementia-free adults with 7-day wrist light sensors, median ~8.1 years of follow-up, and 741 incident cases was popularized by Prevention and others around Nov 8. Average daytime light >1000 lux was associated with ~16% lower dementia risk; ≥0.7 hours (~42 minutes) daily at ≥5000 lux with ~17% lower risk. In exploratory rankings, <42 minutes of bright light outranked several classic predictors such as obesity and alcohol; nighttime light alone was not significant. Observational design, one week of wrist (not eye-level) sensing, and no causal proof apply.

**Links:**

- [ScienceDaily — daytime bright light and dementia risk](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/10/261028232707.htm)
- [Prevention — Nov 8 explainer](https://www.prevention.com/health/a74348186/daylight-decreases-dementia-risk-study/)

**Commentary:** Aiming for roughly three-quarters of an hour of bright outdoor or near-window daylight is a cheap brain-health habit worth trying—not a dementia cure.

### 9. *JAMA Surgery* Nordic registry: bariatric surgery tied to ~19% lower obesity-related cancer risk overall (Public health / Oncology)
**Summary:** Karolinska-led NordOSCo data covered ~784,000 adults aged 18–59 with obesity records in Denmark, Finland, and Sweden, including ~99,600 with bariatric surgery; JAMA and specialty media followed around early November. Versus no surgery, obesity-related cancer aIRR≈0.81 (95% CI 0.77–0.85), similar across ages 18–39 and 40–59 and between sexes; liver, endometrial, and ovarian cancers showed stronger inverse links, while pancreatic cancer risk rose (especially in younger adults, aIRR≈2.21), possibly with surveillance bias. Registry association is not proof of cancer prevention and does not replace screening.

**Links:**

- [JAMA Surgery — bariatric surgery and obesity-related cancer (DOI)](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamasurg.2026.4798)
- [JAMA — research summary](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/2856588)

**Commentary:** For surgery-eligible patients, a broadly lower long-term cancer signal can enter shared decisions—keep screening, and interpret the pancreatic finding separately.

## IV. Sleep & Mental Health

### 10. *Scientific Reports* imaging meta-analysis: sleep disorders share thalamic structural decreases; parasomnias add PCC changes (Sleep)
**Summary:** Florida International University pooled 57 adult structural imaging studies (17 parasomnia, 40 dyssomnia); Consensus and FIU News circulated the work around Nov 8. Across categories, convergent decreases centered on the thalamus (especially pulvinar), embedded in attention and task-execution cortico–striato–thalamic networks; parasomnia studies also showed posterior cingulate decreases tied to valuation/motivation networks. Meta-analytic convergence cannot settle causal direction and is not a bedside diagnostic tool.

**Links:**

- [Scientific Reports — sleep disorders and motivation-linked structural brain changes](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-40818-7)
- [FIU News — explainer](https://news.fiu.edu/2026/sleep-disorders-dont-just-exhaust-you-a-new-fiu-study-shows-they-change-your-brain)

**Commentary:** Chronic insomnia, apnea, or parasomnias are not “just tiredness”—they co-travel with structural markers in attention and motivation networks, so seek assessment instead of waiting.

## V. Evidence & Clarification

### 11. China SAMR: new supplementary test methods target novel illegal additives in “weight-loss” foods (Evidence / Food safety)
**Summary:** China Securities Journal and others reported on Nov 8, 2026 that the State Administration for Market Regulation issued two supplementary food-testing methods for novel derivatives of phenolphthalein-class and bisacodyl-class compounds. These agents act as stimulant laxatives; long-term intake can cause abdominal pain, diarrhea, electrolyte imbalance, and liver/kidney injury. Illicit actors have spiked jellies, preserved fruit, candies, and beverages with new derivatives while falsely claiming weight-loss effects. The methods aim to identify multiple derivatives for enforcement and prosecution.

**Links:**

- [China Securities Journal — SAMR supplementary testing methods](https://www.cs.com.cn/xwzx/msxf/2026/11/08/detail_2026110810050585.html)

**Commentary:** Treat “oil-flushing slim” jellies and drinks as suspect laxative fraud—real weight loss still rests on diet, activity, and legitimate care, not mystery “functional snacks.”

### 12. *Food & Function* crossover RCT: 60 g/day unsalted skin-roasted peanuts shift regional brain insulin responses in older adults (Evidence / Nutrition)
**Summary:** Maastricht University investigators enrolled 31 healthy adults ~60–75 years in a crossover of 16 weeks of 60 g/day unsalted skin-roasted peanuts versus no peanuts, with an 8-week washout; Chinese media summarized on Nov 8. After intranasal insulin, ASL-MRI showed a lower CBF response in a right-hemisphere cluster including hippocampal-related regions (mean difference ~−5.4 mL/100 g/min), which authors interpret as possibly improved regional brain insulin responsiveness; hepatic fat and most circulating glucose, lipid, and inflammation markers were unchanged. Small sample, no energy-matched control, and an imaging marker are not dementia-prevention evidence.

**Links:**

- [Food & Function — peanuts and brain insulin responsiveness](https://pubs.rsc.org/fo/article/17/20/9354/1366338/Daily-consumption-of-unsalted-skin-roasted-peanuts)
- [Guangzhou Daily — Nov 8 report](https://news.dayoo.com/society/202611/08/140000_55021713.htm)

**Commentary:** Unsalted roasted peanuts can fit a nut allotment, but “brain benefits” remain a small-trial biomarker claim—do not add a full bag of calories that crowds out vegetables and energy control.
