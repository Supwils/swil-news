## I. Nutrition & Diet

### 1. Crossover RCT: Daily tomato paste for ~3 months improved selective attention and associative memory, with tentative resting-state network changes (Nutrition)
**Summary:** *Antioxidants* (DOI: 10.3390/antiox15050644; covered by ScienceAlert on 2026-09-09) reports a Barcelona randomized crossover trial (NCT05891977): 47 healthy adults aged 40–55 enrolled, 42 completed; two ~3-month periods separated by a 1-month washout compared daily concentrated tomato paste (~0.5 g/kg body weight; media reports ~35 g/day) with a lycopene-restricted diet. Tomato intake improved selective attention (concentration performance ~+7.2; processing speed ~+8.3) and face–name associative memory (~+0.8); plasma lycopene rose markedly, with a borderline BDNF increase (~15.2 ng/mL between conditions). Resting-state fMRI in 14 participants suggested reduced frontoparietal and auditory network connectivity, but imaging was underpowered with lenient thresholds, and the unblinded design cannot fully exclude motivational effects.

**Links:**

- [DOI: 10.3390/antiox15050644](https://doi.org/10.3390/antiox15050644)
- [ScienceAlert — Tomato paste and brain scans](https://www.sciencealert.com/scientists-had-people-eat-tomato-paste-for-3-months-their-brain-scans-looked-different)

**Commentary:** Treat tomatoes/tomato paste as one dietary lycopene source—not as proven “brain supplements”; this remains a mid-size, unblinded, imaging-limited first trial.

### 2. South Africa cohort MRI: Infants of mothers with antenatal anaemia had ~4% smaller total brain volume; putamen/caudate/corpus callosum differences detectable by ~age 1 (Nutrition/Public health)
**Summary:** *Brain Communications* (DOI: 10.1093/braincomms/fcag318; King’s College London / University of Cape Town; media 2026-09-09) followed ~394 mother–infant pairs in Cape Town, with subgroup MRI from 3 months to 2 years. Infants of mothers with antenatal anaemia (mostly mild) had ~4% smaller mean total brain volume, with smaller putamen, caudate nucleus, and corpus callosum; the callosal gap widened from ~4% at 12 months to ~6% at 24 months. Portable ultra-low-field (64 mT) MRI detected similar differences to conventional 3T. Observational association—despite similar socioeconomic backgrounds—does not prove that iron therapy alone reverses volumes.

**Links:**

- [King’s College London — Anaemia and infant brain development](https://www.kcl.ac.uk/news/anaemia-in-pregnancy-linked-to-differences-in-babies-brain-development-study-finds)
- [The Guardian — Anaemic mothers and babies’ brains](https://www.theguardian.com/global-development/2026/sep/09/babies-born-to-anaemic-mothers-have-smaller-brains-study-finds)

**Commentary:** Prioritize anaemia/iron screening before and during pregnancy over postnatal “brain food” marketing; manage infection-related anaemia alongside dietary iron.

### 3. RCT meta-analysis: Oral nutrition interventions in adults with heart failure improved quality of life, 6-minute walk, and grip strength, with fewer readmissions (Nutrition)
**Summary:** *BMC Nutrition* (2026-09-08; DOI: 10.1186/s40795-026-01470-6) searched through 2026-03-30 and pooled 22 RCTs (4,042 adults with heart failure). Quality of life favored nutrition intervention (7 estimates; SMD ≈ 0.76, I² ≈ 66.9%); 6-minute walk (SMD ≈ 0.65) and handgrip strength (SMD ≈ 0.82) also improved; pooled clinical events OR ≈ 0.54, with heart-failure readmission OR ≈ 0.25, while all-cause mortality and cardiovascular events were not statistically significant. Intervention types were heterogeneous; exploratory category analyses could not rank specific strategies.

**Links:**

- [BMC Nutrition — Nutrition interventions in heart failure](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40795-026-01470-6)
- [DOI: 10.1186/s40795-026-01470-6](https://doi.org/10.1186/s40795-026-01470-6)

**Commentary:** For people with heart failure, structured nutrition assessment and oral support belong on the care plan—not DIY megadose single supplements.

## II. Exercise & Training Science

### 4. Cross-sectional study: Longer daily sitting in community older adults tracked with worse sarcopenia-related markers and lower ADL/IADL independence (Training/Aging)
**Summary:** *PeerJ* (2026-09-08; DOI: 10.7717/peerj.21714) studied 200 community-dwelling adults aged 65–96 in Korea, grouped by daily sitting (<6, 6–8, >8 h). Longer sitting related to poorer calf circumference, grip strength, 5-time chair-stand performance, and ADL/IADL scores; regressions linked sitting with lower ASM (β ≈ −0.415), calf girth (β ≈ −0.442), and grip (β ≈ −0.385), and slower chair stands. Some associations persisted after age/sex adjustment. Cross-sectional design cannot prove that sitting less reverses sarcopenia.

**Links:**

- [PeerJ — Sitting time, sarcopenia and independence](https://peerj.com/articles/21714/)
- [DOI: 10.7717/peerj.21714](https://doi.org/10.7717/peerj.21714)

**Commentary:** For older adults, breaking long sits hourly is often more actionable than obsessing over workout-watch pace.

### 5. Mouse mechanism: Exercise raises cytosolic NADPH via the pentose phosphate pathway, enabling physiological cardiac hypertrophy and limiting ischemia–reperfusion injury (Training)
**Summary:** *Nature Metabolism* (2026-09-08; DOI: 10.1038/s42255-026-01587-9) used a genetically encoded sensor to show exercise increases cytosolic—not mitochondrial—NADPH in cardiomyocytes via pentose phosphate pathway (PPP) activation; blocking PPP or depleting cytosolic NADPH attenuated exercise-induced heart hypertrophy. NADPH promoted growth by inhibiting HDAC3/C/EBPβ; the exercise-activated PPP/NADPH pathway limited acute IR injury and preserved function at 4 weeks. Among 310 Tibetan-related compounds, lyciumspermidine-0527 directly activated rate-limiting G6PD, raised NADPH, and eased IR injury. Animal/cellular work—candidate compounds are far from clinical use.

**Links:**

- [Nature Metabolism — PPP-derived NADPH and the exercising heart](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42255-026-01587-9)
- [DOI: 10.1038/s42255-026-01587-9](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42255-026-01587-9)

**Commentary:** The practical takeaway remains regular aerobic/resistance training for cardiac adaptation—not hunting for unapproved “exercise mimetics.”

**Summary:** *Nature Health* (online ~2026-08-24; CAS reporting in early September 2026; DOI: 10.1038/s44360-026-00180-4) integrated ~109 million incident cancers across 952 locations (2000–2020) with high-resolution pollutant estimates. Each +10 μg/m³ in PM2.5, O₃, and NO₂ associated with ~16.0%, 4.23%, and 11.7% higher all-cancer incidence, attributing roughly 8.82, 2.59, and 6.67 million cases; PM2.5 showed a J-shaped dose–response, with higher attributable risk in women. Eco-epidemiologic attribution remains vulnerable to confounding and exposure misclassification at the individual level.

## IV. Sleep & Mental Health

### 10. *Nature*: Sparse long-range neocortical Sst-Chodl inhibitory neurons can promote cortical synchrony and induce sleep in mice (Sleep)
**Summary:** *Nature* (2026-09-09; DOI: 10.1038/s41586-026-10876-y) shows somatostatin/chondrolectin co-expressing GABAergic neurons are selectively active in low-arousal states and mostly silent in high arousal. Despite extreme sparsity, long-range axons target multiple neocortical regions; selective activation was sufficient to promote multi-region synchrony typical of low arousal and to induce sleep behavior. Authors argue cortical circuits can actively drive sleep, not only follow subcortical commands. Circuit neuroscience in mice—not a prescription for human sleep drugs.

**Links:**

- [Nature — Neocortical long-range inhibition and sleep](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10876-y)
- [DOI: 10.1038/s41586-026-10876-y](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-10876-y)

**Commentary:** Mechanistically, sleep is a pushable brain state; practically, keep fixed schedules and cut nighttime high-arousal inputs while waiting on any future circuit-targeted therapies.

## V. Evidence & Clarifications / Supplements

### 11. Review of European consortia RCTs: Non-nutritive sweeteners replacing sugar did not undermine weight loss/maintenance or heighten sweet preference (Evidence clarification)
**Summary:** *Nutrients* (DOI: 10.3390/nu18111647; News Medical 2026-09-09) reviews multi-year European RCT evidence from SWEET, SWITCH, and Sweet Tooth. Replacing sugar with NNSs did not undermine weight loss or maintenance and sometimes helped, especially among adherent participants; SWITCH found slightly more loss with NNS beverages versus water, below a clinical-meaningfulness threshold. Cardiometabolic markers were generally stable; trials did not support “sweeteners train greater sweet craving” or higher overall intake. Limits: mostly overweight adults in structured programs; pediatric and ultra-long-term effects remain unclear; mixed public–private funding warrants independent reading.

**Links:**

- [News Medical — Non-nutritive sweeteners review](https://www.news-medical.net/news/20260909/Non-nutritive-sweeteners-may-aid-sugar-reduction-without-affecting-weight-or-health.aspx)
- [Nutrients — DOI: 10.3390/nu18111647](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-6643/18/11/1647)

**Commentary:** NNSs can be a sugar-reduction bridge—not a free pass for unlimited “zero-sugar” sweet drinks outside overall diet quality and energy balance.

### 12. Double-blind RCT: 21 days of vitamin D₃ at 10,000 IU/day raised 25(OH)D and shifted some inflammatory markers but did not improve Wingate anaerobic performance (Supplements)
**Summary:** *Frontiers in Physiology* (2026-09-04; DOI: 10.3389/fphys.2026.1867500) randomized 30 physically active men to vitamin D₃ 10,000 IU/day or placebo for 21 days (n=27 analyzed), with 2×Wingate tests and serial blood draws before/after. The vitamin D group’s resting 25(OH)D rose ~+55.7%; anaerobic performance showed no advantage versus placebo. Resting IL-1β and IL-6 were lower and IL-10 higher; post-exercise IL-1β responses attenuated and IL-10 stayed higher, while IL-6 time courses did not differ. Functional recovery was not assessed; short, small, high-dose trial—not a population dosing guide.

**Links:**

- [Frontiers — Vitamin D3, inflammation and Wingate performance](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/physiology/articles/10.3389/fphys.2026.1867500/full)
- [DOI: 10.3389/fphys.2026.1867500](https://doi.org/10.3389/fphys.2026.1867500)

**Commentary:** Don’t expect a brief high-dose vitamin D burst to raise anaerobic power; supplement based on deficiency risk and clinical indication, not pre-season superstition.
