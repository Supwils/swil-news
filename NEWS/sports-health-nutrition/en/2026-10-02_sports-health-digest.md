## I. Nutrition & Diet

### 1. CJASN: In an older cohort, the top third of ultra-processed food intake tracked with 26% higher incident CKD risk, and animal-sourced products stood out (Nutrition)
**Summary:** Healio reported on Oct 2, 2026, that Hyunju Kim, Courtney M. Hill, and colleagues analyzed the Cardiovascular Health Study in the *Clinical Journal of the American Society of Nephrology* (doi: 10.2215/CJN.0000001207). The prospective cohort included 2,661 adults aged 65 or older (mean age 71; 61% women; 95% white). Food-frequency questionnaires from 1989-1990 and 1995-1996 were classified with the NOVA system into plant-sourced and animal-sourced ultra-processed foods. Incident chronic kidney disease was an estimated glomerular filtration rate below 60 mL/min/1.73 m². After a median 6.8 years, and adjustment for demographics and health behaviors, the highest versus lowest tertile had a hazard ratio of 1.26 for total ultra-processed foods (95% CI 1.08-1.47) and 1.37 for animal-sourced ultra-processed foods (1.17-1.60). Each standard-deviation increase in plant-sourced ultra-processed foods had an HR of 1.07 (1.01-1.13), but the tertile trend was not significant (P for trend = 0.12). The highest tertile of minimally processed foods had an HR of 0.80 (0.69-0.95). Replacing three servings of minimally processed food with three servings of ultra-processed food had an HR of only 1.02 (1.001-1.03). There was no association with change in estimated glomerular filtration rate, and estimates weakened when death was treated as a competing risk. The finding is observational, and the diet data are decades old.

**Links:**

- [Healio — Ultraprocessed foods and CKD risk](https://www.healio.com/news/nephrology/20261002/ultraprocessed-plant-and-animalbased-foods-tied-to-ckd-risks)
- [CJASN — DOI 10.2215/CJN.0000001207](https://doi.org/10.2215/CJN.0000001207)

**Commentary:** For an older adult at home, swap processed meats and additive-heavy packaged foods for whole beans, vegetables, and plain staples; this association comes from an older, mostly white cohort and does not set a kidney-disease rate for younger people.

### 2. Food & Function: Healthy young adults eating 40 g of walnuts daily for 8 weeks scored 4.85 points lower on total mood disturbance; anger and fatigue did not hold after correction (Nutrition)
**Summary:** On Oct 2, 2026, News-Medical reviewed an open-label randomized crossover trial in *Food & Function* by Maria Izquierdo-Pulido, Maria Fernanda Zerón-Rugerio, and colleagues at the University of Barcelona (doi: 10.1039/d6fo00807k). Of 84 people screened from December 2021 to February 2022, 36 enrolled, 5 withdrew, and 31 completed both periods (mean age 24; 90.3% women; BMI 21.6). The intervention was 40 g of walnuts with dinner for 8 weeks; the control was no nuts, with a 2-week washout. Both periods included Mediterranean-style diet guidance. Total mood disturbance on the Profile of Mood States was 4.85 points lower during the walnut period. Drops in anger and fatigue were no longer significant after correction for multiple comparisons. Perceived stress, well-being, and positive mood did not differ. Morning urinary 5-hydroxyindoleacetic acid (5-HIAA) and urolithins A and B rose during the walnut period, and higher 5-HIAA tracked with lower total mood disturbance; urinary cortisol did not differ. Diet quality was also slightly higher in the walnut period. Brain serotonin was not measured. A California Walnut Commission release states that the commission supported the trial.

**Links:**

- [News-Medical — Walnuts, mood, and serotonin metabolites](https://www.news-medical.net/news/20261002/Eating-walnuts-daily-may-boost-mood-and-shift-serotonin-related-metabolism.aspx)
- [Food & Function — DOI 10.1039/d6fo00807k](https://doi.org/10.1039/d6fo00807k)

**Commentary:** A handful of walnuts is a reasonable snack for a healthy young adult; treating it as a mood prescription is premature in a small, mostly female, open-label trial supported by the walnut industry.

## II. Exercise & Training Science

### 3. PLOS One: Prefrail and frail older adults who walked near 70% of maximum heart rate were more likely to improve a frailty category over 16 weeks than those who strolled (Training)
**Summary:** Margaret Danilovich of Northwestern University, Daniel S. Rubin of the University of Chicago, and colleagues published a cluster-randomized trial in *PLOS One* on Sep 30, 2026 (doi: 10.1371/journal.pone.0357605). Fourteen retirement communities were randomized to high-intensity or casual-speed walking. The study recruited 165 adults aged 60 or older who could walk at least 10 feet with moderate assistance or less and were prefrail or frail; the analytic cohort was 105 (mean age 79.2; 79% women; 75% non-Hispanic white). High-intensity walking targeted 70% of maximum heart rate, and casual walking stayed below 60%. Supervised sessions lasted 45 minutes, three times a week, for 16 weeks. The primary outcome, improvement by at least one SHARE-FI frailty category, occurred in 32 of 46 high-intensity participants (69.6%) versus 26 of 56 casual-speed participants (46.4%), OR 2.91 (95% CI 1.19-7.13). Gait speed gained an extra 0.08 m/s (0.03-0.13), and 6-minute walk distance an extra 48.99 m (21.1-76.9). Mean achieved heart rate was 71% versus 59% of maximum, and the median number of sessions completed was 35 versus 36. Assessors and participants were masked, but different intensity targets make full masking difficult.

**Links:**

- [PLOS One — High-intensity walking and frailty](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0357605)

**Commentary:** An older adult who can still walk, and who is supervised with a heart-rate check, can aim for a pace that makes them slightly breathless; chest pain, dizziness, or poor balance calls for a clinician or therapist to set the intensity first.

### 4. Journal of Hand Therapy: Twice-weekly eccentric arm training for 8 weeks eased tremor and grip strength in Parkinson's, and adding motion games was not significantly better (Training)
**Summary:** Parkinson's News Today reported on Oct 2, 2026, a single-center randomized trial from Brazil published in the *Journal of Hand Therapy*. Among 25 people with Parkinson's disease who were taking dopamine-replacement medication, 14 did eccentric upper-limb exercise alone and 11 added Nintendo Wii. Mean age was 63.5 in the exercise-only group (43% women) and 59.8 in the Wii group (55% women). Both groups trained twice a week for 8 weeks, 16 sessions. Eccentric work lasted about 35 minutes and targeted elbow bending and wrist movement, with resistance progressed. The Wii group added about 20 minutes of tennis, boxing, cycling, and canoeing. Resting, postural, and kinetic tremor on the MDS-UPDRS lessened in both groups and was still lower one month later. Grip strength and gross dexterity improved in both groups, without a significant between-group difference. Fine dexterity improved only in the Wii group, and that between-group comparison was also not significant. On a smartphone measure, kinetic tremor amplitude fell in both groups; postural tremor amplitude fell significantly only in the exercise-only group; resting tremor amplitude did not improve significantly.

**Links:**

- [Parkinson's News Today — Eccentric arm exercise and tremor](https://parkinsonsnewstoday.com/news/arm-hand-exercises-ease-tremor-parkinsons-trial/)

**Commentary:** Someone with Parkinson's can practice slowly lowering a weight through the arms and wrists under a therapist; a 25-person trial does not show that buying a motion-game console outperforms those exercises alone.

## III. Public Health & Disease Prevention

### 5. Nature Medicine: Pairing small-quantity nutrition supplements with routine immunization in northern Nigeria raised card-verified measles coverage by about 20 points versus routine services (Public health)
**Summary:** A pragmatic cluster-randomized trial by Cécile Cazes, Renaud Becquet, Kevin P. Q. Phelan, and colleagues was published online in *Nature Medicine* on Oct 2, 2026. Twenty health wards in two local government areas of Yobe State, Nigeria, were randomized 1:1. After the routine immunization visit, intervention clinics gave children aged 6-23 months a monthly ration of small-quantity lipid-based nutrient supplements (SQ-LNS; one 20 g sachet a day). Control wards provided the national immunization program only. The primary outcome was card-verified first-dose measles-containing vaccine coverage among children aged 12-23 months. The endline household survey included 1,604 children (801 control, 803 intervention), and 48% of children in intervention clusters had ever received the supplement. The odds ratio for card-verified first-dose measles vaccine was 2.08 (95% CI 1.30-3.35). A difference-in-differences analysis found a 20.1 percentage-point greater gain from baseline than in the control arm (13.7-26.5). Endline coverage was 41.2% in control wards and 55.3% in intervention wards, from baselines of 29.9% and 25.0%. Card-verified zero-dose status had an OR of 0.50 (0.33-0.75). No adverse events were reported as related to co-delivery. Endline coverage remained below the 95% level used for measles herd immunity, and the trial was done in one country with high malnutrition and low vaccination coverage.

**Links:**

- [Nature Medicine — SQ-LNS with routine immunization in Nigeria](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-026-04675-1)

**Commentary:** Where malnutrition is common and vaccine coverage is low, putting a nutrition supplement on the same clinic day as immunization can get more children a documented measles dose; the supplement does not replace the vaccine, and the result does not automatically transfer to places where coverage is already high.

### 6. US medical societies: This fall's flu advice is vaccination from 6 months of age, enhanced products for adults 65 and older, and no yearly repeat of an RSV dose already received (Public health)
**Summary:** A report by CNN's Jacqueline Howard was published by KRDO on Oct 2, 2026. The American Medical Association and the University of Minnesota Vaccine Integrity Project independently reviewed flu, Covid-19, and respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) vaccines; the reviews appeared in *JAMA* in September and informed 2026-27 recommendations from the American Academy of Pediatrics, the American Academy of Family Physicians, the American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists, and the Infectious Diseases Society of America. Sarah Nosal, president of the American Academy of Family Physicians, said flu vaccines reduce the risk of needing to see a doctor by 30% to 60% on average. The advice is an annual flu shot from 6 months of age, with October a suitable time. Adults 65 and older should get a high-dose, adjuvanted, or recombinant product when it is available, and should not delay if it is not. An mRNA flu vaccine is available this year for adults 50 and older. During pregnancy, inactivated or recombinant flu vaccines are recommended, and the nasal spray is not. For Covid-19, the family-medicine group recommends the updated vaccine for adults older than 19, especially people 65 and older, people at high risk of severe disease, and people who have never been vaccinated. Adults 65 and older are advised to get a second dose six months after the season's first dose. Infants aged 6-23 months, some higher-risk children, and immunocompromised people from 6 months of age are also included. After a recent Covid-19 infection, experts advise waiting four to six months. One RSV vaccine dose is advised for everyone 75 and older and for people 50 and older with high-risk conditions; a dose already received does not need to be repeated this year. During pregnancy, RSV vaccination is recommended at 32-36 weeks, and ACOG extended the seasonal window through March 1 rather than January 31. Infants younger than 8 months whose mothers were not vaccinated, or whose vaccination status is unknown, are advised to receive an RSV monoclonal antibody. Nosal said major insurers have indicated they will cover these respiratory vaccines. The US Department of Health and Human Services had not responded to CNN's request for comment.

**Links:**

**Commentary:** In the United States, get the flu shot in October and ask about an enhanced product at age 65 or older; decide on an updated Covid-19 dose by age and risk, check whether last year's RSV dose is already on record, and ask obstetrics about a dose between 32 weeks and March 1 during pregnancy.

## IV. Sleep & Mental Health

### 7. Scientific Reports: Among 9,418 college freshmen, obsessive-compulsive symptoms were positively associated with sleep disturbance, with indirect paths through anxiety and depression (Sleep)
**Summary:** Kaixiao Luo, Chang Li, Yanping Sun, and colleagues at Shandong Sport University published a cross-sectional conditional process analysis in *Scientific Reports* on Oct 1, 2026. The sample was 9,418 college freshmen aged 18-23 (mean 19.60; 3,660 women) who completed relevant subscales of the Chinese College Students' Mental Health Screening Scale; the analytic sample was 9,416. After covariate adjustment, obsessive-compulsive symptoms were positively associated with sleep disturbance (B = 0.284, t = 20.58, P < 0.001, 95% CI 0.257-0.311). The indirect association through anxiety was 0.263 (bootstrap 95% CI 0.232-0.293), through depression 0.098 (0.084-0.114), and through anxiety then depression 0.113 (0.098-0.130). The interaction of obsessive-compulsive symptoms and internet addiction in the anxiety equation was statistically significant but small (B = 0.0057). Participants were nonclinical freshmen, and everything was measured at the same time, so the study cannot order the problems. The ethics review was at Shandong Sport University.

**Links:**

- [Scientific Reports — Obsessive-compulsive symptoms and sleep in freshmen](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-74232-w)

**Commentary:** A first-year student whose checking or intrusive thoughts arrive with poor sleep should have anxiety and depression assessed at the same time; this cross-sectional survey cannot show that treating obsessive-compulsive symptoms will by itself restore sleep.

### 8. BMC Public Health: In 201 middle-aged and older adults in Shijiazhuang, sleep quality moderated the diet-systolic blood pressure association, while the stratified slopes themselves were not significant (Sleep)
**Summary:** Fengge Chen of the Shijiazhuang Center for Disease Control and Prevention, with Ruiting Wu and Beibei Shi of Xinjiang College of Science and Technology, published a cross-sectional study in *BMC Public Health* on Oct 1, 2026. The 2023 data came from 201 adults aged 45 or older in one Shijiazhuang community, using the Mediterranean Diet Score and the Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index. After covariate adjustment, the diet-by-sleep interaction was associated with systolic blood pressure (β = 1.112, t = 2.339, P < 0.05). In simple slopes, better sleepers had a lower point estimate of systolic pressure as the diet score rose (β = -3.255, P = 0.148); the average-sleep and poorer-sleep strata were also nonsignificant (β = -0.046 and 3.164). The paper reports a 95% CI of -7.673 to -1.162 for the better-sleep slope, which does not match P = 0.148, so the stratified result cannot be treated as a proven blood-pressure effect. The authors describe a hypothesis-generating cross-sectional sample that needs larger longitudinal and intervention studies. Ethics approval was from the Shijiazhuang CDC.

**Links:**

- [BMC Public Health — Sleep, Mediterranean diet score, and blood pressure](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12889-026-29721-y)

**Commentary:** Middle-aged and older adults can keep building meals around vegetables, whole grains, beans, and fish while treating insomnia as its own problem; an interaction in 201 people does not promise that better sleep will make a Mediterranean-style diet lower systolic pressure.
