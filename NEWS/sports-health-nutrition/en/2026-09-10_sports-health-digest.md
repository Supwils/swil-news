## I. Nutrition & Diet

### 1. Single-arm Phase 1: Clinical-grade L-glutamine with chemo in advanced pancreatic cancer—median OS ~22 months; about half maintained weight (Nutrition)
**Summary:** *Nature Cancer* (DOI: 10.1038/s43018-026-01225-z; NutraIngredients 2026-09-10) reports the open-label, single-arm Phase 1 GlutaPanc trial at Cedars-Sinai: 16 patients with advanced pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma received gemcitabine plus nab-paclitaxel plus oral clinical-grade L-glutamine 30 g/day. Investigators reported that about half maintained body weight, described signals of cachexia reversal linked to gut-barrier responses, and a median overall survival of ~22 months versus historical benchmarks of roughly 8–13 months; some tumor shrinkage and complete responses were noted. This is a small exploratory trial without a concurrent control arm and should not be read as a general-population supplement recommendation; randomized confirmation is needed.

**Links:**

- [NutraIngredients — L-glutamine and advanced pancreatic cancer](https://www.nutraingredients.com/Article/2026/09/10/l-glutamine-improves-nutrient-absorption-gut-health-among-terminally-ill/)
- [DOI: 10.1038/s43018-026-01225-z](https://doi.org/10.1038/s43018-026-01225-z)

**Commentary:** Treat this as an oncology supportive-care lead, not a reason for healthy people to megadose glutamine; decisions belong with the cancer care team.

### 2. Population cohort: Gut microbiota composition explains systemic inflammation and physiology better than chronological age; Bacteroides 2 linked to later disease (Nutrition)
**Summary:** *Nature Communications* (2026; DOI: 10.1038/s41467-026-77499-9; News Medical 2026-09-09) analyzed paired fecal and blood samples from 1,199 adults within a ~7,493-person cohort, relating age and microbiota to 30 plasma cytokines and 19 biochemical/physiological measures. Microbiota composition explained more variance than chronological age for ~97% of cytokines and ~84% of physiological measures. The Bacteroides 2 enterotype showed an earlier pro-inflammatory, adverse metabolic profile and higher multi-organ incident disease risk over up to ~9 years of follow-up (mean ~8 years); a high-diversity Richness enterotype associated with lower inflammation and lower disease risk. Observational design leaves residual dietary confounding possible; causality is unproven.

**Links:**

- [News Medical — Gut microbiome vs chronological age](https://www.news-medical.net/news/20260909/Gut-microbiome-may-reveal-inflammation-and-future-disease-risk-better-than-age.aspx)
- [Nature Communications — DOI: 10.1038/s41467-026-77499-9](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-77499-9)

**Commentary:** Do not swap routine check-ups for “microbiome age” tests; actionable levers remain diverse plant foods, fewer ultra-processed products, and regular activity that support high-diversity communities.

### 3. *Nature* global assessment: Micronutrient contribution of oysters, mussels, crabs and other aquatic invertebrates is badly undercounted (Nutrition)
**Summary:** *Nature* (DOI: 10.1038/s41586-026-10908-7; EurekAlert / KAUST release ~2026-09-09), led by Zamborain-Mason and colleagues, quantified aquatic invertebrates’ contribution across ~30 essential nutrients and modeled nutrient content for >50,000 SeaLifeBase species. Current global production equates to annual vitamin B12 and selenium requirements for more than five billion people, and iodine, zinc, copper, and omega-3 requirements for more than one billion. In aquaculture, invertebrates are roughly one-third of production by weight yet can supply more than half of certain nutrients. This is a food-system nutrition accounting study—not a call for unlimited harvest; sustainability and food safety remain prerequisites.

**Links:**

- [Nature — Nutritional value of invertebrate aquatic foods](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10908-7)
- [EurekAlert — Shellfish and aquatic invertebrates](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/1143331)

**Commentary:** Shellfish can be a nutrient-dense option for micronutrient gaps—if allergy risk, contaminants, and sustainable sourcing are managed first.

### 4. Feeding RCT: After VLCD weight loss, returning to refined-starch carbs raised complement factor adipsin and activated circulating immune cells (Nutrition)
**Summary:** *Cell Press Blue* (online 2026-09-09; DOI: 10.1016/j.cpblue.2026.100122; NCT03394664) randomized participants after ~15% weight loss on a very-low-carbohydrate diet (VLCD) to isocaloric VLCD, high-starch refined-grain (HC-Starch), or high-sugar (HC-Sugar) diets for 10 weeks; multimodal immune analyses covered ~44 people. HC-Starch shifted NK-cell proportions and raised activation markers on NK cells, monocytes, T cells, dendritic cells, and neutrophils; transcriptomics showed upregulated immune/metabolic pathways and higher plasma adipsin, with mouse data supporting complement mediation. HC-Sugar showed lower immune-activation signals—authors caution this does not make high sugar “healthy.” After VLCD weight loss, starch-rich refined grains may be an under-recognized inflammation driver.

**Links:**

- [Cell Press Blue — Refined carbohydrates and immune markers](https://www.cell.com/cell-press-blue/fulltext/S3051-3839(26)00120-9)
- [DOI: 10.1016/j.cpblue.2026.100122](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cpblue.2026.100122)

**Commentary:** If carbs rise during weight-loss maintenance, favor whole grains and legumes over large refined-starch loads—and do not misread the sugar arm as anti-inflammatory advice.

## II. Exercise & Training Science

### 5. Repeated-measures: Endurance and grip strength peak in late afternoon—cycling power and grip rose ~8.4% and ~9.0% from morning to ~18:00 (Training)
**Summary:** *Chronobiology International* (online 2026-09-07; DOI: 10.1080/07420528.2026.2730559) tested 15 adults (8 men, 7 women; ~26.9±2.9 y) at 06:00, 10:00, 14:00, 18:00, and 22:00 in a randomized, counterbalanced design: 10-min self-paced cycling time trial, handgrip, temperature, glucose, lactate, sleepiness, and RPE. Mean cycling power and grip varied by time of day (both p<0.001), rising ~8.4% and ~9.0% from 06:00 to 18:00; cosinor peaks were ~17:51 and ~17:27. Pre-exercise temperature, sleepiness, glucose, and lactate also showed diurnal patterns; post-exercise RPE did not. Small young healthy sample—extrapolation to older adults or elite competition needs care.

**Links:**

- [Taylor & Francis — Time-of-day variation in endurance and strength](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/07420528.2026.2730559)
- [DOI: 10.1080/07420528.2026.2730559](https://doi.org/10.1080/07420528.2026.2730559)

**Commentary:** Keep max-effort or key strength tests at a consistent clock time; evening sessions may yield higher numbers, but consistency beats chasing a perfect slot.

### 6. Cross-sectional: Maximum-speed single-leg bridge (MS-SLBT) matches late-swing sprint hamstring demands better than the Nordic curl (Training/Injury prevention)
**Summary:** *Journal of Sports Sciences* (online 2026-09-08; DOI: 10.1080/02640414.2026.2730726) compared muscle activation, joint angles, and estimated muscle–tendon unit lengths during the Nordic Hamstring Exercise (NHE) versus the Maximum-Speed Single-Leg Bridge Test (MS-SLBT) in 17 healthy male recreational athletes. MS-SLBT elicited higher peak semitendinosus and biceps femoris activation (~109% vs 82% MVIC; ~104% vs 88% MVIC), with peak activation and external force at longer MTU lengths and joint angles (~40° hip flexion, ~35° knee flexion) closer to late-swing sprinting. Small, male-only sample; this compares assessment tools, not injury-prevention RCTs.

**Links:**

- [Journal of Sports Sciences — MS-SLBT vs Nordic hamstring](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/02640414.2026.2730726)
- [DOI: 10.1080/02640414.2026.2730726](https://doi.org/10.1080/02640414.2026.2730726)

**Commentary:** Sprint-heavy sports can add MS-SLBT as a more sprint-specific hamstring function check; keep NHE for eccentric capacity—they are not interchangeable.

### 7. Systematic review and meta-analysis: Neuromuscular training cuts ACL risk; effect stronger under age 18 and may attenuate with age (Injury prevention)
**Summary:** *Frontiers in Public Health* (DOI: 10.3389/fpubh.2026.1801019) pooled 19 studies: neuromuscular training (NMT) significantly reduced ACL injury risk (OR≈0.456, 95% CI 0.331–0.627; I²≈14.9%). Age strata showed a clear effect under 18 (OR≈0.365) but not ≥18 (OR≈0.567, CI crossing 1); the between-subgroup difference was nonsignificant, yet meta-regression linked older mean age to weaker effects (β≈0.099, p≈0.027). Female-only analyses were directionally consistent. Evidence is predominantly from female cohorts; male data and real-world adherence remain gaps.

**Links:**

- [Frontiers — Age and neuromuscular training for ACL prevention](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/public-health/articles/10.3389/fpubh.2026.1801019/full)
- [DOI: 10.3389/fpubh.2026.1801019](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpubh.2026.1801019)

**Commentary:** Youth teams should hard-schedule FIFA 11+/PEP-style warm-ups 2–3×/week for 10–20 minutes; adult squads still benefit, but expect smaller average risk cuts.

## III. Public Health & Disease Prevention

### 8. Lifecourse Mendelian randomization: Childhood adiposity shapes childhood and midlife plasma proteomes; TNFSF11 may mediate childhood-onset asthma (Public health)
**Summary:** *Nature Communications* (2026-09-10; DOI: 10.1038/s41467-026-77097-9) used lifecourse Mendelian randomization to test genetically predicted childhood adiposity against circulating proteins at four life stages. Childhood adiposity affected 8 proteins in childhood (mean age ~9.9 years), including higher TNFSF11; mediation/colocalization linked TNFSF11 to childhood-onset asthma risk (posterior probability ~88.6%) but not adult-onset asthma (~0.2%). After accounting for adulthood adiposity, childhood adiposity still independently affected 24 midlife proteins, including CST6 and NOTCH3 previously tied to breast-cancer susceptibility. Genetic-instrument findings do not yet justify targeting these proteins clinically.

**Links:**

- [Nature Communications — Childhood adiposity and plasma proteome](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-77097-9)
- [DOI: 10.1038/s41467-026-77097-9](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-77097-9)

**Commentary:** Healthy childhood weight is about more than today’s BMI—it may trail into immune and long-term proteomic paths; prioritize sleep, activity, and fewer sugary drinks over protein-target supplements.

### 9. WHO: Multi-dose vial of maternal RSV vaccine prequalified to expand infant protection (Guidelines/Vaccines)
**Summary:** WHO (2026-09-09) prequalified the multi-dose vial presentation of maternal RSV vaccine RSVpreF (ABRYSVO®), building on single-dose prequalification in March 2025 and Gavi programme decisions, aiming to lower cost and ease large-scale delivery. WHO recommends countries introduce either maternal RSVpreF or the monoclonal antibody nirsevimab; maternal vaccination is advised from week 28 of pregnancy onward to optimize antibody transfer and protect infants for roughly the first six months of life. This advances global access; national rollout still depends on procurement, cold chain, and immunization schedules.

**Links:**

- [WHO — Multi-dose RSV vaccine vial prequalified](https://www.who.int/news/item/09-09-2026-who-prequalifies-multi-dose-rsv-vaccine-vial--paving-the-way-to-protecting-more-infants-globally)

**Commentary:** Pregnancy care should include asking about local access to maternal RSV vaccine or infant monoclonal antibody—not only adult flu shots.

### 10. CDC: ~20,000 lab-confirmed U.S.-acquired cyclosporiasis cases so far—far above the full 2025 season (Food safety)
**Summary:** *Food Safety News* (2026-09-10), citing CDC, reports 19,595 laboratory-confirmed U.S.-acquired cyclosporiasis cases since May 1, plus ~6,149 additional non-confirmed or pending cases, versus ~1,180 confirmed cases in the 2025 season. Michigan alone had reported 14,718 cases, 366 hospitalizations, and two deaths as of ~Sept 3 before shifting to monthly updates. Cyclospora is often linked to contaminated fresh produce; specific food vehicles still require epidemiologic tracing. This is outbreak surveillance, not a single-brand recall list.

**Links:**

- [Food Safety News — Cyclospora outbreak near 20,000](https://www.foodsafetynews.com/2026/09/cdc-reporting-almost-20-000-confirmed-patients-in-cyclospora-outbreak/)

**Commentary:** Wash produce thoroughly in warm months, be cautious with pre-cut salads of unclear origin if high-risk, and seek care for prolonged watery diarrhea while mentioning raw-produce exposure.

## IV. Sleep & Mental Health

### 11. Closed-loop auditory stimulation: Pink-noise bursts timed to slow-wave peaks boosted CSF flow waves during human sleep (Sleep)
**Summary:** *Science Translational Medicine* (MIT News 2026-09-09; DOI: 10.1126/scitranslmed.aea5469) by Levitt, Lewis and colleagues delivered closed-loop auditory stimulation inside an MRI scanner to 14 healthy volunteers: ~50-ms pink-noise bursts locked to non-REM slow-wave peaks. Stimulation increased amplitudes of both slow electrical waves and cerebrospinal-fluid (CSF) flow waves; fMRI suggested vascular constrict–dilate pumping as a driver. Small acute mechanistic study—amyloid clearance and cognitive benefit are unproven; consumer pink-noise apps typically cannot phase-lock to slow waves.

**Links:**

- [MIT News — Pink noise and CSF flow](https://news.mit.edu/2026/pink-noise-burst-may-mean-more-restorative-sleep-0909)
- [DOI: 10.1126/scitranslmed.aea5469](https://doi.org/10.1126/scitranslmed.aea5469)

**Commentary:** Protect deep sleep with regular schedules, less alcohol, and less late-night light—do not expect arbitrary pink-noise tracks to “flush the brain.”

### 12. RCT: Behavioral-health game PlaySmart improved adolescent depressive symptoms over 12 months; no significant anxiety difference (Mental health)
**Summary:** *JAMA Network Open* (DOI: 10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2026.29173; Healio 2026-09-10) randomized 532 adolescents aged 16–19 (baseline mean PHQ-8 ~6.91, mild) to PlaySmart or commercial-game controls on school iPads 1–2×/week for 40–60 minutes over up to 6 weeks (~5 hours total). At 12 months, mean PHQ-8 was ~5.3 (intervention) vs ~6.42 (control), with a significant group-by-time interaction (p=0.03); GAD-7 anxiety did not differ. Early gains in beliefs about psychological services partly mediated later depression benefits. This is prevention, not treatment of clinical depression—individual effects were modest (~1 point) but potentially scalable.

**Links:**

- [Healio — PlaySmart and adolescent depression](https://www.healio.com/news/psychiatry/20260910/specialized-video-game-linked-to-improvement-in-depressive-symptoms-for-adolescents)
- [DOI: 10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2026.29173](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2026.29173)

**Commentary:** Schools can use quality prevention games as an early engagement door—never as a substitute for clinical care when moderate–severe depression is present.

## V. Evidence & Clarification / Supplements

### 13. Multicenter double-blind RCT: KSM-66 ashwagandha root extract 600 mg/day for 8 weeks matched placebo on liver, kidney, and hematology labs (Supplement safety)
**Summary:** *Phytotherapy Research* (DOI: 10.1002/ptr.70315; NutraIngredients 2026-09-10) enrolled 1,002 adults aged 18–65 across sites in India, the United States, Australia, Portugal, and Africa, randomized to KSM-66 root extract 600 mg/day (300 mg twice daily) or placebo; all completed 8 weeks. Primary endpoint was safety: AST/ALT, creatinine, and blood counts stayed within physiological ranges; no serious adverse events in either arm; most rated tolerability excellent or good. Limitations include short duration and limited non-Indian representation; European regulatory concerns about ashwagandha are not settled by this single trial.

**Links:**

- [NutraIngredients — KSM-66 ashwagandha safety trial](https://www.nutraingredients.com/Article/2026/09/10/study-finds-ksm-66-ashwagandha-root-extract-safe-in-sample-of-more-than-1000-adults/)
- [DOI: 10.1002/ptr.70315](https://doi.org/10.1002/ptr.70315)

**Commentary:** Eight-week lab safety ≠ universal suitability or proven benefit; people with thyroid or liver disease, or who are pregnant/breastfeeding, should ask a clinician and use standardized root extracts—not mystery powders.
