## I. Nutrition & Diet

### 1. Nature Metabolism: after nutritionally matched meals, glucose area was similar, while insulin stayed higher from 40 to 120 minutes after the ultra-processed meal (Nutrition)
**Summary:** Zach Hutelin, Alexandra G. DiFeliceantonio, and colleagues published a randomized crossover trial in Nature Metabolism on October 5 (doi: 10.1038/s42255-026-01619-4). Medical News Today reported it on October 11. Of 57 adults aged 18 to 45 with a healthy weight, 32 completed both metabolic sessions and 52 completed functional MRI while viewing food pictures. The ultra-processed and non-ultra-processed meals of about 300 kcal differed by no more than about 1.6% in energy, macronutrients, fiber, sodium, and water. Glucose area under the curve did not differ, but insulin area was higher after the ultra-processed meal, with higher insulin from 40 to 120 minutes; over three hours, energy expenditure and fat oxidation were higher and carbohydrate oxidation was lower. The between-meal difference in peak carbohydrate oxidation was associated with striatal responses to food cues. Bids for the two food categories were similar. The authors note that this was one small meal in young adults of healthy weight, and that the protein sources differed.

**Links:**

- [Nature Metabolism — Metabolic and neural responses to ultraprocessed foods](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42255-026-01619-4)
- [Medical News Today — October 11 report](https://www.medicalnewstoday.com/articles/even-when-nutrient-dense-ultra-processed-foods-alter-insulin-response)

**Commentary:** A similar nutrition label does not mean the same insulin response; swapping an ultra-processed plate for intact foods at the same calories is a reasonable next step, and this single meal does not show that the difference becomes diabetes.

### 2. Clinical Nutrition ESPEN: in 101 adults with fatty liver disease, ice cream sweetened with the protein brazzein produced about half the post-meal glucose rise (Nutrition)
**Summary:** Vasily A. Isakov and colleagues at Russia's Federal Research Centre of Nutrition, Biotechnology and Food Safety published a randomized double-blind crossover trial in Clinical Nutrition ESPEN (doi: 10.1016/j.clnesp.2026.105066). ScienceAlert reported it on October 10 and Al Jazeera on October 11. One hundred one adults with metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease ate 100 grams of vanilla ice cream on three separate days: sucrose, brazzein, or brazzein plus inulin. Continuous glucose monitoring showed that the post-meal rise with the latter two was about half that of the sucrose version, and the reduction was more noticeable in people with type 2 diabetes. The report described no serious adverse effects. ScienceAlert quotes the paper as saying the sugar-free recipes also differed in other ingredients, so the effect cannot be attributed to brazzein alone. The authors say longer studies are needed before repeated substitution can be linked to liver, metabolic, or behavioral benefits.

**Links:**

- [Clinical Nutrition ESPEN — Brazzein ice cream and post-meal glucose](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.clnesp.2026.105066)
- [ScienceAlert — October 10 report](https://www.sciencealert.com/a-protein-2000-times-sweeter-than-sugar-halved-ice-creams-blood-sugar-response)

**Commentary:** For someone with fatty liver or diabetes who still wants dessert, this protein sweetener is an experimental swap for one glucose spike, not evidence that it reduces liver fat or replaces glucose-lowering care.

### 3. Food & Function: 31 adults aged 60 to 75 who ate 60 grams of skin-roasted peanuts daily for 16 weeks showed a favorable blood-flow response to insulin in memory-related brain regions (Nutrition)
**Summary:** On October 10, Medical Xpress reported a randomized crossover trial by Lucia Kerkhof, Peter Joris, and colleagues at NUTRIM, Maastricht University Medical Center, published in Food & Function (doi: 10.1039/d6fo03208g). Thirty-one healthy adults aged 60 to 75 ate 60 grams of unsalted, skin-roasted peanuts daily for 16 weeks and ate none during the control period. Arterial-spin-labeling MRI showed that, after intranasal insulin, cerebral blood flow in one right-hemisphere cluster covering parts of the lingual gyrus, hippocampus, parahippocampal gyrus, and posterior cingulate differed from the control period by −5.4±5.4 mL per 100 g of tissue per minute. Funding came from The Peanut Institute Foundation; the report says the funder had no role in design, conduct, analysis, or writing. This paper does not provide a cognitive-test score that can be read on its own.

**Links:**

- [Food & Function — Peanuts and regional brain insulin responsiveness](https://doi.org/10.1039/d6fo03208g)
- [Medical Xpress — October 10 report](https://medicalxpress.com/news/2026-10-daily-peanut-consumption-beneficially-affects.html)

**Commentary:** Adults over 60 who already eat nuts can keep unsalted skin-on peanuts inside the day's calories, but a 31-person, industry-funded blood-flow result is not evidence that peanuts prevent Alzheimer's disease.

## II. Exercise & Training Science

### 4. International Journal of Obesity: in a dose-response meta-analysis of 34 observational studies, more physical activity in pregnancy lined up with lower risks of excessive weight gain and a large-for-gestational-age infant (Training)
**Summary:** Ziqin Lin, Liuwei Zhang, Ran Li, and colleagues published a dose-response meta-analysis of observational studies in the International Journal of Obesity on October 11. Searches of eight databases left 34 independent studies, with activity harmonized to MET-hours per week. Compared with the lowest activity level, the highest level had a 30% lower risk of excessive gestational weight gain and a 16% lower risk of a large-for-gestational-age infant. Across pregnancy, 10, 20, and 50 MET-hours per week corresponded to 4%, 9%, and 20% lower risks of excessive gain. There was no significant dose-response in the second trimester; in the third trimester the same three doses corresponded to reductions of 8%, 16%, and 35%. This is a summary of observations, not a randomized exercise prescription.

**Links:**

- [International Journal of Obesity — Physical activity, excessive gestational weight gain, and large-for-gestational-age birth](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41366-026-02236-7)

**Commentary:** Pregnancy is not a reason to stop activity that is considered safe; staying active in the third trimester lined up here with a lower risk of excessive gain, and how much is appropriate still depends on obstetric advice rather than chasing 50 MET-hours a week.

### 5. Sport Sciences for Health: in 12 trained women, splitting the same total rest inside the set produced more endurance repetitions and lower fatigue ratings (Training)
**Summary:** Behzad Taati and colleagues published a randomized crossover study in Sport Sciences for Health on October 5. Twelve women aged 18 to 35 with at least two years of training completed traditional sets, 10-second intra-set rests, and 30-second intra-set rests on knee extension, with total rest fixed at 120 seconds. The protocol was four sets at 80% of one-repetition maximum, followed by an endurance test at 60%. Endurance repetitions were 12.8±1.2, 15.8±2.2, and 18.4±1.7. Post-exercise fatigue ratings were 7.00±0.74, 5.25±1.14, and 4.08±1.68. Blood pressure, heart rate, and rate-pressure product did not differ significantly.

**Links:**

- [Sport Sciences for Health — Cluster sets versus traditional sets in women](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11332-026-01944-x)

**Commentary:** With the same total rest, moving some of it inside the set may let trained women complete more knee-extension repetitions and feel less fatigued in that session; this was an acute test in 12 people and one exercise, and it does not say how muscle mass would differ after several weeks.

### 6. Scientific Reports: among 3,093 All of Us participants, higher step counts tracked with better scores on several cognitive tasks, and higher systolic pressure tracked with worse delay discounting (Training)
**Summary:** Olaoluwa O. Okusaga, Yi-Sian Lin, and Elizabeth Atkinson published an observational analysis in Scientific Reports on October 11. The sample was 3,093 All of Us participants with Fitbit step counts and blood-pressure records spanning up to about 14 years. Baseline activity was positively associated with the gradual-onset continuous performance task (β 0.31, 95% CI 0.18 to 0.44), the delay-discounting task (β 0.05, 0.04 to 0.07), and the flanker task (β 0.26, 0.17 to 0.35). Baseline systolic pressure was negatively associated with delay discounting (β −0.01, −0.02 to −0.004). Step-count clusters averaged about 5,108, 8,875, and 14,161 steps a day; the two higher clusters scored higher than the lowest on those three tasks. Emotion recognition was not among the significant results in the abstract.

**Links:**

- [Scientific Reports — Steps, blood pressure, and cognition](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-74379-6)

**Commentary:** Moving from about 5,000 steps toward 9,000 lined up with better attention and decision-task scores, alongside keeping systolic pressure in range; this is an association in people who wear trackers, not a trial showing that walking treats cognitive impairment.

## III. Sleep & Mental Health

### 7. Sleep Medicine: in 462 working adults in Singapore tracked by a ring for up to a year, a personal definition of short sleep fit next-day state better than a single six-hour line (Sleep)
**Summary:** On October 11, StudyFinds reported an observational study by Chun Siong Soon, Michael W. L. Chee, and colleagues at the National University of Singapore, published in Sleep Medicine (doi: 10.1016/j.sleep.2026.109294) and registered as NCT06594549. Four hundred sixty-two healthy working adults, average age about 40, wore an Oura ring and completed daily questionnaires for up to a year. A personal short-sleep line was set one hour below each person's 75th percentile of sleep, and compared with a uniform cutoff under six hours. Both methods flagged about one third of nights, but not the same people or nights. About 76% of people under the uniform rule, and about 86% under the personal rule, usually had a short-sleep streak of only one night; when short nights stacked, mood, stress, and physical markers worsened with each added night. After a short night on a weekday, the next night recovered only about 50 extra minutes. Chee disclosed a board and advisory relationship with Oura, and the company supplied the multi-country comparison data.

**Links:**

- [Sleep Medicine — A personalized definition of short sleep from long-term wearables](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.sleep.2026.109294)
- [StudyFinds — October 11 report](https://studyfinds.com/some-people-sleep-under-5-hours-and-show-no-warning-signs-study-finds/)

**Commentary:** One short night matters less than several nights in a row below your own usual pattern; blood pressure was measured only at enrollment, and the senior investigator has a relationship with the ring company, so "I have always slept five hours, so I am fine" is not the finding.

### 8. BMC Psychology: among 120 patients with poor sleep after intensive care, adding three months of internet cognitive behavioral therapy to eszopiclone improved sleep and depression questionnaires more (Sleep)
**Summary:** Yanqiu Xia, Lijun Cui, and colleagues at the Affiliated Hospital of North Sichuan Medical College published a single-center randomized trial in BMC Psychology on October 11, registry ChiCTR2500104904. From June to September 2025, 120 patients with sleep disturbance who had moved from intensive care to a general ward were split into two groups of 60. The control group received eszopiclone plus routine sleep-hygiene education. The intervention group received the same medication plus three months of internet-based cognitive behavioral therapy. At the end of treatment and one month later, the intervention group improved more on the Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index, the Insomnia Severity Index, the Hamilton Depression Rating Scale, and the SF-36. Anxiety did not differ between groups at the end of treatment and did differ at follow-up. The abstract does not report the point differences on each scale.

**Links:**

- [BMC Psychology — Internet cognitive behavioral therapy for ICU survivors](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40359-026-05702-z)

**Commentary:** Someone whose sleep stayed poor after intensive care can ask whether cognitive behavioral therapy, including an online program, can be added to medicine a clinician has already prescribed; this was one hospital, and it is not a reason to start or stop a sleep drug alone.

## IV. Evidence & Clarification

### 9. BMC Sports Science, Medicine and Rehabilitation: 22 trained men who took 8 grams of L-citrulline or 12 grams of citrulline malate 60 minutes before lifting did not complete more repetitions to failure (Evidence)
**Summary:** Davoud Bayat, Mohammad Azizi, and colleagues published a double-blind, placebo-controlled, counterbalanced crossover trial in BMC Sports Science, Medicine and Rehabilitation on October 6. Twenty-two resistance-trained men made three visits a week apart and, 60 minutes before training, took L-citrulline, citrulline malate, or placebo in random order. The doses, 8 grams and 12 grams, were higher than those commonly used. Training was five sets to failure of barbell back squat and bench press at 70% of one-repetition maximum. Time effects were significant, but neither supplement showed a treatment or interaction effect on total repetitions, nitrate/nitrite, lactate, urea, perceived exertion, or muscle soreness.

**Links:**

- [BMC Sports Science, Medicine and Rehabilitation — Acute citrulline and resistance-training performance](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13102-026-02139-0)

**Commentary:** Taking citrulline or citrulline malate an hour before lifting did not buy extra squat or bench repetitions in this acute trial of 22 men; sleep, protein, and the training plan are the clearer places to spend effort.

### 10. Scientific Reports: in a cross-section of 377 overweight or obese Iranian women, a higher dietary acid load was only mildly related to general-health scores, and the total quality-of-life score was not (Evidence)
**Summary:** Scientific Reports published a cross-sectional study online on October 11. Researchers estimated potential renal acid load and net endogenous acid production from a food-frequency questionnaire in 377 overweight or obese adult women, and assessed quality of life with the SF-36. After adjustment for age, body mass index, physical activity, smoking, marital status, education, occupation, supplements, and total energy, each higher step of net endogenous acid production was associated with a general-health score β of 3.23 (95% CI 0.05 to 6.40). In the same model, the association with physical functioning was no longer significant (β 2.72, −0.33 to 5.78). Potential renal acid load was only borderline for both domains. Neither index was significantly associated with the total SF-36 score.

**Links:**

- [Scientific Reports — Dietary acid load and quality of life](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-72057-1)

**Commentary:** This cross-section is not a reason to eat more meat and cheese in order to raise dietary acid load; the total score was not better, and it does not show that an acid or alkaline diet improves health.
