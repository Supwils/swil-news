## I. Nutrition & Diet

### 1. PREMIER secondary analysis: lifestyle counseling plus DASH yields broader metabolomic gains (Nutrition / Metabolism)
**Summary:** George Washington University investigators and colleagues reanalyzed 690 adults from the randomized PREMIER trial (mostly prehypertension or stage 1 hypertension), published online in *The Journal of Nutrition* (online ~Nov 6, 2026; detailed in Chinese databases on Nov 9). Arms were advice-only, established lifestyle counseling, and established counseling plus DASH. Fasting plasma NMR metabolites were measured at baseline and 6 months; PCA yielded 13 patterns. Versus advice-only, established counseling lowered PC4, VLDL cholesterol, and saturated fatty acids; adding DASH had broader effects—lowering PC9, PC11, and PC12, raising omega-3 fatty acids, and reducing branched-chain and aromatic amino acids, lactate, citrate, and inflammatory GlycA. BMI change tracked multiple PCs; weight loss appeared the main driver, with DASH adding a wider metabolic footprint. Secondary analysis, 6-month follow-up, and metabolite—not clinical-event—endpoints limit inference.

**Links:**

- [The Journal of Nutrition — DASH lifestyle counseling and metabolomic profiles](https://jn.nutrition.org/article/S0022-3166(26)00580-8/abstract)
- [Ebiotrade — Nov 9 Chinese summary](https://www.ebiotrade.com/newsf/2026-11/20261109000421019.htm)

**Commentary:** Pair sustainable weight loss with DASH-style diet quality (more produce and whole grains, less sodium, red meat, and added sugar)—advice alone is unlikely to rewrite your blood metabolic fingerprint.

### 2. *BMJ Nutrition* secondary analysis: swapping animal for plant protein on a low-fat vegan diet tracks weight loss (Nutrition / Protein)
**Summary:** Barnard, Kahleova and colleagues secondary-analyzed a 36-week randomized crossover trial of 62 overweight adults in *BMJ Nutrition, Prevention & Health*; Digital Today followed on Nov 9, 2026. Participants completed 16 weeks each of a low-fat vegan diet and a Mediterranean diet (4-week washout), without calorie prescriptions. On the vegan diet, total protein fell ~33.3 g/day, animal protein ~47.7 g/day, and plant protein rose ~14.4 g/day; the Mediterranean arm showed no significant protein or weight shifts. The parent trial reported ~6.0 kg mean weight loss on vegan versus ~0 kg on Mediterranean. Changes in total and animal protein correlated positively with weight change; plant-protein change correlated negatively; shifts in isoleucine, lysine, methionine, and tyrosine intake also tracked weight positively. Authors stress co-occurring fat, carbohydrate, and energy-density changes, so protein source is not proven causal. The sample was mostly middle-aged women.

**Links:**

- [BMJ Nutrition — plant vs animal protein and body weight](https://nutrition.bmj.com/content/early/2026/10/20/bmjnph-2026-001511)
- [Digital Today — Nov 9 report](https://www.digitaltoday.co.kr/en/view/122998/plant-protein-may-help-weight-loss-more-than-animal-protein-study)

**Commentary:** For weight loss, try replacing some red/processed meat with legumes and whole grains while watching calories and fat density—do not read total-protein drops as advice for people at sarcopenia risk.

### 3. *PNAS*: digestion-resistant plant proteins (Prif) plus fiber rewire gut phenol metabolite profiles (Nutrition / Mechanism)
**Summary:** AbuSalim and Rabinowitz (Princeton Branch, Ludwig Institute) report in *PNAS* that plant proteins resistant to host digestion (“Prif”) act with dietary fiber to shift gut microbial phenol metabolites away from tyrosine-derived phenol sulfate and p-cresol sulfate (linked to uremic toxicity and worse outcomes in some cancer/kidney settings) toward phenylalanine-derived hippuric acid and 3-phenylpropionate (tied to healthier weight and gut markers). Stable-isotope tracing showed harmful phenols largely from bacterial breakdown of host secreted proteins such as mucus; beneficial phenols almost entirely from dietary digestion-resistant protein; fiber suppressed mucus-protein catabolism. ScienceDaily summarized the work on Nov 9, 2026. Evidence is largely mouse tracing and mechanism work—human extrapolation is limited; a companion *Nature Metabolism* paper also shows host cells can make many indoles/phenols once attributed only to microbes.

**Links:**

- [PNAS — digestion-resistant plant proteins and healthy phenol profiles](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2605226123)
- [ScienceDaily — Nov 9 explainer](http://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/11/261105083622.htm)

**Commentary:** “Eat more plants” is not only a fiber-gram contest—varied legumes, whole grains, and produce supply both fiber and hard-to-digest protein; treat this as mechanism, not a new supplement pitch.

### 4. UK sugar-rationing natural experiment: longer early-life exposure linked to lower adult osteoporosis hazard (Nutrition / Life course)
**Summary:** In *The Journal of nutrition, health and aging*, investigators used the end of UK postwar sugar rationing as a natural experiment among 65,346 UK-born UK Biobank participants born October 1951–March 1956 (mean baseline age ~54.6 years); Ebiotrade detailed the study on Nov 9, 2026. Birth month defined never-exposed, in-utero-only, in-utero plus to age 1, and in-utero plus to age 2. Over median ~13.8 years, 1,606 hospital-recorded incident osteoporosis cases occurred. Versus never exposed, fully adjusted HRs were 0.94, 0.78, and 0.77 (P for trend = 0.006). With attained age as the time scale, the longest-exposure HR attenuated to 0.93 (0.81–1.06), limiting causal claims; no interaction with sex or osteoporosis PRS. Exposure is birth-date defined, individual sugar intake unmeasured, and age/cohort effects hard to separate.

**Links:**

- [EM Consulte — sugar rationing and adult osteoporosis (abstract)](https://www.em-consulte.com/article/1850085/association-of-sugar-rationing-during-the-first-10)
- [Ebiotrade — Nov 9 detailed summary](https://www.ebiotrade.com/newsf/2026-11/20261109000420043.htm)

**Commentary:** The actionable takeaway remains: minimize added sugar in pregnancy and the first two years—this is a life-course environment signal, not a midlife “rationing redo” prescription.

## II. Exercise & Training Science

### 5. *Nature Medicine* RCT: thrice-weekly resistance training cuts ~1.1 kg lean soft-tissue loss on tirzepatide (Training / Pharmacotherapy)
**Summary:** Green, Yeap and colleagues treated 120 adults aged 50–70 with overweight or obesity (60 men; 60 postmenopausal women) with tirzepatide for 40 weeks and randomized them to supervised resistance exercise three times weekly or usual activities; results appeared in *Nature Medicine* on Nov 9, 2026. Weight and fat loss were similar (~−22 kg body weight; fat ~−17–18 kg). Total lean soft-tissue loss was −3.57 kg with resistance versus −4.67 kg with usual activity (between-group difference ~1.09 kg, 95% CI 0.43–1.75). Strength rose with exercise and fell without it. Exercise adherence was ~91%. Diabetes and cardiovascular disease were excluded; DXA measured lean soft tissue, not muscle alone; Eli Lilly supplied drug and reviewed protocol/manuscript.

**Links:**

- [Nature Medicine — resistance exercise with tirzepatide RCT](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-026-04752-5)

**Commentary:** On GLP-1/GIP weight-loss drugs, treat thrice-weekly full-body resistance training as standard care—not optional—or you may lose strength along with fat.

### 6. *Frontiers in Endocrinology* RCT: morning and evening exercise both help vessels in older T2D; stiffness signal favors evening (Training / Timing)
**Summary:** Shanghai University of Sport investigators randomized 66 adults aged 60–75 with type 2 diabetes who had been inactive for 3 months to morning (09:00–10:10), evening (19:00–20:10), or control; remotely supervised moderate aerobic plus elastic-band resistance, three ~70-minute sessions weekly for 12 weeks. Final analysis included 58 people. Both exercise arms improved flow-mediated dilation (FMD) and lowered brachial-ankle pulse-wave velocity (baPWV), systolic BP, and mean arterial pressure. In an exploratory head-to-head comparison, baPWV fell more with evening than morning exercise (~−221 vs −89 cm/s; group × time p = 0.034); plasma nitric oxide rose significantly within the evening arm but not the morning arm, with a nonsignificant timing interaction (p = 0.051). The primary three-arm analysis did not establish an optimal time; the sample was small, lacked an afternoon arm, and lifestyle was incompletely controlled. ReachMD covered the work on Nov 9, 2026.

**Links:**

- [Frontiers in Endocrinology — morning vs evening exercise and vascular function in older T2D](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/endocrinology/articles/10.3389/fendo.2026.1976870/full)
- [ReachMD — Nov 9 briefing](https://reachmd.com/news/evening-exercise-shows-arterial-stiffness-signal-in-type-2-diabetes/2488962/)

**Commentary:** The best time is the one you can stick with; if mornings and evenings are equal and sleep is protected, treat evening sessions as a possible bonus—not a reason to skip training.

## III. Public Health & Disease Prevention

### 7. NYU/AHA: ~4 weeks of bedroom HEPA filtration dampens glucose swings in prediabetes (Public health / Environment)
**Summary:** Wittkopp, Newman and NYU Langone colleagues presented a double-blind sham-controlled trial at AHA Scientific Sessions 2026 (~Nov 8; MedicalXpress followed Nov 9) in >150 New York City adults with prediabetes and no known atherosclerosis. Real versus sham portable bedroom air cleaners ran ~1 month. True filtration cut fine particles ~36%. Versus sham, daily glucose variability fell ~8%, and post-breakfast excess glucose and peak glucose fell ~17% and ~13%. The protocol appears in *American Heart Journal*. Results are not yet fully peer-reviewed as outcome papers; short duration, single city, and exclusion of established diabetes limit generalization—filters are not a diet/exercise substitute.

**Links:**

- [NYU Langone — cleaner bedroom air and prediabetes glucose](https://nyulangone.org/news/cleaner-bedroom-air-may-help-manage-blood-sugar-prediabetes)
- [MedicalXpress — Nov 9 report](https://medicalxpress.com/news/2026-11-cleaner-bedroom-air-blood-sugar.html)

**Commentary:** Alongside diet and activity for prediabetes, treat bedroom particulate exposure as a modifiable lever—filtration is an adjunct, not a new glucose drug.

### 8. ADHERE-ASCVD age analysis: digital statin refill nudges work across ages (Public health / Adherence)
**Summary:** A prespecified age analysis of Kaiser Permanente Northern California’s ADHERE-ASCVD trial was presented as a Late Breaking Clinical Trial at AHA 2026 and published in *JACC*; the Division of Research posted on Nov 9, 2026. Among 20,604 adults with ASCVD or type 2 diabetes and prior statin adherence <80%, an extra portal/SMS/email refill reminder after the routine pharmacy notice was tested against usual communication. The parent analysis found 14-day refill rates of ~14.4% versus ~12% (~20% relative increase). By age (<45, 45–64, ≥65), adjusted risk differences were ~1.4, 2.4, and 3.2 percentage points, with no significant age interaction (p = 0.66); adults ≥65 responded to texts no worse than younger adults. Limited to digitally reachable patients; the endpoint is 14-day refill, not long-term adherence.

**Links:**

- [Kaiser Permanente DOR — digital statin outreach by age](https://divisionofresearch.kaiserpermanente.org/all-ages-digital-statin-refills/)
- [JACC — ADHERE-ASCVD age analysis](https://www.jacc.org/doi/10.1016/j.jacc.2026.10.063)

**Commentary:** Do not use “older adults don’t text” as a reason to skip outreach—high-risk patients who already have digital access still benefit from a clear automatic “why and how to refill” nudge.

## IV. Sleep, Circadian Rhythm & Mental Health

### 9. *iScience*: late chronotype plus late eating tracks metabolic-syndrome burden and gut-barrier changes in obesity (Sleep / Metabolism)
**Summary:** Bishehsari and colleagues at UTHealth Houston secondary-analyzed 87 adults with obesity from a randomized trial in *iScience*; the institutional release is dated Nov 9, 2026. Late chronotypes were more often in the higher metabolic-syndrome burden group than early chronotypes (~71% vs 46%, p = 0.025). About 76% were late eaters; combined late chronotype plus late eating showed a rising trend in high MetS burden. Circadian misalignment associated with higher intestinal permeability and a metabolic–inflammatory score, with lower abundance of short-chain-fatty-acid–linked bacteria; barrier improvements after a microbiome-targeted intervention tracked beneficial taxa. Observational and modest in size—does not prove that shifting schedules treats metabolic disease.

**Links:**

- [iScience — circadian misalignment, gut barrier, and metabolic inflammation](https://www.cell.com/iscience/fulltext/S2589-0042(26)03275-X)
- [UTHealth Houston — Nov 9 news release](https://www.uth.edu/news/story/eating-and-sleeping-late-linked-to-higher-health-risks-study-finds)

**Commentary:** Beyond food quality in obesity care, try moving the last meal and bedtime earlier—a modifiable behavior lever still at the association, not prescription, stage.

### 10. NPR / *British Journal of Psychiatry*: about one in four severe depression cases fit a proposed “circadian” subtype (Sleep / Mental health)
**Summary:** Tonini and University of Sydney colleagues, using the Australian Genetics of Depression Study (11,380 adults), classified 2,604 people (~23%) as a circadian depression subtype if they met ≥3 of six features—seasonality, delayed sleep midpoint, social jetlag, evening chronotype, hypersomnia, and morning tiredness—in *The British Journal of Psychiatry*. The subtype had earlier onset, more severe features, poorer self-reported SSRI/SNRI response (OR ~0.88–0.89), and higher polygenic risk for depression, anxiety, ADHD, bipolar disorder, and metabolic/inflammatory/late-sleep traits. NPR featured the work on Nov 9, 2026. The subtype is proposed, not formally established; sleep was self-reported without melatonin biomarkers; the cohort is Australian with European-ancestry genetics—needs independent replication and trials.

**Links:**

- [The British Journal of Psychiatry — circadian subtype of depression](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/the-british-journal-of-psychiatry/article/clinical-and-genetic-correlates-of-a-circadian-subtype-of-depression-in-the-australian-genetics-of-depression-study/A3429C15041E6BA0570968117909DDB6)
- [NPR — Nov 9 feature](https://www.npr.org/2026/11/09/nx-s1-6027788/for-some-people-depression-may-be-tied-to-their-circadian-rhythms)

**Commentary:** If depression comes with stubborn late sleep, seasonal swings, and morning exhaustion, tell your clinician—clock-targeted options may be worth discussing; this is not a DIY cue to stop antidepressants for melatonin.

## V. Evidence & Clarification

### 11. UK COT: insufficient evidence on Echinacea in the maternal diet—pregnant people should avoid supplements unless advised (Evidence / Supplements)
**Summary:** The UK Committee on Toxicity (COT), at SACN’s request, published on Nov 9, 2026 a statement and lay summary on potential health risks from Echinacea in the maternal diet. Animal and short-term non-pregnant human use are generally well tolerated, with rare allergies including anaphylaxis; pregnancy-outcome studies rely on self-report with incomplete dose/product detail. EMA/MHRA already advise against medicinal Echinacea in pregnancy and lactation. COT concludes data are insufficient for a robust safety assessment in pregnancy/breastfeeding; harm at short-term food/supplement levels is considered unlikely but uncertainty is large. Precaution: pregnant people should avoid Echinacea-containing food supplements unless a qualified clinician recommends them.

**Links:**

- [GOV.UK — COT Echinacea maternal-diet lay summary](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/echinacea/statement-on-the-potential-health-risk-from-echinacea-in-the-maternal-diet-lay-summary)
- [GOV.UK — full scientific statement](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/echinacea/statement-on-the-potential-health-risks-of-echinacea-in-the-maternal-diet)

**Commentary:** “Natural cold herb” is not a pregnancy free pass—when evidence is thin, default to not taking it rather than inventing post-hoc safety stories.
