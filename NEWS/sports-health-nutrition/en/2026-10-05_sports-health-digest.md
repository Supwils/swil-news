## I. Nutrition & Diet

### 1. Nature Metabolism: a calorie-matched ultraprocessed meal still raised insulin, while glucose area under the curve did not (Nutrition)
**Summary:** Nature Metabolism on October 5 published an open-access randomized crossover study by Zach Hutelin, Alexandra G. DiFeliceantonio, and colleagues. The full study enrolled 57 healthy-weight adults (mean age 26.21 ± 6.85 years, body mass index 22.75 ± 1.87); 32 completed both metabolic sessions and 52 completed functional MRI. The test meals were about 300 kilocalories, eaten within 10 minutes, and matched within 1.6% on weight, energy density, macronutrients, glycemic index, sodium, and related measures, differing by whether they counted as ultraprocessed under the Nova classification. Insulin area under the curve was higher after the ultraprocessed meal (P < 0.001; insulin analysis n = 31), while glucose area under the curve did not differ (P = 0.92). Metabolic-rate area under the curve was higher (P = 0.02), and respiratory-exchange-ratio area under the curve was higher after the non-ultraprocessed meal (P = 0.01). The authors describe attenuated carbohydrate oxidation and higher fat oxidation after the ultraprocessed meal. Subjective food value did not differ, but brain responses correlated with food valuation were positive for the non-ultraprocessed foods and negative for the ultraprocessed foods in visual cortex and striatum.

**Links:**

- [Nature Metabolism — Metabolic and neural responses to ultraprocessed foods](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42255-026-01619-4)
- [Nature Metabolism — Research briefing](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42255-026-01618-5)

**Commentary:** When calories and macronutrients on the label are close, the less processed version can still ask for less insulin; this was one acute meal in young adults at a healthy weight, continuous glucose would have missed the difference, and a single meal does not establish that ultraprocessed food by itself causes obesity.

### 2. Scientific Reports: pregnancies with preeclampsia had lower Mediterranean diet scores, and extra-virgin olive oil tracked with blood pressure markers (Nutrition)
**Summary:** Marta Larroya, Irene Casas, Fatima Crispi, Sara Castro-Barquero, and colleagues at BCNatal in Barcelona (Hospital Clinic and Hospital Sant Joan de Deu) published a case-control study in Scientific Reports on October 5. Eighty-one preeclamptic pregnancies and 123 uncomplicated pregnancies completed a 151-item food-frequency questionnaire and a pregnancy-adapted Mediterranean Diet Adherence Screener at enrollment. Scores were lower in the preeclampsia group (6.7 vs. 8.5 points, p < 0.001). Adjusted odds ratios were 1.34 (1.09–1.64) for saturated fatty acids and 1.95 (1.30–2.92) for ultraprocessed food, and 0.33 (0.23–0.46) for extra-virgin olive oil, 0.41 (0.25–0.69) for vegetables, and 0.60 (0.41–0.88) for fruit. The abstract does not state the intake increment those ratios refer to. Extra-virgin olive oil correlated with maternal blood pressure, uterine-artery Doppler pulsatility index, and the angiogenic profile. Ethics approvals were Hospital Clinic de Barcelona HCB/2021/0236 and Hospital Sant Joan de Deu PIC-89-21.

**Links:**

- [Scientific Reports — Maternal diet and preeclampsia](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-72517-8)

**Commentary:** Before and during pregnancy, extra-virgin olive oil, vegetables, and fruit can sit on the plate in place of ultraprocessed foods; diet was recalled after the illness was already present, so olive oil here is not a drug that prevents or treats preeclampsia.

### 3. Food & Function: 60 grams a day of unsalted skin-roasted peanuts for 16 weeks changed insulin-related blood flow in one memory-related brain cluster (Nutrition)
**Summary:** On October 5 the Peanut Institute distributed, via EurekAlert, results from NUTRIM at Maastricht University Medical Center, published online in Food & Function (DOI: 10.1039/D6FO03208G). Peter J. Joris, PhD candidate Lucia Kerkhof, and colleagues ran a randomized controlled crossover trial in 31 healthy adults aged 60–75 who ate 60 grams (about two servings) of unsalted, skin-roasted peanuts daily for 16 weeks, with a no-peanut control period. Arterial-spin-labeling MRI measured regional cerebral blood flow after intranasal insulin. Whole-brain analysis found one right-hemisphere cluster covering parts of the lingual gyrus, hippocampus, parahippocampal gyrus, and posterior cingulate; after peanuts, the blood-flow response to insulin in that cluster differed from control by −5.4 ± 5.4 mL/100 g tissue/min, which the authors describe as beneficial. The Peanut Institute Foundation funded the work and had no role in design, conduct, analysis, or writing. The same group reported in Clinical Nutrition in 2025 that this 16-week protocol raised global cerebral blood flow by 3.6% and verbal memory by 5.8%; the October 5 release does not give a new memory-test score.

**Links:**

- [Food & Function — Unsalted skin-roasted peanuts and brain insulin responsiveness](https://doi.org/10.1039/D6FO03208G)
- [EurekAlert — Peanut Institute release, October 5](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/1146448)

**Commentary:** Sixty grams is about two servings and belongs inside the day's energy budget; a brain-imaging signal in 31 people, funded by a peanut foundation, is a reason to keep skin-on nuts in the diet and not a dose that prevents Alzheimer's disease.

### 4. Scientific Reports: eight weeks of a Mediterranean diet lowered weight, blood pressure, and some inflammatory markers in psychiatric patients with metabolic syndrome (Nutrition)
**Summary:** Ali Abdullah Alyousef, Ghedeir M. Alshammari, and colleagues at King Saud University, with collaborators at the Mental Health Hospital in Al-Ahsa, published a randomized crossover trial in Scientific Reports on October 5. Thirty adults with a psychiatric disorder and metabolic syndrome (15 men and 15 women) received a Mediterranean diet and the standard hospital diet for eight weeks each. Compared with the hospital diet, the Mediterranean diet significantly reduced body weight, percentage body fat, visceral fat, blood pressure, fasting blood glucose, and total cholesterol (p ≤ 0.05). Interleukin-1 beta, interleukin-12, and tumor necrosis factor-alpha decreased, while anti-inflammatory cytokines stayed in the normal range. Most metabolic hormones remained within reference ranges; triiodothyronine and melanocyte-stimulating hormone were elevated. The abstract reports significance and does not give the size of the weight or blood-pressure change. Funding was King Saud University ORF-2026-84.

**Links:**

- [Scientific Reports — Mediterranean diet in psychiatric patients with metabolic syndrome](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-72996-9)

**Commentary:** When a psychiatric illness and metabolic syndrome occur together, the care team can move hospital meals toward a Mediterranean pattern; thirty people and an abstract without effect sizes are not grounds to stop medication or to copy the result onto unsupervised outpatient eating.

## II. Exercise & Training Science

### 5. Sport Sciences for Health: with total rest held constant, intra-set rests let trained women complete more endurance repetitions (Training)
**Summary:** Behzad Taati, Fatemeh Karami, and colleagues published a randomized counterbalanced crossover trial in Sport Sciences for Health on October 5, approved as IR.SSRC.REC.1402.182 by the Sport Sciences Research Institute. Twelve recreationally trained women aged 18–35, with at least two years of resistance training and 3.4 ± 0.7 sessions a week over the prior six months, completed traditional sets, 10-second cluster sets, and 30-second cluster sets of seated knee extension, each with 120 seconds of total rest. They performed four sets at 80% of one-repetition maximum, then an endurance test at 60%. Endurance repetitions were 18.4 ± 1.7 for the 30-second clusters, 15.8 ± 2.2 for the 10-second clusters, and 12.8 ± 1.2 for traditional sets (p < 0.0001). Post-exercise fatigue ratings were 4.08 ± 1.68, 5.25 ± 1.14, and 7.00 ± 0.74 (p = 0.0002). Fatigue-to-work and cardiovascular-load-to-work ratios were lower with both cluster structures (p ≤ 0.0013). Post-set blood pressure, mean arterial pressure, heart rate, rate-pressure product, and their areas under the curve did not differ (p > 0.05).

**Links:**

- [Sport Sciences for Health — Cluster sets versus traditional sets with equated rest](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11332-026-01944-x)

**Commentary:** Trained lifters who want more high-quality knee-extension reps in the same total rest can split that rest inside the set, with 30 seconds ahead of 10; twelve women, one acute session, unchanged post-set blood pressure, and no evidence yet on longer-term muscle or fat change.

## III. Sleep & Mental Health

### 6. Sleep Science and Practice: higher lifestyle scores in U.S. adults tracked with longer sleep and less trouble sleeping, and depression accounted for part of the link (Sleep)
**Summary:** Hongxia Zhou, Jiafei Yang, and colleagues published a cross-sectional analysis of NHANES 2013–2020 in Sleep Science and Practice on October 5, with 5,532 U.S. adults. A healthy-lifestyle score from 0 to 6 covered diet quality, smoking, alcohol, body mass index, physical activity, and sedentary time. Depression used the PHQ-9 (0–27). Sleep was duration plus trouble sleeping. Each extra lifestyle point was associated with 0.066 more hours of sleep (95% CI 0.027–0.105, P = 0.002) and an odds ratio of 0.786 (0.730–0.847) for trouble sleeping. Compared with a poor score of 0–2, an optimal score of 5–6 meant 0.258 more hours of sleep (0.121–0.394); odds ratios for trouble sleeping were 0.619 at a score of 3–4 and 0.413 at 5–6. Exploratory mediation assigned 34.23% of the sleep-duration association (P = 0.027) and 46.01% of the trouble-sleeping association (P < 0.001) to depression. The authors describe a possible pathway in cross-sectional data.

**Links:**

- [Sleep Science and Practice — Lifestyle, depression, and sleep](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s41606-026-00204-8)

**Commentary:** Poor sleep is a prompt to look at smoking, alcohol, activity, and daytime mood together with hours in bed; 0.258 hours is about 15 minutes, and a cross-sectional mediation model does not show that treating depression will lengthen sleep.

### 8. Pennsylvania Department of Health: 1,004 measles cases in 2026, with two MMR doses still the main protection (Public health)
**Summary:** WFMZ on October 5 reported a Department of Health release: Pennsylvania has confirmed 1,004 measles cases in 2026 across 39 counties. Twenty-seven cases were added since October 2, with 198 hospitalizations and five confirmed measles-associated deaths. Four cases were in vaccinated people, under 1% of the total. Since the outbreak began in late April, state health center staff have given more than 5,700 MMR doses at 192 pop-up clinics, and department nurses have given more than 8,800 doses in 2026 including state health centers. Providers usually administer about 25,000 MMR doses a month statewide; August exceeded 49,000 and September exceeded 53,000. The department says two doses provide 97% lifetime protection. People with two documented doses do not need another; people with one dose or no record should talk with a clinician or pharmacist. Symptoms can start 7–21 days after exposure, and people are typically contagious from four days before through four days after the rash. The virus can remain infectious in air and on surfaces for up to two hours after the infected person leaves.

### 10. NHS England: spinal muscular atrophy is in an in-service evaluation of the newborn heel-prick test in participating areas (Public health)
**Summary:** BBC reported on October 5 that the NHS in England is running an in-service evaluation of whether spinal muscular atrophy should be added to the routine newborn blood-spot test. The heel prick is usually taken on day five of life and already checks for several rare, serious conditions. SMA is included only in participating areas during the evaluation, and it is not yet a nationwide routine item. The Robert Jones and Agnes Hunt Orthopaedic Hospital in Shropshire and Birmingham Heartlands Hospital provide the West Midlands specialist pathway: a screen that suggests SMA leads to urgent assessment, confirmatory tests, and treatment. Consultant paediatric neurologist Tracey Willis said effective treatments now exist and that earlier treatment is better, with some children then able to sit, stand, and walk. SMA affects about one in 10,000 babies. The most severe form, type 1, usually appears in the first six months and, without treatment, can severely limit life expectancy and involve the breathing muscles.

**Links:**

- [BBC — SMA newborn screening evaluation](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/cxyvry0z1l90o)

**Commentary:** In the English areas taking part, a day-five heel prick that suggests SMA is a prompt for confirmatory testing before weakness is obvious; the evaluation is not yet routine screening for every baby in the United Kingdom.

## V. Evidence & Clarification

### 11. Scientific Reports: beverage-type ultraprocessed intake was inversely associated with colorectal cancer, while its metabolite signature was positively associated (Evidence)
**Summary:** Jinfen Tang, Yingming Jin, and Jianfen Xu of Ningbo No. 2 Hospital, with Yangyang Xie, published an observational analysis in Scientific Reports on October 5. The sample was 94,991 participants with at least two valid 24-hour dietary recalls, complete covariates, and metabolomic data. Ethics approval came from the North West Multi-centre Research Ethics Committee in the United Kingdom. Beverage-type and non-beverage ultraprocessed foods were defined with Nova. Over a mean 11.45 years, 958 incident colorectal cancers occurred. After multivariable adjustment, each 1-SD increase in beverage-type ultraprocessed intake was associated with a 10.4% lower risk (HR 0.896, 95% CI 0.831–0.966, P = 0.004); the highest versus lowest quartile was HR 0.835 (0.709–0.984). Non-beverage ultraprocessed intake had no clear association. A 29-metabolite signature drawn from 251 quality-controlled metabolites was associated with a 10.4% higher risk per SD (HR 1.104, 1.022–1.193, P = 0.012). Both remained significant in a joint model. The authors write that dietary exposure and the signature are weakly correlated and should be read as separate observational associations; the inverse result is not evidence that beverage-type ultraprocessed foods are protective, and the signature may capture circulating metabolism beyond those drinks. Funding was Zhejiang medical science project 2024KY1562 and Ningbo No. 2 Hospital key discipline 2023-Y08.

**Links:**

- [Scientific Reports — Beverage-type ultraprocessed foods, a metabolomic signature, and colorectal cancer](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-73181-8)

**Commentary:** An inverse statistic for ultraprocessed drinks and colorectal cancer stays inside the authors' own limit: it is not evidence of protection. Sugar-sweetened drinks stay on the short list of foods to cut, and screening still follows age and family history.
