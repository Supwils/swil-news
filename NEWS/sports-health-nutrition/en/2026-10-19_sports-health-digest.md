## I. Nutrition and Diet

### 1. American Journal of Clinical Nutrition: A 67-metabolite plasma signature of Mediterranean-diet adherence tracked with lower long-term all-cause mortality in PREDIMED and three U.S. cohorts (Nutrition)
**Summary:** Estefania Toledo, Anacristina Rovayo, Miguel A. Martinez-Gonzalez, Frank B. Hu, and colleagues published an analysis online in the American Journal of Clinical Nutrition on October 19. They applied a previously derived 67-metabolite plasma signature of Mediterranean-diet adherence to all-cause mortality. In PREDIMED, 1,880 participants with baseline metabolomics (mean age 67.1 years; 57.5% women) were followed for 13.2 years, and 457 deaths were recorded. The hazard ratio for the highest versus lowest tertile of the baseline signature was 0.77 (95% CI 0.61-0.98); each standard-deviation increase was associated with 19% lower mortality (10%-26%). The signature was replicated in 12,704 participants from the Nurses' Health Studies I and II and the Health Professionals Follow-up Study: the highest versus lowest quintile hazard ratio was 0.77 (0.70-0.85). In those cohorts, the per-standard-deviation hazard ratio for all-cause death was 0.92 (0.89-0.95), and cancer-related death was not significant. Registration: ISRCTN35739639.

**Links:**

- [American Journal of Clinical Nutrition — Mediterranean-diet metabolomic signature and mortality](https://ajcn.nutrition.org/article/S0002-9165(26)00305-9/fulltext)

**Commentary:** The practical step is still a stable pattern of olive oil, vegetables, legumes, fish, and whole grains with less processed meat; this blood signature is a research marker of adherence and mortality, and the per-standard-deviation association was smaller in the U.S. cohorts than in PREDIMED.

### 2. Nutrition & Metabolism: Among 2,920 Tehran adults free of cardiovascular disease, each point on a gut-microbiota diet index had a hazard ratio of 0.83 over a median 12.5 years (Nutrition)
**Summary:** Mahdieh Golzarand, Parvin Mirmiran, and Fereidoun Azizi published a prospective cohort in Nutrition & Metabolism on October 16. The Tehran Lipid and Glucose Study included 2,920 adults aged 19 or older without cardiovascular disease at entry in 2006-2020 (mean age 39.3 years; 55% women). The dietary index for gut microbiota was calculated from a food-frequency questionnaire and stands in for diet features linked to the microbiota; microbiota were not measured. Over a median of 12.5 years, 233 incident cardiovascular events occurred. Each 1-point increase in the index had a hazard ratio of 0.83 (95% CI 0.77-0.89), and a restricted cubic spline showed a dose-response. Mediation analysis found that the association did not pass through the percentage of weight change, and the direction remained in analyses by sex and weight.

**Links:**

- [Nutrition & Metabolism — Gut-microbiota diet index and cardiovascular disease](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12986-026-01230-0)

**Commentary:** In this urban Iranian cohort, a diet scored as more microbiota-friendly tracked with fewer cardiovascular events even after accounting for weight change; the score is not a stool test, and the study does not show that changing a few foods prevents a heart attack.

## II. Exercise and Training Science

### 3. BMC Geriatrics: Across 61 randomized trials and 3,706 people with sarcopenia or frailty, elastic-band training had more certain evidence for grip strength than machine training had for knee extension (Training)
**Summary:** Kai Wang, Ting-Fu Lai, Hyuntae Park, and colleagues published a network meta-analysis in BMC Geriatrics on October 16. Searches through June 1, 2025, included 61 randomized trials and 3,706 participants. Rankings were graded with the GRADE minimally contextualized framework. Multicomponent training that included resistance work had the broadest benefits, especially for functional mobility, at low certainty. The network estimate for elastic-band resistance training on handgrip was a mean difference of 1.95 kg (95% CI 1.13-2.76; SUCRA 98.6%) at moderate certainty. Machine-based resistance training had a mean difference of 2.96 kg for knee-extension strength (1.59-4.34; SUCRA 65.3%) at low certainty. Dose-response analyses associated greater strength gains with higher-volume elastic-band work and with high-intensity machine work. PROSPERO registration: CRD420251042815.

**Links:**

- [BMC Geriatrics — Network meta-analysis of exercise for sarcopenia and frailty](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12877-026-08352-3)

**Commentary:** For sarcopenia or frailty, match the tool to the weak link: elastic bands are the better-supported option for grip, machines are a candidate for knee extension, and walking and chair rises still need a program that includes strength; the mobility ranking is low-certainty.

### 4. Scientific Reports: After 12 weeks of twice-weekly eccentric training, 37 community-dwelling older adults improved timed walking and single-fiber residual force enhancement (Training)
**Summary:** Hyo-Seong Yeo and Jae-Young Lim published a before-and-after training study in Scientific Reports on October 15. Thirty-seven community-dwelling older adults (70.4 ± 3.3 years) completed supervised eccentric resistance training on an Eccentron device twice a week for 12 weeks, with vastus lateralis biopsies before and after. Body weight and BMI did not change. Skeletal muscle mass (P = 0.017), thigh girth (P = 0.013), and calf girth (P = 0.030) increased. Timed up-and-go (P = 0.001) and stair climbing (P = 0.020) improved. Single-fiber specific force rose in type I (P = 0.017) and type IIa fibers (P = 0.012), unloaded shortening velocity rose in type I fibers (P < 0.001), and fiber cross-sectional area did not change. Absolute and relative residual force enhancement in type IIa fibers had P = 0.027 and P = 0.002. There was no non-training control group. Funding: RS-2025-00560295.

**Links:**

- [Scientific Reports — Twelve weeks of eccentric training and single-fiber residual force in older adults](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-76275-5)

**Commentary:** Adults around age 70 can put slow lowering work into two weekly strength sessions aimed at getting out of a chair and climbing stairs; these 37 completers had no control group, so some of the gain may be practice, and the study does not set a load.

## III. Public Health and Disease Prevention

### 5. American Association for Cancer Research: In a 2024 survey, 50.3% of adults aged 45-49 had heard of more than one colorectal screening test, and 23.2% had never discussed testing (Public health)
**Summary:** Jordan Baeker Bispo and colleagues analyzed the 2024 Health Information National Trends Survey and presented the results at the AACR Conference on the Science of Cancer Health Disparities in Atlanta, October 18-21. AACR issued the release on October 18, and MedicalXpress carried it on October 19. The sample was 3,626 adults aged 45-75 with no history of colon cancer who were eligible for screening. Among those aged 45-49, 50.3% said a doctor had told them there are different colorectal cancer tests, compared with 72.5% of those aged 65-75. The shares who had never discussed these tests were 23.2% and 7%. Within ages 45-49, 33.2% of non-Hispanic Black respondents and 60.7% of non-Hispanic white respondents reported hearing about different options. The presenter cited a figure that about 37% of adults aged 45-49 are up to date with screening. The question asked whether someone had "ever" heard this, so older adults had a longer window, and answers were self-reported. The American Cancer Society funded the work and said the paper was published in the Journal of General Internal Medicine.

**Links:**

- [AACR — Colorectal screening conversations at ages 45-49](https://www.aacr.org/about-the-aacr/newsroom/news-releases/doctors-may-be-less-likely-to-discuss-screening-options-for-colorectal-cancer-with-newly-eligible-younger-adults/)
- [MedicalXpress — October 19 account of the conference results](https://medicalxpress.com/news/2026-10-doctors-discuss-screening-options-colorectal.html)

**Commentary:** At the first checkup after age 45, ask what stool testing and colonoscopy each involve and how often they are done; this survey shows many newly eligible adults have not heard the options, and it does not compare which test lowers deaths.

### 6. PLOS Global Public Health: In a Cape Town diabetes clinic, cough screening had 22.2% sensitivity, and only 9 of 673 adults had molecularly confirmed tuberculosis (Public health)
**Summary:** A diagnostic-accuracy study from the University of Cape Town and University College London was published in PLOS Global Public Health on September 30, and the University of Cape Town summarized it on October 19. Investigators consecutively enrolled 673 adults in routine diabetes care at a community health centre in Khayelitsha (median age 54 years, interquartile range 47-60; 63.8% women; HIV prevalence 17.2%). Everyone gave one sputum sample for Xpert Ultra, regardless of symptoms or chest X-ray. Nine people were positive (1.33%). Sensitivity of any cough was 22.2% (95% CI 2.1-60.0) and specificity was 97.5% (96.0-98.6). Adding fever, weight loss, or night sweats left sensitivity at 22.2% and lowered specificity to 96.0%. Chest X-ray abnormalities suggestive of tuberculosis had sensitivity 55.6% (21.2-86.3) and specificity 95.4%. Specificity of that X-ray reading was 87.6% with prior tuberculosis and 97.2% without it (P < 0.01). The work was preparatory for the BALANCE trial.

**Links:**

- [PLOS Global Public Health — Tuberculosis screening tools in people with diabetes](https://journals.plos.org/globalpublichealth/article?id=10.1371/journal.pgph.0006479)
- [University of Cape Town — October 19 summary of the results](https://www.news.uct.ac.za/article/-2026-10-19-tb-screening-tools-may-miss-cases-among-people-with-diabetes)

**Commentary:** Where tuberculosis is common, absence of cough in a person with diabetes is a weak rule-out; these sensitivity estimates come from nine cases and have wide intervals, so they do not by themselves decide who should have a sputum test.

## IV. Sleep and Mental Health

### 7. SLEEP: Among 70 conscripts aged 18-24, the 24% with elevated anxiety had weaker slow-wave dissipation and a higher share of N3 sleep (Sleep)
**Summary:** An overnight polysomnography study was published in SLEEP on October 14. Seventy young adults in compulsory military service (67 men, ages 18-24) were recorded. Slow-wave dissipation was the change in normalized delta power (0.5-4.0 Hz) from the first to the third non-REM episode. Elevated anxiety was present in 24%, and that group showed weaker slow-wave dissipation and a greater proportion of N3. Anxiety correlated negatively with dissipation and positively with the N3 proportion. Conscripts with elevated anxiety but without elevated depression had less REM sleep than those with both and than those with neither. Higher anxiety went with higher alpha power during REM. Findings for sleepiness, or for depression without anxiety, were limited. The design is cross-sectional.

**Links:**

- [SLEEP — Anxiety, N3 sleep, and slow-wave dissipation](https://doi.org/10.1093/sleep/zsag267)

**Commentary:** When anxiety and unsettled sleep show up together in a young adult, the first targets are a stable sleep schedule and the anxiety itself; this is one night in mostly male conscripts and does not show that more N3 reduces anxiety.

## V. Sports Medicine and Injury Prevention

### 8. Science and Medicine in Football: A women's-football consensus puts a 15- to 20-minute, two- to three-times-weekly multicomponent program forward for non-contact ACL prevention (Sports medicine)
**Summary:** UEFA convened 31 doctors, sports scientists, and physiotherapists plus one player and used the RAND-UCLA Appropriateness Method with three rounds of confidential voting. Part one was published in Science and Medicine in Football on October 14. Consensus was reached on 88 of 101 evidence statements. The panel concluded that ACL injuries in women's football typically occur without contact during high-intensity actions such as cutting, pressing, or landing, and that risk mixes modifiable neuromuscular control, strength, and movement quality with non-modifiable factors. The favored approach was a multicomponent program of plyometrics, strength, and proximal control, performed two to three times a week for 15 to 20 minutes, with the statement that high adherence can reduce non-contact ACL injuries. Implementation points were embedding the work in regular training, adapting it with coaches and players, and addressing time, resources, and coach education.

**Links:**

- [Science and Medicine in Football — Women's football ACL consensus, part one](https://doi.org/10.1080/24733938.2026.2747391)

**Commentary:** Women who play football can keep 15-20 minutes of landing, cutting, and strength work inside two or three weekly sessions across the season; this is an expert consensus, and the abstract does not supply a new percentage reduction in injuries.

## VI. Evidence and Clarification

### 9. U.S. FDA: On October 19 the agency finalized the identity and safety sections of new dietary ingredient notifications and recommended one safety narrative (Evidence)
**Summary:** On October 19, the U.S. Food and Drug Administration announced availability of the industry guidance "New Dietary Ingredient (NDI) Notifications and Related Issues; Identity and Safety Information About the NDI." It finalizes sections VI.A, VI.B, and VI.C of the 2016 revised draft, covering identity of the ingredient and the supplement, history of use or other safety evidence, and the summary of the basis for the safety conclusion. The Federal Register public-inspection notice says the guidance drops the "comprehensive safety profile" in favor of a single safety narrative for every notification, and it describes when more evidence is warranted and which study types are recommended. The guidance states current agency thinking and is not binding. Comments may be submitted at any time under docket FDA-2025-D-6501. Other parts of the 2016 draft remain to be finalized.

**Links:**

- [FDA — October 19 guidance on NDI identity and safety information](https://www.fda.gov/food/hfp-constituent-updates/fda-issues-guidance-identity-and-safety-information-new-dietary-ingredient-notifications)
- [Federal Register — Public-inspection notice of the guidance](https://public-inspection.federalregister.gov/2026-21453.pdf)

**Commentary:** Products already on the shelf were not newly approved or recalled by this notice; the change is how a firm should document identity and the reason it expects a new ingredient to be safe before marketing, and a submitted notification is not proof of benefit.

### 10. Advances in Nutrition: A narrative review puts U.S. Army supplement use near 76%, self-reported adverse effects near 18%, and continued use after those effects (Evidence)
**Summary:** Adrienne Hatch-McChesney, Joseph J. Knapik, Harris R. Lieberman, and colleagues published a narrative review online in Advances in Nutrition on October 16 (accepted October 15). Their literature search closed on September 18, 2026. They summarized supplement use, adverse effects, reasons, and attitudes among U.S. service members and compared them with U.S. civilians and some foreign forces. Army use rose from 53% in 2006-2007 to 76% in 2018-2019. Compared with civilians, service members used more supplements and more products marketed for performance and weight loss. The most used categories were multivitamin-minerals, proteins or amino acids, combination products, and single vitamins or minerals. Self-reported adverse effects were about 18% and highest with combination products and prohormones. Among users, 67% believed the products were safe, 71% believed they worked, and 38% believed government regulation ensures safety. Longitudinal data indicated that people who reported adverse effects mostly kept using supplements. The authors state that the U.S. market still lacks an adequate system for detecting dangerous supplements.

**Links:**

- [Advances in Nutrition — Military supplement use and a proposal for adverse-effect surveillance](https://advances.nutrition.org/article/S2161-8313(26)00171-7/fulltext)

**Commentary:** For strength training or weight loss, combination products and prohormones are the category to check before multivitamins or protein; this is a review of older surveys, and the roughly 18% figure is self-reported across users, not the rate for one product.
