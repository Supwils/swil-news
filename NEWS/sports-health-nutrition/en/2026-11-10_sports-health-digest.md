## I. Nutrition & Diet

### 1. KoGES cohort: Higher dietary diversity linked to lower adult diabetes risk in the British Journal of Nutrition (nutrition/prevention)
**Summary:** A team led by Mi Kyung Kim at Hanyang University analyzed 16,666 adults aged 40+ without baseline diabetes in the Korean Genome and Epidemiology Study (KoGES); over a median ~6-year follow-up, 953 people developed diabetes. The work was recently published in the *British Journal of Nutrition* and covered by Health Chosun and Yonhap on November 10, 2026. Dietary diversity scores (DDS) awarded one point per day for consuming at least one serving from each of five food groups based on Korean Dietary Reference Intakes. After adjustment for age, activity, smoking, alcohol, BMI, fasting glucose, and total calories, DDS >4 versus ≤3 was associated with about 44% lower diabetes incidence in men (IRR ≈0.56) and about 27% lower in women (IRR ≈0.73). Within-group diversity in milk/dairy (both sexes), fruit (men), and vegetables/seaweed/mushrooms (women) also tracked with lower risk. This is an observational cohort; diversity may co-vary with overall diet quality and other healthy behaviors, so “just adding variety” is not proven to prevent diabetes by itself.

**Links:**

- [British Journal of Nutrition — Dietary diversity and type 2 diabetes risk](https://doi.org/10.1017/s0007114526108733)
- [Health Chosun — November 10 English report](https://health.chosun.com/english/site/data/html_dir/2026/11/10/2026111001635.html)

**Commentary:** The practical takeaway is daily coverage of staples, quality protein (including legumes/dairy), and produce—then rotating items within each group—not novelty eating for its own sake.

### 2. Science RCT plus mechanism: Composite dietary fiber inactivates androgens via gut microbes and improves PCOS-related hyperandrogenism and ovulation (nutrition/endocrine)
**Summary:** Collaborators including Jie Qiao and Yanli Pang (Peking University Third Hospital), Changtao Jiang, and Shaoping Nie (Nanchang University) published online in *Science* on November 5, 2026; Chinese nutrition industry briefs highlighted the work on November 10. In a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial, a 12-week composite fiber blend (inulin, resistant starch, and β-glucan) improved hyperandrogenism and restored ovulation-related markers in patients with polyendocrine metabolic ovarian syndrome (PMOS; PCOS-related phenotype in the paper). Mechanistically, fiber enriched *Bacteroides xylanisolvens*, which converts androstenedione via bacterial enzymes into low-activity “coproandrosterone,” further reduced by *Bifidobacterium longum* and others to inactive dihydro-coproandrosterone. Mouse colonization recapitulated androgen lowering and better insulin sensitivity. The evidence chain is strong, but the formula is specific—do not generalize to any fiber supplement as a PCOS cure—and use should be clinician/dietitian-guided, not a substitute for standard care.

**Links:**

- [Science — Targeting hormone conversion through dietary fiber in PMOS](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.aed8952)
- [Peking University Third Hospital — Study briefing](https://www.puh3.net.cn/info/1971/184351.htm)

**Commentary:** For PCOS/hyperandrogenism discussions, treat fermentable fiber from whole grains, legumes, and produce as a food-based lever—not a DIY high-dose powder “cure.”

### 3. Whitehall II in BMC Medicine: Ultra-processed-food metabolomic signatures track with later CVD and diabetes risk (nutrition/metabolism)
**Summary:** UCL and collaborators studied 6,010 Whitehall II adults free of CVD and type 2 diabetes at baseline, estimating ultra-processed (UPF) and minimally processed (MPF) intake via Nova and weight share of foods, then building NMR metabolomic signatures from 233 fasting plasma metabolites. The paper in *BMC Medicine* was covered in Chinese media on November 10, 2026. Elastic-net models selected 34 UPF- and 50 MPF-related metabolites: the UPF signature skewed toward pro-inflammatory/atherogenic markers and lower omega-3/HDL-related features. Over median ~20/~18 years, 1,094 CVD and 853 T2D events occurred. Highest versus lowest UPF-intake tertiles showed about 23% higher CVD and 18% higher T2D risk after diet-quality adjustment; signatures mediated roughly 43%–51% of processing–disease associations. Observational design, self-reported diet, and a single blood draw limit causal claims; routine checkups cannot score personal UPF intake from this signature.

**Links:**

- [BMC Medicine — UPF/MPF metabolomic signatures and CVD/T2D](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12916-026-05081-7)
- [4gTV — November 10 Chinese explainer](https://www.4gtv.tv/article/2026111011000034)

**Commentary:** Cutting how often drinks, snacks, and ready meals show up beats obsessing over whether a blood panel can “detect ultra-processing”—the signature is a research tool, not a new lab order.

### 4. JAMA Network Open: Higher childhood UPF intake linked to stronger dorsal-striatum food-cue responses and greater 1-year adiposity gain (nutrition/pediatrics)
**Summary:** Katie Page and colleagues at USC Keck followed 46 children aged 7–10 with healthy baseline weight in the BrainChild study; results appeared online October 23, 2026 in *JAMA Network Open*, with renewed coverage (e.g., Foodbible) on November 10. Multiple 24-hour recalls estimated UPF energy share (Nova); fMRI measured striatal BOLD responses to food images; BMI, body fat, and waist were remeasured at 1 year. Higher UPF intake associated with greater dorsal (not ventral) striatal responsivity (β≈0.34); stronger dorsal responses predicted larger 1-year BMI and body-fat gains. Accounting for dorsal responsivity attenuated UPF–BMI and UPF–body-fat associations by about 31% and 44%. Small observational sample—cannot prove UPF “permanently rewires” brains or directly causes obesity, and reverse causation remains possible.

**Links:**

- [JAMA Network Open — Childhood UPF, striatal response, and adiposity gain](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2855779)
- [USC Keck — Institutional summary](https://keck.usc.edu/news/children-who-eat-more-ultra-processed-foods-show-stronger-brain-responses-to-appetizing-foods/)

**Commentary:** For school-age kids, swap habitual cookies, fries, and sugary drinks for less-processed staples plus produce and protein—change the food environment; do not label children as brain-damaged.

## II. Exercise & Training Science

### 5. BMC Cancer meta-analysis: Combined aerobic plus resistance training most consistently improves fitness and quality of life in people with cancer (training/oncology rehab)
**Summary:** A systematic review of 89 RCTs (6,571 adult participants living with or beyond cancer) compared structured aerobic, resistance, or combined training with usual care; published about November 4, 2026 in *BMC Cancer* and summarized by Bioengineer/Scienmag on November 10. Combined training raised VO₂peak by about 2.9, 4.5, and 4.2 mL·kg⁻¹·min⁻¹ at 3/6/12 months (above MCID thresholds); at 3 months, quality of life rose ~7.0 points and 6-minute walk ~52 m; at 6 months, lower-body strength increased and body-fat percentage fell. Aerobic alone mainly lifted short-term VO₂peak; resistance alone mainly improved short-term quality of life. Across 86 trials, serious adverse events were rare and similar to control. Longer-term effects vary by endpoint; superiority of combined over single-modality programs remains uncertain—prescription should be individualized and progressive.

**Links:**

- [BMC Cancer — Aerobic/resistance/combined exercise meta-analysis in cancer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12885-026-17276-5)
- [Bioengineer — November 10 summary](https://bioengineer.org/exercise-training-improves-fitness-and-quality-of-life-in-cancer-patients/)

**Commentary:** When medically cleared during or after cancer care, write “aerobic + resistance” into a doable plan rather than relying on walking or machines alone—ask the treating team first.

### 6. BMC Musculoskeletal Disorders meta-analysis: Proprioceptive training linked to ~34% lower relative ankle-sprain occurrence in athletes (training/injury prevention)
**Summary:** Fifteen randomized or cluster-randomized trials evaluated proprioceptive training alone or as an identifiable part of multicomponent injury-prevention programs for ankle injury occurrence; published October 28, 2026 in *BMC Musculoskeletal Disorders*, with recent explainers (e.g., Physiotutors). The pooled relative effect was about 0.659 (95% CI 0.558–0.778)—roughly 34% lower relative occurrence—with low heterogeneity (I²≈22.7%); sport-type subgroups (soccer vs other) showed no significant interaction. Overall certainty was rated moderate; optimal dose and generalizability across levels/populations need more work. The endpoint is injury incidence, not instant “cure” of instability.

**Links:**

- [BMC Musculoskeletal Disorders — Proprioceptive training for ankle-sprain prevention](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12891-026-10677-4)
- [Physiotutors — Research brief](https://www.physiotutors.com/research/proprioceptive-training-ankle-sprain-prevention/)

**Commentary:** A few minutes of single-leg balance, unstable-surface work, or directional landings in weekly warm-ups usually beat buying another ankle brace—especially after a prior sprain.

## III. Public Health & Disease Prevention

### 7. AHA 2026: Medically tailored meals improve diet quality and cardiometabolic markers in adults with T2D and high BP—even with down-dosing (public health/food-as-medicine)
**Summary:** Nour Makarem (Columbia University) and colleagues reported at AHA Scientific Sessions 2026 (Chicago, Nov 6–9) on adults with coexisting type 2 diabetes and hypertension who do not yet qualify for medically tailored meals (MTMs) via advanced complications. Participants were randomized to standard MTM (10 meals/week + nutrition counseling for 8 months) or 10 meals/week for 3 months then tapering by one meal/month; both arms received Life’s Essential 8–aligned education, culturally responsive cooking demos, utensils, and produce. Endocrinology Advisor covered the findings on November 10, 2026. Across arms, Mediterranean Eating Pattern for Americans score rose ~3.15 points at 8 months; odds of better food-security status more than doubled versus baseline; HbA1c fell ~0.5 percentage points and systolic/diastolic BP ~3.7/4.1 mmHg, with no significant between-arm difference. Conference-stage results pending full peer-reviewed detail.

**Links:**

- [Endocrinology Advisor — November 10 MTM report](https://www.endocrinologyadvisor.com/news/aha-medically-tailored-meals-beneficial-for-adults-with-t2d-high-bp/)

**Commentary:** For chronic disease care, high-quality meal support can start dense then taper if paired with cooking skills—the goal is self-sufficiency, not endless delivery.

### 8. OPTIMAL-DIABETES: Systolic BP target <120 mm Hg did not significantly cut major CV events in adults with T2D and raised kidney-function decline events (public health/blood pressure)
**Summary:** The Brazilian multicenter OPTIMAL-DIABETES trial randomized 9,476 adults ≥50 years with type 2 diabetes, systolic BP 130–180 mm Hg, and elevated CV risk to intensive (<120) versus standard (<140) systolic targets; median follow-up ~4 years. An AHA news release said results were published simultaneously in the *New England Journal of Medicine*. Major CV events occurred in 286 (6.0%) intensive versus 315 (6.7%) standard participants (~10% relative reduction, not statistically significant). Kidney-related events were ~5.7% versus 3.7% (~55% higher), mostly eGFR declines; end-stage kidney disease was similar; serious adverse events were similar. Investigators caution against reading this as “intensive BP lowering does not work,” noting benefits may be smaller than in people without diabetes and that care should remain individualized. Interpret alongside ACCORD BP, BPROAD, and related evidence.

**Links:**

- [American Heart Association — OPTIMAL-DIABETES news release](https://newsroom.heart.org/news/intensive-blood-pressure-therapy-did-not-cut-cardiovascular-events-in-adults-with-diabetes)
- [ClinicalTrials.gov — NCT04040634](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04040634)

**Commentary:** With diabetes, BP goals are not “lower is always better”—weigh heart–kidney risk, tolerability, and treatment burden with a clinician rather than chasing a single number.

### 9. Lancet Regional Health—Europe: Stopping GLP-1RAs in type 2 diabetes linked to higher composite MI/stroke/death risk (public health/medication adherence)
**Summary:** Antonio Ceriello and colleagues retrospectively studied electronic records from 301 Italian diabetes clinics (2004–2024), including 80,302 people with type 2 diabetes, no established CVD, who started a GLP-1 receptor agonist; about 60.5% later discontinued. Published November 9, 2026 in *The Lancet Regional Health—Europe* and reported by ABC News on November 10. Versus persistent users, discontinuers had IRR ≈1.66 and HR ≈1.68 for the composite of myocardial infarction, stroke, or death from any cause—roughly ~4 versus ~2 events per 100 person-years. Post-stop changes in weight, glucose, and BP were modest and unlikely to fully explain the gap. Observational design, unknown reasons for stopping, and possible higher baseline frailty among discontinuers mean this does not prove stopping itself causes events, nor does it compare with never starting therapy.

**Links:**

- [The Lancet Regional Health—Europe — GLP-1RA discontinuation and CV outcomes (DOI)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.lanepe.2026.101881)
- [ABC News — November 10 report](https://www.abc.net.au/news/health/2026-11-10/type-2-diabetes-heart-attack-stroke-glp1-stop/107342430)

**Commentary:** If side effects, cost, or supply push you off a GLP-1 drug, plan an alternative glucose/weight and CV-protection strategy with a clinician—do not self-stop on the bet that past weight loss is enough.

### 10. China SAMR/NHC: Phenolphthalein- and bisacodyl-class substances added to non-edible substance list to curb illegal weight-loss food adulteration (public health/food safety)
**Summary:** On November 7, 2026, China’s State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR), with the National Health Commission, announced inclusion of phenolphthalein (phenolthalein)-class and bisacodyl-class substances in the national list of non-edible substances that may be illegally added to foods, alongside supplemental testing methods for newer derivatives. SAMR’s release notes these agents are stimulant laxatives; chronic exposure may cause abdominal pain, diarrhea, electrolyte imbalance, and liver/kidney injury; illicit actors have added novel derivatives to jellies, candied snacks, candy, and beverages with false efficacy claims. Suzhou Nutrition and Health Association’s November 10 briefing listed this among the day’s national regulatory priorities. This is regulatory/methodological news, not a supplement-efficacy trial.

**Links:**

- [SAMR — Crackdown on novel illegal additives in weight-loss foods](https://www.samr.gov.cn/xw/sj/art/2026/art_152eec2a3d2e4c4bb3ee0f9f2867559a.html)
- [Keceyun — Non-edible substance list notice reprint](https://www.keceyun.com/policy/newsdetail/794603.html)

**Commentary:** Snacks or drinks that promise “oil flushing” while causing diarrhea deserve suspicion for hidden laxatives—lose weight with an energy deficit plus protein and resistance training, not covert cathartics.

## IV. Sleep & Mental Health

### 11. JAMA Psychiatry: Actigraphy sleep and rest–activity metrics in remitted depression flag roughly doubled relapse risk (sleep/mental health)
**Summary:** A cohort of 93 adults with remitted depression wore research-grade wrist actigraphy for about 1–2 years (~32,000 complete days; median ~46 weeks); findings in *JAMA Psychiatry* were covered November 10, 2026 (e.g., Nova Online). After adjustment for age, sex, season, and baseline MADRS, lower baseline sleep regularity, lower relative amplitude (RA), lower sleep efficiency, higher wake after sleep onset, and higher nighttime activity associated with roughly twofold higher relapse risk. In time-varying models, greater composite phase deviation and lower RA remained associated; RA stayed significant after concurrent MADRS adjustment. Observational and modestly sized—suggests scalable digital markers, but does not prove wearable-triggered interventions prevent relapse.

**Links:**

- [JAMA Psychiatry — Actigraphy sleep/rest–activity rhythms and depression relapse](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamapsychiatry/article-abstract/2844602)
- [Nova Online — November 10 report](https://www.novaonline.ca/wrist-worn-trackers-may-aid-in-identifying-major-depressive-disorder-relapse-before-symptoms-appear/)

**Commentary:** If schedules grow chaotic and day–night activity contrast blurs during remission, bring it up early at follow-up—do not wait until mood scores have already crashed.

### 12. AJRCCM RCT: Clinician-guided digital CBT-I improves comorbid insomnia and sleep apnea (COMISA) (sleep)
**Summary:** At West China Hospital, Shi, Tang, and colleagues randomized 88 adults with comorbid insomnia and obstructive sleep apnea to six weekly clinician-guided digital CBT-I sessions (sleep restriction, stimulus control, cognitive restructuring, etc.) or sleep-hygiene education alone, with blinded assessments; primary endpoints were ISI and PSQI at post-treatment and 3 months. Published online October 29, 2026 in the *American Journal of Respiratory and Critical Care Medicine*; Lonevi summarized on November 10. At 3 months (ITT): ISI −9.5 vs −4.0; PSQI −4.9 vs −1.8; Epworth sleepiness −3.4 vs −1.0. At six weeks, lab AHI changed by about −6.0 vs +2.0 events/h. Transient ESS rises in weeks 2–4 occurred without reported functional impairment. Single-center; comparator was not CPAP—so this does not replace apnea therapy; AHI was secondary with limited baseline severity detail.

**Links:**

- [AJRCCM — Clinician-guided digital CBT-I in COMISA (DOI)](https://doi.org/10.1093/ajrccm/aamag567)
- [Lonevi — November 10 trial explainer](https://lonevi.com/articles/digital-cbt-i-insomnia-with-sleep-apnoea-comisa-trial-2026)

**Commentary:** When insomnia and snoring/apneas coexist, layer clinician-guided CBT-I inside sleep-medicine care—do not swap sleep-hygiene tips or DIY sleep restriction for airway evaluation and treatment.
