## I. Nutrition & Diet

### 1. JAMA Network Open: a more Mediterranean prenatal diet and one more weekly seafood serving lined up with lower odds of infant colic plus eczema (Nutrition)
**Summary:** Researchers at the Harvard T.H. Chan School of Public Health, Boston Children's Hospital, and the Harvard Pilgrim Health Care Institute reported a study that Medical Xpress covered on October 12. The paper is in JAMA Network Open (doi: 10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2026.38472). Project Viva is an observational cohort: more than 1,300 pregnant women were recruited from prenatal clinics in eastern Massachusetts from April 1999 to November 2002, and food questionnaires were scored with the Mediterranean Diet Score and the Dietary Inflammatory Index. Colic occurred in 26% of infants, and 5% had both colic and eczema. Each extra Mediterranean Diet Score point was associated with a 15% lower risk of having both conditions; each extra Dietary Inflammatory Index point was associated with a 34% higher risk. Each additional weekly seafood serving was associated with a 25% lower chance of both conditions. Dairy, eggs, and nuts did not show the same association. The associations were strongest among infants whose mothers had a history of allergic conditions.

**Links:**

- [JAMA Network Open — Prenatal diet, infant colic, and eczema](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2026.38472)
- [Medical Xpress — Harvard team report, October 12](https://medicalxpress.com/news/2026-10-healthy-diet-pregnancy-infant-colic.html)

**Commentary:** Shifting a pregnancy plate toward vegetables, whole grains, legumes, and regular seafood matches this evidence better than changing formula after symptoms start, and a 1999-2002 cohort still does not prescribe prevention of colic or eczema.

### 2. Gut Microbes: peanut intake at 12 months tracked with gut taxa, and lab fermentation produced short-chain fatty acids and indole-3-lactic acid (Nutrition)
**Summary:** APC Microbiome Ireland at University College Cork announced on October 12 that Emer Shannon, Liam O'Mahony, and colleagues published in Gut Microbes (doi: 10.1080/19490976.2026.2743944). The work combined fecal metagenomes from 12-month-old children (n = 343) and a diet questionnaire with human fecal bioreactors, Bifidobacterium cultures, and peripheral-blood mononuclear cell assays. Peanut consumption was associated with changes that included Prevotella buccae, Megasphaera micronuciformis, and Blautia wexlerae. Fermentation of predigested peanut flour increased short-chain fatty acids, and 188 metabolites changed significantly. Bifidobacterium longum subsp. longum strain 160 metabolized peanut and secreted indole-3-lactic acid; supernatants from growth on peanut raised interleukin-10 and lowered tumor necrosis factor-alpha and interleukin-5 from stimulated immune cells. The authors frame this as one candidate route from early peanut introduction to immune signaling and say it still needs testing in high-risk allergy cohorts.

**Links:**

- [Gut Microbes — Microbial metabolism of peanuts and immunoregulatory metabolites](https://doi.org/10.1080/19490976.2026.2743944)
- [University College Cork APC — October 12 news](https://www.ucc.ie/en/apc/news/apc-news/new-apc-research-reveals-how-gut-microbes-convert-peanuts-into-compounds-involved-in-immune-regulation.html)

**Commentary:** Families already introducing peanut in infancy, as pediatric guidance allows, can read this as a possible microbial pathway, and infants with a serious allergy history still follow a specialist plan.

### 3. npj Ocean Sustainability: a "fish meals" metric converts catch into WHO protein needs, and a 480-person, 10-tonne example covers about 27% (Nutrition)
**Summary:** Johann D. Bell and colleagues published an open-access comment on October 12 in npj Ocean Sustainability that proposes "fish meals" as a way to measure the nutritional contribution of fisheries adaptations. The method combines the World Health Organization minimum protein intake of 0.7 g per kg of body weight per day, public-health advice that fish supply about 50% of protein in Pacific Island diets, an edible-portion recovery of about 60% for tuna, and a protein content of about 23%. On that basis, 150 g of tuna provides about 34.5 g of protein. In the paper's worked example, a coastal community of 480 people with an anchored fish aggregating device yielding 10 tonnes of tuna a year would receive about 27% of the fish meals needed for good nutrition. This is a planning calculation, not a feeding trial. The authors also note that higher-trophic species such as marlin and swordfish carry more methylmercury risk, especially for children and for pregnant and lactating women.

**Links:**

- [npj Ocean Sustainability — Nutritional benefits of Pacific fisheries adaptations](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44183-026-00248-4)

**Commentary:** For an individual shopper the useful conversion is portion size: about 150 g of skipjack or smaller yellowfin can cover a large share of one meal's protein, while marlin and swordfish are poor routine choices for pregnancy and childhood.

## II. Exercise & Training Science

### 4. Nature Metabolism: one 45-minute ride in 25 healthy men changed more than 3,000 proteins across plasma, saliva, and urine (Training)
**Summary:** Nigel Kurgan, Bente Klarlund Pedersen, Atul Shahaji Deshmukh, and colleagues published an open-access resource on October 12 in Nature Metabolism. The discovery cohort was 25 healthy young men (age 29 ± 3 years, body mass index 23 ± 1, VO2max 49 ± 8 ml/kg/min) who cycled for 45 minutes at about 75% of maximum heart rate. Plasma and saliva were sampled before exercise and at about 0, 0.5, 1, 3, and 24 hours afterward; urine was sampled before exercise and at about 0 and 24 hours. Across the three fluids, 10,128 proteins were measured, and 3,007 were differentially regulated immediately after exercise. Proteins with a time effect numbered 2,156 in plasma, 601 in saliva, and 272 in urine; 297 changed in at least two fluids and 7 changed in all three. An independent cohort reproduced consistent responses for many exercise-responsive plasma proteins across age, sex, and exercise modality. Genetically prioritized muscle-secreted proteins were tested in Drosophila. The atlas is public, and the paper does not set a training dose for the general public.

**Links:**

- [Nature Metabolism — Multifluid proteome of acute exercise](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42255-026-01621-w)
- [Nature Metabolism — Research briefing](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42255-026-01642-5)

**Commentary:** A single moderate aerobic session is enough to remodel thousands of proteins in blood, saliva, and urine, so moving today counts; a map from 25 young men does not set weekly frequency or load.

### 5. The Lancet Regional Health – Americas: among 96 adults aged 18 to 45 with early-onset type 2 diabetes, remission at 24 weeks was 54% versus 4% (Training)
**Summary:** The University of Leicester described the RESET for Remission trial on October 9. The paper is in The Lancet Regional Health – Americas, and SciTechDaily carried it again on October 12. This randomized, open-label, blinded-endpoint trial at three centers in England and Canada enrolled adults aged 18 to 45 with early-onset type 2 diabetes and obesity who were not on insulin. Ninety-six people were randomized, mean age 38, with a starting weight just over 100 kg. The intervention was 800 to 900 kcal a day (30% protein, 50% carbohydrate, 20% fat), with two supervised aerobic-plus-resistance sessions and one independent session each week for 12 weeks, then weight maintenance and independent exercise for 12 weeks. Remission at 24 weeks meant HbA1c below 6.5% and no glucose-lowering medication for at least 12 weeks: 27 of 50 (54%) in the intervention group and 2 of 46 (4%) in usual care, about 21 times higher odds after adjustment. The intervention group lost 7.5 kg more, mostly fat, and MRI showed muscle mass was preserved. About nine in ten participants attended the final assessment. At least one adverse event occurred in 43 of 50 intervention participants and 6 of 46 controls, mainly early constipation, headache, dizziness, and fatigue that resolved; there were no serious adverse events.

**Links:**

- [The Lancet Regional Health – Americas — RESET for Remission](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lanam/article/PIIS2667-193X%2826%2900294-2/fulltext)
- [University of Leicester — Trial announcement, October 9](https://le.ac.uk/news/2026/october/diabetes-diet-study-leicester-lancet)

**Commentary:** Adults diagnosed with type 2 diabetes before 45 and not yet on insulin can ask about supervised low-energy eating paired with resistance work; 800 to 900 kcal and stopping medication belong inside monitored care.

## III. Sleep & Mental Health

### 6. University of Western Australia: 88 adults with depression and insomnia improved on both after four weeks of CBT-I (Sleep)
**Summary:** The University of Western Australia publicized the Sleep Well for Mood study on October 12. The paper by Vanika Lall, Melissa Ree, and Romola Bucks appeared online in Behaviour Research and Therapy (doi: 10.1016/j.brat.2026.105146; early online date 26 August 2026). Eighty-eight adults (72% female) received four weeks of cognitive behavioral therapy for insomnia and were analyzed with latent growth curves at baseline, after treatment, and at 12-week follow-up. Depressive symptoms fell by a clinically significant amount. General and sleep-related repetitive negative thinking decreased both by day and at bedtime, and activity increased. Faster improvement in daytime sleep-related repetitive negative thinking and in bedtime general repetitive negative thinking was strongly associated with how much depression improved; activity improvement was moderately associated. The abstract describes trajectories inside people who received insomnia therapy and calls for causal experimental tests. The news release does not report a parallel control group.

**Links:**

- [Behaviour Research and Therapy — CBT-I and depression outcomes](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.brat.2026.105146)
- [University of Western Australia — October 12 news](https://www.uwa.edu.au/news/article/2026/october/treating-insomnia-could-be-key-to-reducing-symptoms-of-depression)

**Commentary:** When depression and insomnia overlap, booking cognitive behavioral therapy for insomnia is the step this study supports; 88 people and no control group leave "treating sleep cures depression" unproven.

### 7. Communications Health: in 7,041 students in northwest China, frequent irregular meals lined up with a 4.55-point higher PHQ-9 (Mental health)
**Summary:** Yunfeng Bai, Boyue Zhao, Yuan Liu, and Feng Zhang published an open-access cross-sectional study on October 12 in Communications Health. From November 2022 to December 2023, an online survey at 10 colleges in five provinces of northwest China left 7,041 participants for analysis, mean age 21.47 years, 65.91% female. Depression was measured with the PHQ-9 and activity with the Physical Activity Rating Scale-3. A PHQ-9 of 10 or higher was present in 8.79%. In the fully adjusted model, frequent breakfast skipping corresponded to a 2.57-point higher PHQ-9 (95% CI 2.26 to 2.88), frequent late-night snacks to 2.11 (1.63 to 2.58), frequent irregular meals to 4.55 (4.05 to 5.05), and frequent alcohol use to 3.52 (2.99 to 4.04). Strong preferences for high-calorie, sweet, and spicy foods corresponded to 1.43, 0.71, and 1.52 points. Each extra point of activity score corresponded to a 0.03-point lower PHQ-9 (-0.03 to -0.02). Light activity described 80.53% of students. Eating items were written for the study, and the authors did not correct for multiple comparisons.

**Links:**

- [Communications Health — Eating behavior, activity, and depression in college students](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44528-026-00040-3)

**Commentary:** If a student changes one habit first, fixing meal timing matches the largest score gap in this survey; a cross-section does not show that irregular meals cause depression, and a high score still belongs with formal mental-health care.

## IV. Public Health & Disease Prevention

### 8. BMC Public Health: among 8,762 students aged 6 to 18 in Leshan, more than one outdoor hour a day lined up with lower myopia odds (Public health)
**Summary:** Jian Yang and colleagues at Leshan Normal University published a cross-sectional study on October 12 in BMC Public Health. From May to November 2025 they used multistage stratified random cluster sampling in Leshan and included 8,762 students aged 6 to 18. Myopia was a spherical equivalent below -0.50 diopters in at least one eye on non-cycloplegic autorefraction. Prevalence was 76.19%. In multivariable logistic regression, more than one hour of outdoor activity a day was associated with lower odds (OR 0.849, 95% CI 0.766 to 0.942). Screen time over two hours a day (OR 1.135, 1.022 to 1.259) and insufficient sleep (OR 1.139, 1.032 to 1.257) were associated with higher odds. Outdoor time, screen time, and sleep came from questionnaires. The authors say longitudinal studies are needed for causation. The abstract does not give the hour cutoff used for insufficient sleep.

**Links:**

- [BMC Public Health — Outdoor activity, screen time, sleep, and myopia](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12889-026-29776-x)

**Commentary:** Families can use three home rules from this study: more than an hour outdoors, screen time kept to two hours when possible, and age-appropriate sleep; non-cycloplegic refraction in one city does not by itself decide glasses.

**Summary:** On October 12, at the World Health Summit in Berlin, the World Health Organization launched a new five-year strategy for its Hub for Pandemic and Epidemic Intelligence. ## V. Mechanism & Limits of Extrapolation

### 11. Nature Communications: in mice, DHHC12 couples amino-acid supply to insulin secretion, and two human variants track with low or high glucose (Mechanism)
**Summary:** An open-access paper in Nature Communications on October 12 describes the palmitoyltransferase DHHC12 as a nutrient-responsive switch. Amino-acid deficiency activates the kinase GCN2, which phosphorylates COPA at threonine 777 and leads to ubiquitination of DHHC12 on two cysteines and its degradation. In male mice, loss of Dhhc12 impaired insulin secretion and caused glucose intolerance. DHHC12 palmitoylates SYT7 to promote glucose-stimulated insulin exocytosis. Two human variants supply a physiological contrast: the hypoglycemia-associated L196P mutant evades degradation, and the hyperglycemia-linked N238I mutant is constitutively degraded. The paper does not convert the pathway into a daily protein dose for people.

**Links:**

- [Nature Communications — DHHC12, amino-acid sensing, and insulin secretion](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-78642-2)

**Commentary:** The paper shows a molecular switch for amino-acid supply inside mouse islets; it is not a reason to raise or cut protein powder, and low or high glucose still follows existing labs and clinical advice.
