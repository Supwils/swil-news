## I. Nutrition & Diet

### 1. CALERIE 2 two-year analysis: modest calorie cuts improved diet quality

**Summary:** A diet-quality analysis of the CALERIE 2 randomized trial (Racette et al., *Am J Clin Nutr*) followed 218 healthy, non-obese adults assigned to calorie reduction or ad libitum eating for two years. The restriction group received dietitian coaching but chose its own foods, and the actual cut averaged about 12%, well below the 25% target. PANDiet, Healthy Eating Index and Dietary Inflammatory Index scores all improved versus controls, and nutritional adequacy was maintained.

**Links:**

- [WashU Profiles — Diet quality and nutritional adequacy during a 2-year calorie restriction trial](https://profiles.wustl.edu/en/publications/diet-quality-and-nutritional-adequacy-during-a-2-year-calorie-res/)
- [mindbodygreen — 2-Year Study on a Sustainable Approach to Nutrition for Healthy Aging](https://www.mindbodygreen.com/articles/major-nutrition-trial-revealed-surprising-benefit-of-eating-less)

**Commentary:** A small, coached calorie reduction need not cost nutrition, but participants were healthy and non-obese and this is a secondary analysis, so do not generalize broadly.

### 2. Diet quality may matter more than processing level (conference abstract, interpret with caution)

**Summary:** A Harvard-led analysis of more than 200,000 people presented at the American Society for Nutrition meeting suggested long-term cardiometabolic risk depends more on overall diet quality than on processing level. The lead researcher stressed this does not endorse ultra-processed foods, and a Science Media Centre expert noted it is an unpublished abstract.

**Links:**

- [News-Medical — Nutritional quality matters more than processing level for long-term health](https://www.news-medical.net/news/20260728/Nutritional-quality-matters-more-than-processing-level-for-long-term-health.aspx)
- [Science Media Centre — Expert reaction to unpublished study on diet quality vs. processing](https://www.sciencemediacentre.org/expert-reaction-to-unpublished-study-suggesting-that-diet-quality-matters-more-than-food-processing-level/)

**Commentary:** This is an observational July abstract and only directional: prioritize enough vegetables, whole grains and legumes rather than fixating on the "processed" label.

## II. Guidelines & Public Health

### 3. WHO issues first global clinical guidelines on child and adolescent obesity

**Summary:** In early October 2026 WHO released its first global guidelines on child and adolescent obesity, estimating 170 million affected in 2024. It strongly recommends structured diet, physical activity and behavioral interventions; drugs and surgery are not recommended for ages 0-9, and for ages 10-19 GLP-1 therapies are only a conditional option after supervised lifestyle programs fail. It also calls for addressing mental health alongside obesity.

**Links:**

- [PAHO/WHO — WHO issues first global guidelines on child and adolescent obesity](https://www.paho.org/en/news/8-10-2026-who-issues-first-global-guidelines-child-and-adolescent-obesity)
- [NutritionInsight — WHO backs diet and activity in first global guidelines](https://www.nutritioninsight.com/news/who-global-guidelines-child-adolescent-obesity.html)

**Commentary:** For families, first-line action remains household-level diet and activity change with medication as a fallback; outlets differ slightly on age thresholds, so check the WHO text.

### 4. WHO dementia risk-reduction guidelines, 2nd edition: up to 45% of risk preventable or delayable

**Summary:** On July 15, 2026 WHO published the second edition of its guidelines on reducing cognitive decline and dementia risk, estimating up to 45% of risk is linked to modifiable factors. It newly recommends reducing air pollution exposure and says hearing aids may be offered for hearing loss. It does not recommend vitamin B, vitamin E, omega-3 or multivitamins for prevention absent a diagnosed deficiency.

**Links:**

- [PAHO/WHO — New WHO guidelines: up to 45% of dementia risk could be prevented or delayed](https://www.paho.org/en/news/16-7-2026-new-who-guidelines-45-dementia-risk-could-be-prevented-or-delayed)

**Commentary:** 45% is an upper-bound estimate, not a guarantee; the practical moves are managing blood pressure and hearing and exercising regularly rather than relying on supplements.

### 5. 750,000-pregnancy study: weight-loss use of GLP-1 drugs not linked to extra obstetric complications

**Summary:** A Danish registry study in *Human Reproduction Open* analyzed about 756,600 singleton pregnancies, only 529 with periconceptional GLP-1 receptor agonist exposure. Higher preterm birth risk appeared only with diabetes use; weight-loss use showed no link to preterm birth or other complications. The authors suggest diabetes itself may explain much of the risk, and the findings are associations only.

**Links:**

- [Human Reproduction Open — GLP-1 RA periconceptional exposure and pregnancy outcomes](https://academic.oup.com/hropen/article/2026/2/hoag015/8526483)
- [SciTechDaily — Study of 750,000 pregnancies challenges fears over weight-loss drugs](https://scitechdaily.com/study-of-750000-pregnancies-challenges-fears-over-popular-weight-loss-drugs/)

**Commentary:** The exposed group is small and current advice to discuss stopping these drugs before conception still stands, so do not change medication on your own.

### 6. 35-year study: Lyme risk tracks acorns and mice, not deer numbers

**Summary:** A 35-year Cary Institute and University of Pennsylvania study in *PNAS* found no statistical relationship between deer abundance and Lyme risk in a New York forest. Acorn mast years boost white-footed mouse numbers the next year, and mice are the key host, allowing risk to be predicted almost two years ahead. Studies in Connecticut and Maryland reached different conclusions, so the question is not settled everywhere.

**Links:**

- [Cary Institute — 35-year study reshapes our understanding of Lyme disease ecology](https://www.caryinstitute.org/news-insights/press-release/35-year-study-reshapes-our-understanding-lyme-disease-ecology?page=1)
- [CIDRAP — Want to predict Lyme disease risk? Count the acorns, not the deer](https://www.cidrap.umn.edu/lyme-disease/want-predict-lyme-disease-risk-count-acorns-not-deer)

**Commentary:** In the year after a big acorn year, be extra careful outdoors with repellent, long clothing and tick checks.

## III. Sleep & Mental Health

### 7. Australian study: about 23% of people with depression show "circadian" features

**Summary:** A *British Journal of Psychiatry* study of 11,380 adults (75% female) from the Australian Genetics of Depression Study found about 23% met at least three of six rhythm-related criteria, such as social jetlag, delayed sleep or seasonality. This group reported earlier onset, more severe symptoms and weight gain during episodes, and reports suggest standard antidepressants may work less well. The data are largely self-reported and cross-sectional, so causation is unknown, and the label is not a formal diagnosis.

**Links:**

- [Fox News — Certain sleep habits could offer clues to a hidden type of depression](https://www.foxnews.com/health/certain-sleep-habits-could-offer-clues-hidden-type-depression-scientists-say.amp)
- [Science Media Centre España — A quarter of people with depression showed circadian disturbances](https://www.sciencemediacentre.es/en/quarter-people-depression-showed-circadian-disturbances-australian-study)

**Commentary:** Disrupted sleep timing may signal a mood problem worth telling your clinician about, while light therapy and melatonin remain untested in this group.

### 8. UK Biobank (Nov 2025 background): sleep regularity may matter more than duration for mental health

**Summary:** An analysis of about 100,000 UK Biobank participants found consistent sleep timing was more strongly linked to mental health than sleep duration alone. It was published in November 2025 and is not this week's news; it is included as background for the previous item.

**Links:**

- [UK Biobank — Beyond the hours slept: inconsistent sleep routines threaten mental health](https://www.ukbiobank.ac.uk/publications/beyond-the-hours-slept-inconsistent-sleep-routines-threaten-mental-health-in-100000-uk-biobank-participants/)

**Commentary:** Observational, but consistent with the item above: fix your wake and bed times before trying to catch up on sleep.
