## I. Nutrition & Diet

### 1. University of Gothenburg × Swedish Food Agency: Higher ultra-processed food energy share linked to poorer diet quality; only weak BMI signal in adults (Nutrition)
**Summary:** Torsten Sällström and colleagues estimated ultra-processed food (UPF) energy shares with NOVA coding across three Swedish national dietary surveys (1,797 adults; 3,099 adolescents; 1,820 young children). The work appears in *Nutrition Journal*, with a University of Gothenburg briefing on Nov 2, 2026. Under the primary classification, UPF averaged about 45%, 52%, and 46% of energy in adults, adolescents, and young children; stricter handling of ambiguous items lowered estimates to roughly 23%–29%. Higher UPF energy share tracked poorer diet-quality indicators (more added sugar; less fiber and whole grains). In adults, each 10 percentage-point rise in UPF energy corresponded to about 0.61% higher BMI (95% CI 0.04–1.18); after energy adjustment, the top versus bottom UPF tertile had an overweight/obesity prevalence ratio of about 1.16. No robust BMI links appeared in younger groups. Authors stress that classification scenarios sharply change estimated UPF shares.

**Links:**

- [Nutrition Journal — UPF intake, diet quality, and weight status in Sweden](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12937-026-01408-5)
- [University of Gothenburg — Nov 2 press release](https://www.gu.se/en/news/poorer-diet-with-a-higher-proportion-of-ultra-processed-food)

**Commentary:** Before acting on any UPF headline, check how foods were coded—wide versus narrow NOVA rules can roughly halve the share; day to day, cutting added sugar and raising fiber/whole grains beats debating every loaf’s processing label.

### 2. UK Biobank cross-section: Higher Mediterranean diet scores linked to modestly lower odds of adult acne; dairy is not one category (Nutrition)
**Summary:** Li et al. analyzed 228,757 UK Biobank participants aged 37–73 years (1,603 with current acne) using food-frequency questionnaires and repeated 24-hour recalls. The study was published in the *Journal of the European Academy of Dermatology and Venereology*, with Healio and King’s College London coverage dated Nov 2, 2026. Each 1-point rise in alternate Mediterranean diet score was associated with lower odds of current acne (adjusted OR ≈ 0.93; 95% CI 0.87–0.99), clearer in women (aOR ≈ 0.90); in men, a meat-dominant, lower-carbohydrate pattern tracked higher odds (aOR ≈ 1.04). Cooked vegetables and yoghurt associated with lower odds; skimmed milk and milk-based desserts with higher odds. Cross-sectional design, small effect sizes, and possible rosacea misclassification in records limit causal claims.

**Links:**

- [JEADV — Diet quality and adult acne in UK Biobank](https://doi.org/10.1111/jdv.70747)
- [Healio — Nov 2: Mediterranean diet and adult acne](https://www.healio.com/news/dermatology/20261102/mediterranean-food-dairy-carbs-diets-that-worsen-and-improve-adult-acne)

**Commentary:** Do not treat “cut all dairy” as acne gospel—yoghurt and sweetened milk products pointed different ways; a Mediterranean-leaning plate is a safer interim stance pending trials.

### 3. *Cell Metabolism* feeding RCT: At matched ~10% weight loss, a very-low-carb ketogenic diet improved hepatic insulin sensitivity 2–3× more than Mediterranean or low-fat plant-forward diets (Nutrition)
**Summary:** WashU investigators randomized 55 adults with metabolically unhealthy obesity (prediabetes plus hepatic steatosis) to very-low-carbohydrate ketogenic, Mediterranean, or very-low-fat plant-forward diets for about five months with all food provided. Results appear in *Cell Metabolism*, with Healio and WashU Medicine coverage around Nov 2, 2026. All groups lost ~10% body weight and raised skeletal-muscle insulin sensitivity by ~50%. Hepatic insulin sensitivity improved two to three times more on keto (P<0.001); intrahepatic triglyceride fell ~67% versus ~45% on the other diets. Twenty-four-hour glucose fell ~20% on keto versus ~8% elsewhere; LDL-C, apoB, and 24-hour triglycerides did not differ among groups. Completers were limited and adherence was food-provision driven, so free-living “low carb” claims need caution.

**Links:**

- [Cell Metabolism — Macronutrient content and cardiometabolic response to weight loss](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cmet.2026.07.020)
- [WashU Medicine — Extra liver benefits of a low-carb diet beyond weight loss](https://medicine.washu.edu/news/low-carb-diet-delivers-added-liver-benefits-beyond-weight-loss/)

**Commentary:** Muscle insulin resistance mostly tracks the weight lost; if liver fat and hepatic insulin action are the priority, very-low-carb may add benefit—under clinical monitoring, without declaring Mediterranean eating “useless.”

## II. Exercise & Training Science

### 4. *Complementary Therapies in Medicine* meta-analysis: Short-bout accumulated breaks from sitting lower post-meal glucose and insulin; standing alone is not enough (Training)
**Summary:** Searching through Dec 10, 2025, authors pooled 31 publications from 29 independent cohorts (573 adults) of randomized crossover trials comparing short-bout accumulated exercise (≤10 min/bout; recovery ≥30 min) with uninterrupted sitting on postprandial glucose, insulin, and triglyceride AUCs. Versus continuous sitting, short bouts cut glucose AUC (SMD −0.53; 95% CI −0.71 to −0.34) and insulin AUC (SMD −0.58); triglyceride AUC was unchanged. Exploratory subgroups favored walking and inter-bout intervals <60 min; standing alone or intervals ≥60 min lacked the same signal. Outcomes are acute postprandial metabolism, not long-term hard endpoints.

**Links:**

- [Complementary Therapies in Medicine — Short-bout accumulated exercise and postprandial metabolism](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ctim.2026.103416)
- [Health.com — Short walking breaks and post-meal blood sugar](https://www.health.com/movement-lower-blood-sugar-after-meals-12061769)

**Commentary:** Desk workers can treat a few minutes of walking every 30–60 minutes as a post-meal glucose tool—standing is not a substitute, and snack-sized moves do not replace weekly structured training.

### 5. *Complementary Therapies in Medicine* RCT: 12 weeks of Baduanjin plus resistance-band training improves gait speed and muscle metrics in older adults with sarcopenia (Training)
**Summary:** Eighty adults aged 60–84 with sarcopenia were randomized 1:1:1:1 to Baduanjin, resistance-band training, multimodal (both), or control; sessions were 30 minutes, five times weekly for 12 weeks (65 completed). Significant group-by-time interactions appeared for gait speed and SPPB; only the multimodal arm improved gait speed within group, and all exercise arms beat control at week 12. SPPB rose in all exercise groups. ASMI rose with resistance-band and multimodal training; grip strength rose within the multimodal group. Modest sample, short duration, and body-composition limits warrant longer, better-measured trials.

**Links:**

- [Complementary Therapies in Medicine — Baduanjin plus resistance-band multimodal RCT for sarcopenia](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ctim.2026.103431)

**Commentary:** Community sarcopenia programs should combine balance/mobility work with resistance rather than choosing one; frequent, stickable band sessions often beat waiting for heavy-gym access.

### 6. *Neuroscience* mouse study: Chronic excessive training keeps hippocampal dopamine and tyrosine hydroxylase elevated in the rest phase (Recovery)
**Summary:** Alisson Luiz da Rocha and colleagues compared a single exhaustive treadmill bout with an 8-week progressive overload protocol that produced excessive training load in male mice, measuring hippocampal dopamine, serotonin, TyrH, and VMAT2. Published in *Neuroscience* and covered by MedicalXpress on Nov 2, 2026, acute exhaustion raised dopamine immediately and lowered TyrH; chronic overtraining left dopamine and TyrH elevated during rest, without clear serotonin change. Sleep and anxiety were not measured; sampling cells were small (~4–5 animals per time point) and male-only, so findings are mechanistic clues—not a clinical overtraining diagnosis.

**Links:**

- [Neuroscience — Excessive training and hippocampal dopamine homeostasis in mice](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroscience.2026.08.006)
- [MedicalXpress — Nov 2: Overtraining may disrupt dopamine regulation at rest](https://medicalxpress.com/news/2026-11-overtraining-disrupt-brain-dopamine-elevated.html)

**Commentary:** Treat this as a recovery reminder, not a human prescription—if insomnia, anxiety, and stalled performance pile up, protect rest days before adding another hard session.

## III. Public Health & Disease Prevention

### 7. *NEJM* CONNECT RCT: CGM in adults with type 2 diabetes not on insulin cut HbA1c ~0.9 percentage points more than routine care at 26 weeks (Public Health)
**Summary:** Across 22 U.S. primary-care sites, 283 adults with type 2 diabetes not using insulin were randomized to real-time continuous glucose monitoring (CGM) or routine care. Published in the *New England Journal of Medicine* on Oct 14, 2026 and highlighted by Harvard Health on Nov 2, CGM lowered mean HbA1c from ~8.8% to 7.2% versus ~8.7% to 8.0% with routine care (adjusted mean difference ≈ −0.9 percentage points; 95% CI −1.1 to −0.6). Time in 70–180 mg/dL was about 21 percentage points higher (~5 more hours/day). No severe hypoglycemia events were reported. Dexcom funded the trial; coverage expansion still depends on guidelines and payers.

**Links:**

- [NEJM — Continuous glucose monitoring in non–insulin-treated type 2 diabetes](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2608571)
- [Harvard Health — Nov 2: CGMs help people who don’t need insulin](https://www.health.harvard.edu/diabetes-and-metabolic-health/continuous-glucose-monitors-benefit-people-who-dont-need-insulin)

**Commentary:** Glucose care is not only “add another drug”—if A1c stays high off insulin, ask about CGM as feedback on meals and movement, not as a gadget for its own sake.

### 8. AHA Scientific Sessions abstract: An 8-month medically tailored meal program improved diet quality, HbA1c, and blood pressure in adults with type 2 diabetes and hypertension (Public Health)
**Summary:** More than 100 adults with type 2 diabetes and high blood pressure joined an 8-month medically tailored meals (MTM) program: one arm received 10 meals/week for 8 months; the other started the same then tapered to 5 meals/week. Both arms included dietitian counseling, Life’s Essential 8 education, and cooking support. An American Heart Association Nov 2, 2026 news release reports ~3-point gains on a Mediterranean-style diet score, more than double the odds of better food-security status, ~0.5% lower HbA1c, and ~4 mm Hg lower systolic and diastolic blood pressure; tapering meals did not erase benefits when paired with home-cooking education. Meeting abstracts are preliminary and not peer-reviewed.

**Links:**

- [American Heart Association — Medically tailored meals improved blood pressure, diabetes control, and diet](https://newsroom.heart.org/news/medically-tailored-meals-improved-high-blood-pressure-type-2-diabetes-and-diet-in-adults)

**Commentary:** “Food is medicine” works best when access lasts and skills transfer—cutting meal dose need not fail if people learn what to cook next.

### 9. AHA survey abstract: Among clinics joining a CKM initiative, only ~22%–28% used CKM staging or PREVENT equations (Public Health)
**Summary:** A baseline questionnaire of 97 clinical sites in the American Heart Association’s Cardiovascular-Kidney-Metabolic Health Initiative (mostly outpatient primary care) found that ~75% screened for CKM-related conditions and ~73% systematically tested CKM risk factors, yet only ~28% used PREVENT equations and ~22% used CKM staging; routine waist circumference measurement was reported by ~22%. Results were released Nov 2, 2026. Self-report, initiative selection, and incomplete site capture introduce bias; the abstract is not peer-reviewed.

**Links:**

- [American Heart Association — Gaps in CKM syndrome tool use](https://newsroom.heart.org/news/survey-finds-gaps-in-use-of-cardiovascular-kidney-metabolic-syndrome-tools)
- [MedicalXpress — Nov 2 coverage of the CKM tool survey](https://medicalxpress.com/news/2026-10-survey-gaps-cardiovascular-kidney-metabolic.html)

**Commentary:** At checkups, ask whether waist, blood pressure, glucose, kidney markers, and integrated risk staging are reviewed together—do not wait for every clinic to finish rolling out new frameworks.

## IV. Sleep & Mental Health

### 10. *Frontiers in Psychiatry* meta-analysis of 11 RCTs: Combined sleep–circadian interventions reduce depressive symptoms and improve insomnia in adults with mental disorders (Sleep/Mental Health)
**Summary:** Searching through Apr 12, 2026, investigators included 11 randomized trials of non-pharmacological sleep–circadian interventions in adults with diagnosed mental disorders (depression, bipolar disorder, serious mental illness, ADHD, and mixed samples). Published in *Frontiers in Psychiatry*, interventions associated with lower depressive symptom severity (SMD −0.51; 95% CI −0.72 to −0.30) and higher treatment response (RR ≈ 1.68); insomnia severity improved (MD ≈ −5.23), as did sleep disturbance/quality (SMD ≈ −0.92) and functional impairment. Certainty ranged from very low to moderate; diagnoses and protocols were heterogeneous; remission data came from only two trials. These approaches do not replace individualized psychiatric care.

**Links:**

- [Frontiers in Psychiatry — Sleep–circadian interventions for depression and sleep outcomes](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychiatry/articles/10.3389/fpsyt.2026.1901978/full)

**Commentary:** When mood problems travel with insomnia or irregular schedules, add sleep–circadian care—regular wake times, light timing, CBT-I pathways—rather than relying on sedatives alone.

## V. Evidence Clarification & Supplement Boundaries

### 11. AHA abstract: FDA adverse-event reports link caffeinated energy drinks and stimulant supplements more often to cardiac-arrest and heart-attack reports (Evidence Clarification)
**Summary:** Researchers reviewed >28,000 FDA adverse-event reports from 2017–2025, including 854 involving caffeine- or stimulant-containing energy drinks or dietary supplements versus ~27,491 reports for products without those ingredients. An AHA Scientific Sessions 2026 abstract, released Nov 2, found cardiac arrest reported ~11× more often, heart attack ~4.5× more often, and ventricular arrhythmia/palpitations ~4× more often with stimulant products; ~45% of related cardiovascular reports involved adults aged 18–34. Spontaneous reports cannot prove causation and suffer reporting bias. The release also cites AHA guidance that up to 400 mg caffeine/day is generally acceptable for most adults, while some energy drinks and pre-workouts pack far more caffeine per fluid ounce than coffee.

**Links:**

- [American Heart Association — Energy drinks and stimulant products in FDA cardiac-event reports](https://newsroom.heart.org/news/energy-drinks-stimulant-products-linked-to-cardiac-arrest-heart-attack-in-young-adults)

**Commentary:** Prefer clearly dosed coffee or tea for alertness; avoid stacking cans of energy drink with high-stim pre-workouts—youth is not cardiovascular immunity, and chest pain or palpitations mean stop and seek care.

### 12. *Nutrients* crossover RCT: 6 mg/kg caffeinated gum raised self-selected cycling power ~10%–13% at fixed RPE in well-trained men (Supplement)
**Summary:** Twelve Tier-3 male athletes with low habitual caffeine intake completed a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled crossover: chew 6 mg/kg caffeinated gum or placebo for 15 minutes, then cycle 10 minutes at OMNI RPE 4 and 10 minutes at RPE 7. Published in *Nutrients*, caffeine raised cadence, total work, and mean/peak power (mean power ≈ 88 vs 79 W at RPE 4; 146 vs 129 W at RPE 7), with higher oxygen uptake but no significant heart-rate or lactate differences. The sample was small, male-only, and laboratory-based, limiting generalization.

**Links:**

- [Nutrients — Caffeinated gum and external work during perceptually regulated cycling](https://doi.org/10.3390/nu18213639)

**Commentary:** If you prescribe intensity by RPE, caffeine can make the same feeling produce more real watts—trial dose and gut tolerance before races, and count total daily caffeine so you do not stack with energy drinks.

### 13. *Journal of Biomechanics*: Verbal cues targeting foot-strike angle immediately cut peak knee abduction moment in female collegiate cutting tasks (Sports Medicine)
**Summary:** Thirty-four female university soccer and basketball athletes completed planned 180° and 90° cuts with force and kinematics captured, then received performance-based verbal cues on foot-strike angle, foot-progression angle, or trunk lean before repeating trials. Published in the *Journal of Biomechanics*, foot-strike cues significantly reduced peak knee abduction moment across both sports and both cuts (e.g., soccer 90° ≈ −0.28 Nm/kg, d ≈ 1.16); foot-progression cues helped soccer athletes on 180° cuts; trunk-lean cues did not significantly change loading. Effects were immediate and lab-based—retention on-field and actual ACL injury rates remain unproven.

**Links:**

- [Journal of Biomechanics — Verbal cues and ACL-relevant knee loading in cutting](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbiomech.2026.113638)

**Commentary:** A concrete “land with this foot shape” cue can lower ACL-relevant load faster than vague “brace your core” talk; lasting prevention still needs that feedback inside weekly neuromuscular sessions.
