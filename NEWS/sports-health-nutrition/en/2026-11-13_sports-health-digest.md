## I. Nutrition & Diet

### 1. AJCN: Peanut intervention remolds the lipidome; peanut lipid signature tied to ~11% lower type 2 diabetes risk (nutrition/metabolism)
**Summary:** Researchers at the German Institute of Human Nutrition Potsdam-Rehbruecke and the University of Barcelona integrated the ARISTOTLE intervention with an EPIC-Potsdam case-cohort analysis in *The American Journal of Clinical Nutrition*, with Health Chosun coverage on November 13, 2026. ARISTOTLE randomized 63 healthy adults aged 18–33 to about 25 g/day skin-roasted peanuts, about 32 g/day peanut butter, or isocaloric control butter for six months; peanut arms altered ~51 within-class fatty-acid sums, mainly very-long-chain saturated fatty acid (VLCSFA)–containing sphingolipids, yielding a peanut lipidomic signature (PeLS). In EPIC-Potsdam, each 1-SD higher PeLS associated with ~11% lower T2D risk (HR 0.89, 95% CI 0.80–0.99) but not CVD; BMI appeared to mediate most of the association (~89%). This is linked intervention–observational evidence—not proof that eating peanuts prevents diabetes—and peanuts remain calorie-dense.

**Links:**

- [American Journal of Clinical Nutrition — Peanut lipids and type 2 diabetes](https://ajcn.nutrition.org/article/S0002-9165%2826%2900336-9/fulltext)
- [Health Chosun — November 13 report](https://health.chosun.com/english/site/data/html_dir/2026/11/13/2026111301386.html)

**Commentary:** Prefer unsalted, low-sugar peanuts or peanut butter as a refined-snack swap, but portion by grams—do not treat a “lipid signature” as a license to overeat nuts.

### 2. Pune study: Traditional Indian plate plus daily walking yields ~60.9% early type 2 diabetes remission at six months (nutrition/lifestyle)
**Summary:** Chellaram Diabetes Institute and collaborating clinics tested a Local Healthy Moderate Carbohydrate (LHMC) program, published in *Diabetes and Metabolic Syndrome: Clinical Research and Reviews* (Elsevier, 2026) and reported by the Indian Express on November 13, 2026. Eighty-seven adults with type 2 diabetes for ≤2 years ate familiar Indian foods—multigrain chapatis, lentils, sprouts, greens, nuts, and fruit—in defined portions, plus ~40 minutes of daily walking and ~10 minutes of deep breathing. At six months, 53 participants (60.9%) achieved remission off glucose-lowering drugs; mean weight fell ~4.67 kg and mean HbA1c from 7.32% to 6.27%. Walking on ≥80% of days associated with ~8.24-fold higher adjusted odds of remission. Authors call for a larger controlled trial and warn patients not to stop medicines without medical advice.

**Links:**

- [Indian Express — Indian diet and early diabetes remission (November 13)](https://indianexpress.com/article/cities/pune/early-type-2-diabetes-remission-indian-diet-study-10962186/)

**Commentary:** For early diabetes, a culturally familiar controlled plate plus near-daily walking may beat imported ultra-restrictive diets on adherence—any medication change still needs a clinician.

### 3. AHA 2026: Medically tailored meals improve glycemia and blood pressure in type 2 diabetes with hypertension; gradual dose-down performs similarly (nutrition/food-is-medicine)
**Summary:** An RCT within the AHA Healthcare × Food Initiative was presented at AHA Scientific Sessions 2026 and summarized by Cardiology Advisor on November 13, 2026. One hundred ten adults with type 2 diabetes and hypertension who lacked advanced complications (and thus usual MTM eligibility) were randomized 1:1 to 10 medically tailored meals/week for eight months or a taper from 10/week for three months down by one meal/week to 5/week in the final month; both arms received Life’s Essential 8 nutrition education and cooking support. At eight months across arms, MEPA diet-quality scores rose ~3.15 points; odds of improved food security more than doubled; HbA1c fell ~0.5 percentage points; systolic/diastolic BP fell ~3.7/4.1 mm Hg. Standard versus tapered dosing did not differ significantly on outcomes.

**Links:**

- [The Cardiology Advisor — Medically tailored meals and cardiometabolic outcomes (November 13)](https://www.thecardiologyadvisor.com/reports/medically-tailored-meals-cardiometabolic-outcomes/)

**Commentary:** If payers cover “partial meal delivery plus education,” multimorbid patients gain more than pamphlets alone; similar results after tapering suggest indefinite full-dose delivery may not be required.

### 4. Nature Communications natural experiment: Sugar rationing in the first 1,000 days linked to slower biological aging and lower risk of some age-related diseases (nutrition/early life)
**Summary:** Chinese University of Hong Kong investigators used the abrupt end of postwar UK sugar rationing as a natural experiment in 54,201 UK Biobank participants; the paper published November 13, 2026, in *Nature Communications*. Exposure to rationing during roughly the first 1,000 days of life associated with lower biological age acceleration, with stronger associations for longer exposure and in females, consistent across genetic-susceptibility strata. Lower risks of age-related cataract and chronic kidney disease were also observed, but not for several other age-related diseases studied. This is historical-policy observational evidence—not a modern prescription for zero sugar in infancy, nor proof that adult sugar restriction reverses biological age.

**Links:**

- [Nature Communications — Early-life sugar rationing and biological aging (November 13)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-79639-7)

**Commentary:** For parenting and public nutrition, keep sugar-sweetened drinks and ultra-processed sweets out of early childhood; do not turn wartime rationing data into today’s extreme sugar-elimination fad.

## II. Exercise & Training Science

### 5. Nature Medicine RCT: Thrice-weekly resistance training during tirzepatide cuts lean-tissue loss by ~1.1 kg and raises strength (training/obesity therapy)
**Summary:** University of Western Australia’s Danny Green and colleagues ran T-REx: 120 adults aged 50–70 with overweight/obesity (without type 2 diabetes) all received tirzepatide and were randomized to thrice-weekly supervised resistance exercise or usual activity for 40 weeks; results appear in *Nature Medicine*, with UWA and News-Medical coverage. Weight loss was similar (~−22.3 vs −22.6 kg), as was fat-mass loss; total lean soft-tissue loss was ~−4.67 kg on drug alone versus ~−3.57 kg with training (between-group difference ~1.09 kg, 95% CI 0.43–1.75). Bench press, lat pulldown, and knee-extension strength fell on drug alone and rose with training. Exercise adherence was ~91%. Supervised gym conditions limit direct translation to unsupervised home routines.

**Links:**

- [Nature Medicine — Resistance exercise and tirzepatide lean mass](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-026-04752-5)
- [UWA News — Resistance training with GLP-1 weight loss](https://www.uwa.edu.au/news/article/2026/november/resistance-training-key-to-maintaining-muscle-function-during-glp1-weight-loss)

**Commentary:** On incretin weight-loss therapy, write “2–3 weekly major-muscle resistance sessions” into the plan—optimize healthy years of function, not only the scale.

### 6. Small RCT: Blood-flow restriction during HIIT recovery (not work bouts) improves cyclists’ lactate threshold and sprint power (training/methods)
**Summary:** New Zealand and UK researchers split 10 trained male cyclists into blood-flow restriction during HIIT recovery intervals versus standard HIIT for four weeks; findings in the *International Journal of Sports Science & Coaching* were covered by TodoMountainBike on November 13, 2026. First lactate-threshold power rose from ~270 to ~290 W (~+7.4%) with BFR versus a slight drop in controls; 30-second sprint mean power rose ~50 W (~+7.5%) versus ~+10 W in controls. With only 10 men, no sham cuff, and short follow-up, certainty is very low—not a general public prescription.

**Links:**

- [TodoMountainBike — BFR during HIIT recovery (November 13)](https://www.todomountainbike.net/en/blood-flow-restriction-hiit-recovery-cycling-performance)

**Commentary:** Recreational athletes should first nail interval intensity and recovery; cuff-based BFR is a coached specialty tool, not something to copy from an n=10 trial.

## III. Public Health & Disease Prevention

### 7. Nature: >844,000 people across four countries validate clinical vs preclinical obesity; BMI alone misclassifies both ways (public health/diagnosis)
**Summary:** An Pan of Huazhong University of Science and Technology and international coauthors validated the Lancet Commission obesity framework in *Nature*, with Xinhua coverage on November 13, 2026. Among 844,108 adults in China, UK, US, and Spanish cohorts, adding waist circumference and waist-to-height ratio to BMI showed BMI alone underestimated obesity in ~5.2%–8.6% and overestimated it in ~1.2%–3.1%; clinical obesity comprised ~22.7%–31.3% of confirmed obesity (population prevalence ~5.6%–10.9%). Over >10 years, ~77%–91% with preclinical obesity did not progress to clinical obesity; versus people without obesity or organ impairment, all-cause mortality HRs were ~1.19 for preclinical and ~1.69 for clinical obesity. Combined anthropometric obesity tracked with mortality; BMI-only obesity did not.

**Links:**

- [Nature — Validation of clinical and preclinical obesity](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-11237-5)
- [Xinhua — Obesity diagnosis beyond BMI (November 13)](http://www.gd.xinhuanet.com/20261113/7a9df52cec8f488b943be3b899afe9ca/c.html)

**Commentary:** Do not stop at BMI: measure waist, check organ and lab markers and daily function, then choose lifestyle gatekeeping versus more intensive clinical care.

### 8. WHO: First guideline on type 1 diabetes management in children and adolescents (guidelines/public health)
**Summary:** On November 13, 2026, WHO released its first dedicated guidance on diagnosis, management, and follow-up of type 1 diabetes in people up to 19 years within the consolidated childhood-illness guidelines, aimed especially at primary and secondary care in resource-limited settings. Recommendations cover point-of-care urine dipstick assessment when blood glucose is unavailable; insulin types, starting doses, regimens, and delivery devices; individualized glycemic targets and monitoring; recognition and management of diabetic ketoacidosis and hypoglycemia; and risk-based screening for retinopathy and nephropathy. Guidance was developed with systematic reviews and GRADE for non-specialist clinicians, program managers, and policymakers seeking more equitable access and outcomes.

**Links:**

- [WHO — Type 1 diabetes in children and adolescents (November 13)](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240126121)

**Commentary:** For families and front-line clinicians, secure insulin and glucose monitoring early and set age- and resource-appropriate targets—do not transplant adult type 2 playbooks onto pediatric type 1 care.

### 9. Lancet Digital Health Bump2Baby and Me: Personalized mHealth coaching from pregnancy to 12 months postpartum cuts gestational diabetes (public health/maternal)
**Summary:** University College Dublin’s Sharleen O’Reilly led a multicenter RCT across Ireland, the UK, Spain, and Australia, published November 13, 2026, in *The Lancet Digital Health* and announced by UCD. Eight hundred sixty-five women at 12–24 weeks’ gestation with elevated gestational diabetes risk all received Bluetooth scales and were randomized to usual care or usual care plus the Bump2Baby and Me app with live health-coach support for nutrition, activity, and well-being. Mean BMI at 12 months postpartum did not differ, but gestational diabetes incidence was lower with coaching (CORDIS cites ~14.0% vs 19.7%, ~30% relative reduction); metabolic markers and breastfeeding also improved in reports. The program was safe with no related adverse events; the app is not yet routinely public.

**Links:**

- [UCD Research — Bump2Baby and Me (November 13)](https://www.ucd.ie/research/news/2026/bump2babyandmeappandcoachingsignificantlyreducesdiabetesriskinpregnantwomen/)
- [CORDIS — Lifestyle app and maternal outcomes](https://cordis.europa.eu/article/id/462156-lifestyle-change-app-boosts-maternal-health-outcomes)

**Commentary:** High-risk pregnancies need reachable diet–activity coaching more than weight loss as the sole success metric; when available, fold digital coaches into formal antenatal pathways.

### 10. eClinicalMedicine meta-analysis: Current smoking linked to ~31% higher all-cause dementia risk; former smokers not above never smokers (public health/brain health)
**Summary:** Curtin University’s Mario Siervo and colleagues updated a systematic review and meta-analysis of 61 prospective cohorts (~2.8 million people) in *eClinicalMedicine*, with university and media follow-up into November 13, 2026. Versus never smokers, current smoking associated with RR ~1.31 for all-cause dementia, ~1.40 for Alzheimer’s disease, and ~1.52 for vascular dementia; ~10 and ~20 cigarettes/day mapped to dementia RRs ~1.16 and ~1.34. Former smoking was not associated with elevated risk overall and showed lower all-cause dementia risk than continued smoking (RR ~0.81). Observational data cannot prove causation; authors urge brain-health messaging in tobacco control.

**Links:**

- [Curtin University — Smoking and dementia risk release](https://www.curtin.edu.au/news/media-release/smoking-linked-to-higher-risk-of-dementia-curtin-led-research-finds/)
- [ebiotrade — Meta-analysis abstract reprint](https://www.ebiotrade.com/newsf/2026-11/20261108083349987.htm)

**Commentary:** Add “protect later-life cognition” to quit reasons: risk scales with intensity, and people who have already quit need not assume they are “just as bad off.”

## IV. Sleep & Mental Health

### 11. Scientific Reports TemPredict: Lower wearable sleep-regularity index tracks with worse depression, anxiety, and loneliness (sleep/mental health)
**Summary:** UCSF/UCSD investigators analyzed Oura ring sleep data and monthly mental-health surveys from 22,894 TemPredict adults in *Scientific Reports*, with multiple outlets covering the work on November 13, 2026. Mean Sleep Regularity Index (SRI, 0–100) was ~74.9 with severe depression versus ~83.7 within normal limits; ~78.9 versus ~83.7 for severe versus normal anxiety; ~81.8 versus ~83.8 for elevated loneliness. Each 5-point SRI drop raised odds of worse symptom severity; associations persisted after adjusting for age, sex, and average sleep duration. Cross-sectional/observational design cannot establish direction; the sample was mostly midlife White wearable users in North America/Europe, with disclosed device-industry ties for some authors.

**Links:**

- [Scientific Reports — Sleep regularity and mental-health symptoms](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-77545-y)
- [Neuroscience News — Irregular sleep, depression, and loneliness (November 13)](https://neurosciencenews.com/irregular-sleep-loneliness-depression-32412/)

**Commentary:** Beyond “get eight hours,” stabilize wake and bedtime windows; when shift work and anxiety feed each other, shrink schedule swing before chasing duration alone.

### 12. MIT Nature Aging: CSF waste-clearing waves during deep sleep are smaller in older adults—not only from sleeping less (sleep/brain health)
**Summary:** Laura Lewis’s lab compared adults ~18–39 and ~60–84 during ~two-hour nighttime fMRI/EEG sessions; results published November 13, 2026, in *Nature Aging* and announced by MIT News. In younger adults, non-REM slow waves strengthened and cerebrospinal-fluid (CSF) waves enlarged; older adults showed significantly smaller average CSF waves tied to weaker slow waves, not merely shorter sleep. Awake tasks suggested reduced neurovascular coupling and breath-hold cerebral blood-flow responses explained part—but not all—of the CSF decline; some older individuals retained youthful CSF waves. No participant had diagnosed neurodegeneration; gray-matter atrophy correlated with greater CSF reduction; pink-noise and other enhancers remain untested in older adults here.

**Links:**

- [MIT News — Sleep waste-clearing waves impaired in older adults (November 13)](https://news.mit.edu/2026/waste-clearing-waves-in-brain-during-sleep-are-impaired-in-older-adults-1113)

**Commentary:** Midlife and older adults should protect deep sleep (regular schedule, limit alcohol, screen for apnea)—treat “falling and staying in deep sleep” as brain maintenance, not only clock hours in bed.

## V. Evidence & Clarification

### 13. Consumer Reports: All 21 kids’ vitamins tested missed label accuracy on at least one nutrient; none exceeded heavy-metal concern levels (supplements/safety)
**Summary:** Consumer Reports tested 21 children’s vitamins (Flintstones, Hiya, Nature Made, SmartyPants, and others) for multiple vitamins plus iodine and zinc, and for arsenic, cadmium, lead, and mercury; the report published November 10, 2026, and updated November 12 remains in this week’s window. Good news: no product exceeded experts’ heavy-metal concern thresholds, and CR said products remain OK to take. Yet every product had at least one nutrient above or below claimed label ranges (some by several-fold); ~29% of 206 nutrient measurements fell outside CR’s accuracy bands; two products had at least one nutrient above the intended age group’s upper intake level. This is a snapshot, not a regulatory finding.

**Links:**

- [Consumer Reports — Kids’ vitamins label accuracy and heavy metals (updated November 12)](https://www.consumerreports.org/babies-kids/kids-vitamins-testing-label-accuracy-heavy-metals-a7079992693/)

**Commentary:** Prioritize food for children’s micronutrients; if using a multi, pick reputable brands and avoid “more is better” gummies—watch vitamin A and some B vitamins against age-specific upper limits.
