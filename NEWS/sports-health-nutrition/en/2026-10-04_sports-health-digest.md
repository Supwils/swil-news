## I. Nutrition & Diet

### 1. American College of Cardiology: heart-healthy eating is overall diet quality, and GLP-1 drugs do not replace food (Nutrition / Guidance)
**Summary:** A writing group chaired by Frank Hu of Harvard published the 2026 scientific statement “Food and Health: Nutrition for Prevention and Management of Cardiovascular Disease” online in the Journal of the American College of Cardiology (doi: 10.1016/j.jacc.2026.08.025). An ACC journal scan on September 30 and a TCTMD interview on October 2 summarize the shift from single nutrients toward foods, dietary patterns, processing, and overall diet quality. The statement supports limiting ultra-processed foods, added sugars, excess sodium, and saturated fat, and favoring minimally processed plant foods. That pattern is not a requirement to eat vegan: fish and other protein sources can remain, with unsaturated fats replacing saturated fats. Mediterranean and DASH patterns have stronger prevention evidence. Preferred drinks are water and unsweetened beverages, sugar-sweetened drinks should be limited, and moderate coffee and tea are acceptable for most people. Hu and colleagues write that the balance of evidence does not support regular alcohol use for health benefits. Hu told TCTMD that GLP-1 receptor agonists, statins, and other drugs cannot replace a healthy diet. Compared with the Dietary Guidelines for Americans, both documents favor fewer ultra-processed foods, less added sugar, and fewer refined grains. They diverge where the federal guidance keeps a saturated-fat cap of 10% of calories while still highlighting full-fat dairy, red meat, and butter; the statement treats those foods as something to eat in moderation rather than without an upper bound. On protein, it emphasizes food sources rather than total grams alone.

**Links:**

- [JACC — 2026 ACC scientific statement on food and health](https://www.jacc.org/doi/10.1016/j.jacc.2026.08.025)
- [TCTMD — ACC statement on healthy eating for CVD](https://www.tctmd.com/news/acc-statement-underscores-importance-healthy-eating-managing-cvd)

**Commentary:** People already using a weight-loss drug can still change two concrete foods, such as swapping sugar-sweetened drinks for unsweetened ones and refined staples for whole grains and legumes, rather than adding full-fat red meat and butter in the name of protein.

### 2. Frontiers in Nutrition: personalized menus cut weight 6.0%–7.3% in a German four-arm trial, and the app did not beat paper plans (Nutrition)
**Summary:** Lea Klein, Christine Dawczynski, and colleagues at Friedrich Schiller University Jena, with researchers affiliated with Preventicus, published the PreVA trial (NCT04717076) in Frontiers in Nutrition on September 23, 2026. NutraIngredients reported it on October 2. The single-center, four-arm parallel randomized trial in Jena enrolled 80 adults with a body mass index from 25 to less than 35 between April and July 2021, assigned to an app, individual coaching, general dietary recommendations, or control. All three intervention arms received individualized, nutrient-optimized menus with stepwise energy reduction and flaxseed oil; delivery and counseling intensity differed. After 12 weeks of energy reduction, weight fell 6.5%, 7.3%, and 6.0% in the intervention arms versus 2.1% in control (p < 0.001), and the loss remained stable through the 16-week maintenance phase. Shares reaching more than 5% weight loss were 66.7%, 78.9%, and 53.3%. Blood pressure fell within intervention arms but not significantly versus control. Total and LDL cholesterol fell versus control only in the individual-coaching and general-advice arms. The app arm lowered fasting glucose and HOMA-IR relative to the other groups. Alpha-linolenic acid rose 52.7%–79.4%, while EPA and DHA did not change. The authors say the largest effect likely came from the personalized menus rather than counseling type or app delivery, and that a larger sample might reveal arm differences.

**Links:**

- [Frontiers in Nutrition — PreVA app-based nutrition trial](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/nutrition/articles/10.3389/fnut.2026.1815820/full)
- [NutraIngredients — App coaching and personalized menus](https://www.nutraingredients.com/Article/2026/10/02/study-app-based-personalized-dietary-coaching-effective-for-weight-loss/)

**Commentary:** Before paying for a weight-loss app, check whether it actually supplies a usable menu and an energy step-down; this 80-person trial, which included flaxseed oil and software-company coauthors, did not show that having an app beat a printed personalized plan.

### 3. BMC Public Health: online nutrition groups were followed by less drinking in older adults with mild cognitive impairment, without a no-counseling arm (Nutrition)
**Summary:** Julia-Sophia Scheuermann, Carolin Donath, and colleagues in Germany and Austria published a secondary analysis of BrainFit-Nutrition in BMC Public Health on October 3, 2026 (ISRCTN10560738). The parent trial was a 2×2 factorial randomized design in which participants received computerized cognitive training and nutrition counseling; alcohol was not the primary endpoint. Of 270 participants with mild cognitive impairment, 189 who drank at baseline and attended at least one nutrition session formed the intention-to-treat sample, with 12-month values imputed from six-month data for 15 people. Mean age was 70.6 years and about 52% were women. One arm received whole-food plant-based group counseling and the other counseling based on German Nutrition Society recommendations. Both covered drinks in a session that advised abstinence first and moderation if alcohol was used. At 12 months both arms drank less, with Cohen's d of 0.30 and 0.39, and the between-arm change was not significant (p = 0.173). Women showed a clearer reduction only in the German Nutrition Society arm (d = 0.33). Among 84 successful reducers, higher baseline intake, older age, and stricter attendance predicted larger reductions, while higher cognitive scores predicted smaller ones. Ethics approval was Erlangen-Nuremberg medical faculty 21-318_1-B.

**Links:**

- [BMC Public Health — Online nutrition counseling and alcohol in MCI](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12889-026-29728-5)

**Commentary:** Group nutrition counseling can be one entry point for older adults with mild cognitive impairment who drink, but everyone in this analysis received counseling, so the within-arm drop does not show that switching diet patterns causes less drinking, and it is not a reason to stop prescribed treatment.

## II. Exercise & Training Science

### 4. Scientific Reports: four preoperative robot sessions for knee osteoarthritis were completable with no adverse events, without proof of benefit (Training)
**Summary:** Ravi Shankar, Poo Lee Ong, and colleagues at Tan Tock Seng Hospital in Singapore, with staff from Articares, published a single-arm feasibility study in Scientific Reports on October 4, 2026 (ClinicalTrials.gov: NCT07539948; NHG DSRB 2024-3529). Knee-LOCOBOT delivered cycling, leg press, and isometric quadriceps work before total knee replacement. Ten of 15 screened patients with knee osteoarthritis enrolled, nine finished all four sessions (90% retention; 100% adherence among completers), and one withdrew for personal reasons unrelated to the device. Six-minute-walk distance moved from a median of 291 m to 360 m, with a median within-person change of +15 m and a mean change of +31 m (p = 0.012, n = 9). With no control arm, the authors state that this change cannot be attributed to the intervention. A numerical rise in right quadriceps strength was not significant (p = 0.05, unadjusted for multiple comparisons), and the exercised-versus-unexercised limb difference was small (Cohen's d = 0.11, p = 0.75). No adverse events were recorded, and 88.9% agreed the device was acceptable. Participants were selected patients with relatively low pain who were heading to surgery. Funding was a CMTi-NHIC joint medtech grant, with no funder role in design or analysis.

**Links:**

- [Scientific Reports — Knee-LOCOBOT feasibility before knee replacement](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-73345-6)

**Commentary:** Before knee replacement, strength and walking practice with a physiotherapist is still the usable plan; four robot sessions show that this device can be finished safely, not that it improves walking or delays surgery.

## III. Sleep & Mental Health

### 5. BMC Psychology: in a Chinese midlife and older cohort, part of the link between childhood adversity and depressive symptoms ran through self-reported sleep duration (Sleep)
**Summary:** Xiaohan Qi of Shandong Second Medical University, Chengchao Zhou of Shandong University, Shujun Chai of Renmin University of China, and colleagues published a mediation analysis in BMC Psychology on October 1, 2026. Using the 2014 and 2018 waves of the China Health and Retirement Longitudinal Study, they included 9,914 participants older than 45. Linear regression with bootstrap tests linked childhood socioeconomic disadvantage (β = 0.465), parental-involved trauma (β = 0.497), maladaptive parental trauma (β = 0.153), and other trauma (β = 1.350) to later depressive symptoms. Self-reported sleep duration statistically mediated each path, with indirect associations of 0.066, 0.043, 0.046, and 1.140. CHARLS ethics approval was Peking University IRB00001052-11015; this paper used de-identified secondary data. The authors describe statistical mediation, not a proven causal chain, and sleep architecture was not measured with devices.

**Links:**

- [BMC Psychology — Childhood adversity, sleep duration, and depressive symptoms](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40359-026-05723-8)

**Commentary:** A visit for depression in midlife or later is a reasonable time to ask how many hours someone usually sleeps; treating sleep as adjustable is fair, while this questionnaire mediation does not mean that longer sleep erases childhood adversity.

## IV. Public Health & Disease Prevention

### 6. China CDC: holiday return travel should not rely on alcohol hand gel for norovirus, and priority groups are still advised to get influenza vaccine (Public health)
**Summary:** An interview by Xinhua reporter Li Heng was published on People's Daily Health on October 4, 2026. Peng Zhibin, a researcher at the Chinese Center for Disease Control and Prevention, said hand, foot, and mouth disease and norovirus can infect someone again, so a past illness does not confer later protection. With more holiday meals and travel, she advised hand hygiene, food hygiene, and personal protection: wash with soap or liquid soap under running water for at least 20 seconds before meals, after the toilet, and after returning from outside. Norovirus is not sensitive to alcohol, so typical alcohol gels do not fully replace washing. Avoid untreated water and raw or unclean food, and cook seafood and shellfish through. Ventilate hotels and guesthouses. Older adults, children, and people with chronic disease should complete influenza vaccination early to lower the risk of severe illness. Fever, rash, vomiting, or diarrhea should prompt medical care. In the same interview, Jiao Juan of Guang'anmen Hospital suggested gentle fist and joint movements on cool mornings and using only one painkiller at a time, and Cai Zhigang of Peking University Hospital of Stomatology said bleeding gums are often plaque, so brushing should not stop because of blood.

**Links:**

- [Xinhua — Autumn health and illness prevention](https://www.news.cn/20261003/cfec2879cf414b879ca04a18c590b8a2/c.html)

### 8. WHO: Bangladesh opened an independent immunization review after the 2026 measles outbreak exposed immunity gaps (Public health)
**Summary:** The WHO Bangladesh office said on October 4, 2026, that, at the government's request and with WHO and partner support, Bangladesh launched an independent review of the Expanded Programme on Immunization and vaccine-preventable disease surveillance. The review runs October 4–14 at national level and across all eight divisions. It will identify strengths, gaps, and priority actions, and look at whether detection and response in the 2026 measles outbreak were timely and adequate, including progress back toward measles and rubella elimination. International participants include WHO headquarters and the South-East Asia Regional Office, UNICEF, the International Federation of Red Cross and Red Crescent Societies, Australia's National Centre for Immunisation Research and Surveillance, and the Measles & Rubella Initiative. Key findings are due on October 14, with a draft report and action plan expected in November. This is not a new vaccine-composition recommendation for ordinary travelers.

**Links:**

## V. Evidence & Clarification

### 9. Maharashtra FDA: nutraceutical checks will cover contents, labels, and sales, after most reviewed ads needed changes (Evidence)
**Summary:** Business Standard reported on October 4, 2026, that Maharashtra Food and Drug Administration commissioner Tukaram Mundhe told The Indian Express the agency will widen nutraceutical scrutiny from labels to contents, consumption, prescription, and selling. He called existing findings disturbing: products claim an ingredient that is not actually present, alongside misleading claims and inappropriate use. In its 2025–26 complaints report released in May, the Advertising Standards Council of India said nutraceuticals accounted for 52% of the food-and-beverage advertising cases it examined, the largest subcategory, and that 96% required modification. India's food regulator classifies nutraceuticals as food and allows tablet and capsule formats, but appearance does not make them medicines. The 2018 advertising and claims rules prohibit food claims to prevent, alleviate, treat, or cure disease unless specifically permitted. Labels must say “not for medicinal use” and warn against exceeding the recommended daily amount. A January 2020 clarification restated that added vitamins, minerals, and amino acids should not exceed the applicable recommended dietary allowance. The article does not publish a failed-product list or measured deviations from this round of testing.

**Links:**

- [Business Standard — Maharashtra FDA to scrutinize nutraceuticals](https://www.business-standard.com/india-news/maharashtra-fda-nutraceuticals-supplements-labelling-claims-fssai-tukaram-mundhe-126100400197_1.html)

**Commentary:** A capsule is not a medicine; if the label claims to treat a disease or pushes a dose above the daily amount, stop and read the ingredient list rather than waiting for a local inspection result.
