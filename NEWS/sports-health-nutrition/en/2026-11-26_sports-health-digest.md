## I. Nutrition & Diet

### 1. UK Biobank cohort: higher MIND diet adherence linked to lower incident epilepsy risk; nuts the clearest food signal (Nutrition)
**Summary:** A prospective cohort in the *American Journal of Clinical Nutrition* followed 107,016 UK Biobank participants with at least two 24-hour dietary assessments; 511 developed incident epilepsy. Each 1-SD higher MIND score was associated with about 13% lower incident epilepsy risk (HR 0.867, 95% CI 0.791–0.951); NHANES showed a parallel cross-sectional inverse association (OR 0.772). Among MIND components, nuts had the clearest inverse link; multi-omics flagged 16 mostly fatty-acid/lipoprotein metabolites plus exploratory protein and brain-structure signals. Observational design cannot prove causation; mediation and protein findings are nominal/exploratory.

**Links:**

- [PubMed — MIND diet, incident epilepsy and multi-omics](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43085661/)
- [Am J Clin Nutr — DOI 10.1016/j.ajcnut.2026.101641](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ajcnut.2026.101641)

**Commentary:** Do not treat nuts as epilepsy medicine—keep leafy greens, berries, whole grains, and modest nuts in a brain-healthy plate, which remains the more evidence-aligned daily move.

### 2. 24-week Chinese RCT: CGM-guided postprandial-targeting diet beat a standard diabetic diet (Nutrition/Clinical)
**Summary:** In *Food Science and Human Wellness*, 175 Chinese adults with prediabetes or unmedicated type 2 diabetes were randomized for 24 weeks to a continuous glucose monitoring (CGM)–guided personalized postprandial-targeting (PPT) diet versus the standardized Chinese Diabetic Diet (CDD). Versus CDD, PPT produced larger HbA1c reduction (about −0.46% vs +0.06%; between-group difference about −0.4%), greater fasting and 2-hour OGTT improvements, and larger drops in postprandial glucose exposure, LDL-C, triglycerides, and weight (about −6.83 vs −0.55 kg). Long-term sustainability and cost-effectiveness need further study.

**Links:**

- [Food Science and Human Wellness — CGM-guided personalized diet RCT](https://doi.org/10.26599/fshw.2026.9250961)

**Commentary:** If you already use CGM in prediabetes/early T2D, clinician-guided meal tweaks from your own postprandial curves may outperform a one-size diabetic menu—without monitoring, master the standard diet first.

### 3. 6-month RCT: under matched calories, balanced, low-carb, and low-fat diets showed similar weight and visceral-fat trajectories (Nutrition)
**Summary:** An *European Journal of Clinical Nutrition* randomized trial compared three individualized calorie-restricted diets—balanced macros, lower carbohydrate, and lower fat—plus an active control of group counseling based on Brazilian and WHO guidance, in overweight adults for six months (~20 kcal/kg/day in intervention arms), with ultrasound visceral and subcutaneous fat. Body weight and visceral fat fell over time (~3% average weight loss) without significant time × group interactions; lipids, glucose, HbA1c, and blood pressure also improved over time without macronutrient-specific differences. Authors caution that final power may have been limited for small-to-moderate between-group effects and that adherence/recall bias preclude claiming equivalence.

**Links:**

- [European Journal of Clinical Nutrition — Dietary interventions and abdominal fat](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41430-026-01845-9)

**Commentary:** For fat loss, prioritize a calorie gap you can sustain—not endless allegiance to balanced versus low-carb versus low-fat labels.

### 4. Narrative review: precision nutrition better predicts postprandial responses, but no CV-event RCTs yet (Evidence check)
**Summary:** A *Nutrients* narrative review maps precision nutrition for cardiovascular prevention: interindividual postprandial glucose, triglyceride, and inflammatory variation is well documented, and microbiome/metabolomics/ML models improve short-term response prediction, yet most lack external validation and rely on surrogates. No randomized trial has tested precision strategies against cardiovascular events; Food4Me found no genotype benefit beyond phenotype, and DIETFITS’s prespecified genotype/insulin-secretion predictors failed prospectively. Individualization that has reached guidelines mostly stratifies on inexpensive measures clinicians already collect (e.g., kidney function, drugs, GLP-1RA-era protein/fiber/fluid targets, potassium-enriched salt).

**Links:**

- [Nutrients — Precision nutrition and cardiovascular disease](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-6643/18/23/3930)
- [Ebiotrade — Precision nutrition CVD review digest](https://n.ebiotrade.com/newsf/2026-11/20261126082803961.htm)

**Commentary:** Stabilize a proven Mediterranean/DASH-style pattern before paying for omics panels—“better prediction” is not yet proof of fewer heart attacks or strokes.

## II. Exercise & Training Science

### 5. Tübingen lifestyle program: higher or improved cardiorespiratory fitness linked to favorable carotid IMT change (Training/Prevention)
**Summary:** In *Atherosclerosis*, 208 adults at risk for type 2 diabetes in the Tübingen Lifestyle Intervention Program had cardiorespiratory fitness (CRF), carotid intima–media thickness (cIMT), and metabolic measures at baseline and 9 months. Most cardiometabolic markers improved, but cIMT change varied widely: baseline CRF and ΔCRF independently associated with ΔcIMT; upper-tertile cIMT change averaged about +14% versus about −16% in the lower tertile. Each SD greater CRF improvement linked to lower odds of cIMT progression (OR ~0.05) and higher odds of regression (OR ~13), with wide confidence intervals. Small sample, short follow-up, and Southern German Caucasian participants limit generalizability.

**Links:**

- [Atherosclerosis — Cardiorespiratory fitness and cIMT change](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.atherosclerosis.2026.121945)
- [Medical Dialogues — Higher CRF and arterial damage progression](https://medicaldialogues.in/cardiology-ctvs/news/higher-cardiorespiratory-fitness-linked-to-lower-risk-of-arterial-damage-progression-study-finds-183804)

**Commentary:** In prediabetes lifestyle programs, track walk/treadmill fitness—not only the scale and labs—as a practical signal that arteries may still be benefiting.

### 6. Science Advances: 8-week HIIT remodeled muscle proteomes more in obesity and T2D; AMPKγ2 a candidate regulator (Training/Mechanism)
**Summary:** Lean men and men with obesity or type 2 diabetes underwent skeletal-muscle proteomics/phosphoproteomics before and after 8 weeks of high-intensity interval training (HIIT). Baseline proteins stepped from lean to obesity to T2D; HIIT drove stronger mitochondrial up-regulation and proteasomal down-regulation in obesity/T2D, with diagnosis-dependent phosphoproteomic insulin-signaling adaptations. AMPKγ2 (PRKAG2) rose across phenotypes and increased incorporation into AMPKα2β2 complexes; overexpression in human myotubes raised basal glucose oxidation. Findings are from men; cell work does not prove γ2 is required for training benefit.

**Links:**

- [Science Advances — Muscle proteomics after HIIT](https://www.science.org/doi/full/10.1126/sciadv.aeg8071)

**Commentary:** Metabolic disease is not a reason to avoid structured HIIT when medically appropriate—muscle “remodeling room” may be larger; dose still follows tolerance and clinical clearance.

### 7. RCT meta-analysis: under common prescriptions, longer vs shorter interset rest for 1RM gains remains uncertain (Training)
**Summary:** A *Frontiers in Physiology* systematic review/meta-analysis (search through Sep 8, 2026) included 21 randomized trials comparing prescribed interset rest during ≥4 weeks of resistance training. Under common prescriptions, between-group differences for upper-body 1RM (4 trials, 88 participants; Hedges' g ≈ 0.12) and lower-body 1RM (8 trials, 165; g ≈ 0.23) were unclear; confidence intervals allowed either direction and equivalence was not established. Authors advise choosing rest by intended workload, next-set performance, and available training time.

**Links:**

- [Frontiers in Physiology — Longer vs shorter interset rest for strength](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/physiology/articles/10.3389/fphys.2026.2032957/abstract)

**Commentary:** Write rest into the plan: for heavy strength work, recover enough for quality reps; when time is tight, ~2-minute fixed rests often beat open-ended “until I feel ready” without clear strength penalties.

### 8. Nutrients SR/MA: exercise, diet, and combined programs in older adults show outcome-specific benefits; combined superiority is not robust (Training/Nutrition)
**Summary:** A *Nutrients* systematic review and meta-analysis (PROSPERO CRD420261425358; search to Jun 20, 2026), summarized Nov 26, included 65 reports with 34 contributing 36 outcome-specific pools. Exercise improved weight status, adiposity, muscle mass, strength (overall strength g ≈ 0.67), and walking endurance (g ≈ 0.64); diet improved weight status and walking endurance; combined programs improved weight, whole-body fat, and walking endurance (g ≈ 0.80) in primary analyses, but those three combined estimates lost significance after excluding high risk-of-bias trials. Certainty was mostly very low to low; authors state combined superiority/synergy is not established.

**Links:**

- [Ebiotrade — Exercise and diet interventions in older adults meta-analysis](https://n.ebiotrade.com/newsf/2026-11/20261126082748283.htm)

**Commentary:** For older adults, prioritize structured movement you can do; add nutrition support for malnutrition or sarcopenia risk—do not assume “exercise plus a supplement” automatically doubles the benefit.

## III. Sleep & Mental Health

### 9. JMIR meta-analysis: digital insomnia tools improve severity and efficiency, but total sleep rises only ~6–12 minutes (Sleep)
**Summary:** Investigators from the First Hospital of China Medical University pooled 44 RCTs (10,521 participants; 14 countries; 2004–2026) in the *Journal of Medical Internet Research*. Digital health interventions improved insomnia severity (SMD ≈ −0.91) and sleep efficiency (SMD ≈ 0.59); total sleep time SMD ≈ 0.20 equated to roughly 6–12 extra minutes—well below a ~30-minute clinical-meaningfulness threshold. Prediction intervals for severity and total sleep crossed the null; GRADE certainty was very low to moderate, and blinding was rarely possible. Authors position digital tools as low-intensity stepped-care options, not standard substitutes for face-to-face CBT-I.

**Links:**

- [JMIR — Efficacy of digital health interventions for insomnia](https://www.jmir.org/2026/1/e90905/)
- [Health Chosun — Digital insomnia apps and total sleep time](https://health.chosun.com/english/site/data/html_dir/2026/11/26/2026112601875.html)

**Commentary:** Expect insomnia apps to cut time awake in bed and raise efficiency—not to add half an hour of sleep; moderate-to-severe cases still warrant formal CBT-I evaluation.

## IV. Evidence & Clarifications

### 10. Slovenian market omega-3 supplements: only ~44% closely matched labeled EPA+DHA (Supplements)
**Summary:** *Frontiers in Nutrition* analyzed 26 commercial omega-3 food supplements for fatty-acid composition, oxidation, and vitamin E. Only about 44% closely matched declared EPA+DHA totals; close agreement for individual EPA and DHA was about 39%. Peroxide values ranged ~1.3–26.2 meq O₂/kg oil (~80.8% <10); among applicable non-krill products most had p-AV ≤20, yet only about half met Totox ≤26. Total vitamin E correlated inversely with MDA; EPA+DHA content itself did not. Label inaccuracy undermines informed choice and intake assessment.

**Links:**

- [Frontiers in Nutrition — Omega-3 supplement label accuracy](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/nutrition/articles/10.3389/fnut.2026.1998103/full)

**Commentary:** Prefer third-party/pharmacopeial testing and oxidation quality over headline milligrams—and favor oily fish in the diet when feasible for long-chain omega-3s.

### 11. Frontiers SR: lifestyle programs improve obesity-related metabolism, but microbiome shifts do not reliably track clinical gains (Evidence check)
**Summary:** A *Frontiers in Nutrition* systematic review (summary dated Nov 26, 2026) included 28 RCTs with direct gut-microbiota outcomes: 20 diet-only, 4 exercise-only, and 4 combined. Interventions produced variable shifts in taxa such as Akkermansia, Faecalibacterium, Bifidobacterium, and Roseburia without consistent diversity/composition effects; weight, fat mass, visceral adiposity, and selected insulin/lipid/inflammatory markers often improved, yet clinical gains were not consistently accompanied by detectable microbiome changes. Heterogeneity precluded meta-analysis; relative effectiveness or synergy across modalities remains uncertain.

**Links:**

- [Frontiers in Nutrition — Lifestyle interventions and gut microbiome](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/nutrition/articles/10.3389/fnut.2026.1950930/abstract)

**Commentary:** Judge diet and activity programs by weight and metabolic markers—not by whether a stool test says the microbiome “improved.”

### 12. Target-trial emulation: compression garments did not lower lower-extremity running-injury odds in marathon training (Sports medicine)
**Summary:** *PLOS One* (Nov 12, 2026) emulated a target trial with SUMMUM-2017 data on 329 half-/full-marathon runners free of lower-extremity running-related injury (RRI) at baseline, followed for 16 weeks. Mixed logistic regression found no significant association between compression-garment use and RRI in any 2-week window (OR 1.07, 95% CI 0.48–2.40). Compression grade and compliance were unrecorded, so specific pressure doses remain untested.

**Links:**

- [PLOS One — Compression garments and running-related injuries](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0362483)

**Commentary:** Wear compression for comfort if you like it—do not substitute it for progressive loading, strength work, and neuromuscular warm-ups for injury prevention.
