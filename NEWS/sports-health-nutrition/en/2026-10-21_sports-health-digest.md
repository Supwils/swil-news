## I. Nutrition and Diet

### 1. Journal of Agriculture and Food Research: Across 13 randomized trials and 690 adults, sesame lowered total cholesterol by 11.65 mg/dL and LDL cholesterol by 9.14 mg/dL (Nutrition)
**Summary:** Woorawee Inthachat, Chaowanee Chupeerach, Dunyaporn Trachootham, and Piya Temviriyanukul published a systematic review and meta-analysis in the October 2026 issue of the Journal of Agriculture and Food Research; Crossref deposited the record on October 20. Searches covered CENTRAL, Embase, EBSCO Open Dissertation, PubMed, and Scopus. Mean differences were pooled with random-effects models, and risk of bias was assessed with RoB 2. Thirteen randomized trials and 690 adults compared sesame oil (4.5-30 g/day), sesame seeds (25-60 g/day), or the lignan sesamin (200 mg/day) with control. Sesame lowered total cholesterol by 11.65 mg/dL (95% CI -18.73 to -4.57) and LDL cholesterol by 9.14 mg/dL (95% CI -14.40 to -3.89). Triglycerides and HDL cholesterol did not change significantly overall. Significant reductions in total and LDL cholesterol were seen with whole sesame products (oil or seed) and with interventions lasting at least eight weeks. The abstract calls for larger samples, interventions of three to six months, quantified lignan content, and clinically relevant endpoints.

**Links:**

- [Journal of Agriculture and Food Research — Sesame, sesamin, and the serum lipid profile](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jafr.2026.103379)

**Commentary:** Using sesame to nudge lipids means the trial doses, about 25-60 g of seeds or 4.5-30 g of oil a day for at least eight weeks, and an LDL drop of about 9 mg/dL; that does not replace lipid-lowering medicine, and cardiovascular events were not the measured endpoint.

## II. Exercise and Training Science

### 2. Journal of Thermal Biology: In 40 healthy adults after one eccentric session, hot water, cold water, hot air, and sitting did not separate 72-hour strength recovery (Training)
**Summary:** Freya Bayne and colleagues published a randomized parallel trial in the October 2026 issue of the Journal of Thermal Biology (article 104551; the institutional record lists it online on September 18), and the University of East London summarized it on October 21. Forty healthy, physically active adults completed eccentric knee extensions and were randomized to four groups of 10: 30 minutes in 42°C water, 15 minutes in 9°C water, 30 minutes of 30°C air, or 30 minutes sitting at 18°C. Maximal voluntary contraction fell by about 36% immediately after exercise in every group. There was a main effect of time (P = 0.001) and of group (P = 0.032), with no time-by-group interaction (P = 0.93). At 72 hours, hot water, hot air, and control were back to about 88%-89% of baseline and cold water to about 82%, and the between-group difference was not statistically significant. Creatine kinase-MM returned to baseline by 48 hours after hot water and was still elevated at 72 hours after cold water (time-by-group interaction P = 0.02). Heat shock protein 72 rose more after hot water and hot air (interaction P = 0.001). Pain and soreness remained above baseline at 72 hours in every group, and no thermal strategy fully restored them.

**Links:**

- [Journal of Thermal Biology — Thermal recovery strategies and strength after muscle damage](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jtherbio.2026.104551)
- [Medical Xpress — University of East London note on heat and cold, October 21](https://medicalxpress.com/news/2026-10-ice-cold-affect-recovery.html)

**Commentary:** For the three days after one session that makes muscles sore, an ice bath did not restore strength beyond sitting still; heat changed heat-shock protein and creatine kinase trajectories without a clear drop in soreness, and each arm had only 10 people.

### 3. GeroScience: In 441 Hungarian adults aged 60 or older, personalized telephone lifestyle advice did not lower all-cause mortality (Training)
**Summary:** Szilárd Váncsa, Anett Szakolczai, Roland Molontay, and colleagues published PROACTIVE-19, a nationwide open-label two-arm randomized trial, in GeroScience on October 19. The trial started in Hungary in March 2020, during the first wave of COVID-19. Adults aged 60 or older were assigned 1:1 to general or personalized lifestyle counselling by telephone, covering mental health, smoking, physical activity, diet, and alcohol. The planned primary endpoint was a composite of COVID-19 severity; because infections were few, all-cause mortality was assessed. The modified intention-to-treat analysis included 441 people (214 general, 227 personalized). The hazard ratio for all-cause mortality was 0.66 (95% CI 0.28-1.53), with no significant difference. Fruit and vegetable intake changed over time (beta = 0.052, 95% CI 0.026-0.074), and smoking fell among current smokers (beta = -0.372, 95% CI -0.528 to -0.222). Among baseline drinkers, estimated alcohol intake fell more in the personalized group (beta = -1.357, 95% CI -2.425 to -0.187), especially in women. BMI rose among participants with obesity in the personalized group (beta = 0.068, 95% CI 0.013-0.119). Registration: NCT04321928.

**Links:**

- [GeroScience — Telemedicine lifestyle advice for older adults during the pandemic](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11357-026-02585-1)

**Commentary:** An extra, more personalized lifestyle phone call for adults over 60 is not a reason to expect lower mortality; people who already drank may drink less, BMI rose among those with obesity in the personalized arm, and the trial was open-label.

## III. Public Health and Disease Prevention

### 4. WHO, WMO, UNICEF, and UNFPA: On October 21 they released priority indicators for extreme heat in pregnant women, newborns, and children (Public health)
**Summary:** On October 21, the World Health Organization, the World Meteorological Organization, UNICEF, and the United Nations Population Fund released a first set of priority indicators so countries can track how extreme heat affects pregnant women, newborns, and children. The release cites existing evidence that extreme-heat exposure is associated with 26% higher odds of preterm birth; that in 2024, infants under one year experienced 389% more heatwave days than in the 1986-2005 baseline; and that an estimated 1.5 billion children are exposed to heatwaves that are becoming more frequent, longer, or more severe. Countries are asked to monitor preterm birth, stillbirth, low birthweight, pregnancy hypertension, child deaths, and health-facility visits during extreme heat, and school attendance can show how heat disrupts children's lives. Bruce Gordon, acting director of WHO's Department of Environment, Climate Change, One Health and Migration, said health systems need the data to protect people during heatwaves and to build climate risk into routine care. Pascale Allotey, director of WHO's Department of Sexual, Reproductive, Maternal, Child and Adolescent Health and Ageing, said outcomes that are not counted cannot be answered.

**Links:**

- [WHO — October 21 framework on extreme heat, pregnancy, newborns, and children](https://www.who.int/news/item/21-10-2026-new-global-framework-launched-to-track-increasing-health-risks-of-extreme-heat-for-pregnant-women--newborns-and-children)

**Commentary:** In pregnancy and with infants at home, a heat warning is a health exposure, so cooling, fluids, and avoiding the hottest hours come first; these indicators are for health systems to count, not a trial of a cooling product.

**Summary:** On October 21, the Chinese Center for Disease Control and Prevention reported that on October 20, local time, the U.S. National Academy of Medicine awarded the grand-prize stage of the Healthy Longevity Global Competition in Washington. The project "Optimal Health for the Oldest Old," led by Xiaoming Shi, placed third and was the only top-three project from Asia. Co-investigators include Chen Mao of Southern Medical University, Yuebin Lyu of China CDC's Institute of Environmental Health, and Kai Yu of the Nestle Research Institute of Health Sciences. The notice summarizes three existing lines of work: studies in adults aged 80 or older relating blood pressure, lipids, and body mass index to death, cognition, and other outcomes, with the claim that evaluation should consider age and function; leisure activity standing out at younger older ages and diet mattering more at the oldest ages; and leadership of the group standard on a suitable blood-pressure range for the oldest old in China (T/CPMA 017-2020) and the health-industry standard on a suitable body-mass-index range and weight management (WS/T 868-2025). The grand-prize stage selected 14 winners from 147 semifinalists. The team also reports a biomarker cohort of more than 4,000 centenarians. The October 21 notice does not publish new blood-pressure or body-mass-index cut points.

**Links:**

**Commentary:** After age 80, blood pressure, lipids, and body mass index need age and function in the reading; younger older adults still need leisure activity, and the oldest ages need enough suitable food. ### 6. U.S. CDC: In 2025, 38.9% of high school students ate fruit daily and 25.0% met daily aerobic activity, with 16.1% meeting both aerobic and muscle-strengthening guides (Public health)
**Summary:** Caitlin Merlo, Ryan Saelee, Shannon L. Michael, and colleagues reported the 2025 U.S. Youth Risk Behavior Survey in an MMWR supplement on October 15. The comparison uses national samples of 20,103 students in 2023 and 10,710 students in grades 9-12 in 2025. In the seven days before the 2025 survey, 38.9% ate fruit at least once a day, 62.5% ate vegetables at least once a day, 27.0% ate breakfast daily, 30.4% had not drunk soda, 50.8% had not drunk sports drinks, and 58.3% drank at least three glasses of plain water a day. Fruit at least twice a day was 28.1%, and vegetables at least three times a day were 16.2%. Daily aerobic activity of at least 60 minutes on all seven days was 25.0%, muscle-strengthening on at least three days was 50.6%, and meeting both was 16.1%. Physical education on all five days was 19.8%, and 57.6% had played on at least one sports team in the past 12 months. From 2023 to 2025, daily fruit, vegetables, and at least three glasses of plain water rose, daily physical education fell, and sports-team participation rose. Male students' daily breakfast was 31.3% versus 22.5% for female students; daily aerobic activity was 31.5% versus 18.2%. Questions record frequency rather than servings, and missingness exceeded one third for some items, including sports drinks, because not every student was asked every question.

**Links:**

- [CDC MMWR — Diet and physical activity among U.S. high school students, 2025](https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/volumes/75/su/su7501a2.htm)

**Commentary:** U.S. high school students still show only about one in four meeting daily aerobic activity and about one in six meeting both aerobic and strength guides; fruit and vegetable frequency rose, while the higher bar of fruit twice a day and vegetables three times a day stays low, and the numbers are self-reported counts.

### 7. U.S. CDC: Among adults with a first-degree family history of cancer, genetic testing was 12.9% when a clinician had discussed family risk and 2.7% when not (Public health)
**Summary:** Abe E. Sahmoun published a cross-sectional analysis in Preventing Chronic Disease on October 15, 2026, volume 23. The data are the 2023 National Health Interview Survey, limited to U.S. adults with a first-degree family history of cancer. The 9,476 respondents represent about 72.6 million adults. Half reported a clinician discussion of family cancer risk, and 7.8% reported genetic testing for cancer risk. Testing was 12.9% among those who reported a discussion and 2.7% among those who did not (P < 0.001). In fully adjusted models, clinician discussion had an adjusted odds ratio of 5.35 for testing (95% CI 4.23-6.76). Older age, female sex, higher education, and a personal cancer history were also associated with testing. The analysis used survey weights and propensity-score matching. Exposure and outcome were self-reported, and the design does not show that the discussion itself caused appropriate testing.

**Links:**

- [CDC Preventing Chronic Disease — Family cancer-risk discussion and genetic testing](https://www.cdc.gov/pcd/issues/2026/26_0179.htm)

**Commentary:** Adults with a parent, sibling, or child who has had cancer can ask whether genetic counseling applies; discussion and testing travel together, most people were still untested even after a discussion, and the result is cross-sectional self-report.

### 8. American Society of Human Genetics: In population sequencing of more than 525,000 adults across 14 health systems, about 1.75% carried an actionable variant and screening rose within six months (Public health)
**Summary:** On October 20, the American Society of Human Genetics released an observational study presented by Catherine Hajek of Helix. Familial hypercholesterolemia, hereditary breast and ovarian cancer, and Lynch syndrome are estimated to affect 1% to 2% of the population. The Helix Research Network enrolled more than 525,000 adults from 2018 to 2026 and sequenced pathogenic or likely pathogenic variants in nine genes tied to those three conditions. About 9,200 carriers were identified, 1.75% of participants. Carriers who had not previously received recommended screening started it at higher rates than non-carriers. Lynch syndrome carriers were more than eight times as likely to start a first colonoscopy, and carriers linked to familial hypercholesterolemia or hereditary breast and ovarian cancer also increased cholesterol testing and mammography. The changes began within six months of the result. Risk-reducing surgery and cholesterol-lowering treatment also increased, and elevated cholesterol, breast cancer, and colorectal cancer were found more often among carriers. This is real-world observation across 14 U.S. health systems, and the society release does not link a journal paper.

**Links:**

- [ASHG — October 20 release on population genetic screening and preventive care](https://www.ashg.org/publications-news/press-releases/population-genetic-screening-prompts-lasting-preventive-health-actions/)

**Commentary:** Population sequencing can find people who do not know they carry a risk variant and can start the colonoscopy, cholesterol test, or mammogram they were missing; this is a company network's meeting report, and it does not yet show that cancer deaths fell in this cohort.

## IV. Sleep and Mental Health

### 9. BMC Psychiatry: Among 15,396 adult psychiatric outpatients in Dongguan and Hangzhou, three sleep profiles had adjusted odds ratios of 1.22-1.25 for active suicidal ideation (Sleep)
**Summary:** Keke Chen, Qian Xu, Dan Wen, and Jiang Xue published a cross-sectional, person-centered analysis in BMC Psychiatry on October 21. The sample was 15,396 adult outpatients with sleep complaints at one psychiatric specialist hospital in China. Latent profile analysis used seven Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index dimensions. Three Symptom Checklist-90 items covered active suicidal ideation, death-related thoughts, and guilt. Logistic regression adjusted for age, sex, and the other Symptom Checklist-90 dimensions, with false-discovery-rate correction. After adjustment, the Mild Disturbance, Severe Insomnia, and Moderate Non-Medicated profiles had adjusted odds ratios of 1.22-1.25 for active suicidal ideation. Death-related thoughts showed suggested associations with the Medication Use but Functional and Moderate Non-Medicated profiles in exploratory adjusted analyses; those associations did not remain significant after false-discovery-rate correction, and the overall profile effect was marginal. Guilt was not associated after adjustment. The Moderate Non-Medicated profile had the highest symptom rates despite milder sleep impairment. Ethics approval was Hangzhou Seventh People's Hospital 2023-009. A single hospital and a cross-sectional design do not show that a sleep profile caused these thoughts.

**Links:**

- [BMC Psychiatry — Sleep profiles and suicide-related symptoms in 15,396 adult outpatients](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12888-026-08780-w)

**Commentary:** Adults already in psychiatric care for sleep complaints are a group in which clinicians can ask directly about active suicidal thoughts; the odds ratios only reach 1.22-1.25, and they describe one point in time.

### 10. Frontiers in Public Health: Among 1,529 early-primary children in Chongqing, each extra point of baseline sleep problems had an odds ratio of 1.144 for anxiety six months later (Sleep)
**Summary:** Jiao Chen, Yiling Liu, Qin Liu, and Fan Zhang published a short longitudinal observation in Frontiers in Public Health on October 20. The study sits in a child and adolescent growth cohort in one urban district of Chongqing. Three primary schools contributed 1,529 children in grades 1-3 who completed both surveys (782 boys and 747 girls), with a mean age of 8.00 ± 1.0 years. Baseline was May-June 2024 and follow-up was November-December 2024, with no intervention between them. Sleep used a five-item Pittsburgh index, anxiety used the GAD-7, and depression used the short Children's Depression Inventory. All three total scores were lower at follow-up than at baseline (P < 0.001). After adjustment for confounders and baseline mood, each extra point of baseline sleep score had an odds ratio of 1.144 for later anxiety (95% CI 1.071-1.222), 1.097 for depression (1.019-1.180), and 1.146 for anxiety and depression together (1.049-1.251). Each extra point of change in the sleep score had an odds ratio of 1.486 for the two occurring together (1.359-1.626). The scales are screening tools, and the sample is one urban district.

**Links:**

- [Frontiers in Public Health — Sleep and anxiety-depression symptoms in school-aged children](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/public-health/articles/10.3389/fpubh.2026.1927475/full)

**Commentary:** If sleep problems worsen over six months in the early primary years, anxiety and mood screens belong in the same look; these scales do not diagnose, and the children came from three schools in one Chongqing district.

## V. Evidence and Clarification

### 11. Journal of the International Society of Sports Nutrition: A narrative review finds limited evidence that prebiotics improve athletic performance, with clearer signals for gut and upper-respiratory symptoms (Evidence)
**Summary:** Sean A. Sage, S. J. Abbott, Renee Korczak, and Neil C. Williams published a narrative review in the Journal of the International Society of Sports Nutrition on October 19. It summarizes prebiotic intake in athletes, covering gastrointestinal structure and function, immune responses, recovery, sleep, wellness, mood, and exercise performance. Where athlete data are thin, findings from sedentary or clinical groups are discussed for possible relevance. The authors state that high-quality evidence remains limited. Several studies suggest prebiotics may improve gut-barrier integrity, reduce the incidence or severity of gastrointestinal symptoms, raise the salivary secretory immunoglobulin A secretion rate, and shorten or lessen upper-respiratory symptoms in trained people. Adaptation, recovery, sleep, mood, and cognition are listed as areas that still need testing in athletes. Existing studies often lack gut microbiome and metabolome measures and lack robust performance tests after a period of supplementation. This is not a meta-analysis, and the abstract gives no pooled effect size or recommended dose.

**Links:**

- [Journal of the International Society of Sports Nutrition — Prebiotics in sport and exercise](https://doi.org/10.1080/15502783.2026.2748763)

**Commentary:** People who train do not yet have enough randomized evidence to treat a prebiotic as a performance supplement; the clearer signals are gut symptoms and upper-respiratory symptoms, still without microbiome metabolites and measured performance.
