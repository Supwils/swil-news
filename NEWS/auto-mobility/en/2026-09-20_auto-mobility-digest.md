## I. Policy & Markets

### 1. California Governor Newsom signs six EV and charging bills to speed public chargers and ease apartment access (Policy / US)
**Summary:** On September 20 in Los Angeles, Governor Gavin Newsom signed SB 969, SB 1283, AB 1820, SB 1213, SB 1267, and SB 615 to make electric vehicles and charging infrastructure more affordable, accessible, and easier to build. SB 969 modernizes pre-activation inspection rules so manufacturer-tested public chargers can go online faster; SB 1283 clarifies that streamlined permitting also covers related work such as trenching, electrical upgrades, solar canopies, and battery storage; AB 1820 reduces permitting barriers and fees for charging at apartments and mixed-use housing. The other bills improve transparency for zero-emission truck incentives, help homeowners-association residents access charging, and give shoppers clearer battery information. The package builds on MyFirstEV, which offers eligible first-time ZEV buyers an instant rebate of up to $3,500 on a new vehicle or $1,750 on a used one, backed by $135.5 million in state funds matched dollar-for-dollar by automakers. California now has more than 216,445 public and shared charging plugs, including more than 20,000 DC fast-charging ports; in Q2 2026, Californians bought 86,857 new ZEVs, or 19.1% of new vehicle sales.

**Links:**

- [Office of Governor Gavin Newsom — Newsom signs legislation to accelerate California’s EV future](https://www.gov.ca.gov/2026/09/20/governor-gavin-newsom-signs-legislation-to-accelerate-californias-ev-future-expand-consumer-choice-and-strengthen-energy-independence/)

**Commentary:** With federal charging support shrinking, California is shifting the bottleneck from funding to whether apartments and HOAs can actually get plugs installed.

### 2. TEDA Forum: China NEV sales top 10.65 million in eight months at 52.4% penetration; policy focus turns to chips and solid-state batteries (Policy / China)

On the retail side, China Automobile Dealers Association vice president Wang Du said the national fleet has reached 371 million vehicles, while average new-car gross margins are about -21.4% and dealer satisfaction with OEMs about 56.3%.

## II. Automakers & Products

### 3. Nikkei: Toyota to start China EREV production in April 2027, targeting about 400,000 units in 2028 (Product strategy / China)
**Summary:** Nikkei Asia and CnEVPost reported on September 20 that Toyota plans to begin producing extended-range electric vehicles in China for the first time in spring 2027, broadening its powertrain mix in the world’s largest NEV market. Production is slated for April, starting at several hundred units a month, with about 200,000 units targeted for 2027 and around 400,000 for 2028. Toyota sold about 200,000 BEVs and 180,000 plug-in hybrids worldwide in 2025; an executive said intensifying BEV price competition in China makes it necessary to avoid being pulled into that fight. Meanwhile, CPCA data show China EREV retail sales at about 81,000 in August and about 616,000 in the first eight months, down roughly 15.85% year over year, with EREVs at about 8.06% of NEV retail in August. Nissan already offers the NX8 with EREV variants in China, and Xiaomi launched its Sky Nomad extended-range SUVs on September 7.

## III. Batteries & Charging

### 5. Sunwoda’s 15C LFP pack: about 10%–97% in nine minutes at room temperature, with unlimited ultrafast sessions under warranty (Battery)
**Summary:** Electrek reported on September 20 that Chinese battery maker Sunwoda presented 15C LFP fast-charging technology at a battery industry conference in Zaozhuang, Shandong. The company claims a charge from 10% to 70% in about five minutes and from 10% to 97% in about nine minutes at room temperature, adding roughly 100 km of range per minute. It also claims that at -20°C the pack can still move from 10% to 97% in about 15 minutes, adding about 700 km of range. Sunwoda says the cells can meet 10-year or 300,000 km warranty terms with no limit on ultrafast charging sessions. Customers include Geely, Dongfeng, and GAC. The company also showed an open charging ecosystem combining solar, storage, and ultrafast chargers.

### 7. New York City picks It’s Electric to deploy about 700 Level 2 curbside chargers (Charging / US)
**Summary:** The Cool Down reported on September 20, citing InsideEVs, that New York City selected Brooklyn startup It’s Electric for a citywide curbside charging expansion of about 700 Level 2 posts across all boroughs, with extra focus on neighborhoods dense with Uber, Lyft, and taxi drivers—supporting a goal of electrifying rideshare fleets by 2030. A prior pilot of 88 Flo curbside chargers saw EVs occupying spaces about 70% of the time, some sites at 99% occupancy, and power delivered during about 85% of occupied time. The new stainless-steel posts have no dangling cords, screens, or card readers and draw power from nearby apartment buildings to cut install cost and time; no more than two chargers per block, with units to be made in Queens. It’s Electric said total funding has reached about $15 million and that it has contracts in Boston, Los Angeles, San Francisco, and Philadelphia.

**Links:**

- [The Cool Down — New York is getting 700 curbside EV chargers](https://www.thecooldown.com/green-tech/curbside-ev-chargers-new-york-city/)

**Commentary:** In garage-scarce cities, curbside plugs matter more than highway megawatt chargers for whether rideshare can go electric.

## IV. Autonomous Driving & Mobility

### 9. NHTSA and SAE launch ASCEND for data-driven AV competency standards; Waymo and Zoox advance the same day (Autonomous driving / US)
**Summary:** In the September 20 TechCrunch Mobility column, NHTSA and SAE agreed to launch ASCEND, a government–industry effort to accelerate data-driven automated driving system (ADS) performance standards. Administrator Jonathan Morrison said at Pittsburgh’s AI Horizons Summit that ADS competency is the top priority and that a federal standard could reduce the patchwork of state and local rules. The column notes that current FMVSS were not written for vehicles such as Tesla’s Cybercab and Zoox robotaxis that lack steering wheels, pedals, and conventional mirrors. The same roundup says Waymo has restarted robotaxi service in San Antonio about five months after a flash-flood incident, launched service in Las Vegas, and plans Singapore mapping and testing, while Zoox’s 100-vehicle cap in Las Vegas expires September 25, clearing room to expand its commercial fleet.

**Links:**

- [TechCrunch — TechCrunch Mobility: How do we know when an AV is safe enough?](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/20/techcrunch-mobility-how-do-we-know-when-an-av-is-safe-enough/)

**Commentary:** Steering-wheel-free robotaxis are already taking paid fares; regulators are finally drafting a federal answer to “safe enough.”
