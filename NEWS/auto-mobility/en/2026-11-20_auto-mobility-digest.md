## I. Policy & China–US–Europe Markets

### 1. US lawmakers near a deal to ban Chinese connected cars, with a possible Mercedes carve-out (Policy)
**Summary:** According to Bloomberg reporting relayed by CnEVPost on November 20, bipartisan lawmakers in Washington are closing in on legislation that would ban Chinese automakers from selling vehicles with data-transmission capabilities in the United States, aiming to send a bill to the president by year-end. An earlier Senate panel version would have barred automakers more than 15% owned by Chinese entities from selling connected vehicles; Mercedes has lobbied because BAIC and Eric Li’s related stakes put it near that threshold. Talks include looser treatment of passive, non-voting ownership. Chinese brands are already largely shut out by tariffs and connected-vehicle hardware/software rules; the bill would turn that de facto barrier into statute.

**Links:**

- [CnEVPost — US lawmakers near deal to ban Chinese cars with Mercedes carve-out](https://cnevpost.com/2026/11/20/us-lawmakers-near-deal-ban-chinese-cars/)

**Commentary:** After tariffs and tech bans, a statutory sales ban would harden the US market’s closure to Chinese-linked automakers into lasting law.

### 2. EU governments back provisional safeguards on hybrid passenger-car imports, targeting a Dec. 1 start (Policy)
**Summary:** MLex reported on November 20 that EU governments backed provisional safeguard measures on imports of hybrid passenger vehicles, with the European Commission targeting a December 1 start and publication possibly as early as next week. Prior EU–China political arrangements pointed to a first-year quota of about 350,000 units, rising roughly 6% a year for four years. The Commission’s investigation notice said hybrid imports rose about 49% to roughly 1.42 million units from July 2025 to June 2026 versus 2023. Vehicles assembled inside the EU are generally expected to fall outside the import quota.

**Links:**

- [MLex — EU governments back provisional safeguard on hybrid vehicle imports](https://www.mlex.com/mlex/articles/2552919/eu-governments-back-provisional-safeguard-on-hybrid-vehicle-imports)
- [EUR-Lex — Notice of initiation of safeguard investigation on hybrid vehicles](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/C/2026/5780/oj)

**Commentary:** Member-state backing for provisional measures means the hybrid route Chinese brands used to skirt BEV tariffs is being formally narrowed.

### 3. CPCA sees November NEV retail at about 1.15 million, with penetration above 70% for the first time (Market)
**Summary:** On November 19 the China Passenger Car Association projected about 1.63 million narrow passenger-car retail sales in November, down 1.1% month on month, and about 1.15 million NEV retail sales for a 70.6% share—on track to top 70% for the first time. In the first half of the month, NEV retail reached 447,000 units, down 13% year on year but up 7% versus the same period in October, with penetration at 73.2%. The association said the record share owes more to faster ICE contraction than to expanding total demand; Double 11 promotions and trade-in support offered only a limited floor under a still-soft market.

## II. Autonomous Driving & Robotaxis

### 4. Waymo issues two software recalls across 3,871 robotaxis for highway construction and emergency direction (Autonomy)
**Summary:** The Verge reported on November 20 that Waymo is rolling out two software recalls across its fleet of about 3,871 robotaxis. The updates will stop vehicles from entering closed highway construction zones and improve how they respond to police and emergency crews directing traffic. The move follows California’s ban on Waymo freeway operations and the company’s subsequent nationwide pause of driverless highway running, after incidents involving construction breaches and ignored officer signals.

**Links:**

- [The Verge — Waymo issues recalls to fix its highway problems](https://www.theverge.com/transportation/1039153/waymo-issues-recalls-to-fix-its-highway-problems)

**Commentary:** Software recalls after scale-up show highway edge cases remain the most regulator-sensitive weak spot in robotaxi operations.

## IV. Batteries & Charging Networks

### 11. Ford begins shipping LFP cells from its Michigan plant for Fathom prototypes (Battery)
**Summary:** Ford said BlueOval Battery Park Michigan has started producing and shipping prismatic LFP cells to Louisville Assembly Plant for Fathom small electric pickup prototypes. The plant uses CATL-licensed technology and is designed for about 40 cells per minute, or roughly 20 GWh a year—below earlier plans of up to about 40 GWh. Pack production is planned for 2027 ahead of a targeted Fathom launch in the fourth quarter of 2027. The CATL link continues to draw US political scrutiny even as Ford argues licensing was the fastest path to low-cost domestic LFP.
