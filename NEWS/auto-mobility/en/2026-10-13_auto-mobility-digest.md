## I. Policy and markets

### 1. China's finance and tax authorities will cut the NEV VAT export rebate from 13% to 9% on April 1, 2027 (Policy / China)
**Summary:** Ministry of Finance and State Taxation Administration Announcement 2026 No. 29, issued on October 12, lowers the value-added-tax export rebate for new-energy vehicles from 13% to 9% starting April 1, 2027. The rate follows the export date on the customs declaration. Products subject to consumption tax keep the consumption-tax refund or exemption. 21st Century Business Herald reported on October 13, citing Red Star Capital Bureau, that the list covers 67 product categories, including cars, SUVs, and commercial vehicles. China Passenger Car Association secretary-general Cui Dongshu told Red Star that the long buffer makes this a structural adjustment, not a full withdrawal of export support. Four percentage points will raise export costs and press low-margin models, while export growth still rests on the supply chain rather than the rebate. A company source cited in the report said a vehicle with an offshore price of 150,000 yuan would lose 6,000 yuan of rebate. Analyst Liu Hao said the lithium-battery export rebate already fell from 9% to 6% on April 1, 2026, and is due to be cancelled on January 1, 2027.

**Links:**

- [State Taxation Administration policy library — Ministry of Finance and STA Announcement 2026 No. 29](https://fgk.chinatax.gov.cn/zcfgk/c102416/c5252906/content.html)
- [21st Century Business Herald — NEV VAT export rebate to be cut to 9%](https://www.21jingji.com/article/20261013/herald/fe6405a2039fa8cd7207e76af1cc96b8.html)

**Commentary:** The notice sets the rate after April 1, 2027; until then it remains 13%, and any rush to export during the window is a market inference, not a published quota.

## III. Autonomous driving

### 7. Baidu Apollo Go sets a public community trial in Hong Kong's Tung Chung for October 21 to November 3 (Autonomous driving / Hong Kong)
**Summary:** Dimsum Daily reported on October 13 that the Transport Department lodged a paper with the Islands District Council the same day supporting Baidu's Apollo Go in Hong Kong's first community public trial of autonomous cars in Tung Chung. Councillors get a preview on October 20. Rides for Islands District residents run from 2 p.m. to 6 p.m. each day from October 21 through November 3, 14 days in all. The loop starts at Yat Tung Estate Car Park No. 1, runs via Chung Yan Road, Yu Tung Road, and Yi Tung Road to the Sheraton Hong Kong Tung Chung Hotel, and returns to the same car park. It is about 5.1 kilometres and about 15 minutes, with no intermediate drop-offs. Six hundred tickets are available, each vehicle carries at most two passengers, and up to 1,200 people can be hosted. Councillors receive 540 of the tickets to share with residents. A backup operator stays on board. Riders must be at least eight years old or 1.35 metres tall. The report said members of the Autonomous Vehicle Applications Facilitation Working Group inspected the cars and rode them on October 10 and found the run orderly.

**Links:**

- [Dimsum Daily — Baidu opens free robotaxi trials in Tung Chung](https://www.dimsumdaily.hk/baidu-opens-free-tung-chung-robotaxi-trials/)

**Commentary:** This is a 14-day ticketed community loop with an operator in the car, not an open commercial driverless ride-hailing service.

## IV. Charging and vehicle-grid integration

### 9. Tesla extends Powershare home backup to the Model 3 and Model Y in the United States, without feeding the grid (Charging / United States)
**Summary:** electrive reported on October 13 that Tesla has extended Powershare Home Backup, previously limited to the Cybertruck, to the Model 3 and Model Y in the United States, so the traction battery can supply a home during an outage. For a household using 30 kWh a day, Tesla estimates that Premium and Performance versions can cover more than two days, and Standard versions up to about two days, depending on charge and use. Compatibility is guaranteed only for vehicles ordered in the United States and Puerto Rico on or after October 1, 2026; earlier cars depend on hardware. The setup needs a Powerwall 3 and a compatible Wall Connector, supplies up to 11.5 kW continuously, and must be activated through Tesla support. These two models cannot currently send power back to the public grid. Support for Powerwall 2 is planned, with no date. Tesla has not said whether other markets will get the feature.

**Links:**

**Commentary:** This is backup power for a home outage, and it requires a Powerwall 3; it is not an open vehicle-to-grid sales product.

### 10. Mercedes launches a bidirectional package for private customers in Germany, with the first 500 wallboxes at €1,990 (Charging / Germany)
**Summary:** electrive reported on October 13 that Mercedes-Benz and The Mobility House launched MB.CHARGE Home Pro Intelligent, letting customers send electricity from the vehicle battery back to the public grid. In Germany it starts with the electric GLC and the new C-Class, with later over-the-air extensions planned for the VLE, GLA, EQS, CLA, CLA Shooting Brake, and GLB. The ChargeLine Bidi wallbox is an 11 kW DC unit for 400-volt and 800-volt cars and uses ISO 15118-20. A smart meter is required. Through the end of March 2027, the first 500 customers can buy the wallbox for €1,990 instead of €2,990. Every hour the car is connected is paid, whether it is charging, discharging, or idle, and early customers also get a €250 charging credit. Mercedes says someone connected for an average of 12 hours a day can currently expect more than €500 a year. On that example, the introductory price pays back in about four years, and the regular price in about six.

Urban markets received 90% of the new stations; rural markets received 68 of the 664.

### 12. Seattle opens applications for up to 200 curbside Level 2 chargers by 2030 (Charging / United States)
**Summary:** The Seattle Department of Transportation wrote on its site on October 13 that it will work with Seattle City Light, using the voter-approved transportation levy, to install up to 200 public curbside Level 2 charging stations by 2030, starting with neighborhoods that have the fewest chargers and the greatest need. A 2023-2024 pilot installed chargers at 31 locations. Applications stay open through fall 2027, and installation runs on a rolling basis from fall 2027 through 2030. The department said about one in four newly registered vehicles in the greater Seattle area are EVs, and that Level 2 charging adds about 12 to 30 miles of range per hour. Chargers in Burien, SeaTac, Shoreline, and Tukwila, inside City Light's service area but outside Seattle, will use a separate transportation-electrification budget and are not part of the 200 levy-funded Seattle sites.

**Links:**
