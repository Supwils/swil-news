### 2. BYD lifts 2026 overseas sales outlook to 1.9–2.0 million and targets more than 2.5 million abroad in 2027（Market / China–Global）
**Summary:** CnEVPost reported on Sep 7, citing a Deutsche Bank note, that BYD management raised 2026 overseas sales guidance to about 1.9–2.0 million vehicles—above the January 1.3 million and March 1.5 million targets—and aims for more than 2.5 million overseas sales in 2027. Overseas deliveries reached about 1.162 million in the first eight months (+85.7% YoY), while domestic sales fell about 32.7% to roughly 1.506 million. Overseas profit per vehicle was about RMB 20,000 in H1; the company also reiterated flash-charging network targets of 20,000 stations in China by end-2026 and 6,000 planned overseas.

## II. Autonomous Driving & Robotaxi

### 4. NHTSA opens Audit Query AQ26002 into Tesla Cybercab self-certification, covering about 1,000 wheel-free vehicles（Autonomy / U.S.）
**Summary:** After Tesla began commercial driverless Cybercab rides in Austin without steering wheels or pedals, NHTSA opened Audit Query AQ26002 to examine the process and technical data behind Tesla’s claim of Federal Motor Vehicle Safety Standards compliance, covering an estimated ~1,000 vehicles. CNBC and Electrek report the probe will review how Tesla treated certain human-driver-centric standards as inapplicable; the audit itself does not establish a violation or order a halt. Texas records cited about 420 Tesla autonomous vehicles registered, including roughly 45 Cybercabs. Unlike Zoox, which obtained a federal exemption for a wheel-free design, Tesla did not petition for a similar exemption—making the certification path a central regulatory flashpoint.

**Links:**

- [CNBC — U.S. auto safety regulator opens probe into nearly 1,000 Tesla Cybercabs](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/04/us-auto-safety-regulator-opens-probe-into-nearly-1000-tesla-cybercabs.html)
- [Electrek — Tesla Cybercab is already under NHTSA investigation after launch](https://electrek.co/2026/09/04/tesla-cybercab-nhtsa-investigation-fmvss-certification/)

**Commentary:** Wheel-free commercial service collides with rules written for human drivers—the next robotaxi gate is certification narrative, not demo miles.

## III. Batteries & Charging

### 5. UK’s Osprey launches Osprey Go: Autocharge plug-and-charge plus dynamic pricing across its network（Charging / UK）
**Summary:** On Sep 7, Osprey Charging launched Osprey Go, rolling out Autocharge automatic authentication and dynamic pricing on its UK public network. After a one-time app setup, drivers can plug in and walk away; charging starts automatically at the best Osprey price for that site and time, without tapping a card or reopening the app. Osprey says it is now the largest UK public network offering Autocharge and is still evaluating Plug & Charge. Dynamic pricing is being extended across more sites this month to align customer rates with grid peak/off-peak conditions.

## IV. M&A, Localization & Two-Wheel Mobility

### 7. Geely’s 34% Ford España stake enters China’s antitrust review as Valencia JV moves ahead（M&A / China–Europe）
**Summary:** On Sep 7, China’s State Administration for Market Regulation published notice that Geely Holding has signed an agreement to acquire 34% of Ford España, S.L.; after closing, Ford would hold 66% and Geely 34%, with joint control of the Spanish entity whose key asset is the Almussafes plant near Valencia (design capacity about 500,000 vehicles a year). The July JV plan targets operations in H1 2027 and first new-model production in 2028, helping Geely shorten delivery times and mitigate EU countervailing duties on China-built EVs. Closing still requires approvals in China, the EU, Spain, and other jurisdictions.
