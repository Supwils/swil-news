## I. Markets & Policy

### 1. Tesla’s U.S. EV Share Rebounds to About 52% After Federal Credits Expire (Markets / U.S.)
**Summary:** The Wall Street Journal reported late on Sep 12—and multiple outlets echoed on Sep 13—that after the federal EV purchase credit ended on Sep 30, 2025, the overall U.S. EV market contracted while Tesla’s share recovered from its 2025 low. Motor Intelligence data put Tesla at roughly 52% of U.S. EV sales through the first eight months of 2026, up from about 43% in the same period of 2025; Tesla’s own U.S. volume fell about 16%, while the broader EV market fell about 30%. Cox Automotive estimates a somewhat higher share near 55%. Coverage attributes the shift to legacy automakers trimming EV production and lineups once subsidies disappeared, moving the market from broad expansion to consolidation among fewer players.

**Links:**

- [WSJ — Tesla Is Reclaiming the U.S. EV Market as Legacy Automakers Retreat](https://www.wsj.com/business/autos/tesla-is-reclaiming-the-u-s-ev-market-as-legacy-automakers-retreat-71ec2053)
- [Basenor — Tesla's US EV Market Share: 5 Numbers That Tell the Story](https://www.basenor.com/blogs/news/teslas-us-ev-market-share-5-numbers-that-tell-the-story)

**Commentary:** A bigger slice of a smaller pie is not demand recovery—it suggests the credit often subsidized rivals’ price gaps more than it durably lifted mass EV adoption.

**Summary:** Citing CAAM, MIIT reported that in August 2026 China produced 1.653 million NEVs and sold 1.643 million, up 18.9% and 17.8% year over year, accounting for 60.6% of new-vehicle sales; January–August NEV output and sales reached about 10.668 million and 10.650 million, or roughly 52.4% of the market. ### 6. Spain Grants WeRide, Uber, and AVOMO the Country’s First National L4 Passenger-Vehicle Permit (Policy / Europe)
**Summary:** WeRide, Uber, and Moove Cars’ AVOMO said on Sep 10 that Spain’s DGT issued the first national Level 4 autonomous passenger-vehicle permit under the ES-AV framework for mapping, route validation, and readiness testing in Greater Madrid. The initial phase covers about 20 GXR vehicles with an in-car specialist on board—not unrestricted paid driverless service. Reuters and others confirmed a commercial target by end-2026, subject to further operational and regulatory milestones. Companies call it the first national EU approval for WeRide’s GXR and WeRide’s ninth autonomy-related permit worldwide.

**Links:**

### 8. Vinci Consortium Commits About €90M to a European Megawatt Truck-Charging Corridor (Charging / Europe)
**Summary:** Industry reporting on Sep 12 said Vinci Concessions, Epopee Gestion, and Ademe Investissement are investing about €90 million in a first wave of roughly 20 high-power heavy-truck chargers, developed and operated by Vinci’s Voltix brand along major French and broader European freight corridors.
