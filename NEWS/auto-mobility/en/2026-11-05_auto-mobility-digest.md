## I. Policy & Markets

### 1. MIIT seeks comment on EV battery “digital ID” rules tying a 24-digit code to the full life cycle (Battery Policy / China)
**Summary:** On Nov 5, China’s Ministry of Industry and Information Technology opened public consultation on the draft Interim Provisions on Digital Identity Management for New Energy Vehicle Traction Batteries, with feedback due by Dec 6. The roughly 20-article draft defines a digital ID indexed by the national-standard 24-digit battery code, linking design, production, sales, vehicle fitment, repair, dismantling, recycling, and reuse. Automakers must disclose key battery details—code, maker, warranty period, cathode chemistry, and more—to owners via the vehicle system or app, and work with battery makers to track end-of-life utilization; MIIT will build the data system and share information with NDRC, ecology, and transport authorities.

**Commentary:** Recycling policy is moving from principles to enforceable codes and accountability—before the retirement wave peaks, regulators are putting traceability in owners’ and enforcers’ hands.

### 2. MIIT Batch 413 vehicle catalog: only 15 firms file 23 NEV passenger models as rules curb “rush-to-market” cars (Regulation / China)
**Summary:** MIIT published proposed Batch 413 of the Road Motor Vehicle Manufacturers and Products Announcement: 475 new auto products, down 212 from Batch 412, with about 58 fewer NEV models. Only 15 companies filed 23 NEV passenger-car variants; truly new models were scarce, startups were largely absent, and joint-venture brands such as Roewe, Lincoln, and Buick were comparatively active. In a Nov 5 National Business Daily interview, CPCA’s Cui Dongshu said the slower filing pace aims to tighten R&D validation and favor higher-quality growth, against a backdrop of four-ministry draft rules requiring “two winters and one summer” environmental tests and at least 30,000 km of reliability mileage to deter “rush-to-market” cars that cut real-vehicle validation. Cui also warned short-term supply shrinkage may slow launches, with limited impact if later batches rebound.

### 4. More than a dozen MEPs urge an EU security review of BYD connected cars, floating procurement and site curbs (Policy / Europe)
**Summary:** Reuters and follow-on reports said 14 members of the European Parliament on Nov 4 asked the European Commission in writing to assess whether BYD vehicles pose a security threat and asked the EU cybersecurity agency to weigh listing BYD as a high-risk supplier. Lawmakers also queried curbs on Chinese connected cars near strategic dual-use infrastructure and exclusion from public procurement. French MEP Francois Kalfon cited China’s 2017 national intelligence law and the U.S. military’s ban on BYD vehicles at Kadena Air Base on Okinawa. The Commission typically has about six weeks to reply.

### 13. Tokai Kogyo acquires Kinugawa Rubber: cross-keiretsu seals consolidation as Japan’s supplier walls loosen (Supply Chain / Japan)
**Summary:** Just Auto reported on Nov 5 that Toyota-affiliated Tokai Kogyo Holdings completed on Nov 2 the purchase of 100% of voting shares in Nissan-affiliated Kinugawa Rubber Industrial Holdings from domestic financial investors including the Development Bank of Japan; the price was undisclosed.
