### 6. Germany's network regulator approves storage and bidirectional-charging rules, with full implementation due by September 2027 (Charging / Europe)
**Summary:** pv magazine reported on October 2 that Germany's Federal Network Agency approved MiSpeL, rules for integrating electricity storage and charging points into the market. Storage can now take both supported renewable electricity and grid electricity without losing support eligibility for the renewable power stored in the battery. Electricity drawn from the grid and later fed back can be balanced, and lower levies and network charges apply to those volumes. Bidirectional charging points are generally treated like storage systems. Households with photovoltaic systems of up to 30 kW can use a flat-rate option that generally needs one meter, but that option still requires European Commission approval under state-aid rules. Network and metering operators have until the end of September 2027 to implement the rules, and they can apply earlier if the operators agree.

**Links:**

- [pv magazine — Germany approves rules for storage, bidirectional charging](https://www.pv-magazine.com/2026/10/02/germany-approves-rules-for-storage-bidirectional-charging/)

**Commentary:** Vehicle batteries that send power back to the grid now have a settlement path aligned with storage, while the simpler household option still waits on an EU state-aid review.

### 8. California's new law requires local robotaxi technicians and allows fines if a vehicle blocks responders for more than 30 minutes (Regulation / United States)
**Summary:** TechCrunch reported on the evening of October 1 that California Governor Gavin Newsom signed Senate Bill 1246. The law requires autonomous-vehicle companies to tell local jurisdictions the location and status of vehicles during system-wide failures and to provide local incident technicians who can assist with crashes and obstructions. Companies can be fined if a robotaxi blocks first responders during an emergency for more than 30 minutes. Remote drivers must be based in the United States and hold a U.S. driver's license. The law takes effect in July 2028, and the California Department of Motor Vehicles will set guidelines, including required response times. Waymo said it was grateful for amendments that keep it feasible for operators to serve Californians.

**Links:**

- [TechCrunch — Robotaxi operators will face fines for blocking first responders](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/01/robotaxi-operators-will-face-fines-for-blocking-first-responders/)

**Commentary:** The fines and local technicians do not take effect until July 2028, and direct remote driving is limited to licensed people based in the United States.

### 11. Rivian recalls about 14 R2s after under-torqued high-voltage battery fasteners that can cause a loss of power (Recall / United States)
**Summary:** TechCrunch reported on October 2 that Rivian issued a recall after fasteners on the R2 high-voltage battery pack might not be properly tightened, which can cause a loss of power while driving. The company said only about 14 vehicles may be affected and that it will repair or replace damaged parts at no cost. Rivian told TechCrunch that all of the affected vehicles are at service centers being inspected. A National Highway Traffic Safety Administration filing said Rivian found the defect on September 17, when a manufacturing operator at the Normal plant noticed an automated station had under-torqued a fastener; the station was then reprogrammed to reject improperly torqued fasteners. Rivian told the regulator it is not aware of any related customer reports, crashes, injuries, or deaths. This is the second R2 recall; one last month concerned backup-camera visibility and was addressed with a software update.

**Links:**

## IV. Supply Chain

### 12. Floods halt Toyota and Honda assembly in Thailand, with preliminary auto-supply losses of at least 1.018 billion baht (Supply chain / Southeast Asia)
**Summary:** The Nation reported on October 2 that Toyota and Honda suspended vehicle assembly in Thailand after flooding stopped parts deliveries. Koji Iwanami, president of Honda Automobile (Thailand), said the Prachin Buri and Ayutthaya plants would stop from October 2 through October 6 because suppliers in Chonburi and Rayong could not deliver, with normal operations expected to resume on October 7 and overtime planned afterward to recover output. Toyota Motor Thailand suspended its Samrong plant in Samut Prakan, the Ban Pho and Gateway plants in Chachoengsao, and a plant run by Toyota Auto Works. More than 320 millimeters of rain fell in three days, just-in-time deliveries stopped, and Toyota gave no restart date. Pimjai Leeissaranukul, chair of the Federation of Thai Industries, said flooding from September 24 to 30 affected Bangkok and 14 other provinces, and that preliminary losses to the auto supply chain and related industries were at least 1.018 billion baht.

### 13. Suzuki asks Indian suppliers to leave one day a week for maintenance, aiming for a six-day pattern by September 2027 (Supply chain / India)
**Summary:** The Hindu BusinessLine, citing Reuters on October 2, reported that two people said Suzuki Motor has asked suppliers in India to halt production one day a week for machine maintenance. Maruti Suzuki is preparing to lift annual production to 4 million cars by 2030 from about 2.4 million. In August, Suzuki Motor president Toshihiro Suzuki asked suppliers to plan capacity on a six-day schedule, and Maruti has since asked them to sign declarations by year-end confirming that lines supplying the automaker do not run all seven days. The people said Suzuki wants a pattern of 20 hours a day, six days a week, by September 2027, with four hours of downtime each night and a full day for maintenance.
