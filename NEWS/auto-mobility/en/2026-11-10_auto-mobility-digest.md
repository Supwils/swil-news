## I. Markets & Policy

### 1. China’s October NEV exports hit a record ~582,000 units, up ~145% year over year (Market / China)
**Summary:** The South China Morning Post reported on Nov 10 that China Passenger Car Association data show Chinese brands sold about 582,000 pure-electric and plug-in hybrid vehicles outside mainland China in October, up about 144.7% year over year. It was a second straight monthly record and surpassed September’s prior high of about 558,000 units. The tally includes vehicles exported from China and those assembled at Chinese brands’ overseas plants. The report partly attributes stronger overseas demand to a Middle East energy shock that pushed up petrol prices and accelerated the shift toward electrification in more markets.

### 2. California reopens at least ~$55 million in funding for electric school buses and charging (Policy / US)
**Summary:** electrive reported on Nov 10 that California has reopened its Zero-Emission School Bus and Infrastructure (ZESBI) incentive program with at least about $55 million in state funding, jointly financed by the California Air Resources Board and the California Energy Commission and administered by Catalyst Mobility (formerly CALSTART). Vehicle and charger incentives can be requested in a single first-come, first-served application. Pre-applications opened Nov 3; formal applications begin at 10:00 a.m. Pacific on Nov 17, and applicants must request at least one charger for every electric school bus. Successful awards can add up to about $40,000 per bus to offset higher costs after the federal Commercial Clean Vehicle Credit (45W) ended and tariffs rose; up to about 10% of each grant can cover training, repairs, yard upgrades, and related costs.

**Links:**

- [electrive — California reopens $55m funding programme for electric school buses](https://www.electrive.com/2026/11/10/california-reopens-55m-funding-programme-for-electric-school-buses/)

**Commentary:** As federal incentives fade, state-level “bus + charger” packages are what keep school-bus electrification moving.

## II. Autonomous Driving & Mobility Regulation

### 3. D.C. robotaxi bill gets a major rewrite: mileage tax halved, union rights pledged—still may stall in committee (Policy / US)
**Summary:** Axios, WJLA, and others reported on Nov 10 that Transportation Committee Chair Charles Allen unveiled a revised bill the same day. It would cap commercial driverless fleets at about 200 vehicles per operator and about 800 citywide at once; cut the mileage tax to about 7.5 cents per mile (roughly half the original proposal); and slash the commercial-operations permit fee from about $6 million to about $15,000 plus about $750 per vehicle. The rewrite would also grant collective-bargaining rights to rideshare drivers and AV maintenance workers, and require equitable service across all eight wards, wheelchair-accessible fleet timelines, and crash reporting. Mayor-elect Janeese Lewis George and some committee members want to wait until next year, leaving the votes to clear committee uncertain.

**Links:**

- [Axios — D.C.'s robotaxi bill gets a major rewrite. It may not be enough.](https://www.axios.com/local/washington-dc/2026/11/10/waymo-robotaxi-dc-bill)

## IV. Batteries, Swapping & M&A

### 10. CATL: expanding swap stations to accelerate sodium-ion adoption; >3,000 passenger-car sites targeted by year-end (Battery / China)
**Summary:** CnEVPost reported on Nov 10, citing Bloomberg, that CATL chairman Robin Zeng told a Benchmark Mineral Intelligence event in Shanghai that sodium-ion batteries offer enough range for daily urban travel, while more swap stations can ease weekend long-trip range anxiety. CATL said its swap network already covers about 200 Chinese cities and aims for more than 3,000 passenger-car stations by year-end, building stations itself and leasing batteries to consumers. Sodium-ion is framed as a hedge against lithium-price swings; CATL previously expected about 10,000–20,000 vehicles to use its sodium-ion cells this year. Subsidiary CAES plans to deploy Naxtra sodium-ion packs at scale across the Choco-Swap network starting in 2026.

**Links:**

- [CnEVPost — CATL says expanding swap network will help accelerate sodium-ion battery adoption](https://cnevpost.com/2026/11/10/catl-swap-network-help-sodium-ion-adoption/)

**Commentary:** Sodium-ion’s breakthrough depends less on chemistry alone than on a “short range + swappable” product definition.
