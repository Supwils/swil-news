## I. Policy & Markets

### 1. South Korea plans battery leasing that could halve Ioniq 5 upfront cost (Policy)
**Summary:** The Korea Economic Daily and KED Global reported on November 25 that a forthcoming “EV industry competitiveness” package due November 27 will push battery leasing that splits ownership of the vehicle body and the pack, requiring amendments to the Motor Vehicle Management Act, which today treats the battery as an inseparable vehicle part. Hyundai Motor Group would lead the consumer trial: Hyundai sells the body while Hyundai Capital owns the battery and charges a monthly fee. For an Ioniq 5 Long Range Modern listed around 52.9 million won, stripping the pack and applying subsidies could put the buyer’s outlay near 25 million won. Batteries are said to make up about 40% of EV cost; Chinese brands’ Korean share rose from about 1.1% in 2021 to about 33.9% last year. Monthly fees are still unsettled, with discussion around 300,000–400,000 won, near midsize ICE fuel bills; insurance, subsidies, and purchase-tax rules would be rewritten in parallel.

**Links:**

- [KED Global — Korea plans half-price EVs by separating battery ownership](https://www.kedglobal.com/automobiles/newsView/ked202611250010)
- [Hankyung — Half-price Ioniq 5 via battery lease](https://www.hankyung.com/article/2026112502651)

**Commentary:** Splitting ownership is Korea’s answer to Chinese price pressure—success hinges on whether the monthly fee and residual rules beat simply buying the pack.

### 2. CPCA: Nov 1–22 China NEV passenger retail 702,000 units, down 13% YoY (Market)
**Summary:** Yicai and Guandian, citing the China Passenger Car Association on November 25, said nationwide NEV passenger retail reached 702,000 units from November 1–22, down 13% year on year but up 7% versus the same period last month; year-to-date retail was about 9.57 million, also down 13%. Maker wholesale hit 965,000 NEVs in the same window, up 3% YoY and 13% versus last month, with YTD wholesale about 14.188 million (+9%). Overall passenger retail was about 984,000 (−25% YoY); NEV retail penetration was about 71.3% and wholesale penetration about 76.6%. Separate coverage noted that after a crackdown on “rushed-to-market” cars, NEV purchase-tax exemption listings slowed sharply, with only about 55 new passenger models added in November.

### 3. EU delays Tesla Assisted Driving review: TCMV meeting moved to Dec. 15 (Policy)
**Summary:** CNBC and Electrek reported on November 25 that a European Commission spokesperson told Reuters the Technical Committee on Motor Vehicles meeting that could consider EU-wide recognition of Tesla Assisted Driving (the Europe name for Full Self-Driving) has slipped from December 1 to December 15; two earlier meetings ended without a vote. The system is a hands-off, eyes-on supervised driver assist. After the Dutch RDW’s provisional type approval in April, roughly a dozen countries have followed, including Italy, the most populous so far, but bloc-wide approval needs at least 15 of 27 member states representing 65% of the EU population. Reports warn an EU-level rejection could void national approvals already granted; France and Sweden remain cautious on speed-margin and safety issues.

**Links:**

- [CNBC — EU postpones discussion to approve Tesla's self-driving tech](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/25/eu-postpones-discussion-to-approve-teslas-self-driving-tech.html)
- [Electrek — EU pushes Tesla FSD vote meeting to December 15](https://electrek.co/2026/11/25/eu-delays-tesla-fsd-vote-december-15-tcmv/)

**Commentary:** A calendar slip is not a rejection, but repeated no-votes show EU ADAS mutual recognition is stuck on politics, not software cadence.

## II. Autonomous Driving & Robotaxi

### 4. Motional aims for early-December driverless Uber rides in Las Vegas (Autonomy)
**Summary:** Axios reported on November 25 that Hyundai-majority Motional, after a two-year tech overhaul, plans to launch fully driverless rides via Uber in Las Vegas in early December. The company shelved a 2023 scale-up after concluding its stack was too costly to deploy profitably, and has shifted to an AI-first approach including large driving models. Its Ioniq 5 robotaxis leave Hyundai’s line with autonomy hardware already integrated and share data with Hyundai’s 42dot ADAS unit. The initial fleet will stay small while Motional waits for a next-gen platform with cheaper sensors and compute. Las Vegas already hosts Zoox and Waymo; Motional and Zoox could become the first two driverless providers available through Uber in the same U.S. city.

**Links:**

- [Axios — Motional retools for the robotaxi race](https://www.axios.com/2026/11/25/motional-robotaxi-uber-hyundai)

**Commentary:** Safe demos are table stakes—the robotaxi race is now about factory-grade cost curves and who owns the passenger marketplace.

## III. Batteries, Charging & Energy Networks

### 6. NIO Power: 10,000+ swap stations and ~RMB 350B of batteries by 2030 (Battery)
**Summary:** Electrek reported on November 25 that at NIO IN 2026 in Guangzhou, NIO said its energy arm NIO Power aims to operate more than 10,000 battery-swap stations by 2030, manage over RMB 350 billion (about $52 billion) worth of battery packs, and deliver more than 10 billion kWh a year via charging and swapping. As of November 11 NIO had about 4,252 swap stations and 5,584 charging stations in China; reaching 10,000 implies roughly 1,400 new swap sites a year for four years—about double recent pace. The company has co-built more than 900 stations with partners to lighten its balance sheet, and fifth-generation stations serve all NIO brands including firefly.

### 7. BYD starts Malaysia Flash Charging rollout: 12 points by year-end, 130 by end-2027 (Charging)
**Summary:** electrive reported on November 25 that BYD Malaysia will deploy Flash Chargers in two phases: twelve points at Denza showrooms and strategic sites by year-end (three already live in Balakong, Glenmarie, and Old Klang Road), then urban hubs, fuel stations, and highways toward about 130 points by end-2027. Each T-shaped unit has two suspended cables rated up to 1,500 kW; a Denza D9 demo charged from 10% to 97% in about 9 minutes 23 seconds. From December 9 users will need the BYD Flash Charging app, with broader access for all Malaysian EV owners planned later that month. Malaysia is the third ASEAN market after Indonesia and Cambodia to start Flash Charging.

### 10. New Jersey NEVI wins “Fully Built Out” status for 19 corridor DCFC sites (Charging)
**Summary:** The New Jersey Department of Transportation announced on November 25 that FHWA has granted “Fully Built Out” status to the state’s NEVI formula program, advancing a statewide contract for 19 DC fast-charging stations along Alternative Fuel Corridors including the Turnpike, Garden State Parkway, and Atlantic City Expressway. The build covers 152 public 150 kW ports, each with both CCS and J3400 (NACS) connectors, spaced no more than about 50 miles apart and within about one mile of the highway. The designation gives New Jersey more flexibility in how remaining NEVI funds are used.

### 12. French industry minister: BYD Hambach production could start late 2027–2028 (Localization)
