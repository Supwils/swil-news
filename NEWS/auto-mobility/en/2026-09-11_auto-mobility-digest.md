## I. Policy & Markets

### 1. Nine ministries issue China's 15th Five-Year Plan for intelligent NEVs: ~70% passenger NEV share by 2030 (Policy / China)
**Summary:** MIIT and eight other ministries jointly issued the Smart Connected New Energy Vehicle Industry Development 15th Five-Year Plan (document dated September 9, published September 10), with CCTV Finance, National Business Daily and others providing detailed coverage on September 11. 2030 headline goals include consolidating full-chain strengths and joining the ranks of global auto powers; raising NEV shares of new passenger- and commercial-vehicle sales to about 70% and 40%, respectively; and achieving scaled deployment of vehicles with automated-driving capability. Sub-targets include average passenger-car fuel use near 3.3 L/100 km, average BEV energy use near 11.5 kWh/100 km, and high-level automation on expressways, urban freeways and some city roads. The plan newly embeds capacity early-warning controls, tightens conditions for new standalone NEV OEM projects, and pushes M&A plus orderly exit of inefficient capacity.

- [CCTV Finance — Automated-driving-capable vehicles to scale by 2030](https://finance.cctv.com/2026/09/11/ARTIttsXqrUP53bFpN1v1Dmm260911.shtml)

**Commentary:** Policy messaging shifts from scale chasing to capacity discipline, safety floors and smarter automation—the next scoreboard is energy use, market access and brand reputation abroad, not monthly volume alone.

### 2. Europe sells ~2.79 million new electrified cars in Jan–Jul, up ~33% year over year (Market / Europe)
**Summary:** JD Power / EV Volumes reported on September 10 that Europe sold about 2.789 million new electrified passenger cars (BEV + PHEV) in January–July 2026, up roughly 33.2% year over year. The big five markets—Germany, the UK, France, Italy and Spain—together took about 66.3% of European electrified sales, with Germany ~23.0%, the UK ~18.0% and France ~12.5%. Separately, ICCT's July 2026 monitor put BEV share of new European cars at about 22%, up roughly five percentage points from the same period in 2025. Italy posted one of the fastest big-five growth rates, about 77.1% year over year.

**Links:**

- [JD Power — Surging European EV Markets Present Opportunities](https://www.jdpower.com/business/news/surging-european-ev-markets-present-opportunities/)
- [ICCT — European Car Market Monitor: July 2026](https://theicct.org/publication/european-car-market-monitor-july-2026/)

**Commentary:** Europe's electrification is still accelerating, but growth remains concentrated in incentive- and infrastructure-sensitive big markets—regional divergence is the real story.

## II. Electric Commercial Vehicles & Major OEMs

### 3. Tesla publishes European Semi specs: ~550 km loaded range, ~800 kW peak charging (Product / Europe)
**Summary:** Electrek and others reported on September 11 that Tesla Europe, Middle East & Africa unveiled European Semi specifications and a regional website, with customer deliveries targeted for 2027—days before IAA Transportation in Hannover (September 15–20). Quoted figures include up to ~550 km range at 40-tonne GCW, ~1 kWh/km consumption, ~9,100 kg curb weight, Megacharger peaks up to ~800 kW recovering ~60% of range in about 30 minutes, and ePTO up to ~25 kW. European pricing and on-sale timing remain unannounced. Coverage notes U.S. demos have shown ~1.2 MW charging, while Europe's 800 kW figure better matches MCS corridor rollout and local heavy-duty charging standards.

**Links:**

- [Electrek — Tesla reveals European Semi specs: 550 km range, 800 kW charging](https://electrek.co/2026/09/11/tesla-european-semi-specs-550-km-800-kw/)

## III. Autonomous Driving & Mobility Platforms

### 4. Waymo becomes available on both Waymo and Lyft apps in Nashville for the first time (Mobility / U.S.)
**Summary:** Lyft announced on September 9 that Nashville is the first market where riders can be matched with fully autonomous Waymo vehicles on both the Waymo and Lyft apps. Eligible Standard, Priority Pickup, Wait & Save and Extra Comfort trips that start and end inside the Waymo-on-Lyft service area can be matched to the shared fleet at no extra cost. Availability will ramp over coming weeks as both apps dynamically dispatch from one fleet; Lyft highlights Flexdrive's role in charging, cleaning, maintenance and depot operations. The dual-app model contrasts with exclusive Waymo–Uber setups elsewhere and shows Waymo pursuing owned demand plus multi-platform distribution in parallel.

**Links:**

- [Lyft Investor Relations — Waymo Rides Now Available on the Lyft App in Nashville](https://investor.lyft.com/news-events-presentations/press-releases/detail/204/waymo-rides-now-available-on-the-lyft-app-in-nashville)
- [Axios — Why Lyft could be Waymo's ideal ride-hailing partner](https://www.axios.com/2026/09/09/lyft-waymo-partner-uber-nashville)

**Commentary:** The robotaxi fight is shifting to who owns the rider front door versus who runs fleet ops—Lyft is betting on being indispensable backstage rather than competing for brand primacy.

## IV. Batteries & Charging Networks

### 5. Geely cites ~1,800 kW ultra-fast charging tests in China; Europe remains "homes first" (Charging / China–Europe)
**Summary:** newmobility.news reported on September 11 that Geely, via its energy arm (including Zeekr Power-related capabilities), is testing ~1,800 kW high-power stations in China and claims ~60% pack recovery in about five minutes—above the ~1,500 kW "Flash Charging" peak often associated with BYD's network, which coverage says has already topped 10,000 sites and is racing toward ~20,000 by year-end. Geely's Europe lead told Autocar the continent needs a more pragmatic sequence: maximize home-charger access first, then introduce ultra-fast-capable cars and ultra-high-kW hardware. Planning permission and grid constraints still bound European timelines.

**Links:**

- [newmobility.news — Geely plans to chase BYD in Europe with 1,800 kW megawatt chargers](https://newmobility.news/en/2026/09/11/geely-plans-to-chase-byd-in-europe-with-1-800-kw-megawatt-chargers/)

**Commentary:** China is turning peak kilowatts into an arms race; Europe is still gated by permits and grids—"who hits 1 MW+ first" is not the same contest as "who scales usable charging first."
