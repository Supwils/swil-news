### 1. Leapmotor launches the A10s at 92,800 yuan, with LiDAR and 505 km of CLTC range (New model / China)
**Summary:** CnEVPost reported on October 18 that Leapmotor launched the A10s in China on Sunday at 92,800 yuan (about $13,810, at 6.7220 yuan to the dollar), 6,000 yuan above the previous top 505 LiDAR trim. Listed as the A10 505S, it uses a 90 kW front motor and a 53 kWh lithium-iron-phosphate battery for 505 km of CLTC range, matching that existing LiDAR variant. It has LiDAR, a Qualcomm SA8295 cockpit chip, and an SA8650 driver-assistance chip, with urban, highway, and parking-space-to-parking-space navigation; charging from 30% to 80% takes about 16 minutes. Leapmotor delivered 105,656 vehicles globally in September and 666,539 in the first nine months; meeting its previously announced target of 1 million vehicles would require about 111,154 a month in the fourth quarter.

### 3. Mercedes-AMG GT SUV: up to 1,169 hp in Europe, and DC charging up to 710 kW (New model / Germany)
**Summary:** Mercedes-Benz Media released the new AMG GT SUV in Affalterbach on October 17, the first full-size SUV developed entirely in-house in AMG's 59-year history and the second model on the AMG.EA architecture. The GT 63 SUV uses three axial-flux motors for up to 860 kW (1,169 hp); the GT 55 SUV reaches up to 600 kW (816 hp); the entry GT 53 SUV uses two synchronous motors for a combined 400 kW (544 hp). Continuous output is up to 530 kW. Usable battery capacity is 106 or 128 kWh on an 800-volt system. The 128 kWh pack charges at up to 710 kW DC, can add up to 450 km of WLTP range in 10 minutes, takes up to 12 minutes from 10% to 80%, and supports range of up to about 700 km. The top variant does 0-100 km/h in 2.5 seconds and, with the Driver's Package, reaches 270 km/h. The GT 53 SUV can be ordered from October 20 at 113,050 euros; the GT 63 and GT 55 follow in spring 2027. The U.S. press note lists the GT 63 peak at 1,153 hp and says the 2028 model reaches U.S. dealers in summer 2027.

### 6. Four ministries meet more than 30 automakers and demand tighter testing and advertising (Policy / China)
**Summary:** Xinhua Daily Telegraph on October 18 carried a Xinhua dispatch dated October 16: reporters learned from the Ministry of Industry and Information Technology that four ministries held a meeting with major automakers, telling them to resist irrational competition and to strengthen management of innovative design and development testing. On October 15, MIIT's Equipment Industry Department I, the Ministry of Public Security's Traffic Management Bureau, the Ministry of Ecology and Environment's Department of Atmospheric Environment, and the State Administration for Market Regulation's Quality Development Bureau met more than 30 key producers. They walked through the draft notice on innovative vehicle design and development testing and briefed companies on problems found in earlier inspections. Companies were told to state the limits and test status of innovative designs truthfully, to avoid false, exaggerated, or misleading claims, to stop using scenes that violate traffic law in advertising, and to stop encouraging drivers to ignore safe-driving duties. The ministries said they will issue the notice, raise the safety baseline, keep inspecting key producers, and publicly expose companies found in violation.

## III. Autonomous Driving and Public Money

### 8. The Verge: U.S. cities use street rights, airports, and emergency powers to constrain robotaxis (Autonomous driving / United States)
**Summary:** The Verge wrote on October 18 that federal autonomous-vehicle legislation has stalled for years, so cities and states are writing their own rules. Minneapolis is considering an ordinance that would require a human monitor in an autonomous vehicle, which would effectively ban driverless operation. The San Diego City Council passed a resolution seeking to keep Waymo out of the airport and to give local agencies direct control of autonomous vehicles in emergencies. Austin firefighters are concerned that the Tesla Cybercab has no steering wheel or pedals and would be hard to move, and they want dedicated towing protocols and direct human communication. Kentucky, Oklahoma, South Dakota, and Iowa are also considering tighter rules. California bills would give cities authority over fleet size, operating hours, or local permit bans, and would require companies to give police and fire departments a physical override or an emergency remote shutoff. Culver City explored a pause on building permits for robotaxi fleet facilities, and Santa Monica used noise and traffic rules to restrict 24-hour charging hubs.

**Links:**

- [The Verge — Cities want to put the boot on robotaxis](https://www.theverge.com/transportation/1013220/robotaxi-self-driving-car-laws)

**Commentary:** The survey is about local authority: Minneapolis is still discussing a required safety monitor, while San Diego and Austin are focused on airport access and how a car with no steering wheel gets towed.

### 9. Los Angeles Times: California's clean truck and bus voucher program has spent over $1 billion and faces fraud and idle-fleet questions (Policy / United States)
**Summary:** The Los Angeles Times reported on October 18 on California's Clean Truck and Bus Voucher Incentive Project. More than $1 billion has been spent, subsidies can cover up to 90% of a vehicle's price, and officials say the program has helped businesses and transit agencies deploy nearly 12,000 trucks, buses, and vans. State authorities are investigating an EV manufacturer after a civil ruling found that nearly $1 million in public funds went to vehicles that did not exist. Court filings from the attorney general's office are also examining whether vehicle identification numbers were altered so one vehicle could draw multiple vouchers. One in four participating automakers has ceased production or left the zero-emission market. The Times obtained mileage records for roughly 2,300 program vehicles, and nearly half were driven less than the state's conservative benchmarks. At least 5,340 vehicles, or 45%, still burn fossil fuels because they are hybrids or run on natural gas. Porterville has bought nearly 30 electric buses and vans since 2018, and only three buses still run. L.A. Metro used $15.7 million for 93 battery buses that are driven 36% less than its fossil-fuel buses. LADOT's 155 electric vehicles, bought with $20.2 million in vouchers, cover half the mileage of the fossil fleet; one $630,000 BYD bus added in June 2024 has logged 555 miles. A CARB proposal to inspect eligible models in Riverside has not been implemented.

## IV. Battery Recycling and Technology Supply

### 10. Battery recycling is short of feedstock: hydrometallurgy capacity exceeds 4 million tonnes, against about 500,000 tonnes retired in 2025 (Battery / China)
**Summary:** CNR on October 18 republished China Business Journal. At a workshop on battery recycling and overseas compliance, Wang Dechuang, chair of the scrap-battery branch of the China National Resources Recycling Association, said the branch counts about 500,000 tonnes of power batteries retired in China in 2025, while hydrometallurgy plants already have more than 4 million tonnes of lithium-battery recycling capacity. He expects feedstock shortages and excess capacity to persist for two to three years. MIIT's public figure is different: comprehensive utilization of retired new-energy-vehicle power batteries exceeded 400,000 tonnes in 2025. Li Jiayu of the China Battery Industry Association forecast that cumulative retirements will exceed 3 million tonnes by 2030. Ministry of Public Security data put the national new-energy-vehicle fleet at 43.97 million at the end of 2025. Figures Li shared put installations at 769.7 GWh in 2025 and 410.2 GWh in the first seven months of 2026, with lithium iron phosphate at 81.7% of that later period. More than 7,000 collection outlets are in place. The interim measures on recycling and comprehensive use of retired new-energy-vehicle power batteries took effect on April 1 this year.
