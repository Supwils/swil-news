## I. Policy & Markets

### 1. China nine-ministry “15th Five-Year” NEV plan: ~70% passenger-NEV share by 2030 (Policy / China)
**Summary:** People’s Daily Online, citing Economic Daily on Sep 16, reported that MIIT and eight other ministries have issued the Intelligent Connected New Energy Vehicle Industry Development Plan for the 15th Five-Year period. By 2030, new-energy passenger cars and commercial vehicles should account for about 70% and 40% of new-vehicle sales in their respective segments, vehicles with automated driving functions should reach scaled application, and China aims to further consolidate full-chain advantages as a leading auto power. The plan also sets quantitative targets for average fuel use and BEV electricity consumption, plus higher-level automated driving use cases on expressways, urban expressways, and some city roads. MIIT officials also flagged rising protectionism abroad and irrational domestic competition as key challenges.

## II. Autonomous Driving & Regulation

### 3. NHTSA Special Order requires Tesla to defend Cybercab legality under oath (Autonomous / US)
**Summary:** Automotive World and Electrek reported that the US National Highway Traffic Safety Administration escalated its Cybercab self-certification probe: after opening an audit query following the Sep 3 Austin commercial launch, NHTSA issued a formal Special Order dated Sep 10 requiring sworn answers to about 21 requests by Sep 30 on how a vehicle without a steering wheel, pedals, or mirrors meets FMVSS written for human drivers. Focus areas include the foot-brake rule (FMVSS 135), mirror requirements, and how Tesla can lawfully sell the vehicle without a Part 555 exemption; incomplete or untruthful answers can trigger penalties on the order of about $139 million. Coverage contrasts Tesla’s self-certification path with Amazon Zoox, which obtained a temporary exemption.

**Links:**

- [Automotive World — NHTSA orders Tesla to defend Cybercab certification](https://www.automotiveworld.com/news/nhtsa-orders-tesla-to-defend-cybercab-certification/)
- [Electrek — NHTSA orders Tesla to prove its Cybercab is legal to sell, under oath](https://electrek.co/2026/09/15/nhtsa-tesla-cybercab-special-order-fmvss-certification/)

**Commentary:** Regulators are not rejecting robotaxis; they are saying self-certification is not a free pass while the rulebook is still being rewritten—and Cybercab’s commercial clock is now tied to compliance.

**Summary:** Electrek and Auto Express reported on Sep 16 that Volkswagen unveiled the ID.3 GTI, an electric hot hatch based on the ID.3 Neo to mark the GTI’s 50th anniversary. A rear-mounted motor delivers about 322 hp (240 kW) and 545 Nm, enabling 0–62 mph (0–100 km/h) in about 5.6 seconds with a limited top speed near 124 mph (200 km/h); it is VW’s first RWD GTI and billed as the most powerful series-production GTI. The car uses an about 79 kWh battery for roughly 372–373 miles of WLTP range, with DC peak charging around 183 kW and about 10–80% in roughly 29 minutes. Adaptive DCC sports chassis, progressive steering, launch control, and a GTI mode are standard; pre-sales are expected in early 2027, with pricing to come.

### 6. Tesla deploys first “Accordion” prefab Supercharger: 16 stalls per truck, ~20% cheaper to install (Charging / Global)
**Summary:** Electrek reported on Sep 16 that Tesla’s charging team deployed its first “Accordion” pre-assembled Supercharger unit: it folds for transport so one truck can carry about 16 V4 stalls, installs about 20% cheaper than a conventional site, commissions without an on-site energizing technician, and fits between parking spaces when curb-behind cabinets are impractical. ### 7. Xpeng opens first global X-Energy megawatt charging station in Hong Kong with 400 kWh storage (Charging / Hong Kong)
**Summary:** CnEVPost reported on Sep 16 that Xpeng, with local operator Halo, opened its first self-developed X-Energy megawatt charging station worldwide in Hong Kong. Peak output is about 1 MW with current up to about 1,000 A, plus roughly 400 kWh of storage that keeps grid input near 240 kW to avoid costly distribution upgrades; the site is open to all brands and is described as Hong Kong’s first public megawatt charger. Xpeng said similar stations will come to Europe, after CEO He Xiaopeng outlined a target of more than 1,000 self-operated supercharging stations across 13 European countries. The report notes 1 MW is station-level peak output; Xpeng has not yet launched a car that can accept megawatt charging.
