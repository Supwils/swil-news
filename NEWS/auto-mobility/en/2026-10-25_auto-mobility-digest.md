## I. Policy and Rules

### 1. United Kingdom: the 2030 zero-emission car share is still under review and the 80% target may be cut (policy / UK)
**Summary:** The Guardian reported on October 25 that the UK zero-emission vehicle mandate requires all new cars and vans sold from 2035 to be fully electric, with interim 2030 targets of 80% of new cars and 70% of new vans. In a consultation that ended Friday, the 2030 car target could be lowered to 70% or even 50%. Analysis from the Energy and Climate Intelligence Unit, seen by the paper, says a cut to 50% could mean an extra 147 million tonnes of carbon-dioxide equivalent, about 58% of the likely emissions from burning oil produced by the Rosebank field. A government spokesperson said the mandate is under review, no decision has been taken, and the end points remain a 2030 phase-out of new petrol and diesel cars and a fully zero-emission fleet by 2035. The Guardian also wrote that more than half of UK new cars last month were electric if hybrids are included, and about 28% were electric only. EV Powered, citing senior industry sources, reported the same day that Prime Minister Andy Burnham had recused himself because of his wife’s links to the parent of charging network Be.EV, with First Secretary of State Louise Haigh taking the decision; Treasury and the business department want the 2030 target cut from 80% to 50%, transport favors nearer 60%, and energy wants no change. Downing Street refused to comment on the recusal.

### 2. United States: NHTSA proposes cyan lights to mark autonomous driving, and fitting them would not be mandatory (AV rules / US)
**Summary:** The Verge reported on October 23 that the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration proposed changes to Federal Motor Vehicle Safety Standard No. 108 that would allow cyan marker lamps to tell police and other road users when an automated driving system is operating the vehicle. The lamps would be optional for manufacturers. The agency wants any company that adds them to follow one standard for color, brightness, and position rather than a mix of signals. The proposal would also drop requirements for in-cabin hazard switches, manual high-beam controls, and turn-signal levers, and update the 12.8-volt test standard so automakers can use modern LED architectures. TechCrunch’s October 25 mobility briefing added that the lights would stay steadily illuminated in autonomous mode and be visible from the front, rear, and both sides, and that this is not a mandate that every driverless car carry the lamps.

**Links:**

- [The Verge — Federal regulators want robotaxis to signal “I’m driving” with cyan lights](https://www.theverge.com/transportation/1017435/nhtsa-autonomous-vehicle-cyan-lighting-fmvss)
- [TechCrunch — Mobility briefing: blue-green is the color of autonomy](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/25/techcrunch-mobility-blue-green-is-the-color-of-autonomy/)

**Commentary:** This is a proposed rule released on October 23; cyan lamps are an optional common signal, not equipment that is already required.

## II. Autonomous Driving

### 3. Madrid: three robotaxi efforts are in pilot stage, while paid service and insurance still lack a finished rule (autonomous driving / Spain)
**Summary:** La Vanguardia reported on October 25 that Waymo has permission from the Community of Madrid and the national traffic authority to road-test 16 Jaguar I-Pace vehicles in Madrid, Alcobendas, and Getafe, with a goal of welcoming first riders in 2027. An initial 20 GXR vans from Uber, WeRide, and Avomo are already driving around Hortaleza and Arturo Soria, and Bolt said it will deploy Lucid electric cars with Nvidia software in 2027. The paper wrote that current rules allow only pilots with a person aboard, so the commercial service the companies describe cannot yet operate in practice, and the taxi-license system is still built around a driver. ABC quoted Cesvimap, part of Mapfre, and Mutua Madrileña the same day: insurers lack a claims history they can price, there is no single autonomous-vehicle insurance product, and fewer crashes would not automatically mean cheaper cover because sensors and electronics raise repair costs. ABC relayed Waymo’s comparison that, versus human drivers and regardless of fault, crashes with serious or fatal injuries were 95% lower, pedestrian-injury collisions 93% lower, and cyclist-injury collisions 86% lower.

## III. Automakers and New Models

### 5. Audi: a roughly $4 billion Georgia plant is planned, and Volkswagen Group says committees must still approve it (capacity / US)
**Summary:** Audi’s October 25 release said it plans its first U.S. factory in Georgia, with about $4 billion of investment, about 4,500 jobs, and capacity of up to 200,000 vehicles a year. Construction is scheduled to begin in 2027 and production in the early 2030s, building premium SUVs for North American customers and making North America a third production pillar alongside Europe and China. CNBC reported the same day that President Donald Trump announced the project at a Georgia rally on October 24, and that chief executive Gernot Döllner told a Sunday roundtable the United States is the world’s second-largest premium market and local production is also meant to reduce tariff exposure. CNBC said the company expects the plant to build the Q7, Q8, and Q9, with roughly half of output exported, and that U.S. tariffs cost Audi 1.2 billion euros last year. Volkswagen Group’s statement the same day said the decision to build a new Audi plant in the United States is still subject to approval by the relevant committees; chief executive Oliver Blume and supervisory board chair Hans Dieter Pötsch both described it as a potential or prospective opportunity.

## IV. Charging, Trade, and Supply

### 8. Singapore: Tesla opens four 250 kW bays to other EVs, and SimplyGo adds six smaller operators (charging / Singapore)
**Summary:** The Straits Times reported on October 25 that four Tesla charging bays at Dunearn Village in Bukit Timah are open to all electric cars. Rated at 250 kW, they are the first Tesla high-speed bays in Singapore open to non-Tesla vehicles. The other 18 Tesla sites remain Tesla-only. Non-Tesla drivers pay 0.63 to 0.91 Singapore dollars per kWh, against 0.47 to 0.67 for Tesla owners, with the rate depending on time of day, and drivers need the Tesla app. The paper said the bays can add more than 280 km of range in 15 minutes, while most public fast chargers in Singapore are rated at 50 to 120 kW. SimplyGo added cross-operator search and payment in early October for Busways, Busways Public, G. Tech, City Energy Go, MNL Asia, and EV Mobility, covering more than 1,100 points at 332 locations, from 7 kW AC to 240 kW DC, at 0.30 to 0.86 Singapore dollars per kWh. Larger operators including Charge+, CDG Energy, Shell Recharge, and SP Mobility are not on the platform.
