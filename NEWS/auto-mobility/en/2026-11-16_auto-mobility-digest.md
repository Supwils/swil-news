## I. China Market and Exports

### 1. CPCA: China NEV retail hits 219,000 in Nov 1–8, penetration rises to 74.9%

**Summary:** CPCA weekly data show passenger NEV retail of 219,000 units for Nov 1–8, down 7% year on year but up 27% from the same period in October. NEV penetration reached 74.9%, above October's monthly record of 67.5%, while total passenger car retail fell 21% year on year to 293,000. CPCA says high oil prices have weakened demand for gasoline cars, and local subsidies and year-end promotions should keep the market stable, though the high prior-year base makes a year-on-year decline for November hard to reverse.

**Links:**

- [CnEVPost — China NEV retail, first 8 days of Nov 2026](https://cnevpost.com/2026/11/11/china-nev-retail-first-8-days-nov-2026/)

**Commentary:** Record penetration alongside weaker absolute sales suggests China is moving from volume expansion to a zero-sum fight in which gasoline cars are replaced ever faster.

### 2. China's October NEV exports reach 606,000, up about 140% year on year

**Summary:** CAAM's October data show NEV output and sales of about 1.94 million, up 13.1% year on year, including 606,000 exports (up about 140%, or 31.2% of the monthly total) and 1.334 million domestic sales. NEVs made up a record 64.3% of total vehicle sales, the fourth straight month above 60%. BYD sold more than 300,000 battery-electric vehicles in October for the first time.

**Links:**

- [electrive — China's electric car exports surge by 140 per cent](https://www.electrive.com/2026/11/11/chinas-electric-car-exports-surge-by-140-per-cent/)

**Commentary:** With domestic demand soft and exports doubling, Chinese automakers are pushing capacity pressure overseas, which is raising trade friction.

### 3. October NEV passenger wholesale about 1.8 million; BYD around 490,000

**Summary:** CPCA estimates October NEV passenger wholesale at about 1.8 million units, up 12% year on year and 8% month on month. BYD sold about 490,000 NEVs (3.62 million year to date), Leapmotor delivered 106,100 (up about 51%, a fourth straight month above 100,000), and XPeng and Xiaomi each topped 50,000 monthly deliveries. Subsidies expiring at the end of December and Double 11 promotions pulled demand forward.

## II. US and European Policy and Trade

### 4. EU to cap Chinese hybrid imports at 350,000 a year from December

**Summary:** Multiple outlets report that from Dec 1, 2026 the EU will limit imports of Chinese hybrids and plug-in hybrids to 350,000 a year, with growth of 6% a year thereafter, versus roughly 800,000 expected this year. Reports say the formal terms have yet to be confirmed, and some sources say EU-built cars are exempt or that the cap applies to all origins. Chinese brands held about 25% of Europe's plug-in hybrid market in the first half of 2026.

**Links:**

- [InsideEVs — China Agrees To Limit Hybrid Imports To EU To 350,000 Annually](https://insideevs.com/news/811656/china-eu-trade-limit-350000/)
- [CnEVPost — EU plans Chinese hybrid import curbs](https://cnevpost.com/2026/10/07/eu-plans-chinese-hybrid-import-curbs/)

**Commentary:** The EU is escalating from tariffs to quantity quotas, capping Chinese hybrids' biggest growth market and making local production the main way forward.

### 5. After the US midterms: Democrats win Congress, but auto tariffs and fuel rules unlikely to change soon

**Summary:** Democrats won both chambers on Nov 3, but the 25% Section 232 duties on imported vehicles and most parts rest on presidential national-security authority, and overriding a veto needs two-thirds majorities. Federal clean-vehicle tax credits were ended by the 2025 budget law, and restoring the $7,500 credit would need Republican votes. Analysts expect oversight, USMCA-review pressure and faster state-level EV and charging policy changes instead.

**Links:**
