## I. Policy and Rules

### 1. South Korea: paid driverless robotaxi pilot targeted for Gwangju in 2028 (Policy / South Korea)
**Summary:** Yonhap reported that the Ministry of Land, Infrastructure and Transport discussed autonomous-driving rules on October 23 at the Regulatory Rationalization Committee in the presidential office. It plans to license "autonomous driving service operators" responsible for remote monitoring, passenger safety, crash response, and cybersecurity, alongside coexistence measures with the taxi industry and accident-liability guidelines. The ministry aims to finish legal revisions next year and start a driverless robotaxi pilot in the demonstration city of Gwangju in 2028. Yonhap Infomax quoted Park Jun-hyung, director general for mobility and automotive policy, as saying the budget for 3,000 demonstration vehicles is before the National Assembly, with 200 vehicles this year and 2,800 more next year, and that Hyundai will set up a mass-production line for those vehicles at its Ulsan plant. Vehicles without a driver's seat would be allowed to leave pilot zones for depot transfers or charging under a standing rule rather than a case-by-case regulatory sandbox. Operators would have to store data in Korea and obtain a separate permit to transfer it abroad, and automakers' cybersecurity management systems would be tightened. Formal safety standards are to follow UNECE rules; before those standards are in place, a business-to-business performance certification is to start at the end of this year.

### 2. United States: scope of the 50% additional duty on Canadian auto-related goods changes on October 30 (Tariffs / United States, Canada)
**Summary:** The White House published a presidential proclamation that further changes which Canadian products pay the additional duty imposed to offset what the administration calls Canadian discrimination against U.S. motor-vehicle commerce. Products in Annex I, Part A, will no longer pay the 50% ad valorem duty imposed under Proclamation 11048, while products in Annex I, Part B, will be brought under that duty. The rate stays at 50% and applies on top of Section 232 duties. The changes cover goods entered for consumption, or withdrawn from warehouse for consumption, on or after 12:01 a.m. eastern time on October 30, 2026. GOV.HOUSE reported on October 24 that this is the second scope change in seven weeks, after Proclamation 11065 of September 8, and that the rate remains at the statutory ceiling.

## II. Autonomous Driving

### 4. Tesla: public robotaxi safety page shows 3.1 million miles without a safety monitor through October 18 (Robotaxi / United States)
**Summary:** Tesla's robotaxi safety page reports 3.1 million autonomous miles with no safety monitor in the vehicle from January 22 through October 18, 2026, including 862,000 miles after dark, 113,000 in rain, and 575,000 in rush hour. Miles by city are listed as 2,118,015 in Austin, 608,130 in Dallas, 225,964 in Houston, 61,053 in Orlando, 53,117 in Tampa, and 46,262 in Miami. The page counts zero people injured by a robotaxi, zero robotaxi airbag deployments, and zero pedestrians or cyclists hit in collisions where the robotaxi made first contact. It says the tally includes only crashes reported to NHTSA under the standing general order while no safety monitor was aboard, and that miles and events with a safety monitor are excluded. Tesla says two events were not counted as injuries: an SUV was struck by another vehicle and then redirected into a robotaxi waiting at a stop sign, an occupant of the other vehicle was taken to hospital, and nobody in the robotaxi or the SUV was hurt; in another case a rider claimed injury after the car drove over a pothole, and the investigation found no injury associated with the event. Using 2024 Texas police data, the page estimates that human drivers would have had about 9.1 injury collisions over the same Texas mileage. Las Vegas, Jacksonville, Phoenix, and San Antonio are listed as coming soon, with no dates. When the page was checked on October 24, its live mileage estimate, based on the fleet's current pace, read 3,552,793 miles.

## III. Electric Vehicles, Batteries, and Supply Chains

### 6. Seat: Martorell EV line drops Friday and Monday shifts over a cell shortage (Supply chain / Spain)
**Summary:** La Vanguardia reported on October 24 that Seat scheduled stoppages on Line 1 at Martorell, where the Cupra Raval and Volkswagen ID.Polo are built, because battery cells were unavailable. The Catalan news agency confirmed that all three assembly shifts stopped on Friday and that all three Monday shifts will also stop. The body shop halted overnight from Thursday into Friday and resumed on Friday morning. The company called it a one-off supply problem and said it hopes to resume normal work on Tuesday, October 27. CCOO spokesperson Rafael Guerrero said there was no clear arrival date for the cells and that the stoppage could last longer. Unions estimated that the scheduled interruptions affect about 1,900 vehicles. La Vanguardia said the line, launched in June, is still ramping and is approaching 1,000 vehicles a day. The news agency cited an earlier company figure of capacity for 1,200 cars a day and a plan to build 60,000 to 70,000 by the end of 2026. PowerCo, Volkswagen Group's battery subsidiary, supplies the cells, which are packed at a workshop on the Martorell site with about 500 workers, capacity for about 1,200 packs a day, and an investment of about 300 million euros. The plant's two combustion lines continue to run. Recent Seat material cited by La Vanguardia says the Raval has taken more than 51,000 orders since April.
