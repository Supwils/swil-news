## I. EVs & Policy

### 1. UK Budget: £100m over two years to cut local slow-charging prices, about £150 a year for drivers who rely on them (Policy / UK)
**Summary:** HM Treasury said on Oct 28 with Budget 2026 that from spring 2027 it will provide £100 million over two years to cut prices at lower-powered public chargers near homes—typically residential street points where cars can sit for hours or overnight—saving drivers who rely on them about £150 a year on average; such chargers make up roughly a third of the current network. The government said UK EV sales are up 36% year on year and public charge points exceed 124,000 (+7.4% YoY), while an independent review found average prices for lower- and higher-powered public charging have risen about 69% and 94% in real terms since 2021. It will also launch an industry-led “tariff porting” taskforce to explore letting drivers use cheaper domestic energy tariffs at certain public chargers. As of Oct 21, cross-pavement home chargers no longer need planning permission.

**Links:**

- [GOV.UK — Cutting electric vehicle charging costs](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/cutting-electric-vehicle-charging-costs)

**Commentary:** Subsidies are aimed first at the most expensive, no-driveway slow charge—public pricing has become an explicit brake on UK electrification.

### 2. U.S. House bill would lift the new EV tax credit to $15,000: referred to committee, no cosponsors yet (Policy / US)
**Summary:** Electrek and EV News Desk reported on Oct 28 that Rep. Greg Landsman (D-OH) introduced the 21st Century Vehicle Choice Act (H.R. 10881) on Oct 22. It would raise each half of the new clean-vehicle credit (30D) from $3,750 to $7,500—up to $15,000 total—raise the used-vehicle credit to as much as $8,000 or 60% of the sale price, lift the home-charger credit from 30% to 50%, and delete OBBB termination dates to make the incentives permanent. Income caps on the new credit would fall about 25%; anyone allowed a 30D credit in a prior tax year would get $0 on a later new EV. The bill was referred to three committees and, as of the reports, had no cosponsors, hearings, or votes. Federal new- and used-vehicle credits remain unavailable for vehicles acquired after Sep 30, 2025.

**Links:**

- [Electrek — Dems introduce new $15,000 EV tax credit bill](https://electrek.co/2026/10/28/15000-ev-tax-credit-bill-landsman-no-chance/)
- [EV News Desk — A House bill would raise the new EV credit to $15,000. It is not law.](https://evnewsdesk.com/articles/2026-10-28-vehicle-choice-act-ev-credit/)

**Commentary:** Big headline numbers with zero legislative progress; if shoppers wait for it, the bill could hurt the market it aims to help.

## II. Batteries, Recalls & Charging Networks

### 4. VW battery thermal-propagation recall expands to Audi Q4: more than 85,000 EVs worldwide (Recall / Global)
**Summary:** Electrek reported on Oct 28 that VW Group’s battery-related recalls now include Audi: Germany’s KBA database lists about 12,734 Audi Q4 e-tron / Q4 Sportback e-tron vehicles globally. Together with earlier ID. Buzz, ID.3, ID.4, ID.7 and Cupra Born actions, the related recalls cover about 85,402 EVs worldwide. In the U.S., VW of America filed with NHTSA on Oct 23 for about 9,048 MY 2024–2025 Q4s; certain charging profiles combined with state of charge over 80% may trigger thermal propagation. Root cause is still unknown; owners are advised to limit charging to 80%, and U.S. dealers can run diagnostics and apply a temporary SoC limit. Audi said it is unaware of claims, crashes, fires, injuries, or deaths tied to the Q4 battery issue.

**Links:**

- [Electrek — Volkswagen’s EV battery recall reaches over 85,000 with Audi](https://electrek.co/2026/10/28/volkswagens-ev-battery-recall-reaches-over-85000-with-audi/)

**Commentary:** Shared battery platforms spill across brands; the 80% cap buys time—root cause and the final remedy will decide residual value.

### 5. CATL: sulfide all-solid-state cells enter 20 Ah sampling, ~500 Wh/kg target, small-batch production in 2027 (Battery / China)
**Summary:** On Oct 27 CATL told investors via the Shenzhen Stock Exchange Hudongyi platform that it continues to invest in all-solid-state batteries, sees its tech as industry-leading, and expects small-batch production in 2027 with larger-scale manufacturing around 2030. Follow-up coverage on Oct 28 said the sulfide program has moved from a ~10 Ah validation platform to 20 Ah sample trials, with an R&D team of more than 1,000 and an energy-density target of about 500 Wh/kg. That figure is a development goal, not a confirmed production-cell spec; CATL has not disclosed trial volumes, OEM customers, or vehicle launch dates. Chairman Robin Zeng previously rated all-solid-state maturity at level 4 on a 1–9 scale; interface stability, cost, and yield remain industrialization hurdles.

### 6. IONITY: first BESS live at Wertheim; plans ~50 European charging-park storage systems in 2027 (Charging / Europe)
**Summary:** electrive reported on Oct 27 (widely carried on Oct 28) that fast-charging operator IONITY commissioned its first external battery energy storage system (BESS) at Wertheim, Baden-Württemberg—a Sungrow setup of about 1,028 kWh and 500 kW peak—lifting the site from four to six Alpitronic HYC400 points, with two more ABB points up to ~350 kW planned. The main use case is bridging until a medium-voltage grid upgrade that can take up to about three years. IONITY plans roughly 50 BESS deployments across European parks in 2027 totaling more than 20 MWh, and is developing a Hemsbach site with a >223 kWp solar canopy and >2 MWh of storage.

**Links:**

- [electrive — Ionity plans battery storage systems at 50 charging parks](https://www.electrive.com/2026/10/27/ionity-plans-battery-storage-systems-at-50-charging-parks/)
