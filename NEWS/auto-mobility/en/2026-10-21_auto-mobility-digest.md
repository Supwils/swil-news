## I. Policy and the China Market

### 1. MIIT: L2 driver-assistance penetration is above 70%, and scaled autonomous-driving pilots will speed up (Policy / China)
**Summary:** China News Service reported on October 21 that Xin Guobin, vice minister of industry and information technology, told the opening of the 2026 World Intelligent Connected Vehicles Conference in Beijing that passenger cars with L2 combined driver assistance now exceed 70% penetration in China, and that the first L3 conditional automated-driving models are already in on-road pilots in designated areas. He said the ministry will expand intelligent-connected-vehicle use cases in a controlled way, advance market-access and road pilots cautiously, and speed up scaled demonstrations in priority autonomous-driving scenarios. The ministry will also push the vehicle-road-cloud pilot, implement mandatory autonomous-driving standards, and accelerate standards for automated parking, operational monitoring, and data security.

### 2. CPCA: NEV retail sales were 520,000 units from October 1-18, down 13% year on year (Market / China)
**Summary:** National Business Daily on October 21 cited the China Passenger Car Association: from October 1 through 18, retail sales of new-energy passenger cars were 520,000 units, down 13% from the same period last October and down 2% from the same period last month. ## II. Autonomous Driving

### 3. GM: eyes-off highway driving will extend to pickups and large SUVs, with no timeline (Autonomy / United States)
**Summary:** Reuters reported on October 21 that General Motors, at a technology event in New York, said the eyes-off highway system it had already scheduled for the Cadillac Escalade IQ in 2028 will extend to its highest-selling pickups and other large SUVs, without giving a timeline for those vehicles. Chief product officer Sterling Anderson said the highway system does not require the driver to watch the road, and that GM will eventually expand it to other roads. The company is developing the central computer in-house so vehicles can take remote updates. Axios reported that the architecture delivers about 40 times the computing power with 30% less wiring, and that an OnStar AI assistant is set to debut on the 2027 Cadillac Lyriq through a software update. The Verge added that GM says its fourth-generation EV platform will use lithium-manganese-rich cells with 33% higher energy density than conventional lithium iron phosphate and 20% higher efficiency per kilowatt-hour, built by Ultium Cells, its joint venture with LG Energy Solution, in Spring Hill, Tennessee, and that GM aims to use AI to cut vehicle development time in half.

## III. Batteries, Charging, and Recalls

### 6. Volkswagen: three KBA battery recalls cover 72,668 vehicles worldwide, with an 80% charge cap until a fix (Battery / Europe, United States)
**Summary:** Electrek reported on October 21 that three recalls under investigation by Germany's Federal Motor Transport Authority, the KBA, cover 72,668 vehicles worldwide, including 9,881 in Germany. The vehicles are certain ID. Buzz, ID.3, ID.4, and ID.7 models built from 2022 to 2025, plus Cupra Born models from 2022 to 2024. Volkswagen said a supplier defect means some charging profiles combined with a state of charge above 80% may trigger thermal propagation, and that the system will temporarily limit charging to 80% until a remedy is available. A separate U.S. recall covers 22,524 ID.4 vehicles from model years 2023 to 2026, NHTSA campaign 26V630, with cells from SK Battery America. Unless owners opt out by October 21, Volkswagen intends to apply the 80% cap remotely, with the app reflecting it on or about October 27 and risk letters expected on November 27. electrive said the KBA has reported no property damage or injuries and listed reference numbers 17137R, 17101R, and 17103R.

Neither company has announced site counts, prices, or an opening date. Paul Tan said the Denza Z9 GT is currently the only model sold in Malaysia that can use the full flash-charging rate.

### 9. Octopus offers fleets up to 30% off public charging on partner networks (Charging / United Kingdom)
**Summary:** Autocar reported on October 21 that UK energy supplier Octopus launched FleetBoost, a monthly per-driver subscription that cuts public charging by up to 30% on MFG EV Power, Ionity, and Be.EV, with more networks hoped for in the coming months. The current combined network puts 99% of the UK within 14 miles of a FleetBoost site. Octopus told Autocar it expects the membership cost to be covered by about two charging sessions. Autocar cited a Rightcharge report that in 2025 public charging was 27% of fleet sessions but 57% of electricity spending. The most expensive networks can cost 93 pence per kWh, compared with less than 7 pence per kWh on a low overnight home tariff. For a Tesla Model Y, Autocar said running costs can range from 1.5 pence to 21 pence per mile, against the latest HM Revenue and Customs mileage rates of 16 pence per mile for a comparable diesel and 17 pence for petrol.
