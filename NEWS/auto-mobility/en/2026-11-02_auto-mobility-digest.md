### 3. European Parliament ENVI set to vote Nov 4 on 2035 car CO₂ target: compromise would cut 100% to 90% (Policy / EU)
**Summary:** Agence Europe and PoliData’s week-ahead notes around Nov 2 say the European Parliament’s Environment, Public Health and Food Safety Committee (ENVI) is due to vote on Nov 4 on the 2035 new-car CO₂ rules. The Commission’s December 2025 proposal would lower the 2035 fleet-wide cut versus 2021 levels from 100% to 90%, with the remaining about 10% coverable by credits for EU-made low-carbon steel, e-fuels and biofuels. An ICCT analysis previously warned that weakening car and van CO₂ standards could cost Europe’s passenger-car industry up to about 185,000 jobs by 2035; keeping current standards would leave employment roughly stable, while adding industrial measures that support EU-based EV production could raise net employment by about 320,000 jobs versus 2025.

## II. Autonomous Driving & Mobility

### 4. Japan eyes uniform certification from December for steering-wheel-free Level 4/5 vehicles to aid robotaxi mass production (Policy / Japan)
**Summary:** Nikkei reported on Nov 2 that Japan’s Ministry of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism is preparing to revise vehicle safety standards so Level 4 and Level 5 driverless vehicles can be certified under a uniform rule set from December, replacing case-by-case approvals. Certified vehicles would not need steering wheels, brake/accelerator pedals, rearview mirrors or driver-monitoring systems, cutting cost and freeing cabin layouts; passenger taxis and buses would still face dummy crash tests, while unmanned cargo vehicles would be exempt, though both must include failure-safe measures. The change tracks UN WP29 rules agreed in June and expected to become international standards in December; the government targets about 10,000 Level 4-capable buses, taxis and similar vehicles by fiscal 2030. A ministry vehicle-safety meeting on Nov 4 is set to discuss the plan.

**Links:**

- [Nikkei Asia — Japan weighs standards for self-driving cars without steering wheels](https://asia.nikkei.com/business/automobiles/japan-weighs-standards-for-self-driving-cars-without-steering-wheels)
- [EVwire — Japan to uniformly certify steering-wheel-free Level 4/5 vehicles from December](https://evwire.com/p/japan-mlit-steering-wheel-free-autonomous-vehicles-unified-certification-december)

**Commentary:** Vehicle rules are shifting from one-off approvals toward a mass-production template—but paid driverless rides still need separate operating permits.

### 5. Ohio House Bill 1034 proposes a statewide AV framework allowing driverless operation and limiting local restrictions (Policy / US)
**Summary:** In its Nov 2 week-in-review, Driverless Commute noted that Ohio Rep. Adam Holmes introduced House Bill 1034 on Oct 28 to create a statewide framework for fully autonomous vehicles on public roads when they meet safety, registration and operating requirements. Manufacturers would need emergency-response interaction plans explaining how first responders can reach fleet staff, identify AVs and remove disabled vehicles; the bill would also set insurance rules for autonomous rideshare and clarify traffic-violation responsibility when no human driver is present. A key provision would generally bar local governments from imposing their own AV operating limits while preserving some airport authority over fees and designated pickup points. As of the report, the bill had been introduced but had not advanced in committee.

## III. Batteries & Charging

### 7. E.ON and BYD launch Home Charge in Germany: 12 euro-cents/kWh smart-home-charging bonus for at least 24 months (Charging / Europe)
**Summary:** Multiple outlets reported on Nov 2 that E.ON and BYD launched their first joint home-charging offer in Germany, “E.ON Home Charge.” BYD battery-electric and plug-in hybrid owners set a departure time and target state of charge in the Mein E.ON app and, in Smart mode, earn 12 euro-cents per kWh for home charging for at least 24 months; new E.ON customers can also receive a one-off about €80 bonus. E.ON estimates roughly €20 a month saved at about 170 kWh of monthly charging, or about €490 over two years; BYD’s example of 2,000 kWh a year implies about €240 annually. The partners announced the tie-up in September and plan to expand to other European markets and add bidirectional (V2G) charging, without disclosing timing or eligible models.

### 8. UK’s Allye unveils MEV depot charger buffered by reused EV batteries: up to about 400 kW without a heavier grid hookup (Charging / Batteries)
**Summary:** BatteryIndustry reported on Nov 2 that Britain’s Allye Energy launched the Mobile Energy Vault (MEV), a depot-oriented fast charger that stores energy in complete second-life EV battery packs drawn slowly from an existing site connection, then delivers up to about 400 kW DC to two vehicles at once. Launch variants are the MEV200 (about 155 kWh storage) and MEV300 (about 232 kWh); pricing starts at about £70,000, or from about £1,900 a month as “EV Charging as a Service,” with first deliveries targeted for Q2 2027. Allye says new UK depot grid connections often take 18–36 months, while an MEV can commission in hours via industrial sockets or a fixed link and can be relocated later.

**Links:**

- [BatteryIndustry.net — Allye launches 400 kW EV fast charger powered by reused batteries](https://batteryindustry.net/allye-launches-400-kw-ev-fast-charger-powered-by-reused-batteries/)

**Commentary:** Peak power can be “moved” with batteries, but daily energy still tracks the weak grid’s recharge budget—second-life buffers ease interconnection waits, they do not create unlimited kilowatt-hours.
