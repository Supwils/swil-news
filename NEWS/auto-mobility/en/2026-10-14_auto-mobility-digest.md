## I. Policy and markets

### 1. CPCA: China NEV passenger retail was 281,000 in October 1-11, down 13%, with penetration at 61.2% (Market / China)
**Summary:** Data released by the China Passenger Car Association on October 14 show retail sales of 281,000 new-energy passenger vehicles from October 1 to 11, down 13% year on year and 7% from the same period last month. Year-to-date retail sales were 8.039 million, also down 13%. Retail penetration was 61.2%, below September's record 67.0% and the 70.3% recorded in the first 13 days of September. Automakers wholesaled 301,000 NEVs, up 6% year on year and down 11% from the same period last month, with wholesale penetration at 78.1%. Year-to-date wholesale volume was 11.75 million, up 9%. Retail sales of all passenger vehicles were 459,000, down 24% year on year and up 7% from the same period last month. CnEVPost, citing the association, said September NEV retail sales had already fallen 16.7%, a ninth straight monthly decline, and that gasoline-vehicle production and wholesale sales in the first two weeks of October fell 51% and 53%.

- [CnEVPost — China NEV retail sales fall 13% in October 1-11, penetration 61.2%](https://cnevpost.com/2026/10/14/china-nev-retail-sales-fall-oct-1-11/)
- [Sing Tao — CPCA: 281,000 NEV retail sales in October 1-11](https://m.stnn.cc/detail/6acf3a4038930222089c4b40.html)

**Commentary:** The 281,000 figure covers only the first 11 days of October, and the wholesale gain is export-heavy, so it does not show that showroom demand has recovered.

### 2. BYD, Geely, Chery, and CAAM welcome the China-EU understanding on hybrids and EVs (Policy / China and Europe)
**Summary:** Securities Times reported on October 14 that BYD, Geely Holding, and Chery each issued statements on the second meeting of the China-EU Trade and Investment Consultations. BYD said the outcome contained trade differences and avoided a further escalation, creating a stable, predictable, and sustainable setting for China-EU hybrid trade. Geely Holding welcomed the effort to handle auto-trade differences through mutual respect and equal consultation. Chery said the two industries complement each other and supported continued dialogue. 10jqka quoted a same-day statement from the China Association of Automobile Manufacturers: under WTO rules, the two sides reached an understanding on hybrid and battery-electric trade on three principles — based on facts, non-discrimination, and compliance. None of the statements published a quota, a tariff rate, or an effective date.

## II. Automakers and capacity

### 3. Ford opens Fathom EV pickup invites at $29,945 with destination; official preorders wait until early 2027 (New model / United States)
**Summary:** Electrek and InsideEVs reported on October 14 that Ford has opened a preorder-invite list for the Fathom electric pickup. Electrek said the standard-range truck is priced at $28,350 before a $1,595 destination charge, still under $30,000 with that fee. InsideEVs, citing Ford, put the starting price at $29,945 including the destination charge. Official preorders are expected in early 2027, with deliveries later that year, and the first prototypes are scheduled to come off the line at Louisville Assembly. It is the first model on Ford's Universal EV platform and uses a lithium-iron-phosphate battery. InsideEVs added a 400-volt architecture, standard rear-wheel drive, and an available dual-motor all-wheel-drive version. Range, towing, and payload have not been published. Every truck will have BlueCruise hardware and bidirectional charging, plus CarPlay and Android Auto. A Ford spokesperson told Electrek that the stated 2027 launch is unchanged.

### 4. Volvo delays U.S. EX60 deliveries as Torslanda output runs at about a tenth of the 2,000-a-week plan (Capacity / Europe and the United States)
**Summary:** Automotive News reported on October 14 that the EX60 ramp at Volvo's Torslanda plant in Sweden is off plan, and that U.S. dealers will stay short of cars deep into 2027. InsideEVs, citing that report, said the plant was meant to build about 2,000 EX60s a week and that a person familiar with the situation put current output at about one tenth of the target. The bottlenecks described are fitting the high-voltage battery into the body and building the drive motors. Dealers had previously told Automotive News that Volvo expected to sell about 7,000 EX60s in the United States this year; only a small number have arrived. Spokesperson Jacquelyn Johnson confirmed that availability will "remain limited" in the near term, that a "significant number" of cars already allocated should reach dealers early next year, and that the delays are not about quality, safety, or product performance. She said megacasting and cell-to-body construction have lengthened the ramp. Production began in late April.

**Links:**

- [Automotive News — EX60 launch stumbles, leaving U.S. dealers short](https://www.autonews.com/volvo/an-volvo-ex60-production-woes-1014/)
- [InsideEVs — The EX60 was supposed to sell in the thousands in the U.S. this year](https://insideevs.com/news/811883/volvo-ex60-us-deliveries-delay/)

### 5. Hyundai opens the Pune plant at 170,000 units a year, taking India capacity close to 1 million (Capacity / India)
**Summary:** The Korea Herald reported on October 14 that Hyundai Motor Group Executive Chair Chung Euisun attended the inauguration of a new plant in Pune, India. Built on a former General Motors site acquired in 2023, it starts at 170,000 units a year, with plans to reach 250,000 within two years and 320,000 by 2030. Together with two Chennai plants totaling 824,000 units, Hyundai's annual capacity in India is now close to 1 million. Including Kia's Anantapur plant, group capacity is 1.42 million and is projected to pass 1.5 million after 2028. Pune begins with the second-generation Venue for India and exports to more than 30 countries, freeing Chennai lines to speed EV output. Hyundai plans a dedicated compact electric SUV for India early next year. Chung called charging infrastructure the main bottleneck in the EV shift and said the company will use more locally made parts. Hyundai Motor India aims to lift exports to 30% of production by 2030.

**Links:**

- [The Korea Herald — Hyundai's Pune plant takes India capacity close to 1 million](https://www.koreaherald.com/article/10902512)

**Commentary:** Close to 1 million adds Pune's initial 170,000 units to existing Chennai capacity; 320,000 units and a 30% export share are 2030 targets.

### 7. Croatia's Verne starts fully driverless test rides in Zagreb and hopes to open them commercially next year (Autonomous driving / Europe)
**Summary:** eNCA carried an AFP report on October 14 that Verne, a Croatian company backed by Uber and using Pony.ai technology, has run a commercial robotaxi service in Zagreb since April with a safety operator on board. Chief executive Marko Pejkovic invited an AFP journalist on Tuesday to ride what he called Europe's "first" fully automated robotaxi trips, which he hopes to open to the public commercially next year. He said fully driverless testing began in August on a roughly 22-kilometer route between headquarters and the airport. The fleet of about 30 vehicles covers the city center, the airport, and selected nearby areas. The company said about 1,500 people use it in Zagreb, with more than 10,000 on a waiting list. Rides can be ordered in the Verne or Uber app, and the fare is locked at 1.99 euros. Completed commercial rides average 4.8 out of five.

## IV. Supply chain

### 8. Seven South Carolina suppliers invest $82 million to feed Scout's Traveler and Terra (Supply chain / United States)
**Summary:** The South Carolina Daily Gazette reported on October 14 that Spartanburg Steel, Magna DRIVE, KV Final, Röchling Automotive, Magna Mirrors, Cooper Standard, and Bant Boru will invest a combined $82 million in existing plants in Spartanburg, Duncan, and Piedmont and hire 200 people in the state. The parts are for electric and hybrid-like versions of the Traveler SUV and Terra pickup that Scout Motors plans to build at its plant under construction in Blythewood. Spartanburg Steel accounts for about half of the money, with a $40 million expansion and 60 jobs. Scout chief executive Scott Keogh called the expansions a step toward keeping more of the supply chain in South Carolina. The Gazette said the plant is on schedule to build its first test vehicles by the end of the year, with phased testing through 2027, and that vehicles are now expected to reach the market in 2028. Full capacity is planned at 200,000 vehicles a year. The supplier spending sits on top of the $2 billion and 4,000 direct jobs Scout has already pledged.
