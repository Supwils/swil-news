## I. Major Automakers & Products

### 1. Tesla confirms Cybertruck wind-down around mid-2027 to free capacity for Cybercab (Product / US)
**Summary:** CNBC, TechCrunch and others reported that Elon Musk said on X on Nov 6 Tesla will be “winding down” Cybertruck production at Gigafactory Texas around mid-2027 to make room for higher Cybercab robotaxi output. TechCrunch Mobility’s Nov 8 roundup cited industry estimates of about 71,000 Cybertrucks sold since late-2023 deliveries—far below early talk of roughly 250,000 units a year. Around the same weekend, the U.S. configurator showed Premium AWD rising from about $84,990 to about $92,490 and Cyberbeast from about $99,990 to about $102,490, while the base Dual Motor could no longer be ordered; other reports said Tesla plans an extra shift before the line is dismantled because of backlog demand. The Cybertruck’s specialized tooling remains unique to the Texas plant.

### 3. Chery unveils Volt-dragon: ~1.44 MW ultra-fast charging, claiming ~500 km in five minutes (Charging / China)
**Summary:** CNN Brasil reported from Chery’s Global Innovation Conference and International User Summit in Wuhu on Nov 8 that the company launched Volt-dragon, an ultra-fast charging system with a peak of about 1.44 MW. Chery claims about 500 km of range recovery in five minutes and roughly 6 minutes 50 seconds for a 10%–97% charge, without naming compatible models or test conditions, and said vehicles would need architectures of about 800 V or higher. The cable-and-connector set weighs about 2 kg; claimed grid-to-connector efficiency is about 96.5%, with immersion liquid cooling and noise below about 50 dB. Chery plans to start with about 100 stations across 10 Chinese cities and targets more than 20,000 V2G-capable points across markets by 2029; demos also included solar-plus-storage microgrids and robotic plug-in arms.

**Links:**

## II. Autonomous Driving & Mobility

### 4. Nvidia approaches Korea’s MOLIT to put Hyperion/Halos into driverless robotaxi pilots (Autonomy / Korea)
**Summary:** Seoul Economic Daily reported exclusively on Nov 8 that Nvidia has approached Korean autonomous-driving firms and the Ministry of Land, Infrastructure and Transport (MOLIT) about applying its Hyperion in-vehicle compute-and-sensor platform and Halos safety framework to the government-led driverless robotaxi pilot. If adopted in the first region, Jeonnam–Gwangju, the stack could sit on Hyundai-supplied Ioniq 5–based vehicles and combine with local software from RideFlux and Autonomous A2Z. MOLIT plans Level 4 driverless taxi services from 2028, aims to add four to five pilot cities in 2027, and wants the fleet to grow from about 200 to about 3,000 vehicles; Nvidia has also widened talks with Socar and other mobility/data players. Korean reactions are mixed: some worry about re-adapting software for unclear gains, others see a path into a likely global standard.

**Links:**

- [Seoul Economic Daily — Nvidia Seeks Role in Korea's Robotaxi Pilot Program](https://en.sedaily.com/technology/2026/11/08/nvidia-eyes-koreas-robotaxi-pilot-to-spread-self-driving)

**Commentary:** Chip vendors are racing to become the default base layer of national pilots—whoever lands in the rulebook is closer to becoming next-gen ADAS commodity hardware.

## III. Batteries, Charging & Software

### 7. U.S. public DC fast-charging ports reach about 80,930; Tesla still ~half the network (Charging / US)
**Summary:** Alternative Fuels Data Center figures as of Nov 1, 2026 put U.S. public DC fast-charging ports at about 80,930, up roughly 25% year over year, with a net monthly gain of about 2,485—the largest monthly rise in two years—including about 1,368 temporarily unavailable ports. By network, Tesla Superchargers lead with about 40,504 ports (~50%), followed by Electrify America (~6,081), EVgo (~5,414) and ChargePoint (~5,002). Station locations are estimated around 16,460, or about 4.92 ports per site on average. Federal NEVI formula funding is in its final fiscal-year window while states keep awarding and commissioning sites.
