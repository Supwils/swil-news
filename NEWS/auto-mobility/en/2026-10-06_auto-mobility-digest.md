## I. Electric Vehicles and Investment

### 1. Kenya signs a proposed $3 billion green-mobility memorandum with Endelevu, and Ruto names Geely (Policy / Kenya)
**Summary:** The Presidential Communication Service and The Star reported on October 6 that President William Ruto witnessed a memorandum of understanding on electric-vehicle manufacturing between the Government of Kenya and Endelevu Enterprise Corporation at State House, Nairobi. Ruto said the memorandum sets a framework for an integrated green-mobility ecosystem, with a proposed investment of $3 billion, and called it one of the largest electric-mobility commitments in the region. In his remarks he addressed Endelevu and Geely as partners. The proposal includes an assembly plant for 50,000 four-wheel vehicles a year, a second plant for 100,000 two-wheelers and light-mobility vehicles a year, 1,000 solar-powered charging hubs, and a digital platform to manage up to 100,000 green vehicles. He put the employment figure at about 2,000 direct jobs, more than 20,000 indirect jobs among suppliers, logistics firms, and service providers, and up to 80,000 further opportunities in fleet management, operations, and services. He also said a memorandum is a framework, not the finish line, and that the project still has to move from signature to definitive agreements, groundbreaking, and production.

The specification table lists a Powerwall 3 for Model 3 and Model Y home-backup installation, and says Powerwall 2 and Powerwall+ compatibility is coming soon. On an estimate of 30 kWh a day starting from a full battery, a Model 3 or Model Y can extend backup by over two days. The specification table lists maximum continuous power of 11.52 kW.

## II. Autonomous Driving

### 4. Waymo starts fully driverless trips in Detroit, initially for employees only (Autonomy / United States)
**Summary:** The Detroit Free Press reported on October 6 that Waymo spokesman Chris Bonelli confirmed by email that day that the company is operating fully autonomous vehicles in Detroit. Ride-hailing is not yet open to the public, and the riders for now are Waymo employees. Driving continues downtown and in Corktown, Midtown, Hamtramck, and Indian Village, with a gradual expansion across the broader metropolitan area. Bonelli said the company will follow its safety framework and serve public riders after the technology is validated in the city and local operations are ready. The paper noted that less than a month earlier the company still said trained specialists remained at the wheel and that fully autonomous testing in Detroit had not begun. In its note to the paper, Waymo said that over more than 270 million fully autonomous miles in its operating cities, the Waymo Driver was involved in 95% fewer crashes causing serious or fatal injury than human drivers in the same areas.

### 8. Vietnam's public charging stations must serve compatible vehicles and show prices from July 1, 2027 (Charging / Vietnam)
**Summary:** TechNode Global reported on October 6 that Vietnam's Ministry of Industry and Trade has issued national technical regulation QCVN 32:2026/BCT for charging stations, effective July 1, 2027. The report does not give a separate signing date for the circular. The rule covers stations in public areas, car parks, and rest stops, and outdoor or stand-alone sites. It does not cover basements or indoor garages, private stations, wireless chargers, in-car chargers, or test equipment. Public stations must meet technical standards for connectors, charging modes, and specifications so compatible vehicles can share them, and control systems must not block charging for setups already declared compatible. Commercial stations must show the price, billing method, and electricity used before and during each session, and protect that data from tampering. Stations installed before July 2027 may keep operating, with a six-month safety review and up to two years to fix shortcomings; later repairs or expansions must meet the new standard. The report says the regulation itself does not specify penalties.

## IV. Hydrogen Buses and Labor

### 9. Toyota and Isuzu's new Sora hydrogen bus is rated at more than 300 kilometers (New model / Japan)
**Summary:** Toyota's global newsroom dates the launch of the new Sora fuel-cell route bus to October 5, and electrive reported the vehicle on October 6. The bus combines Isuzu's battery-electric full-flat route-bus platform with Toyota's fuel-cell system. Toyota's urban-model specifications list length, width, and height of 10,545 mm, 2,485 mm, and 3,440 mm, and a capacity of 70 people: 19 seated, 50 standing, and one crew member. The fuel-cell stack is rated at 113 kW. Two AC induction motors produce 220 kW and 970 Nm combined. Three 70 MPa hydrogen tanks total 471 liters, and the propulsion battery is lithium-ion. Toyota says total hydrogen-tank capacity is about 20% lower than the first generation, while range is about 100 kilometers longer, at over 300 kilometers. Refueling takes about 10 minutes, and the time can be cut by about half once compatible high-speed filling stations are in place. A one-touch wheelchair securement option is expected to be orderable from around April 2027. The newsroom page does not list a price.

### 10. Unifor sets an October 17-18 strike-authorization vote on the Stellantis contract (Labor / Canada)
**Summary:** Unifor said on October 6 that talks with Stellantis remain at an impasse and that it will hold strike-authorization votes. Authorization gives the bargaining committee the power to call a strike if necessary and does not by itself mean a walkout. The unresolved issues include the future of Brampton Assembly and a core economic pattern that the company has made conditional on an agreement to close and sell the plant. Votes will take place on October 17 and 18, with results on October 19. National president Lana Payne said in the release that the issues go beyond Brampton to job security for all members and the future of Stellantis operations in Canada, and that a strike remains a last resort. The Detroit Free Press reported the same day that the union represents about 8,000 Stellantis auto workers in Canada, that Brampton employs more than 2,000 members, that the company earlier this month entered a memorandum with defense contractor Roshel to sell the plant, that the contract expired on September 20, and that formal talks stalled on September 11.
