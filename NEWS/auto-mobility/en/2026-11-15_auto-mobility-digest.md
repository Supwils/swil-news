## I. Policy & Markets

### 1. Rivian, Lucid, and Scout say they are not counting on Democrats to restore U.S. federal EV buyer credits (Policy / United States)
**Summary:** CNBC reported on Nov 15 that the CEOs of Rivian, Lucid, and Scout Motors are not building business plans around a return of federal EV purchase support, even as Democrats are expected to control both chambers of Congress from January 2027. Clean-vehicle tax credits for newly acquired vehicles ended after Sept 30, 2025, removing incentives that previously reached up to about $7,500. Cox Automotive / Kelley Blue Book data cited in coverage still showed higher average transaction prices for new EVs than for the broader market as of September 2026. Executives said they would welcome help if it arrives, but are not assuming a fast policy reversal.

### 2. Japan’s Jan–Oct BEV passenger sales hit about 111,876 units, clearing 100,000 for the first time before year-end (Market / Japan)
**Summary:** EVX News, citing Sankei Shimbun and dealer-association tallies, said Japan sold about 111,876 battery-electric passenger cars from January through October 2026, including about 16,262 kei EVs—already above the roughly 60,677 sold in all of 2025. October alone added about 14,644 units for a record share near 4.6%. Toyota led year-to-date with about 27,549 units, edging Nissan’s about 26,642 for the first time on that measure. Coverage notes METI’s CEV subsidy applications may close around December, with Model 3 / Model Y grants set to fall from January 2027, helping explain the recent rush.

### 3. China’s October NEV sales reached about 1.94 million with exports near 606,000; Tesla Shanghai’s export share hit a record ~87% (Market / China)
**Summary:** China’s Ministry of Industry and Information Technology, citing CAAM, said NEV production and sales in October 2026 reached about 2.017 million and 1.94 million units, up about 13.8% and 13.1% year on year, or about 64.3% of total auto sales. NEV exports were about 606,000, up roughly 1.4 times. On CPCA figures reported by CnEVPost, Tesla’s Shanghai plant wholesaled about 67,829 vehicles in October, exporting about 59,012—or about 87%—leaving implied China deliveries of about 8,817, the lowest since July 2022; year-to-date exports have overtaken domestic sales. The domestic-soft / export-strong split remains central to reading China’s NEV cycle.

## III. Battery Swap & Charging Networks

### 7. Nio opens China’s northernmost swap station in Heihe, claiming ~3-minute swaps in about -48°C cold (Battery swap / China)
**Summary:** CnEVPost reported on Nov 15 that Nio commissioned its northernmost China battery-swap station in the Heihe Free Trade Zone, Heilongjiang, where temperatures can fall to about -48°C. The fifth-generation station serves Nio, Onvo, and Firefly. An insulated chamber and round-the-clock thermal management keep in-station packs at about 15°C–30°C, with automatic equipment preheating; Nio says users can finish a swap in about three minutes without leaving the car. As of that day, Nio counted about 4,262 swap stations nationwide (about 1,083 on highways) and about 9,855 combined charge and swap sites, including about 102 swap stations in the northeast.

**Links:**

- [CnEVPost — Nio opens northernmost swap station in China](https://cnevpost.com/2026/11/15/nio-opens-northernmost-swap-station-china-heihe/)

**Commentary:** Pushing swap stations into deep cold is really a test of pack thermal management—more decisive for winter usability than headline swap seconds.

### 8. UK motorway operator Moto plans a dozen-plus solar farms to power EV hubs amid slow grid connections (Charging / United Kingdom)
**Summary:** The Daily Mail on Nov 15, following a Sunday Telegraph interview, reported that Moto—operator of about 70 UK service stations—is planning more than a dozen solar farms beside its sites. Consent is already secured for four locations, including about 7 MW at Wetherby, about 7.15 MW at Donington, and about 4 MW at Stirling, with about 11 more under negotiation. CEO Ken McMeikan said grid connections can take around four years and lag charging demand; Moto is buying or leasing nearby farmland for solar-plus-storage. UK Department for Transport analysis previously found that as of July 2024 only about 10% of England’s motorway service areas had the power capacity needed for rapid-charging demand through at least 2035.
