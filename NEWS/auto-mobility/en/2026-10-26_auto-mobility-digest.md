## I. Policy and Rules

### 1. United Kingdom: the ZEV-mandate review is looking at credits for vehicle-to-grid hardware, which could let makers sell an extra petrol car (policy / UK)
**Summary:** The Guardian reported on October 26 that UK ministers are considering vehicle-to-grid technology inside the review of the zero-emission vehicle mandate. A carmaker that fits bidirectional charging could earn a credit and, under the consultation that closed Friday, use it to sell another petrol car without the same fine risk if it misses the ZEV targets. Ben Nelmes, chief executive of New AutoMotive, said one or even two extra credits worth about 4,000 pounds each would hand manufacturers thousands of pounds per car for kit that costs a few hundred pounds, while few drivers own the expensive charger needed to use it. The paper cited a National Energy System Operator forecast that V2G cars could provide 1.2 gigawatts by 2030, equal to a power station, and as much as 41 gigawatts by 2050, more than a quarter of peak demand. Octopus Energy has claimed drivers could save 620 pounds a year. Environmental groups are braced for weaker sales targets that could mean an extra 147 million tonnes of carbon by 2050.

**Commentary:** The V2G credit is still text in a closed consultation, while 4,000 pounds is a think-tank estimate and the 1.2 and 41 gigawatt figures are grid-operator scenarios.

Today's opinion states the new-vehicle share and the sales-ban timetable. It does not publish new subsidies or penalties.

### 3. European Union: the Council adopts the Eurovignette revision, with road-charge cuts of up to 75% for low-emission trucks and buses (policy / EU)
**Summary:** ANSA reported from Brussels on October 26 that EU member states gave final approval to a revision of the Eurovignette road-charging directive so heavy-vehicle tolls line up with carbon-dioxide classes. Until June 30, 2031, a member state may cut tolls by up to 75% for low-emission trucks and buses, and from July 2031 that reduction may be kept at 50%. Zero-emission trucks and buses may be exempt from infrastructure and user charges until June 30, 2031, and may receive a reduction of up to 75% after that date. Charging itself stays optional, but a state that charges must follow the directive. The original proposal would have counted trailers in the tariff. The June deal with Parliament postponed that question and requires the European Commission to assess it by June 30, 2029.

**Links:**

- [ANSA — EU gives final approval to Eurovignette rules, with toll relief for green trucks and buses through 2031](https://www.ansa.it/canale_motori/notizie/istituzioni/2026/10/26/ok-finale-ue-alle-norme-eurobollo-zero-pedaggi-per-camion-e-bus-green_c2438539-3296-4da8-947e-625eb979d42d.html)

**Commentary:** The 75% cut and the exemption are optional ceilings for each member state, and they do not automatically lower every motorway toll in the Union.

### 4. United States: NHTSA publishes the FMVSS 108 proposal, and cyan autonomous-driving marker lamps stay optional (AV rules / US)
**Summary:** On October 26 the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration published a notice of proposed rulemaking in the Federal Register to amend Federal Motor Vehicle Safety Standard No. 108. The proposal would limit certain requirements for manual turn signals, hazard lights, and high-beam switches to vehicles that still have manual driving controls. It would also allow cyan automated-driving-system marker lamps as optional equipment. If fitted, the lamps would have to meet the proposed performance rules: steady burning while the system is driving, cyan as defined in SAE J578, at least one lamp visible from the front, each side, and the rear, a total of four to eight lamps, and a minimum mounting height of 15 inches. NHTSA said it does not have enough information to require the lamps on every ADS-equipped vehicle. Comments are due November 25, 2026.

**Links:**

- [Federal Register — Modernization of FMVSS No. 108 to accommodate ADS-equipped vehicles](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/26/2026-21794/federal-motor-vehicle-safety-standards-modernization-of-fmvss-no-108-to-accommodate-ads-equipped)

**Commentary:** Today's document is a proposal open for comment through November 25, and the cyan lamp is optional equipment that would have to follow one standard if a manufacturer installs it.

### 7. Volkswagen China: an L3 road-test permit in Hefei, with technology readiness still aimed at 2027 (autonomous driving / China)
**Summary:** Gasgoo reported on October 26 that Volkswagen Group China has received a Level 3 autonomous-driving road-test permit in Hefei. The company said the permit was granted under China's strictest current L3 framework and meets the latest mandatory safety-design standard. It can now run real-road tests and large-scale data collection in the city, and that testing has started. Volkswagen described the permit as another step toward L3 technology readiness in 2027. The in-house C7H chip, which the company says can support up to Level 4, is still in development. The first Volkswagen China Technology Day is set for October 28 in Hefei. The company did not disclose test models, routes, or fleet size.

## III. Markets and New Models

### 8. Europe: Dataforce says Chinese brands passed Japanese brands for a full quarter (market / Europe)
**Summary:** Bloomberg News reported on October 26 that, according to Dataforce, registrations of Chinese-branded cars roughly doubled from a year earlier in the three months through September and reached 12.7% of sales in the region, ahead of Japanese manufacturers including Toyota at 11.9%. That is the first full quarter in which Chinese brands outsold Japanese brands. The September share rose to a record 14.5%, almost three points above August, led by hybrids. Five years ago Chinese brands were barely 1% of European sales. Chery's Jaecoo 7 was Britain's best-selling model in March and September. The report also said Chery is preparing production in Barcelona, BYD is nearing completion of a plant in Hungary, and Leapmotor has started building the B10 at Stellantis's plant in Zaragoza. Volkswagen and Renault still lead the region.

**Links:**

- [BusinessMirror / Bloomberg — Chinese carmakers overtake Japan in Europe for the first time](https://businessmirror.com.ph/2026/10/26/chinese-carmakers-overtake-japan-in-europe-for-first-time/)

**Commentary:** The 12.7% versus 11.9% comparison is the third-quarter total, 14.5% is September alone, and the comparison is with Japanese brands while European manufacturers still hold the larger share.

## IV. Charging and Energy

### 10. India: Blue Energy and HPCL open a heavy-truck battery-swap station in Bhiwandi, with swaps in under five minutes (charging / India)
**Summary:** The Hindu BusinessLine reported on October 26 that Blue Energy Motors, with Hindustan Petroleum, has opened Bhiwandi's first battery-swap station for electric heavy trucks, extending the Mumbai-Pune electric freight corridor the company launched in October 2025. The station can swap a pack on Blue Energy's electric heavy trucks in less than five minutes, and it adds public charging for electric two-wheelers, cars, and commercial vehicles. Chief executive Anand Mimani called the site a first step toward a wider network. The company also said more than 1,400 of its green heavy trucks are running on major Indian freight routes, past 150 million green kilometers, cutting more than 44,000 tonnes of carbon dioxide, which it equates to the absorption of about 1.8 million trees.

### 11. United States: Energea commits a $20 million charging portfolio, with a first $10 million facility for ChargedEV (charging / US)
**Summary:** EV Infrastructure News reported on October 26 that Energea, until now a solar investor, launched Energea Portfolio 4 USA LP and committed $20 million to finance charging stations. The first transaction is a $10 million credit facility for charge-point operator ChargedEV, which develops and owns Tesla V4 Supercharger sites. Energea said the facility will initially draw on two operating stations in Georgia and fund ChargedEV's expansion in the southeastern United States, then take from profits once those sites are operating. The structure uses monthly payments, a fixed interest rate, seven-year amortization after an interest-only period, a first-priority claim on the station assets, and an option to buy a minority stake later. The firm is negotiating a similar structure with a second developer and intends to back more U.S. operators.
