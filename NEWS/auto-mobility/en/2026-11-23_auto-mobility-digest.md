## I. Policy & Trade

### 1. EU Draft Extends Hybrid/PHEV Import Quotas Beyond China to Japan, Turkey, UK and Korea (Policy)
**Summary:** GlobalChinaEV and Gazzetta reported on Nov 23 that a leaked draft of an EU safeguard measure would set first-year hybrid and plug-in hybrid import quotas of about 350,000 vehicles for China, 310,000 for Japan, 230,000 for Turkey, 190,000 for the United Kingdom and 105,000 for South Korea, based on 2025 import volumes and aimed at vehicles cleared from Dec 1, with caps rising about 6% a year thereafter. The European Commission has not published the text and figures may still change. Quotas would follow where a car is built, not the badge; EU-built cars would not count. WTO rules require non-discriminatory safeguards, so the probe covers all third countries; Japan, Korea, the UK and Turkey pressed for transparency at a mid-November WTO goods council.

### 2. Nine Chinese Ministries Push Recycling Build-Out: Full-Category Traction Batteries, Vehicle-Battery Scrap and Digital IDs (Policy)
**Summary:** Chinese EV industry outlets reported on Nov 23 that the Ministry of Commerce and eight other agencies recently issued opinions on deepening renewable-resource recycling, with a dedicated chapter on “new trio” solid waste: NEV traction batteries, PV modules and wind-turbine blades. The document calls for digital battery identity cards, extended producer responsibility, revised comprehensive-utilization rules, “vehicle-and-battery integrated” scrapping and dismantling, and stronger coverage of non-vehicle power, storage and consumer batteries. Industry estimates put China’s traction-battery recycling market above RMB100 billion by 2030; CATL affiliate Brunp and peers are adding capacity.

**Links:**

- [EV Industry — Nine ministries advance full-category traction-battery recycling](https://www.evchanye.com/2026/1123/57149.shtml)

**Commentary:** Before the retirement wave peaks, China is locking in traceability, entry barriers and integrated scrapping—recycling is shifting from a compliance cost to a ticket into secondary-material supply.

## II. Autonomous Driving & Robotaxis

### 3. Waymo Again Pauses Bay Area Service Ahead of Storms and Flood Risk (Autonomy)
**Summary:** The Los Angeles Times reported on Nov 23 that Waymo suspended San Francisco Bay Area robotaxi service from 4 a.m. Monday amid forecast heavy rain and coastal flooding, without saying when rides would resume. Mayor Daniel Lurie said the city had coordinated storm plans with Waymo and welcomed the pause; several Waymo vehicles stalled in last week’s storms, forcing a one-day halt. ### 4. Tesla Opens Public Cybercab Rides in Dallas, Its Second U.S. Purpose-Built Robotaxi Market (Robotaxi)

## IV. Batteries, Charging & Commercial Vehicles

### 9. Reuters: China’s Electric-Truck Boom Lifts Lithium Demand, but Prices Stay Soft on Oversupply Bets (Battery)
**Summary:** Reuters reported on Nov 23 that stronger Chinese electric-truck sales amid higher oil prices are an unexpected demand bright spot in a weak lithium market. BofA data show about 600,000 electric trucks sold in the first 10 months, up 45% and about 30% of truck sales across sizes; heavy trucks led with about 262,000 units, up 68%. In the four months after U.S.–Israeli strikes on Iran began Feb 28, Chinese heavy e-truck exports more than doubled to about 16,823 units, roughly half to South and Southeast Asia. Analysts say e-trucks may use nearly 7% of lithium supply this year and 12% by 2030, yet patchy storage demand and new supply have nearly halved prices since May highs as buyers wait.

**Links:**
