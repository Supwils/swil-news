### 2. ICCT: delaying or weakening EU car CO₂ standards could cut about 185,000 direct jobs by 2035 (Policy / Europe)
**Summary:** The International Council on Clean Transportation finds that delaying EU passenger-car CO₂ standards by about five years—or otherwise weakening them—could leave direct employment in EU car production, battery production/recycling, and charging infrastructure about 6% below 2025 levels by 2035, or up to about 185,000 jobs. Keeping current standards leaves employment broadly stable (about 2% above 2025); pairing them with industrial support for EU-based BEV and supply-chain production could raise employment about 10% (around 320,000 jobs). The study covers direct jobs only and excludes retail/aftersales and broader indirect employment, as the EU reviews its 2030/2035 targets.

**Links:**

- [ICCT — Weakening EU CO₂ standards could cost up to 185,000 jobs by 2035](https://theicct.org/pr-weakening-the-eu-co2-standards-could-cost-up-to-185000-jobs-in-europes-automotive-sector-by-2035/)
- [ICCT — Powering jobs: Employment under the EU’s passenger car CO₂ standards](https://theicct.org/publication/employment-under-the-eu-passenger-car-co2-standards-oct26/)

**Commentary:** The jobs argument is flipped: slowing electrification may destroy more employment than protecting combustion plants—another ledger for Brussels’ standards review.

### 3. U.S. DOE launches BattMan battery manufacturing accelerator to commercialize lab tech at home (Policy / US)
**Summary:** On Oct 28 the DOE Office of Critical Minerals and Energy Innovation announced the Battery Manufacturing Accelerator (BattMan), led by Pacific Northwest National Laboratory with Argonne, Oak Ridge, the National Laboratory of the Rockies, and university partners. It aims to use digital twins, process monitoring, and closed-loop control to cut cost and time from lab discovery to U.S. factory scale. DOE also released a National Strategy for American Leadership in Batteries targeting a more integrated domestic supply chain by 2030 and technology/manufacturing leadership by 2035. The push is supply-side, contrasting with the earlier end of federal vehicle purchase tax credits.

**Links:**

- [U.S. Department of Energy — DOE launches Battery Manufacturing Accelerator](https://www.energy.gov/cmei/ammto/articles/doe-launches-battery-manufacturing-accelerator-strengthen-us-competitiveness)
- [PNNL — BattMan](https://www.pnnl.gov/projects/battman)

**Commentary:** U.S. EV policy is shifting from “subsidize buyers” to “subsidize battery factories”—demand still leans on states and operators.

### 4. GM customer program: 66 U.S. EVs need a second pack swap after ineligible reman modules (Battery / US)
**Summary:** Coverage on Oct 29 followed GM Customer Satisfaction Program N262565450: some remanufactured high-voltage packs installed after prior replacements included modules ineligible for reuse, requiring another full pack swap. GM counts 66 U.S. vehicles across select Chevrolet Equinox EV, Blazer EV, Silverado EV and BrightDrop units, plus certain Cadillac and GMC HUMMER EV / Sierra EV models. Dealers will replace the pack at no charge with new or remanufactured stock depending on the vehicle and availability; the program runs through Nov 30, 2028, and work must be done by trained techs at authorized EV dealers.

### 6. California tightens public-charger reliability rules; RIDE rideshare EV incentives up to about $20,300 (Charging / Mobility / US)
**Summary:** Canary Media on Oct 29 reviewed California’s push for more reliable public charging: CEC reliability and data-sharing rules now apply to installers funded by state agencies or utilities, and CALeVIP applicants competing for about $55 million must use approved hardware/software and report hourly performance; publicly funded DCFC installed on or after Sep 28, 2025, must support OCPP 2.0.1. Separately, the CPUC’s RIDE program opens applications Nov 2 for income-qualified Uber, Lyft, and HopSkipDrive drivers with at least 4,500 trips in the past 12 months—up to about $20,300 for a new ZEV or about $14,200 for a used one, plus up to about $1,170 a year in charging support for up to four years.

**Links:**

### 10. Spiro Uganda plans to nearly triple mega battery-swap hubs to about 11 for e-motorcycle riders (Battery swap / Africa)
**Summary:** TechTrends Uganda reported on Oct 29 that electric-motorcycle operator Spiro Uganda plans to expand mega battery-swapping hubs from four to about 11 by December 2026, with each hub able to charge at least 200 batteries at once, on top of more than 360 swap stations nationwide. Existing mega hubs are in Mukono, Soroti, Jinja, and Bukoto in Kampala. A Ministry of Energy official urged a regulatory framework covering safety, metering, tariffs, billing, and licensing for e-mobility infrastructure. The buildout aims to cut wait times for commercial riders.
