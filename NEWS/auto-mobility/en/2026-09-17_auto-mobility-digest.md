## I. Policy & Markets

### 1. CPCA: China September passenger NEV retail seen at about 1.11 million; share may hit 65.7% (Market / China)
**Summary:** On September 17, the China Passenger Car Association (CPCA) said “Golden September” volume remains soft: narrow passenger-car retail is forecast at about 1.69 million units, up about 9.7% month on month but down about 24.6% year on year on a high 2.241 million base. Passenger NEV retail is expected at about 1.11 million, up about 10.5% from August, with retail penetration around 65.7%—a potential record—while still down about 14% year on year. Trade-in and local subsidies continue to provide a floor, but leading makers’ September retail targets are up less than 10% month on month, weaker than a typical quarter-end push.

### 2. Europe August BEV share hits 30.5% as registrations jump about 54% (Market / Europe)
**Summary:** Reuters and others reported on September 17 that across 16 key European markets, August battery-electric registrations reached about 202,833 units, up about 54.2% year on year, for a roughly 30.5% share (EU estimate about 31.2%), citing E-Mobility Europe, New Automotive and partners. Year-to-date BEV registrations totaled about 1.67 million, up about 33.1%, with a 22.5% share—ahead of some full-year forecasts near 21%–23%. France’s August share was about 38.3% and Germany’s about 32.5%; Norway about 98.7% and Denmark about 85.9%.

### 3. BYD adviser: Europe long term needs 3 assembly plants plus 1 battery plant; second site due this year (Expansion / Europe)
**Summary:** CnEVPost reported on September 17, citing Bloomberg and Reuters, that BYD Europe adviser Alfredo Altavilla said the company will ultimately need three vehicle assembly plants and one battery factory in Europe to hit sales targets and meet EU localization rules. The Hungary plant is starting production; a second assembly site is expected to be chosen by year-end, preferably by acquiring and refurbishing an existing factory, with Spain and France preferred and Italy a backup. EU BEV tariffs, possible “Made in Europe” rules, and hybrid-import pressure reinforce local manufacturing. Overseas sales rose about 86% year on year to about 1.162 million in the first eight months; August overseas volume hit a record about 189,466.

**Links:**

- [CnEVPost — BYD needs 3 assembly plants and 1 battery factory for long-term European expansion](https://cnevpost.com/2026/09/17/byd-needs-3-assembly-plants-1-battery-factory-long-term-europe/)

**Commentary:** Tariffs are rewriting “export cars” into “build locally”—BYD’s Europe story is shifting from share to plant maps.

## II. Autonomous Driving & Mobility

### 4. Lucid and Bolt partner to deploy about 25,000 Level 4 vehicles in Europe (Autonomy / Europe)
**Summary:** Reuters and TechCrunch reported on September 17 that U.S. EV maker Lucid and European mobility platform Bolt plan to deploy about 25,000 vehicles based on Lucid’s upcoming Midsize platform, aimed at SAE Level 4 and expected to use Nvidia’s Hyperion architecture, across major European cities. Coverage also notes no disclosed vehicle order, funding, or deployment timeline, and that the Midsize program was previously delayed; Lucid already has a separate U.S. robotaxi effort with Uber and Nuro.

### 5. Waymo resumes San Antonio rides after flood-detection software and ops updates (Autonomy / U.S.)
**Summary:** Local media reported on September 17 that Waymo passenger service is again available in San Antonio, including airport trips. The company said it completed software and operational changes to better detect and avoid floodwaters, including location-specific limits in high-risk zones and adjusted weather alerts and operating triggers. Service had been paused for about five months after an April incident in which an empty vehicle entered a flooded lane; Waymo had also filed a voluntary software recall related to flooded high-speed lanes. The company said the updates were validated in other cities that ran through the summer.

**Links:**

- [News 4 San Antonio — Waymo rides resume in San Antonio after floodwater safety updates](https://news4sanantonio.com/news/local/waymo-rides-resume-in-san-antonio-after-floodwater-safety-updates)

**Commentary:** Commercial autonomy’s next gate is not only “can it drive,” but whether it can keep running through extreme weather.
