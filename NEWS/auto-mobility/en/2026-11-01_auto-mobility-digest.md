### 3. UK law locks in 20% VAT on public and workplace EV charging from Nov 1 (Policy / UK)
**Summary:** HM Treasury’s Budget 2026 and updated HMRC VAT guidance state that from Nov 1, 2026, electricity supplied through publicly accessible and workplace EV charge points is standard-rated at 20% VAT and is not treated as “domestic use.” The legislation confirms HMRC’s position after a tribunal suggested some public charging might qualify for the 5% reduced rate in the Charge My Street case. Home charging keeps domestic treatment. The government also announced a “tariff-porting” taskforce to study letting drivers use residential tariffs at local lower-powered public chargers, plus about £100 million over two years for cost-reduction support (expected from spring 2027).

**Links:**

- [ChargerFeed — UK writes 20% VAT on public and workplace EV charging into law from Nov. 1](https://chargerfeed.com/article/uk-20-percent-vat-public-workplace-ev-charging-law-tariff-taskforce-november-2026)
- [GOV.UK — VAT and CCL on EV charging deemed not for domestic use](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/vat-and-climate-change-levy-on-electric-vehicle-charging-deemed-not-to-be-for-domestic-use-public-ev-charging)

**Commentary:** The tax gap between public and home charging is now statute—drivers without driveways get a taskforce and future funding, not an immediate price fix.

## II. Autonomous Driving & Mobility

### 4. London mayor calls for a robotaxi cap and more TfL powers over permits and fleet size (Policy / UK)
**Summary:** The Guardian reported on Nov 1 that Mayor Sadiq Khan is calling for a cap on robotaxis in London and lobbying the UK government to give Transport for London stronger oversight. He cited air quality, congestion and livelihoods for private-hire and black-cab drivers, saying London “isn’t San Francisco.” The comments come as ministers prepare rules for driverless services; Uber/Wayve already offer limited supervised rides, and Freenow by Lyft with Baidu Apollo Go is testing about 24 vehicles in Brent with hopes of public driverless rides as early as next year. The London Assembly Transport Committee has also urged TfL authority to issue/withdraw Automated Passenger Service permits and set fleet limits under 2027 rules.

**Links:**

### 6. Italy’s Salvini backs swift EU approval of Tesla Assisted Driving; national green light still pending (Policy / EU)
**Summary:** After a call with Elon Musk on Oct 29, Italian Deputy Prime Minister and Transport Minister Matteo Salvini said Italy “supports a swift EU approval” of Tesla Assisted Driving (the European name for FSD Supervised). The feature remains SAE Level 2 with a driver ready to take over. Dutch authority RDW granted provisional type approval in April 2026; Italy’s national recognition request is still under review, so Salvini’s remarks are political support, not a domestic go-live. The EU Technical Committee on Motor Vehicles did not vote in October, and a December vote is not assured. EU-wide approval needs at least 15 member states representing at least about 65% of the EU population.

## III. Batteries & Charging

### 7. China exempts qualifying sodium-ion batteries from consumption tax through 2028 as Hina plans ~10 GWh in Wuhan (Batteries / China)
**Summary:** CarNewsChina reported on Nov 1 that under MOF Announcement No. 20 (2026), specified lithium-ion batteries face a 2% consumption tax from Sept 1, 2026 (rising to 4% from Sept 1, 2027), while qualifying sodium-ion, solid-state and fuel-cell batteries are exempt through Dec 31, 2028. Hina Battery signed with Wuhan’s Jiangxia District on Oct 14 for a planned ~10 GWh sodium-ion plant (phase one ~3 GWh; construction aimed for early 2027), and is partnering with Chengli Special Automobile on sanitation EVs. A FAW Jiefang J6P tractor with a ~339 kWh Hina pack was reported to keep more than 90% usable capacity at about −40°C; Hina lists energy density of at least ~145 Wh/kg. Falling lithium prices have narrowed sodium-ion’s cost edge, so the tax gap is a temporary policy wedge.

**Links:**

- [CarNewsChina — China exempts sodium-ion batteries from tax as Hina targets 10 GWh](https://carnewschina.com/2026/11/01/china-exempts-sodium-ion-batteries-from-tax-as-hina-targets-10-gwh-commercial-ev-push/)

**Commentary:** Policy is putting sodium-ion in the saddle; commercial proof still has to beat cheaper LFP in cold-weather fleet duty cycles.
