## I. EVs & Major Automakers

### 1. Volkswagen pauses Golf EV development: Wolfsburg ~€300m short of cost goals; 2030 launch still targeted (OEM / Europe)
**Summary:** After VW Group posted an about €5.9 billion third-quarter operating loss—its worst quarter since the 2015 Dieselgate scandal—brand CEO Thomas Schäfer said in an internal memo reviewed by Reuters that Wolfsburg cannot afford to keep developing the all-electric Golf right now; the plant is about €300 million (~$334.5 million) short of its cost-cutting targets. The Golf EV had been aimed at around 2028 and was already delayed toward 2030; the works council said the pause should not change the 2030 launch timing. Gasoline Golf production is slated to move to Mexico in 2027, while electric Golf assembly is still planned for Wolfsburg near the end of the decade.

## II. Autonomous Driving & Mobility

### 5. Toyota × Waymo: bZ4X robotaxi validation in Japan from 2027, with later right-hand-drive market plans (AV)
**Summary:** On Oct 30 Toyota and Waymo announced the next phase of their AV collaboration: building on an April 2025 preliminary agreement, they will begin testing and validating an autonomous Toyota bZ4X fleet in Japan in 2027, with deployment to the UK and other Waymo markets planned in following years; vehicles will be assembled and fitted with Waymo hardware under one roof at Toyota’s Takaoka plant in Aichi. Waymo’s co-CEO said the focus starts with right-hand-drive markets such as Japan and the UK; Toyota said the initial fleet will be small, and Waymo still plans a 2027 Japan pilot with Jaguar I-PACE units. The partners will also explore Waymo tech for personally owned Toyotas and, with Woven by Toyota, how real-world data can improve safety for other road users.

### 6. Shanghai draft taxi ordinance seeks orderly, lawful autonomous taxi deployment (Policy / China)
**Summary:** Shanghai’s draft Taxi Ordinance has opened for public comment, calling for the orderly, lawful and safe use of autonomous driving in the taxi industry under guidance from transport and public-security authorities, while encouraging big data, blockchain, AI and new-energy equipment for greener operations. The draft brings cruise taxis and ride-hailing under one framework and keeps fleet-size controls, operating-right allocation and real-time data feeds into the regulator’s platform. Analysts say the bill aims to move robotaxi service from special pilots toward a normal legal and multi-agency oversight footing.
