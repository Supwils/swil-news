### 1. CPCA: China passenger NEV retail 596,000 in Sept 1–20, down 9% YoY; penetration 67.9% (Market / China)
**Summary:** CPCA data released September 23 show passenger NEV retail sales of 596,000 units from September 1–20, down 9% year on year but up 14% from the same period in August; year-to-date retail reached 7.27 million, down 12% YoY. Wholesale NEV volumes were 740,000 over the same window (+8% YoY, +25% vs. August’s comparable period), with YTD wholesale at about 10.518 million (+9% YoY). NEV retail and wholesale penetration hit 67.9% and 73.9%, respectively. Broader passenger-car retail was 878,000 (−22% YoY). Pure-ICE light-vehicle output in the first three weeks of September was about 330,000 (−52% YoY). CPCA said this year’s “Golden September” has become a zero-sum fight with gains concentrated in NEVs, and expects September NEV retail up about 10.5% month on month with share around 65.7%.

### 2. Reuters: Middle East oil shock recharges EVs—U.S. slumps while Europe and “rest of world” surge (Market / Global)
**Summary:** A Reuters analysis published September 23 argues the Iran-war oil shock is reigniting EV demand via high gasoline and diesel prices. Benchmark Mineral Intelligence puts global NEV sales growth at only about 4% YoY for January–August, masking sharp regional splits: U.S. sales fell about 33% YoY in August and about 21% YTD after federal subsidies ended; China NEV sales were down about 12% over the same span, but against a weaker overall car market, with NEV penetration hitting about 65% in August. Europe jumped about 36% YoY in August (about +29% YTD), while EV sales outside the big three markets roughly doubled. Wood Mackenzie says BEVs have already reached TCO parity with ICE in China and models an “electric shock” high-oil scenario in which a structural shift could arrive faster if conflicts persist.

## III. Batteries & Charging

### 6. GM begins delivering EVs whose cells use fully recycled cathode active material (Battery / United States)
**Summary:** Just Auto reported on September 23 that General Motors has begun delivering EVs built with battery cells whose cathode active material (CAM) is made entirely from recycled nickel, cobalt and manganese. The closed-loop trial with Cirba Solutions routed retired GM packs to an Ohio plant for black-mass recovery, then processed CAM that met virgin-material performance benchmarks. Ultium Cells (GM–LG Energy Solution JV) produced the cells; GM assembled modules and packs at Factory Zero and Spring Hill before customer delivery. GM cited industry estimates that nearly 14 million EV batteries worldwide could reach end of life by 2040, calling the pilot proof that battery value can extend beyond first life.

**Links:**

- [Just Auto — GM rolls out EVs using fully recycled battery materials](https://www.just-auto.com/news/gm-evs-recycled-battery-materials/)

**Commentary:** Closed-loop recycling is moving from lab demos into cars that customers can actually take home.

### 7. Geely unveils fifth-gen Smart Charging at up to ~2,250 kW; claims 10%–70% in ~4 min 30 sec (Charging)
**Summary:** Geely Auto Group on September 23 introduced Geely Smart Charging and a fifth-generation station peaking at about 2,250 kW, powered by the Xingrui PowerMind energy AI co-developed with StepFun. The system aims to predict cell temperatures about 30 seconds ahead and keep average charge temperature below ~55°C (peak below ~65°C). Geely said real-world tests on batteries used in the Lynk & Co 10 and Zeekr 001 charged 10%–70% in 4 minutes 30 seconds and 10%–97% in 8 minutes 40 seconds; a next-gen ultra-short blade battery raises peak charge rate to about 6C. The Next Web noted that under Europe’s AFIR, a core-network charging pool must provide about 600 kW total by 2027—far below one Geely connector’s peak.

**Summary:** BYD announced on September 23 that its 2,000th highway flash-charging station will open September 24 at the Baoying service area in Yangzhou, Jiangsu—about three months ahead of the year-end target set on March 5, ahead of Mid-Autumn and National Day travel. By end-August the network had delivered about 210 million kWh and served more than 1.83 million users, nearly one-third from other brands. First UK sites are live, with about 300 planned this year.
