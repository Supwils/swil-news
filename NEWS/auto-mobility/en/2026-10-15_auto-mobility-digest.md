### 4. CNBC: the U.S. 2031 fleet fuel-economy target is now 34.9 mpg, and an analyst sees compliance costs falling by hundreds of millions of dollars (Policy / United States)
**Summary:** CNBC reported on October 15 that lower U.S. fuel-economy standards and the end of fines for missing them could give automakers a financial tailwind running into the billions of dollars over several years. In late September, NHTSA cut the 2031 light-duty fleet target to 34.9 miles per gallon, from the 50.4 miles per gallon the Biden administration finalized in 2024. Congress voted last year to eliminate federal fines for fuel-economy violations. TD Cowen analyst Itay Michaeli said costs from fines and credits have already come down this year and could add a few to several hundred million dollars of earnings. He said automakers can also sell fewer "compliance cars" and more high-margin pickups and larger engines. GM said in September it will bring a new generation of small-block V-8s to the Silverado and Sierra, and Ford has increased V-8 availability on the F-150. Analyst John Murphy expects hybrids to rise from 14% of the market last year to about 34% by the end of the decade; some months have already been about 19%. Of roughly 300 million vehicles on U.S. roads, the Alliance for Automotive Innovation estimates about 7.5 million are electric.

**Links:**

- [CNBC — Lower fuel-economy targets could boost pickups and V-8 engines](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/15/cafe-fuel-economy-v8-trucks.html)

**Commentary:** The 34.9 mpg target is a rule from late September; today's piece is about how it changes model investment, not a new regulation.

### 5. Volvo expands with Nvidia: the next car uses Hyperion, Alpamayo, and Halos, and the company describes a gradual move to eyes-off and mind-off driving (Autonomous driving / Europe)
**Summary:** On October 15 Volvo Cars said it is expanding a partnership with Nvidia that began in 2017, combining Volvo's safety engineering with the Hyperion sensor and compute architecture, Halos OS, and the Alpamayo Stack driving software so future cars can drive door to door, from highways and city streets through parking. The technology will debut in a next-generation Volvo as part of the company's recently announced largest-ever product push, with a gradual introduction of eyes-off and mind-off driving. Chief executive Håkan Samuelsson and Nvidia chief executive Jensen Huang both framed the deal as safety paired with artificial intelligence. Volvo did not disclose the contract value or the first model. The Verge, quoting software chief Alwin Bakkenes the same day, described the point-to-point system as Level 2++, able to handle traffic lights, stop signs, and unprotected left turns, planned before 2030, and without lidar for now. TechCrunch quoted him saying the first vehicle will debut within the decade.

### 6. Minneapolis City Council's 7-5 vote fails to override the mayor, so robotaxis are not forced to carry a safety monitor (Autonomous driving / United States)
**Summary:** Minnesota Reformer reported on October 15 that the Minneapolis City Council voted 7-5 on Thursday to override Mayor Jacob Frey's veto and fell short of the nine votes required. The Workers Drive Minneapolis ordinance passed 7-6 on October 1, and Frey vetoed it the next day. It would have required commercial autonomous operators, including Waymo, to hold a city license and keep a safety monitor in the driver's seat with immediate access to steering, braking, and acceleration, paid at minimum rates. Frey said in a statement, "Requiring a driver in a driverless car doesn't make sense." Lead author Council Member Robin Wonsley said after the vote that she would introduce another proposal to regulate commercial autonomous vehicles. The report puts the state's rideshare driver count at about 10,000. Waymo says it now provides more than 500,000 fully autonomous rides a week in 15 major U.S. cities, none of which require a human monitor, and the timing of commercial service in Minnesota is unclear. The St. Paul City Council voted Wednesday to direct staff to study autonomous vehicles and suggest rules that protect workers and the public.

### 9. Arizona opens the first 3 of up to 79 federally funded highway fast chargers (Charging / United States)
**Summary:** 12News reported on October 15 that Arizona has opened the first three of up to 79 planned fast-charging stations, in Tucson on Interstate 10, Green Valley on Interstate 19, and Wellton on Interstate 8. The program is backed by a $76.5 million federal grant. The state transportation department has opened bidding for 49 more sites along Interstates 10 and 40 and selected U.S. and state routes. State and federal rules place stations no more than 90 miles apart and within three miles of a highway. Most sites will offer both the North American Charging Standard and CCS, with a design target of a full charge in 30 minutes or less. The stations are privately owned. Operators cover 20% of construction and operating costs, and federal funds cover the rest. Proposals for the next round are due in January 2027. Construction on those later sites is scheduled to begin in 2028 and is expected to take about a year.

### 11. Washington, D.C., opens multifamily charging rebates of up to 80% for public plugs, funded with $1,000,500 (Charging / United States)
**Summary:** The District Department of Energy and Environment said on October 15 that it has launched a Multifamily Electric Vehicle Charging Assistance Program with Catalyst Mobility, offering free technical assistance and rebates for buying, installing, and maintaining chargers at or near multifamily buildings. The program uses $1,000,500 from the Volkswagen settlement fund. Mayor Bowser had assigned the department $8.125 million of those mitigation funds. Rebates cover up to 80% of project costs for publicly accessible charging and up to 60% for charging available only to residents, for Level 1 and Level 2 equipment. Applications opened on September 17, selection is expected by December 2026, and the completion deadline is October 31, 2027. Eligible applicants include multifamily owners, installers authorized by an owner, site hosts within a quarter-mile of a multifamily building, and charging companies authorized to install curbside charging.

**Links:**

- [DOEE — District launches the Multifamily Electric Vehicle Charging Assistance Program](https://doee.dc.gov/release/district-launches-multifamily-electric-vehicle-charging-assistance-program)

**Commentary:** October 15 is the official launch announcement; applications have been open since September 17, and $1,000,500 is this program's pot, not the full $8.125 million.
