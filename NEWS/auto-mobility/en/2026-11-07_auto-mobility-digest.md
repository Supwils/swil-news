## I. Policy & Markets

### 1. 14 EU lawmakers urge cybersecurity review of BYD, including possible public-procurement bans (Policy / EU)
**Summary:** Reuters and follow-on reports on Nov 4–5 said 14 members of the European Parliament, led by French MEP François Kalfon, submitted written questions asking the European Commission to have the EU cybersecurity agency assess whether BYD connected cars pose a security threat, consider listing BYD as a high-risk supplier, exclude it from public procurement, and restrict Chinese vehicles at strategic dual-use infrastructure. The filing cited China’s 2017 national intelligence law and the recent U.S. military ban on BYD vehicles at Kadena Air Base in Okinawa. BYD said data in the cloud or in vehicles cannot be remotely accessed from China, that customers control their data, and that BYD Europe does not share such data with China-based affiliates. The Commission typically has about six weeks to reply.

### 2. MIIT seeks comment on “digital ID” rules for EV power batteries using 24-digit codes (Policy / Batteries)
**Summary:** On Nov 5, China’s Ministry of Industry and Information Technology opened public consultation on interim rules for new-energy vehicle power-battery digital IDs. The draft uses the national-standard 24-digit battery code as a unique index linking design, production, sales, vehicle fitment, maintenance, dismantling, recycling, and reuse. It would apply to battery makers and sellers, vehicle and battery-swap operators, scrap and recycling firms, and related platforms, aiming to strengthen full-chain supervision under existing recycling rules. Feedback is due around Dec 6; the rules are proposed to take effect in 2027 on a date still to be set.

- [Li-Battery China — MIIT: power batteries to get digital IDs](https://www.libattery.net/news/details3254.html)

**Commentary:** Turning batteries into lifelong traceable assets matters more for recycling and second life than any slogan about circularity.

## III. Automakers, Products & Safety

### 7. GM recalls about 67,380 Cadillac and Chevrolet EVs over low-speed brake software defect (Recall / US)
**Summary:** Per NHTSA filings and coverage by Electrek and Motor1, General Motors is recalling about 67,380 EVs because brake-system control-module software can cause a temporary loss of braking at low speed. Affected models include certain 2023–2027 Cadillac Lyriq, 2025–2026 Celestiq, 2026–2027 Vistiq, and 2024–2027 Chevrolet Blazer EV units (Lyriq/Blazer EV mostly performance AWD variants). The issue can appear when lightly braking in a sharp low-speed turn over a bump or driveway lip; normal braking usually returns on the next press. GM counted about 24 complaints, 20 minor accidents, and two alleged minor injuries. The free remedy is an OTA or dealer software update; owner letters are expected around Dec 14.

**Links:**

- [Electrek — GM recalls 67,000 Cadillac, Chevy EVs over brake software defect](https://electrek.co/2026/11/04/gm-recalls-67000-cadillac-chevy-evs-over-brake-software-defect/)
- [Motor1 — GM Recalls 67,380 EVs Over Temporary Brake Loss](https://www.motor1.com/news/814525/gm-recalls-blazer-cadillac-brakes/)

**Commentary:** Drive-by-wire software faults are reproducible and patchable by OTA—but brand trust still takes a hit comparable to mechanical recalls.

## IV. Charging & Supply Chain

### 11. U.S. public DC fast charging tops about 80,930 ports, up ~25% year over year; Tesla still ~half (Charging / US)
**Summary:** EVChargingStations.com, citing Alternative Fuels Data Center data as of Nov 1, 2026, counted about 80,930 public DC fast-charging ports nationwide, up roughly 25% year over year. Net additions were about 2,485 versus the prior month—the largest monthly change in two years—with deployment above about 2,000 stalls per month over the past two months. Tesla Superchargers account for about 40,504 ports (~50% share), followed by Electrify America (~6,081), EVgo (~5,414), and ChargePoint (~5,002). Average ports per location rose to about 4.92; about 1.7% of ports were temporarily offline.

### 12. Japan’s Tokai Kogyo acquires Kinugawa Rubber, lifting seal ranking to about No. 4 worldwide (M&A / Japan)
**Summary:** Just Auto reported on Nov 5 that Toyota-affiliated Tokai Kogyo Holdings completed on Nov 2 the acquisition of 100% of voting shares in Nissan-affiliated Kinugawa Rubber Industrial Holdings from financial investors including the Development Bank of Japan; the price was undisclosed. The deal lifts Tokai Kogyo to roughly the world’s fourth-largest supplier of vehicle-body rubber seals such as weatherstrips, from about eighth. Coverage framed the move as a break from traditional keiretsu walls, seeking scale in purchasing, R&D, and production to meet Chinese competition and cut overlapping capacity, including possible plant consolidations abroad.

**Links:**
