## I. Electric vehicles, markets, and policy

### 1. China and the EU reach a shared understanding on hybrid exports; the "more than half" cut is against a four-year no-change projection (Policy / China and the EU)
**Summary:** Wen Wei Po reported on October 10, citing China's Ministry of Commerce, that Commerce Minister Wang Wentao and EU Trade and Economic Security Commissioner Maros Sefcovic held the second session of their trade and investment consultation mechanism in Beijing on October 8-9 and produced 16 outcomes covering trade and investment balance, export controls, intellectual property, and WTO reform. The list says the two sides reached an understanding on hybrid-vehicle trade in a manner consistent with WTO rules, and that company price-undertaking and review procedures in the electric-vehicle anti-subsidy case will continue. 2eu.brussels quoted Sefcovic's estimate that the arrangement for hybrids and plug-in hybrids could cut projected exports by more than half over four years compared with a no-policy-change scenario, a difference of several million vehicles, while the mechanism has not been published. The same report says this is not a 50% drop from current or 2025 sales, and that existing countervailing duties on battery-electric vehicles were not removed. The EU side also discussed lower most-favored-nation tariffs on seven lines, including auto parts, olive oil, and footwear, covering about 4 billion euros of current EU exports to China and at least 225 million euros of duty savings if implemented; the joint statement is more cautious and says the sides will keep exploring tariff cuts. Rare-earth and permanent-magnet licences would use the existing fast track, and export controls themselves were not abolished. Wen Wei Po quoted Renmin University researcher Liu Ying as saying the EU had earlier wanted a voluntary export restraint to cut China's hybrid share in Europe from about one-third to 15%, which China opposed. The sides scheduled a ministerial video call in January 2027 and a third session in March 2027.

### 3. William Li: battery-electric cars were 42.8% of the auto market in August, and assisted driving is on more than 950,000 vehicles (Market / China)
**Summary:** Economic Information Daily published an interview on October 10 with Nio founder, chairman, and CEO William Li. Citing the China Association of Automobile Manufacturers, he said new-energy vehicles were 60.6% of new-car sales in August, battery-electric cars were 70.7% of new-energy sales and 42.8% of the whole auto market, and that battery-electric penetration of the overall market was about 30% a year earlier. He said China's passenger-car parc has reached 370 million and that the industry should prepare for a clear year-on-year drop in domestic retail for the full year. On reported results, Nio's adjusted operating profit was 1.25 billion yuan in last year's fourth quarter and 70 million yuan in this year's first quarter. Second-quarter deliveries were 60,945 for the Nio brand, 29,124 for Onvo, and 17,589 for Firefly. He expects per-vehicle cost in the second half to be about 16,000 to 17,000 yuan higher than at the end of last year. Cumulative research and development over 11 years exceeds 68.8 billion yuan. Non-vehicle business topped 10 billion yuan last year. Assisted-driving subscriptions cost 380 yuan a month, and the take-up rate is close to 20%. He said the assisted-driving system, built on a world model and closed-loop reinforcement learning, is deployed on more than 950,000 vehicles, with more than 7.7 billion km and 98 million hours of safe driving, and that it avoided or reduced the impact of 8.35 million potential incidents. The interview also recaps the September 28 charging-and-swapping deal with Geely: Geely takes 30% of Nio Energy with 100% of Yiyi Interconnection plus 640 million yuan in cash, and Nio takes 10% of Haohan Energy. He still cites a 2030 target of 10,000 swap stations, about 20 GWh of storage, and 10 billion kWh a year.

## IV. Supply chain

### 8. Thailand's industry federation: eastern auto production is preparing to restart on October 14, and output is not yet at full capacity (Supply chain / Thailand)
**Summary:** The Bangkok Post reported on October 10 that the Federation of Thai Industries said flood-hit auto production in the east is preparing to resume from October 14. Surapong Paisitpattana, advisor and spokesman for the federation's Automotive Industry Club, said many parts makers can produce and supply again, while many plants are still cleaning machinery, restoring electrical systems, and bringing workers back, so output has not returned to full capacity. Flooding in Chon Buri in late September stopped many tier-two and tier-three suppliers. Most of the affected plants are in Amata City Chonburi, and the water has receded. The disruption also reached factories outside the flooded area because transport was interrupted. Toyota, Isuzu, Ford, Nissan, and Honda had parts shortages and production delays. Surapong said battery-electric vehicle production was largely unaffected and that the impact fell mainly on internal-combustion components; an ICE vehicle has more than 30,000 parts, and a missing one stops delivery. The total value of flood damage is still being assessed. The industry is confident the disruption will not significantly affect Thailand's 2026 production target of 1.5 million vehicles, including 950,000 for export and 550,000 for domestic sales. Amata chairman Vikrom Kromadit said relief includes a one-year waiver or 50% cut in estate maintenance fees and a 50% discount on utility charges, including tap water, from this month. Toyota Motor Thailand's October 9 statement still projects Samrong to resume on October 14, Gateway on October 16, and Ban Pho on October 19, subject to flooding and parts deliveries.
