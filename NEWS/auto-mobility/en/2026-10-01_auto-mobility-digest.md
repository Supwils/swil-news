## I. Market and Automakers

### 1. BYD September NEV sales hit 463,561, a 2026 high, while the first nine months are still down (Sales / China)
**Summary:** BYD voluntarily reported September production and sales to the Hong Kong exchange on October 1. New-energy vehicle sales were 463,561 units, up 16.98% from 396,270 a year earlier and the highest month of 2026, and up 5.28% from August. Production was 463,864. Passenger-vehicle sales were 456,713, including a record 273,143 battery-electric cars, up 33.21%, and 183,570 plug-in hybrids, down 2.36%. CnEVPost, citing company data, said overseas sales were 180,700, up 153.59% year over year and down 4.63% from August, about 38.98% of the month's NEV sales. Overseas volume for the first nine months was 1,342,960, up 92.66%, while total NEV sales of 3,131,576 were still down 3.94%.

### 4. Tesla September registrations rise across Europe, including 128.3% in Portugal (Sales / Europe)

## II. Policy and Road Charges

### 7. The U.S. fuel-economy final rule lowers the 2031 fleet target to just under 35 mpg (Policy / United States)
**Summary:** NHTSA's final rule recalibrating Corporate Average Fuel Economy standards, published in the Federal Register on September 30 and effective November 30, amends standards for passenger cars and light trucks for model years 2022 through 2031. USA Today reported on October 1 that a September 28 Transportation Department release lowers the required fleetwide average by 2031 from more than 50 miles per gallon to just under 35. Officials said the change would make new cars cheaper by about $1,300 on average. The agency's own analysis showed drivers could spend an extra $1,624 on fuel over the life of a car bought under the new rules. The paper, citing AAA, said the U.S. average gasoline price was $4.43 a gallon on September 30. The Alliance for Automotive Innovation called the rule an appropriate course correction aligned with the law and current market conditions.

**Links:**

- [USA Today — Trump's MPG rules may cost drivers more on gas than they save on cars](https://www.usatoday.com/story/cars/shopping/2026/10/01/trump-fuel-economy-rollback-costs/92027702007/)

### 8. Nova Scotia's EV and plug-in hybrid road levy takes effect on October 1 (Policy / Canada)
**Summary:** The Nova Scotia government levy starts on October 1 for electric and plug-in hybrid passenger vehicles, light trucks, and vans registered under 5,001 kilograms, charged when a vehicle permit is issued or renewed. Battery-electric vehicles pay CA$500 every two years and plug-in hybrids pay CA$250. Conventional hybrids that cannot be plugged in are exempt, as are municipal, fire, and police vehicles. A September 25 provincial release said Nova Scotia is the fourth Canadian province with a dedicated EV charge: Saskatchewan charges CA$300 a year, Alberta has charged CA$200 a year since 2025, and Quebec plans a CA$125 annual charge from January 2027. electrive said the Electric Vehicle Association of Atlantic Canada called the levy short-sighted and irresponsible.

**Links:**

- [Nova Scotia — Electric and plug-in hybrid vehicle levy](https://novascotia.ca/ev-hybrid-levy/)
- [electrive — Nova Scotia introduces new levy on EVs and plug-in hybrids](https://www.electrive.com/2026/10/01/nova-scotia-introduces-new-levy-on-evs-and-plug-in-hybrids/)

**Commentary:** Federal purchase incentives are still in place, while a provincial road charge starts today, so the buying subsidy and the cost of use are now separate ledgers.

## IV. Ride-hailing Platforms

### 12. California settles driver-classification claims with Lyft for $272.5 million, without changing current classification (Ride-hailing / United States)
**Summary:** The California Labor Commissioner's Office announced on October 1, with state and local enforcement partners, the largest wage-and-hour settlement in state history over allegations that Lyft misclassified drivers as independent contractors. Lyft will pay $272.5 million plus accrued interest, and 87% goes directly to drivers. The case covers April 6, 2016, through December 15, 2020, including alleged failures on minimum wage, overtime, rest-break premiums, and expense reimbursement. The office is forgoing $5.45 million in penalties payable to the state so that more than 1,600 drivers who filed wage claims get additional funds. Voters approved Proposition 22 in November 2020, creating a separate classification for qualifying app-based drivers, so the settlement does not require Lyft to reclassify drivers going forward and does not cover later work.

**Links:**

- [California DIR — Record-setting $272.5 million settlement with Lyft](https://www.dir.ca.gov/DIRNews/2026/2026-78.html)

**Commentary:** The settlement closes the books from before Proposition 22. It does not rewrite how the platform classifies drivers now.

### 13. Philippine regulators summon 19 ride-hailing firms over an alleged failure to apply a fare increase (Ride-hailing / Southeast Asia)
**Summary:** Philstar reported on October 1 that the Land Transportation Franchising and Regulatory Board issued show-cause orders to 19 transport network companies after drivers complained that the platforms were not fully implementing an approved fare increase. The companies must explain in writing within three working days why their accreditation and authority to operate should not be suspended or canceled. A hearing is set for October 7. The board did not name the companies. Under the new fares, a 20-peso increase in the base fare sets sedans at 65 pesos, AUVs and SUVs at 75, hatchbacks at 55, and premium vehicles at 165, with a pickup charge of 15 pesos per kilometer. Chairman Greg G. Pua Jr. said drivers pay for fuel, so a fare that is not implemented hits them rather than the platforms.

**Links:**

- [Philstar — 19 ride-hailing firms summoned over alleged failure to implement fare hike](https://www.philstar.com/headlines/2026/10/01/2560268/19-ride-hailing-firms-summoned-over-alleged-failure-implement-fare-hike)

**Commentary:** The regulator approved a higher fare, while the apps may still be charging the old one, so driver fuel costs and platform commissions are out of line.
