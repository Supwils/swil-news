## I. Autonomous Driving & Mobility Platforms

### 1. Tesla Robotaxi lands in San Antonio, competing city-by-city with Waymo (Robotaxi / US)
**Summary:** TechCrunch and Axios reported on Nov 9 that Tesla said its robotaxi service would open in San Antonio, Texas, from about 5 a.m. local time Monday. The service area covers downtown and nearby neighborhoods and extends northwest toward The Shops at La Cantera and Six Flags Fiesta Texas; airport trips are not yet authorized. Tesla did not publish fleet size, operating hours, or whether safety monitors will ride along; prior launches typically start with a small fleet and a limited geofence. Robotaxi Tracker data cited locally suggest as little as about one registered vehicle, versus Waymo’s roughly 100-vehicle San Antonio fleet with coverage north to San Antonio International Airport. Tesla already runs limited robotaxi service in Austin, Dallas, Fort Worth, Houston, Miami, and Orlando, putting it in direct overlap with Waymo across multiple Texas cities.

### 2. NHTSA opens recall-adequacy probe after Waymo school-bus pass-bys persist (Safety / US)
**Summary:** TechCrunch reported on Nov 9 that the U.S. National Highway Traffic Safety Administration said in a Nov 6 filing that Waymo completed a November 17, 2025 software recall meant to stop illegal pass-bys of stopped school buses with flashing lights, yet a majority of later reported incidents occurred after that update. NHTSA’s Office of Defects Investigation has opened a “recall query” to assess whether the remedy was adequate. The issue dates to October 2025 footage from Atlanta and later Austin reports—including at least five incidents cited by Austin Independent School District—of robotaxis maneuvering around buses unloading children. Waymo said it has significantly improved school-bus behavior, has received no related citations since March 2026, and will respond to NHTSA’s query.

**Links:**

- [TechCrunch — Waymo robotaxis kept illegally passing school buses after software fix, feds say](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/09/waymo-robotaxis-kept-illegally-passing-school-buses-after-software-fix-feds-say/)

**Commentary:** The next test for AV software recalls is not whether a patch shipped, but whether the real world stops repeating the same failure.

### 3. Uber launches Uber Parking via SpotHero’s 13,000+ spots, with robotaxi staging in mind (Mobility / US)
**Summary:** Uber said on Nov 9 that Uber Parking is live in the app, letting users reserve and pay for parking after the company absorbed SpotHero about nine months earlier. The network spans more than 13,000 garages, lots, and valet sites across more than 400 U.S. and Canadian cities. The initial launch covers about 15 U.S. cities, including Atlanta, Austin, Boston, Chicago, Dallas, Denver, Los Angeles, Miami, Nashville, New York City, Philadelphia, San Diego, San Francisco, Seattle, and Washington, DC. Near-term focus is human drivers, but Uber also plans to use lots as staging and waiting spots for partner autonomous fleets to cut deadheading and congestion; Uber One members can earn about 10% back in credits on eligible parking bookings.

### 5. BMW to expand LFP packs on selected Neue Klasse EVs for cost–range trade-offs (Battery / Europe)
**Summary:** InsideEVs reported on Nov 9 that CEO Milan Nedeljkovic told BMW’s third-quarter earnings call the company will expand lithium iron phosphate (LFP) use on selected Neue Klasse models for buyers who prefer a lower price over maximum range. BMW still sees cylindrical NMC cells as superior for the range most Neue Klasse cars deliver, but acknowledged LFP’s different cost–range balance. He did not name the next models or a launch date outside China, and did not promise that lower cell costs would flow straight into sticker prices. China already previews the split: the long-wheelbase iX3 30L uses an about 77 kWh LFP pack with a claimed CLTC range of about 630 km, while the 40L and 50L keep NMC. Analysts see BMW’s 2028 Europe-focused compact Neue Klasse EV as a likely candidate.

**Links:**

- [InsideEVs — BMW To Expand Use Of Cheaper LFP Batteries In Its EVs](https://insideevs.com/news/814874/bmw-confirms-lfp-batteries-evs/)

**Commentary:** A premium brand embracing LFP shows chemistry is becoming a pricing tool, not only a range badge.

## III. Batteries, Charging & Supply Chain

### 7. BMW adds Gen6 cell localization: Samsung SDI in Europe, LG Energy Solution in the U.S. (Supply chain / Global)
**Summary:** BMW Group’s U.S. newsroom said on Nov 9 that it has contracted additional cell suppliers for sixth-generation (Gen6) high-voltage batteries: Samsung SDI in Europe and LG Energy Solution in the United States, supplying from established regional plants from the late 2020s. With the nominations, European cell sourcing will include CATL, EVE Energy, and Samsung SDI, while LG Energy Solution joins BMW’s U.S. supply. Gen6 packs are assembled at five new plants near vehicle factories—Irlbach-Straßkirchen in Germany, Debrecen in Hungary, Shenyang in China, Woodruff in the U.S., and San Luis Potosí in Mexico. Purchasing board member Nicolai Martin framed the move as “local for local” diversification to shorten transport distances and strengthen regional resilience and competitiveness.

**Links:**

### 8. ZF unveils ~30,000-rpm High Revolution Drive claiming ~25% less bulk and half the magnets (Technology / Europe)
**Summary:** InsideEVs reported on Nov 9 that ZF’s High Revolution Drive integrates the motor, power electronics, and reduction gearing in one housing, with the motor spinning up to about 30,000 rpm versus about 18,000 rpm on ZF’s comparable Select units. ZF claims nearly 25% less weight and volume, about half the magnet mass, and efficiency still near about 93%. The shown unit delivers up to about 268 hp, with gearing of about 18:1 producing peak wheel torque of about 3,500 Nm. Production versions are targeted at about 200–268 hp on both 400- and 800-volt platforms, reusing Select’s modular architecture. Talks with automakers are underway, with production aimed around 2030 and no named vehicle programs yet.

**Links:**

- [InsideEVs — ZF’s 30,000-RPM EV Motor Makes Drive Units Smaller And Lighter](https://insideevs.com/news/814857/zf-new-high-rpm-motor/)

**Commentary:** Spinning faster buys fewer rare-earth magnets and a lighter drive unit—supply pressure is pushing motor physics harder.

### 9. Greenspot and Kempower plan ~30 French retail fast-charging sites, ~200 kW feeding four points each (Charging / Europe)
**Summary:** electrive reported on Nov 9 that French operator Greenspot plans to install Kempower fast chargers at about 30 retail locations by the end of 2027. Each site gets one about 200 kW Power Unit feeding two Dual Satellites for four charge points with dynamic power sharing. The concept targets supermarket and mid-size retail lots and can connect on France’s commercial “tarif jaune” without a dedicated transformer station, cutting build time and capex. About five sites are already live at Bricomarché, Intermarché, and Aldi locations. Greenspot finances, installs, and operates the hardware under a third-party investment model. It already runs more than 1,500 charge points in France and aims for about 8,500 by 2030.

### 11. CPCA: Oct NEV retail/wholesale penetration hits records ~67.5%/67.3% as exports prop up wholesale (Market / China)
**Summary:** China News Service reported on Nov 9 that the China Passenger Car Association released October national passenger-car data: retail sales were about 1.643 million units, down about 27.1% year over year and up about 1.5% month over month. NEV retail penetration rose to about 67.5% and wholesale penetration to about 67.3%, both record highs. CPCA said exports exceeding domestic demand are stabilizing wholesale volumes and profits, and expects November to warm modestly month over month with a narrower year-over-year decline, while warning export front-loading may not last.

### 12. After the midterms: Democrats can probe U.S. auto tariffs and EV credits, not easily repeal them (Policy / US)
**Summary:** Automotive Manufacturing Solutions analyzed on Nov 9 that Democrats’ Nov 3 midterm wave is set to give them both chambers—at least about 54 Senate seats and a House majority above 250—yet a presidential veto still blocks easy rewrites of auto policy. Restoring a roughly $7,500 EV tax credit, tougher fuel-economy rules, or CAFE fine collection would need Republican votes to survive a veto. Section 232 duties of about 25% on imported vehicles and most parts rest on presidential national-security authority, so congressional levers are more likely oversight hearings, USMCA annual reviews, and pressure in trade talks. Democratic wins in Michigan’s statehouse and Texas’s governorship could also shift EV and infrastructure rules faster at the state level than in Washington.

**Links:**

- [Automotive Manufacturing Solutions — Democrats can probe auto tariffs but not scrap them](https://www.automotivemanufacturingsolutions.com/electrification/democrats-can-probe-auto-tariffs-but-not-scrap-them/2764251)

**Commentary:** Elections change who can interrogate tariffs and credits; they do not instantly rewrite the federal rules plants schedule against.
