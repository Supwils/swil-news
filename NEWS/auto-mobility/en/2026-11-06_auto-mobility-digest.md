## I. Policy & Markets

### 1. China NEA: more than 40 million charging facilities by 2030 to support over 110 million EVs (charging policy / China)
**Summary:** At a National Development and Reform Commission “15th Five-Year Plan” briefing on November 6, National Energy Administration planning official Ge Ju said China will push electricity substitution so that electricity accounts for about 35% of final energy use by 2030. In transport, China will build a city-area, highway-corridor and rural-point charging and battery-swap network, targeting more than 40 million charging facilities by 2030—enough to support over 110 million electric vehicles.

**Links:**

- [Cailian Press — NEA: charging facilities to exceed 40 million by 2030](https://www.cls.cn/detail/2527216)
- [Science and Technology Daily — China charging facilities to top 40 million by 2030](https://www.stdaily.com/web/gdxw/2026-11/06/content_609612.html)

**Commentary:** The target lifts “not enough chargers” into national energy planning—the next fight is high-power, highway and rural coverage quality, not just pile counts.

### 2. CPCA: China’s October NEV retail penetration hits 67.8% as ICE share shrinks toward one-third (market / China)
**Summary:** D1EV and others reported on November 6, citing China Passenger Car Association figures, that national passenger-car retail reached about 1.693 million units in October, down year on year but up month on month. NEV retail was about 1.148 million, for a 67.8% retail penetration rate—roughly two of every three new cars sold. Trade-in rules, high fuel prices and faster NEV product launches are cited as shrinking ICE share; whether penetration clears 70% depends on ICE contraction, subsidy delivery and year-end push.

**Links:**

- [D1EV — October NEV penetration at 67.8%](https://www.d1ev.com/newsflash/315962)

**Commentary:** Higher penetration is mainly share reshuffling inside a replacement market—volume pressure and structural electrification can coexist.

### 3. Thailand plans 35% excise tax on imported BEVs and REEVs from 2027 to force localization (policy / Southeast Asia)
**Summary:** The Bangkok Post reported on November 6 that Thailand’s Finance Ministry is advancing a third-phase EV policy after cabinet backing: from January 1, 2027, the excise tax on imported battery-electric and range-extended EVs rises to 35% from about 10%–15% today, with a tax-inclusive method that can make the effective burden higher. Plants meeting local-content or Made-in-Thailand rules may keep a ~10% rate, while others face 15%–35%, pushing brands such as XPeng toward more local production and parts sourcing.

**Links:**

- [Bangkok Post — Ministry mulls third phase of EV promotion](https://www.bangkokpost.com/business/motoring/3349560/ministry-mulls-third-phase-of-ev-promotion)
- [Thai Government — Import BEV/REEV excise tax set at 35%](https://www.thaigov.go.th/th/news/170834)

**Commentary:** After subsidies bought market share comes a tax wall for factories—ASEAN is shifting from import incentives to forced supply-chain localization.

### 4. UK October BEV share hits a yearly high of 32%, still trailing the ZEV Mandate (market / UK)
**Summary:** SMMT data show UK new-car registrations rose about 5.4% in October to 152,730. Battery-electric registrations climbed about 32.7% to 48,858, taking a 32.0% share—the highest of the year—while PHEVs held about 15%. Year-to-date BEV share is only about 26.6%, below the 2026 ZEV Mandate target near 33%. SMMT warns the demand gap could widen to about 14.5 percentage points by 2028 and calls for deeper Mandate reform this year.

**Links:**

- [SMMT — Year-end push lifts EV sales but Mandate gap set to widen](https://www.smmt.co.uk/year-end-push-lifts-ev-sales-but-mandate-gap-set-to-widen/)
- [electrive — UK BEV registrations reach annual high with 32 per cent market share](https://www.electrive.com/2026/11/05/uk-bev-registrations-reach-annual-high-with-32-per-cent-market-share/)

**Commentary:** Monthly peaks lean on discounts; annual compliance leans on policy relief—the UK transition is stuck between looking strong and missing the Mandate.

## II. Autonomous Driving & Mobility

### 5. Uber: about 700 robotaxis by year-end, targeting over 2,000 in 2027 with SF and Houston driverless launches (robotaxi / US)
**Summary:** Smart Cities Dive reported on November 6 that Uber CEO Dara Khosrowshahi told the Q3 call the platform expects about 700 robotaxis by year-end and more than 2,000 in 2027. ### 10. Nio’s William Li again warns China’s cars are getting too heavy: ~20% more battery weight can roughly double road damage (industry / China)
**Summary:** CnEVPost reported on November 6 that Nio founder William Li told a Wuzhen World Internet Conference auto forum that some models pack ~120 kWh batteries weighing 700–800 kg. Coverage also notes China’s EV energy-consumption standard effective January 1, 2026, ties limits to curb weight for the first time.

**Links:**

## IV. Batteries, Charging & Supply Chain

### 11. VW Group limits ~5,000 UK EVs to 80% charge via OTA as ~85,000 vehicles worldwide face battery checks (safety / Europe)
**Summary:** Autocar reported on November 6 that selected 2022–2025 ID.3, ID.4, ID.7, ID.Buzz and Cupra Born models may have battery components below quality standards that can overheat and pose a fire risk above 80% charge. About 5,000 UK cars already have an OTA 80% charge cap; roughly 85,000 are affected worldwide, with some markets also listing Audi Q4 e-tron. VW reports no confirmed injuries but some property damage; owners will be called in for about an hour of checks and module replacements where needed, with no timeline given.

**Links:**

- [Autocar — 5000 electric Volkswagens hit with 80% charge limit over fire risk](https://www.autocar.co.uk/car-news/new-cars/5000-electric-volkswagens-hit-80-charge-limit-over-fire-risk)

**Commentary:** OTA charge caps before physical recalls are now the standard battery-risk playbook—short-term range and trust take the hit.
