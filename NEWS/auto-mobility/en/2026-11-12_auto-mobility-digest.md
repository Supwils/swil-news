## I. Policy & Markets

### 1. UK and EU weigh another delay on EV battery rules of origin to avoid ~10% tariffs (Policy / Europe)
**Summary:** The Guardian and electrive reported on Nov 12 that most electric vehicles traded between the UK and EU would face about 10% tariffs from Jan 1, 2027 unless the sides agree on another delay to battery rules of origin. The European Commission is consulting industry on options that include a roughly one-year postponement or a two-year scheme allowing compliant domestic sales to offset non-compliant exports; UK carmakers prefer about two years. ACEA estimates that about 82% of roughly 520,000 electric cars and vans expected to be exported from the EU to the UK in 2027 could face tariffs, implying about €1.47 billion in extra costs. The stricter rules were already delayed from 2024 to end-2026; battery-materials investors oppose further slippage, and talks may intensify after a Nov 20 UK–EU summit.

**Links:**

- [The Guardian — UK car industry to press EU for two-year delay to Brexit EV tariffs](https://www.theguardian.com/business/2026/nov/12/uk-car-industry-rules-of-origin-brexit-ev-tariffs-eu)
- [electrive — EU and UK could delay mutual EV tariffs once more](https://www.electrive.com/2026/11/12/eu-and-uk-could-delay-mutual-ev-tariffs-once-more/)

**Commentary:** When local battery capacity lags the statute calendar, another delay often looks more politically realistic than flipping on a 10% tariff overnight.

### 2. Canada budget proposes ~CAD 587M to renew ZEVIP for 55,000+ public chargers (Policy / Canada)
**Summary:** Electrek and Canadian government materials say Budget 2026 proposes about CAD 587 million over five years, starting in 2027–28, for Environment and Climate Change Canada to renew the Zero-Emission Vehicle Infrastructure Program (ZEVIP) and support more than about 55,000 additional federally backed chargers where gaps are largest—roughly doubling the federally supported network. About CAD 45 million more is earmarked for charging R&D, bringing charging-related measures above about CAD 634 million alongside roughly CAD 4 billion for grid and electricity infrastructure. ZEVIP typically covers up to about 50% of eligible project costs, or up to about 75% for Indigenous-led projects, with caps near CAD 2 million per project. Funding still depends on parliamentary approval.

**Links:**

### 8. CATL’s Qiji Energy targets ~2,000 China truck swap stations by end-2027 and a Europe push (Battery / China)
**Summary:** The Guardian reported on Nov 12 that CATL is expanding heavy-truck battery swapping via subsidiary Qiji Energy, with drivers completing swaps in about five minutes versus roughly 90 minutes of charging downtime previously. China sold more than about 400,000 e-trucks last year—over 90% of the global total. CATL aims for about 2,000 swapping stations in China by the end of 2027 and plans to bring the technology to Europe, with UK hubs eyed for around 2027. Standardized packs plus swap networks are framed as a practical answer to range anxiety on freight corridors.
