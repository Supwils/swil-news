## I. Policy & Markets

### 1. EU Parliament environment committee backs softer 2035 car CO₂ cut: 100% to 87.5% (Policy / Europe)
**Summary:** On Nov 4, the European Parliament’s Environment Committee voted to ease the 2035 CO₂ reduction target for new passenger cars from 100% to 87.5%—going further than the Commission’s earlier 90% proposal—and to cut the 2035 van target from 100% to 80%. The text passed 48–30 with two abstentions and would let manufacturers offset up to about 7.5 percentage points via credits tied to EU-made low-carbon steel, while broadening the zero-emission definition toward eligible renewable-fuel vehicles. The package still needs a December plenary mandate and talks with member states before becoming law.

### 2. Japan’s MLIT to certify L4/L5 vehicles without steering wheels or pedals under unified rules from December (Policy / Japan)
**Summary:** Nikkei and follow-on reports on Nov 4 said Japan’s Ministry of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism told its vehicle-safety study group it will revise Road Transport Vehicle Act safety standards from December 2026 so Level 4 and Level 5 vehicles without a driver can receive uniform type certification instead of case-by-case reviews. Qualifying vehicles would no longer need a steering wheel, brake pedal, mirrors or driver-monitoring system; passenger carriers would still face dummy crash tests, while cargo-only driverless vehicles could be exempt. The change aligns with UN WP.29 rules agreed in June and expected to become an international standard in December, supporting Japan’s goal of about 10,000 Level 4 buses, taxis and similar vehicles by fiscal 2030.

**Commentary:** Certification is shifting from prototype reviews to mass-production rules—the next robotaxi bottleneck is regulatory design freedom, not only software demos.

### 6. Five ministries’ conservation action plan: build full-lifecycle management for NEV traction batteries (Battery policy / China)

## II. Autonomous Driving & Mobility

### 7. Waymo confirms ending Uber exclusivity in Austin and Atlanta from January 2028, pushing its own app (Robotaxi / United States)
**Summary:** TechCrunch reported on Nov 4 that Waymo co-CEO Tekedra Mawakana told Bloomberg the company will end exclusivity with Uber in Austin and Atlanta starting in January 2028 and offer rides directly through the Waymo app, as it does elsewhere. She said Uber initially provided scale, but riders faced a “crapshoot” over whether they would get a robotaxi or a human-driven car, and Waymo wants a direct rider relationship. Uber told TechCrunch it still plans to host Waymo vehicles on its app for as long as both sides agree; earlier reporting put the existing contract into at least May 2028.

## IV. Batteries & Charging

### 13. UK £190 million motorway charging scheme clears CMA subsidy advice review; government decision next (Charging / United Kingdom)
**Summary:** TechTimes reported on Nov 4 that the Competition and Markets Authority’s Subsidy Advice Unit finished its mandatory 30-working-day assessment of OZEV’s Strategic Charging Infrastructure Scheme, a £190 million plan to reinforce electricity connections at English motorway service areas and then offer subsidised capacity to open-access charge-point operators. SCIS replaces the earlier £70 million Rapid Charging Fund pilot that issued no awards; it includes a partially waived “second-comer charge” meant to encourage multi-operator competition at sites. The SAU cannot veto the scheme; the go/no-go call remains with government, and geographic scope is limited to England under devolved powers.

### 14. Mercedes-Benz developing programmable power converters to decouple cell strings from up to ~800V output (Technology / Europe)
**Summary:** Renewable Energy Magazine reported on Nov 4 that Mercedes-Benz said the previous day it is developing a new Power Converter for future EVs using programmable micro-converters to control energy flows of individual cells, deliver a stable high-voltage output of up to about 800 volts, and support AC/DC conversion plus bidirectional charging. Current-generation hardware already reaches about 3 kW. Multiple converters in parallel would set high-voltage output in software rather than by series cell count, decoupling battery architecture from HV supply and potentially merging functions now split across separate power-electronics boxes. A production timeline was not announced.

**Links:**

- [Renewable Energy Magazine — Mercedes-Benz developing new power converter for future EVs](https://www.renewableenergymagazine.com/electric_hybrid_vehicles/mercedesbenz-developing-new-power-converter-for-future-20261104)

**Commentary:** Handing pack geometry to software points to the next HV-platform fight: programmable power electronics.
