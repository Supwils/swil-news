## I. Electric vehicles, markets, and policy

### 1. Volkswagen premieres the all-electric ID. Tiguan; European pre-sales open October 13 from 42,695 euros (New model / Europe)
**Summary:** Volkswagen Group announced the world premiere of the ID. Tiguan on October 9. The mid-size electric SUV succeeds the ID.4 and ID.5 and will be sold alongside the Wolfsburg-built Tiguan with petrol, diesel, and hybrid powertrains. Brand CEO Thomas Schaefer said the Tiguan is the group's best-selling model worldwide and that the ID.4 and ID.5 are among its most successful electric cars; the electric version will be built in Emden and launched at the beginning of 2027. Volkswagen listed three powertrains: a 140 kW (190 PS) rear-drive model with a 58 kWh battery, a 210 kW (286 PS) rear-drive model with a 77 kWh battery, and a 220 kW (299 PS) all-wheel-drive model, also with 77 kWh. WLTP range reaches up to 600 km. Towing capacity is up to 1,800 kg with the smaller battery and 2,000 kg with the larger one. Pre-sales start in Germany and many other European countries on October 13 from 42,695 euros, in Trend, Life, Style, and R-Line.

### 3. Reuters: U.S. EV sales down 30.7% through September, while Europe's share rises to 23.2% (Market / U.S. and Europe)
**Summary:** A Reuters report carried by Sowetan on October 9 compared EV trends in the United States and Europe over the first nine months of this year. Motor Intelligence data show U.S. EV sales down 30.7% year on year, at 6% of overall sales, compared with 8.5% a year earlier, when buyers rushed to beat the expiry of a $7,500 federal tax credit. ACEA figures put EVs at 23.2% of the European car market, up from 17.7% a year earlier. The same report says U.S. hybrid sales rose 23% in the first three quarters and accounted for 15.6% of total sales in that period, while CarGurus said used-EV sales rose 19%. Motor Intelligence estimates Tesla's U.S. sales fell 14% and Rivian's rose 29% with the R2; Ford's EV sales fell 68% and GM's fell 43%. Transport & Environment said more than 150 EV models were on sale in Europe in the first half of 2026, against about 100 in 2024, with many priced below 25,000 euros.

### 4. Japan's EV subsidy budget is about 89% drawn, and Model Y outdraws Honda and Nissan (Policy / Japan)
**Summary:** Automotive World reported on October 9, citing Nikkei, that Japan's 110 billion yen (about $700 million) EV subsidy budget is nearly exhausted halfway through the fiscal year. Payouts on eligible EVs sold from April through September would reach 97.4 billion yen, or 89% of the total, and applications could close as early as December. Toyota leads with 34.5 billion yen, about 31%, helped by the updated bZ4X, which qualifies for the maximum 400,000 yen per vehicle. Tesla's Model Y accounted for 15.3 billion yen, ahead of Nissan's Leaf and Honda's Super-One. Passenger EV sales rose 150% year on year to a record 70,273 units in the six months to September, or 3.7% of new-vehicle sales. EV sales by other foreign brands fell 26% to about 7,900. A METI official said the budget is being used about one month faster than expected; the ministry is requesting the same 110 billion yen for fiscal 2027. Suzuki's e-Sky is scheduled for November from 2.12 million yen.

**Links:**

- [Automotive World — Tesla outdraws Honda and Nissan in Japan's EV subsidy rush](https://www.automotiveworld.com/intelligence/tesla-outdraws-honda-and-nissan-in-japans-ev-subsidy-rush/)

## II. Autonomous driving

### 5. Tesla's European page now says Assisted Driving after Germany called the FSD name misleading (Regulation / Europe)
**Summary:** Electrek and CNBC reported on October 9 that Tesla has renamed "Full Self-Driving (Supervised)" to "Tesla Assisted Driving" in Europe. Electrek wrote that the German site no longer mentions Full Self-Driving and that the old address redirects to the new page. Germany's Federal Ministry of Transport said on October 6 that the old "FSD" name was somewhat misleading because the system does not take over the entire driving task and the driver must remain attentive; Tesla offered the new name in September talks with Transport Minister Steffen Bilger. They also agreed the system may exceed the speed limit by at most 10%, a position Germany will support in the EU approval process. Electrek, citing Reuters, said the version approved in the Netherlands allows up to 50% over the detected limit. Tesla's page lists eight EU countries where the system is approved: the Netherlands, Lithuania, Estonia, Denmark, Belgium, Slovenia, Czechia, and Croatia. Germany is not among them. An EU-wide vote is not expected before December at the earliest. In the United States the old name is still sold at $99 a month. CNBC added that Tesla's second-quarter update counted 1.48 million "active" FSD subscriptions and did not say how many came from free trials.

### 6. Stellantis CEO says the Wayve tie-up can shorten assisted-driving development (Autonomous driving / Italy)
**Summary:** Reuters reported from Turin on October 9 that Stellantis CEO Antonio Filosa, speaking with Wayve CEO Alex Kendall at the Wave technology event, said the partnership with the British startup can cut development time and cost for advanced driver-assistance systems. He was referring to level 2++, which allows hands-off driving while the driver still supervises. Filosa said Stellantis development cycles are currently about 24 months and that working with a technology partner is a way to compress that timeline; automaker scale, he said, can also lower the cost of hands-free driving. The companies announced in May that Wayve's AI driving software would be integrated into STLA AutoDrive, with a first product targeted for 2028 in North America. The Turin event included hands-free demonstrations in Fiat 500e and Maserati Grecale development vehicles. Kendall said the spread of Jeep, Fiat, Peugeot, and Maserati lets one AI system be tuned differently by brand. Reuters also noted that Wayve works with Nissan and Mercedes.

### 10. Believ agrees a 15-year Ayrshire rollout of more than 600 chargers (Charging / United Kingdom)
**Summary:** EV Fleet World reported on October 9 that charge-point operator Believ has partnered with East, North, and South Ayrshire councils to install more than 600 chargers at about 300 locations, for residents, visitors, and drivers without private parking, including the Isle of Arran. Installation of the first new points is expected to begin in early 2027. The 15-year agreement runs with Transport Scotland and the Scottish Futures Trust. It combines more than 2.8 million pounds from the Scottish Government's EV Infrastructure Fund with more than 15 million pounds of committed capital from Believ, which will operate and maintain the points. The split is roughly 180 chargers in East Ayrshire, 210 in North Ayrshire, and 220 in South Ayrshire, plus passive infrastructure for more than 450 additional chargers. At least five accessible charging locations are planned in each council area. Believ will also take over management of existing ChargePlace Scotland chargers.

### 11. The European Commission selects 46 more strategic raw-material projects, with about 21.1 billion euros of expected capital (Supply chain / EU)
**Summary:** POLITICO and electrive reported on October 9 that the European Commission named a second batch of 46 critical-raw-material strategic projects across 16 member states, chosen from 102 applications. A Commission question-and-answer note says that, with the 47 projects selected in March 2025, the EU now has 93 strategic projects inside the Union; 13 more in third countries and overseas countries and territories were selected in June 2025. This batch is eight extraction projects, 11 processing, 19 recycling, and eight integrated projects, covering 15 of the 17 strategic raw materials. electrive counts seven lithium, 12 nickel, 10 cobalt, five manganese, and four graphite projects on the battery side; Germany received eight, more than any other member state. The projects are expected to need about 21.1 billion euros of capital investment to become operational, and strategic status does not itself award EU funding. Industry commissioner Stephane Sejourne said that, taken with the 2025 projects, the EU could fully meet extraction targets for lithium, nickel, rare earths, magnesium, and tungsten, processing targets for lithium and rare earths, and recycling targets for lithium, cobalt, and rare earths. Permitting is capped at 27 months for extraction and 15 months for processing and recycling. Of the 2025 projects, 25 have completed environmental impact assessments and 17 have construction permits.
