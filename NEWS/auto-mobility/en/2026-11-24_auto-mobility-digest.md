## I. Policy & Trade

### 1. EU Formalizes Hybrid Import Quotas: About 191,780 Chinese Units Over 200 Days From Dec 1 (Policy)
**Summary:** Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/2614 was published on Nov 24, opening temporary safeguard quotas for 200 days from Dec 1 on new third-country hybrid passenger cars, covering HEVs, mild hybrids and PHEVs (range extenders excluded). China’s total allocation is about 191,780 units across three tranches (86,301 / 88,219 / 17,260), with Japan around 170,900, Türkiye about 126,300, the UK about 105,400 and South Korea about 67,900; once a quota is exhausted, further imports are barred, while in-quota shipments still pay the normal duty. The Guardian and electrive note the measure targets a surge in hybrid imports amid the EU–China trade imbalance; range-extenders remain treated under the existing BEV tariff track.

### 2. NHTSA Proposes Pushing Mandatory AEB Full Compliance to September 2031 (Safety Policy)
**Summary:** Reuters reported on Nov 24 that the U.S. National Highway Traffic Safety Administration is proposing to give automakers two more years—until September 2031 instead of 2029—to equip nearly all new cars and light trucks with advanced automatic emergency braking, while seeking comment on technical changes. The rule had been estimated to save at least about 360 lives and prevent about 24,000 injuries a year when fully in force. The Alliance for Automotive Innovation, which sued arguing the current standard is “practically impossible” with available technology and challenged the roughly 62 mph avoidance requirement, welcomed Tuesday’s delay proposal.

**Links:**

- [Investing.com / Reuters — US proposes AEB deadline of 2031](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/us-proposes-to-give-automakers-until-2031-to-install-automatic-emergency-braking-5048923)

**Commentary:** Safety mandates are sliding from fixed calendars back into negotiable feasibility—regulatory pace is yielding to industry litigation and technical disputes.

## II. Autonomous Driving & Robotaxis

### 3. Waymo Recalls Nearly 4,500 Robotaxis After Closed-Freeway-Lane Incidents (Autonomy)
**Summary:** CNBC reported on Nov 24 that Alphabet-owned Waymo told NHTSA its new recall of nearly 4,500 U.S. autonomous vehicles followed 11 incidents since Oct 1 in which robotaxis entered cone-closed freeway construction lanes, including three on a single day Nov 9; the company said no collisions or injuries were reported and a remedy is still in progress. The filing comes as California’s DMV barred Waymo’s driverless vehicles from statewide freeways this month and Waymo paused commercial freeway rides elsewhere in the U.S.; a separate recall covers responses to manual traffic direction, while NHTSA continues probes tied to school-bus stops and a Santa Monica child collision.

**Links:**

- [CNBC — Waymo recall prompted by robotaxis entering closed freeway lanes](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/24/waymo-recall-prompted-by-robotaxis-entering-closed-freeway-lanes.html)

**Commentary:** The recall elevates construction-zone perception from an edge case to the center of the ledger—verifiable safety still constrains scale more than city-count narratives.
