### 1. Tesla Opens Cybercab Personal Sales to Select Customers: About $29,990 Plus ~$299/Month (Autonomous Driving / US)
**Summary:** AFP, Reuters and The Verge reported on Nov 14 that Tesla invited “select customers” to buy the steering-wheel-free, pedal-free two-seat Cybercab for personal use at about $29,990 before tax, plus a mandatory ~$299 monthly Autonomy Subscription and a ~$1,000 non-refundable deposit, with estimated December delivery. ## II. Policy & Markets

### 2. Germany Plans Sharp 2027 EV Purchase-Grant Top-Up While Keeping the ~€3 Billion Cap (Policy / Europe)
**Summary:** German public broadcaster tagesschau and Handelsblatt, citing a Finance Ministry budget-cleanup draft, reported that Berlin plans to raise 2027 EV purchase-grant funding from about €803 million to about €1.72 billion—an increase of roughly €917 million—because demand has far exceeded forecasts. Money would come from the Climate and Transformation Fund, but the overall 2026–2029 envelope of about €3 billion would stay unchanged, pulling future-year capacity forward. The grant targets private buyers, with a maximum of about €6,000 for vehicles first registered from Jan 1, 2026; the Environment Ministry had estimated the pot could cover about 800,000 vehicles. Coverage notes that if applications keep their current pace, the scheme may end earlier than the original 2029 horizon.

### 3. Luxembourg Extends EV Purchase Subsidies Through Mid-2030, Up to About €6,000 (Policy / Europe)
**Summary:** newmobility.news reported on Nov 13 that Luxembourg Environment Minister Serge Wilmes said the Klimabonus Mobilitéit purchase incentive will remain through June 30, 2030, at up to about €6,000, with a simpler claims process; from January 2027 dealers can deduct the grant on the invoice. Grants are efficiency-tiered: models using no more than about 16 kWh/100 km can receive the full amount, while about 16.1–18 kWh/100 km receive about €3,000, with extra rules for large families and seven-seaters. Support for private charging gear also runs through end-2030, including up to about €1,400 for a bidirectional charger. The report contrasts that with Flanders and the Netherlands, which ended direct private-buyer subsidies at the end of 2024.

**Links:**

- [newmobility.news — Luxembourg keeps EV subsidies until 2030](https://newmobility.news/en/2026/11/13/neighbors-pull-the-plug-luxembourg-keeps-ev-subsidies-until-2030/)

**Commentary:** While neighbors pull subsidies, a small market is competing with four years of policy certainty for efficiency-minded buyers.

### 4. MIIT Seeks Comment on Power-Battery “Digital ID” Rules Using a 24-Digit Code Across the Lifecycle (Policy / China)
**Summary:** CNR on Nov 14 carried a Guangming commentary noting that China’s Ministry of Industry and Information Technology on Nov 5 opened public consultation on interim rules for a new-energy vehicle power-battery digital identity. The draft would use the national-standard 24-digit battery code as the sole index linking design, production, sales, vehicle fitment, repair, dismantling, recycling and reuse data. Automakers would have to provide owners—via in-car systems or apps—with the code, maker details, rated energy, warranty and cathode chemistry, among other fields; related platforms would need Class III or higher cybersecurity protection. The piece cites estimates that retired power-battery volume could exceed about 1 million tonnes by 2030, arguing digital IDs would shrink grey recycling and improve traceability.

**Links:**

- [CNR — NEV power batteries to get digital IDs; full-chain oversight needed](https://auto.cnr.cn/2015xc/20261114/t20261114_527872306.shtml)

**Commentary:** Giving every pack a registry identity before the retirement wave arrives is how compliance and used-battery residual values get a shared language.

### 6. BYD Unveils Abu Dhabi Solar-Storage-Charging Station With Flash Charging and a Robotic Plug-In Arm (Charging / Middle East)
**Summary:** The Driven reported on Nov 14 that BYD showcased an Abu Dhabi desert charging site combining solar generation, battery storage, Flash Charging and a robotic arm that opens the charge-port cover and plugs in, calling it the first installation to unite all four. BYD says Flash Charging can take a compatible EV from about 10% to 70% in roughly five minutes and from about 10% to 97% in about nine minutes; stored solar power can support weak-grid or off-grid sites. The arm uses vision and force control and can pair with automated valet parking. BYD aims to open about 6,000 Flash Charging stations worldwide by the end of 2027.
