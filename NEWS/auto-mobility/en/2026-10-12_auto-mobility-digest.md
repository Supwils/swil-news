### 2. CPCA: September passenger NEV retail sales fall 16.7% to 1.084 million, while penetration hits 67% (Market / China)
**Summary:** CnEVPost reported on October 12 that the China Passenger Car Association released Monday figures showing September passenger NEV retail sales of 1.084 million, down 16.7% year on year and up 7.8% from August, a ninth straight monthly decline. Retail sales for the first nine months were 7.758 million, down 12.8%. NEVs were a record 67.0% of passenger-vehicle retail sales, up 9.1 percentage points from a year earlier. Penetration was 84.5% for domestic brands, 44.9% for luxury brands, and 15.9% for mainstream joint-venture brands. Wholesale sales rose 11.3% year on year and 10.0% month on month to 1.666 million, with wholesale penetration at 65.4%. September NEV passenger exports were 558,000, up 163.5%. BYD's retail sales were 246,816, Geely's 119,682, Leapmotor's 78,202, and Tesla's 64,837.

### 3. China-EU hybrid arrangement: first-half exports to the EU hit $7.5 billion, and the mechanism is still unpublished (Policy / China and Europe)
**Summary:** Reuters reported on October 12 that China's arrangement to curb hybrid exports to the European Union will hit automakers that used plug-in hybrids to avoid the bloc's extra tariffs on Chinese-made EVs. Chinese customs data put hybrid exports to the 27 EU member states at $7.5 billion in the first half, triple the $2.5 billion of a year earlier. EU Trade Commissioner Maros Sefcovic said on Friday that the arrangement would cut imports by several million cars over four years, and Reuters wrote that Brussels and Beijing have not yet disclosed how it will be implemented. China's Ministry of Commerce said on Monday that the arrangement allows export growth to continue and guarantees China a quota no smaller than that given to any other source of imports. Renault chief executive Francois Provost said the same day that the import surge is unsustainable for Europe, but that the agreement is preferable to a trade war.

**Links:**

- [Reuters — China's $7.5 billion EU hybrid boom faces new curbs under a China-EU deal](https://d2233.cms.socastsrm.com/2026/10/12/chinas-7-5-billion-eu-hybrid-boom-faces-new-curbs-under-china-eu-deal/)

## II. Autonomous driving

### 5. NHTSA opens DP26010 after a petition says Tesla Robotaxis speed in school zones; the fleet estimate is about 1,000 (Regulation / United States)
**Summary:** Electrek reported on October 12 that the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration's Office of Defects Investigation opened file DP26010 on October 9 to review a defect petition received on September 23. The petition alleges that 2026 Robotaxis exceed posted limits in active school zones while the automated driving system is engaged, covering both the Model Y and the Cybercab. The agency estimates about 1,000 vehicles and labels that count an estimate. The evidence is three social-media videos attached to the petition. NHTSA's tally lists four incidents and no crashes, injuries, or deaths. Federal rules give the agency 120 days from receipt of a petition to grant or deny a formal defect investigation, which puts the deadline around January 21, 2027.

### 6. London Assembly: the evidence is not enough to welcome driverless passenger service, and it wants permits and a fleet cap for TfL (Regulation / United Kingdom)
**Summary:** The London Assembly website published the Transport Committee report "Who's driving the future of autonomous transport in London?", with a publication timestamp of October 12. The report says the committee has not heard sufficient evidence to persuade it that London should welcome autonomous passenger services. It recommends that, when the full regulations under the Automated Vehicles Act are in place in 2027, the power to issue, withdraw, and set conditions on Automated Passenger Services permits should go to Transport for London, along with the power to cap how many vehicles an operator can deploy. The Register reported the same day that Waymo hopes to launch a commercial fleet of "low hundreds" this year if approved, that Uber and Wayve hold private-hire licences for supervised self-driving rides, and that Freenow and Baidu are aiming for a fully driverless launch in 2027. A YouGov survey commissioned by the committee found 42% opposed and 29% in support. A ride-hail union wants the rollout stopped; the report does not recommend a ban.

**Links:**

- [London Assembly — Who's driving the future of autonomous transport in London?](https://www.london.gov.uk/who-we-are/what-london-assembly-does/london-assembly-work/london-assembly-publications/whos-driving-future-autonomous-transport-london)

## III. Batteries, charging, and materials

### 7. Trafigura and EnergyX: a 10-year offtake for 100,000 metric tons of battery-grade lithium, with Chile aimed at 2028 and Texas at 2029 (Materials / United States and Chile)
**Summary:** Trafigura announced in Geneva on October 12 that it has signed a binding 10-year offtake with EnergyX for 100,000 metric tons of battery-grade lithium products from projects in the United States and Chile. Lithium carbonate will come from Project Black Giant in Antofagasta, and lithium hydroxide from Project Lonestar in Texas. Trafigura will buy 66% of Phase 1 offtake from Black Giant and 30% of Phase 1 offtake from Lonestar. The company is targeting commercial production at Black Giant in 2028 and at Lonestar in 2029, and said the offtake supports EnergyX's financing from the U.S. Export-Import Bank and other project lenders. Reuters reported the same day that the agreement is worth about $2 billion at current prices near $20,000 a metric ton and is take-or-pay. Production has not started.

### 8. ACC says its Douvrin plant has made its five-millionth French cell; a 12.9 GWh second block is planned for 2027 (Battery / France)
**Summary:** electrive reported on October 12 that Automotive Cells Company, the joint venture of Stellantis, Mercedes-Benz, and TotalEnergies subsidiary Saft, said at the Paris Motor Show that its Douvrin plant in France has produced its five-millionth cell. Current output is enough for nearly 40,000 vehicles. A second block of 12.9 GWh is planned to start production in 2027 for Mercedes-Benz electric vehicles, taking the site to just under 26 GWh; the final plan is three blocks totaling about 40 GWh. The cells are already used in eight Stellantis models, including the Peugeot E-3008 and E-5008, Jeep Compass, DS N°7 and N°8, Citroen C5 Aircross, and Opel Grandland. The same report recalls stops announced at the end of September: Sochaux from October 23 to 30 and Rennes from October 22 to 30, because long-range battery supply still trails demand.
