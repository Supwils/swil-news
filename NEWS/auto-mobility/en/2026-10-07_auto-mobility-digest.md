### 1. Porsche's Capital Markets Day puts the electric 718 into a first full production year in 2028 (Strategy / Germany)
**Summary:** Porsche's newsroom on October 7 said the company presented its Sportwagenschmiede '35 strategy through 2035 at a Capital Markets Day at the Weissach development centre. The release says the all-electric 718 Boxster and Cayman are expected to support sales in their first full year of production in 2028. Also in 2028, a new B-segment SUV with combustion and plug-in hybrid powertrains will be offered alongside the current all-electric Macan, with a noticeable sales and profit contribution expected in 2029 after the production ramp. A Future Package already agreed with employee representatives includes a socially responsible reduction of 9,000 jobs and job security for the core workforce until 2035; management positions are to fall 40 percent and the direct and indirect workforce 25 percent in the medium term, with a strategic target of 30 percent. MotorTrend reported the same day that chief executive Michael Leiters said the 911 will never be electric.

### 2. Jaguar publishes Type 01: 850 volts, 118 kWh, up to 1,030 PS (New model / United Kingdom)
**Summary:** Two Jaguar Land Rover media releases dated October 7 announced the all-electric four-door GT Type 01, unveiled in New York, with the Gaydon release datelined 7 October 2026. Official figures are an 850-volt architecture, a 118 kWh usable battery, an expected EPA range of up to 400 miles, up to 320 miles at a steady 75 mph, and up to 450 miles (720 km) WLTP. A 350 kW DC charge can add up to 200 miles in as little as 13 minutes. Three motors produce up to 1,030 PS and 1,365 Nm, with 0-62 mph in 3.2 seconds, 50:50 weight distribution, and a drag coefficient of 0.23. The car will be built in Solihull on the Jaguar Electric Architecture, with drive units and battery packs in Wolverhampton and pressed panels in Halewood. Orders open in early 2027, client deliveries are expected in the second half of that year, UK prices start at 130,000 pounds, and the US release lists an anticipated start of $130,500. JLR said it has invested 11.5 billion pounds since announcing a 15 to 18 billion pound five-year programme in 2024, and nearly 9,000 Solihull employees have been retrained to build electric vehicles.

## II. Autonomous Driving

### 4. Musk welcomes German backing as the EU vote on FSD slips to December at the earliest (Driver assistance / Europe)
**Summary:** Reuters reported from Berlin on October 7 that Elon Musk replied "Danke schoen" on X to German transport minister Steffen Bilger's push for EU approval of Tesla's Full Self-Driving. Bilger's statement on Tuesday called for prompt EU approval. Germany agreed with Tesla that the system may exceed the speed limit by up to 10 percent and will support that approach in the European process, and Tesla offered to rename the system Tesla Assisted Driving. Sweden, France, and others remain concerned about speeding, and an EU vote expected in October has been pushed back to at least December. A Reuters special report the same day said seven other EU countries have granted national approvals after the Netherlands, and that seven traffic-safety researchers found Tesla's European study does not measure crash rates and cannot support a claim of fewer fatal crashes. The report says an EU-wide vote needs 55 percent of member states representing 65 percent of the EU population, and that approval would be the first hands-free advanced driver-assistance system allowed on any road; current rules allow hands-free operation only on highways.

## III. Batteries and Charging

### 6. California's fast-charge rebate window opens, with up to $100,000 per port at 275 kW and above (Charging / United States)
**Summary:** The CALeVIP project page says Fast Charge California Project Window 2 began accepting applications at 9:00 a.m. PT on October 7 and closes at 5:00 p.m. PT on January 14, 2027. The implementation manual lists at least $27.6 million for this window; Electrek the same day reported the figure as at least $28 million. Ports with a guaranteed output of 150 kW to 274.99 kW qualify for up to $55,000, and ports at 275 kW or more qualify for up to $100,000, covering up to 100 percent of approved costs subject to those caps. Applications need an issued permit and a final utility service design, sites must be open to the public, charging hubs, hotels, and business districts are no longer eligible, and at least half of the connectors at each funded site must be CCS. Funds are reserved first come, first served, with priority for disadvantaged communities, low-income communities, and tribal lands, and applicants have 450 days after reservation to finish installation. The next window runs from February 24 to May 27, 2027, with a cap of $55,000 per port.

### 7. Believ signs a 15-year deal for more than 600 public charge points across Ayrshire (Charging / United Kingdom)
**Summary:** electrive reported on October 7 that charging operator Believ signed a 15-year partnership with East, North, and South Ayrshire councils to install more than 600 new public charge points at about 300 locations, with the first chargers expected from early 2027. The split is about 180, 210, and 220 points, and the South Ayrshire coverage includes the Isle of Arran. Each council area will have at least five accessible sites. Believ will also take over existing ChargePlace Scotland chargers in the three areas and provide passive provision at more than 450 locations. Funding is more than 2.8 million pounds from the Scottish Government's Electric Vehicle Infrastructure Fund and more than 15 million pounds of private investment from Believ. It is Believ's first public-sector partnership in Scotland; the company operates more than 2,300 public charge points in the UK. Cabinet secretary Stephen Flynn said the 2.8 million pounds will support 600 new chargers in Ayrshire.
