## I. Electric Trucks & Major OEMs

### 1. Tesla Semi Nevada plant begins volume production, targeting ~50,000 trucks a year (Trucks / Electrification)
**Summary:** TechCrunch and Electrek reported on September 25 that Tesla officially launched high-volume Semi production at a dedicated factory next to its first Gigafactory in Nevada, saying deliveries “start now.” The plant is rated for as many as about 50,000 trucks a year (roughly 1,000 per week) and about 3,000 local jobs. The Long Range version claims about 500 miles at roughly 82,000 lbs GVW; Standard Range is listed around 325 miles. Prior media quotes put pricing near $290,000 and $260,000 respectively. Customer trucks at the event carried logos from PepsiCo, DHL, US Foods, Einride, IMC Logistics and WattEV, among others; a pre-taped Musk video said partial automation features would arrive in the “near future.”

### 2. ZET SCALE alliance orders 2,500 electric Class 8 trucks with Tesla as primary supplier (Orders / Freight)
**Summary:** Electrek and ESG News reported that the ZET SCALE alliance—facilitated by Smart Freight Centre and Catalyst Mobility and backed by shippers including Microsoft and PepsiCo—placed the largest U.S. battery-electric Class 8 initial order to date: 2,500 trucks, with a longer-term target above 10,000. Tesla won the primary supplier slot via RFP, with Kenworth, Volvo and RIDE as secondary suppliers—so not all 2,500 will be Semis. The alliance said the order alone would nearly double electric Class 8 trucks on U.S. roads; the same week, IMC Logistics said it is adding 50 Semis for California port and inland warehouse runs.

### 5. ACEA-commissioned study: European battery capacity may miss IAA localisation needs (Policy / EU supply chain)
**Summary:** Finance Connect reported on September 25 that a Mobility Global study for ACEA finds European battery supply unlikely to meet proposed Industrial Accelerator Act (IAA) localisation requirements. Even with cell capacity projected at about 306 GWh by 2032, supply lagged demand in all three scenarios. If the IAA took effect in 2028, roughly three million cars might fail “made in the EU” criteria and related incentives for lack of European batteries; the gap looks worse for medium- and heavy-duty vehicles, while cathode/anode active-material shortages may linger until 2038–2040.

**Links:**

- [Finance Connect — European battery capacity set to fall short of IAA localisation demand](https://finance-connect.com/european-battery-capacity-set-to-fall-short-of-iaa-localisation-demand/)

**Commentary:** Europe wants localisation gates to defend electrification—then runs into battery and materials capacity first.

## III. Batteries & Charging

### 6. Geely’s fifth-gen smart ultra-fast charger peaks near 2.25 MW; pulse “healing” claims ~20% more cycle life (Charging / Batteries)
**Summary:** Electrek reported on September 25 that Geely unveiled its fifth-generation smart charging station in Ningbo, rated up to about 2,250 kW peak. In tests on packs used in the Lynk & Co 10 and Zeekr 001, charge rose from 10% to 70% in about 4 minutes 30 seconds and to 97% in about 8 minutes 40 seconds. Geely’s “lithium-ion pulse restoration” sends micro-pulses to reactivate lithium ions that accumulate at the anode during repeated fast charging; with AI-managed charging it claims up to about 20% more cycle life. It also detailed a next-gen ultra-short blade pack for more affordable models, saying about 10% less heat and peak charging up to 6C.

**Links:**

- [Electrek — Geely's AI fast charging 'heals' EV batteries for 20% more cycle life](https://electrek.co/2026/09/25/geely-ai-fast-charging-heals-ev-batteries-pulse-restoration/)

**Commentary:** The ultra-fast race is moving from “minutes to charge” to “charge fast without killing the pack”—durability is the next battlefield.

### 7. CATL starts battery-cell trial production at Debrecen, Hungary plant planned for ~100 GWh (Supply chain / Europe)
**Summary:** Just Auto and PR Newswire reported that CATL has begun cell trial production at its new Debrecen, Hungary plant after meeting permit preconditions, aiming to set up, optimise and validate lines before series production. Designated cell capacity is about 100 GWh—CATL’s largest manufacturing base outside China. Construction began in 2023; module assembly started in autumn 2024 with about 537,000 modules supplied to date, and cell equipment installation finished in Q2 2026. CATL said the hub will supply leading European OEMs that have expanded EV capacity in Hungary, alongside its Erfurt, Germany plant.

### 8. Waymo opens public robotaxi service in Denver across ~60 square miles (Autonomous driving)

### 10. Kodiak AI names Dallas–Houston as first long-haul driverless freight lane, aiming for end-2026 (Freight AV)
**Summary:** On September 25, Nasdaq-listed Kodiak AI said it plans to launch unsupervised long-haul driverless service on the Dallas–Houston freight lane by the end of 2026. Since August it has run a driverless launch program on its driverless-ready platform and says it consistently completes end-to-end deliveries between its Lancaster, Texas hub and Houston with no safety-observer steering interventions, including on surface streets. The ~219-mile I-45 corridor is among the densest U.S. freight lanes; Kodiak has operated there with safety drivers since 2019 for more than 3.5 million autonomous miles and is also stress-testing rare scenarios on closed courses to close its safety case.

### 11. Uber to shut Hong Kong’s FlyTaxi standalone app and fold it into Uber by year-end (Mobility platforms)
