### 3. U.S. draft bill would levy ~$130 yearly fee on EVs, ~$35 on plug-in hybrids (Policy / US)
**Summary:** USA Today, FOX56, and others reported on Nov 11 that the House Transportation and Infrastructure Committee’s BUILD America 250 Act would impose an annual federal registration fee of about $130 on covered battery-electric vehicles and about $35 on covered plug-in hybrids, rising by about $5 a year from around FY2029 to caps of about $150 and $50, collected by states at registration for Highway Trust Fund–related uses. The draft has cleared committee but still needs full House and Senate passage; current highway authorities run through Dec 11. Coverage notes the fee could, if enacted, align with a December timing window—but the legislative outcome remains uncertain.

**Links:**

## II. Autonomous Driving & Mobility Regulation

### 4. Finland approves Tesla Assisted Driving for immediate customer offer (Autonomy / Europe)
**Summary:** Euronews reported on Nov 11 that Finland’s Traficom approved Tesla’s Assisted Driving (TAD) system for Finnish roads, saying the company can start offering it to customers in Finland immediately. The system can steer, accelerate, and brake automatically, but drivers must remain in supervisory control; it was renamed from Full Self-Driving (FSD) Supervised to stress that it is driver assistance, not full autonomy. EU-wide rules for higher automation remain gridlocked. The Netherlands, Estonia, Belgium, Lithuania, and Denmark have already authorized similar systems; Tesla also offers the tech in the United States, Australia, Canada, and South Korea.

**Links:**

- [Euronews — Finland approves Tesla assisted driving cars ahead of EU vote](https://www.euronews.com/2026/11/11/finland-approves-tesla-assisted-driving-cars-ahead-of-eu-vote)

**Commentary:** Europe’s path is increasingly “approve nationally first, catch up at EU level later”—tighter naming and fragmented access arriving together.

### 6. NHTSA opens a recall query on ~2,895 Waymo vehicles over school-bus violations (Regulation / US)
**Summary:** TechCrunch, Reuters, and others reported that NHTSA’s Office of Defects Investigation opened a recall query covering about 2,895 vehicles with Waymo’s fifth-generation driving system to assess whether a November 2025 software remedy was adequate. The agency said a majority of reported incidents of vehicles passing stopped school buses with flashing red lights and/or extended stop arms occurred after Waymo said the fix had been completed. Waymo said it has improved school-bus behavior, received no related citations since March 2026, and will respond. A recall query is an investigation, not a final finding that the remedy failed.

## IV. Batteries & Energy Replenishment

### 11. CATL: swap network covers ~200 cities; says it will help accelerate sodium-ion adoption (Battery / China)
**Summary:** CnEVPost reported on Nov 10 that CATL chairman Robin Zeng told a Benchmark Mineral Intelligence event in Shanghai that sodium-ion batteries offer enough range for daily urban travel, while a rapidly expanding swap network can ease weekend long-trip range anxiety. CATL said swap stations already cover about 200 Chinese cities and aims for more than 3,000 passenger-car stations by year-end. Swapping can replace packs in minutes; the company is promoting sodium-ion as a lithium complement and plans scaled Naxtra sodium-ion use across its swap network.

**Links:**

- [CnEVPost — CATL says expanding swap network will help accelerate sodium-ion battery adoption](https://cnevpost.com/2026/11/10/catl-swap-network-help-sodium-ion-adoption/)

**Commentary:** Sodium-ion’s breakout often hinges less on slides than on an infrastructure story that “you can swap and go farther.”
