## I. Market and Channels

### 1. Cui Dongshu: the auto industry’s September sales-profit margin was only 1.4%, with January–September profit down 23% (market / China)
**Summary:** Cui Dongshu of the China Passenger Car Association wrote on October 27 that from January to September 2026 China produced 23.37 million vehicles, down 4% year on year, with revenue of 8,043.5 billion yuan, up 2.6%, costs of 7,162.7 billion yuan, up 3.8%, and profit of 267.5 billion yuan, down 23%, for a sales-profit margin of 3.3%. In September alone, output was 3.06 million units, sales revenue 1,037.4 billion yuan, up 0.6%, costs 925.7 billion yuan, up 2.5%, and profit 14.2 billion yuan, down 68%, for a 1.4% margin near a four-year low. He blamed rising upstream costs and weak demand, citing oil, nonferrous metals, memory chips and lithium carbonate, plus fuel-car pressure and the Mid-Autumn holiday in September. The average operating-revenue profit margin for industrial enterprises above designated size was about 5.68% over the same span.

**Commentary:** The 1.4% figure is the September month alone and 3.3% is the nine-month cumulative; flat revenue with collapsing profit shows how price wars and cost pressure are still chewing the margin.

### 2. Audi locks in its dual-brand China split: rings to FAW, lettermark to SAIC, with a transition through end-2027 (channels / China)

**Summary:** Electrek reported on October 27 that Elon Musk replied “It was never a real contest” under a Robotaxi Tracker chart for Austin. The chart shows Tesla logging about 438,000 driverless miles in Austin in the week ending October 18, slightly above Waymo’s roughly 406,000 weekly average in the city in the second quarter; nationwide, Waymo averaged about 3.90 million rider-only miles a week in Q2 while Tesla’s best week was about 558,000, a gap of roughly seven times. Austin accounted for about 79% of Tesla’s weekly driverless miles; Texas DMV lists show about 463 Cybercabs registered in the state, 337 of them added in 30 days. On the Q3 call Tesla said the Austin Cybercab fleet grew about tenfold in a month and still faces software limits on highways and weather.

### 5. Baidu Apollo Go and Lyft-owned Freenow unveil the RT6 in London, aiming for driverless commercial service in 2027 (autonomous driving / UK)
**Summary:** Automotive World reported on October 27 that Freenow, owned by Lyft, and Baidu’s Apollo Go unveiled the RT6 robotaxi in London, with fully driverless tests targeted next year and commercial service in 2027. The partners have tested with safety drivers in Brent since July; Freenow UK general manager Danny O’Gorman expects “tens of vehicles” in the next year or two and said regulatory guidelines from Transport for London could arrive within a week. The RT6 is purpose-built for ride-hailing, with a detachable steering wheel and a unit cost of about US$37,000. TfL said no operator has yet registered vehicles with the Vehicle Certification Agency, making unsupervised permits unlikely this year; Waymo, Wayve with Uber, and Pony.ai are also active in London. The piece warned that UK tariffs on China-built EVs could blunt the RT6’s cost edge.

**Links:**

- [Automotive World — Baidu and Lyft target 2027 for driverless London service](https://www.automotiveworld.com/news/baidu-and-lyft-target-2027-for-driverless-london-service/)

**Commentary:** London is as much a licensing and trade-policy test as a sensor-stack contest for Chinese robotaxis.

## III. EVs and New Models

### 6. Ford: home bidirectional hardware for the Fathom is about half the prior price, with more than 10 kW to the house (EV / US)
**Summary:** TechCrunch reported on October 27 that Ford launched two home charge-and-discharge products for its forthcoming Fathom electric pickup. The Home Power Station is about US$650, roughly 50% below the first-generation bidirectional charger, for time-of-use shifting and home discharge; Home Backup Power bundles that unit with a Home Backup Panel for about US$2,100, roughly 60% cheaper, to power some or all household loads in an outage. Ford said the Fathom starts at US$28,350 and can deliver “over 10 kW” to a home. Prices exclude installation, which for existing V2X setups can run up to about US$10,000. Axios the same day noted that PG&E’s V2G pilot still has fewer than 70 enrolled customers, while Ford said it is working with five major utilities to simplify interconnection.

## IV. M&A and Supply Chain

### 10. Tata Motors: about 87.81% of Iveco shareholders accept the €3.91 billion commercial-vehicle takeover (M&A / global)
**Summary:** The Hindu BusinessLine reported on October 27 that shareholders representing about 87.81% of Iveco equity—some 238.16 million shares—accepted Tata Motors’ commercial-vehicle unit’s roughly €3.91 billion offer for the Italian truck maker. Including treasury shares, the potential holding is about 89.42%. Final results are due October 29, with settlement targeted for October 30, still subject to offer conditions being met or waived. Below 95%, a Dutch squeeze-out is not available, though Tata can still pursue later delisting steps.

### 11. Clarios plans to acquire Ecobat’s U.S. critical-mineral recovery plants, processing more than 40 million batteries a year (supply chain / US)
**Summary:** Ecobat announced on October 27 that Clarios, a global low-voltage automotive battery and energy-storage company, plans to acquire its business, including three U.S. critical-mineral recovery and processing plants in City of Industry, California; Indianapolis; and Middletown, New York, plus the Dallas office. The sites together process more than 40 million batteries a year and recover lead, tin, antimony and other materials. Closing is expected in early calendar 2027, subject to customary conditions including regulatory approvals. Both sides framed the deal as strengthening domestic circular supply of critical minerals.

**Links:**

- [Ecobat — Clarios strengthens U.S. critical mineral supply chain with acquisition of Ecobat](https://ecobat.com/2026/10/clarios-strengthens-u-s-critical-mineral-supply-chain-with-acquisition-of-ecobat/)

**Commentary:** This consolidates the low-voltage lead-acid recycling loop rather than traction-battery materials, but it still matters for localizing automotive electrical-system supply.
