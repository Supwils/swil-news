## I. Markets & Policy

### 1. Thailand moves toward higher excise taxes on imported EVs, favoring local production (Policy / Southeast Asia)
**Summary:** Bloomberg and Channel News Asia reported on September 10 that Thailand's national EV policy board agreed in principle to a three-tier excise-tax structure: the lowest rates for vehicles made in Thailand with high local content, a middle band for imports tied to investment plans, and the highest rates for fully imported models—expected to exceed the current ~10% excise on imports. Excise chief Pornchai Thiraveja said the shift aims to blunt pressure from low-priced imported EVs, especially Chinese brands, on Thailand's large auto manufacturing base. A finance ministry official said specific rates should be set by month-end, with a possible grace period. The Board of Investment said electrified vehicles (EV/hybrid/PHEV) took ~55% of new-car registrations in the first seven months of 2026, outpacing ICE for the first time.

### 2. China's lithium-battery consumption tax sparks cost pass-through fights; entry-level BEVs feel it most (Policy / China)
**Summary:** National Business Daily and others reported on September 10 that, under a Ministry of Finance-led notice, lithium primary and lithium-ion batteries began facing a 2% consumption tax from September 1, 2026, rising back to 4% from September 1, 2027. EVE Energy and Lishen have notified customers of ~2% surcharges on ex-tax supply prices from September 1. Because the tax is embedded in price and cannot be credited when OEMs buy packs for NEV assembly, suppliers and carmakers are negotiating who absorbs the hit. Analysts say sub-RMB100,000 pure EVs and long-range models using large outsourced LFP packs are most exposed, while sodium-ion/solid-state (tax-exempt paths) and integrated leaders may accelerate market bifurcation.

**Links:**

- [National Business Daily — Battery tax, who pays? Supply-chain cost sharing heats up](https://www.nbd.com.cn/articles/2026-09-10/4577863.html)
- [JRJ — Lithium batteries taxed at 2%; EVE and Lishen add 2% cost](https://finance.jrj.com.cn/2026/09/10193558406292.shtml)

**Commentary:** After more than a decade of tax holidays, traction batteries enter an embedded-tax era—the thinnest-margin entry BEVs are first in line to be squeezed.

## II. Autonomous Driving & Mobility

### 4. Spain's DGT grants WeRide, Uber, and AVOMO the country's first national L4 passenger-AV permit (AV / Europe)
**Summary:** EFE and ABC reported on September 10 that Spain's traffic authority (DGT) issued the first national public-road authorization for Level 4 autonomous passenger vehicles under the ES-AV framework to WeRide, Uber, and AVOMO (Moove Cars' AV unit). The first phase plans ~20 WeRide GXR vehicles for HD mapping, route validation, and readiness testing in high-demand areas of Greater Madrid, initially with an onboard specialist. Commercial passenger operations are targeted for late 2026 if operational and regulatory milestones are met. The permit is also described as the first national EU-level homologation path for the WeRide GXR and Madrid as the fourth city in the WeRide–Uber alliance—and the partners' first planned European AV deployment.

## IV. Batteries & Charging

### 11. Hyundai–Kia and LG Energy Solution launch second-life battery ESS pilot tied to fast charging (Battery / Korea)
**Summary:** The Korea Herald reported on September 10 that Hyundai Motor Group, with Kia, LG Energy Solution, Hyundai Engineering, and Wonik PNE, launched Korea's first pilot linking retired Hyundai/Kia EV batteries directly to EV fast-charging storage at Wonik PNE's Hwaseong site. The demo uses a ~200 kWh used-battery energy storage system (UBESS) built from packs recovered from E-GMP EVs, charging off-peak and discharging to fast chargers at peak to ease grid stress, cut costs, and extend battery life. Hyundai/Kia lead technical validation and business models; LGES builds the UBESS and runs diagnostics/safety checks; Wonik PNE operates and tests the equipment.
