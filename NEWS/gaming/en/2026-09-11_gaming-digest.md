### 1. Bloomberg details why Sony walked away from Physint: budget, deadlines, and Death Stranding’s commercial return (Publishing)

## II. Streaming, Regulation & Esports

### 5. Florida sues Netflix, alleging family and children’s viewing data fueled its ad business (Regulation)
**Summary:** According to The Next Web and CBS12, Florida Attorney General James Uthmeier sued Netflix, claiming the company collected detailed viewing and behavioral data on families and children—then used it to build an advertising business despite earlier privacy-forward, ad-light positioning. The roughly 66-page complaint was filed around September 9 in St. Johns County circuit court and continued to drive coverage on September 11. The state cites the Florida Deceptive and Unfair Trade Practices Act and the Florida Digital Bill of Rights, seeking injunctions, deletion of allegedly deceptively collected data, civil penalties, and other relief; Uthmeier said the state would seek “billions” in damages. Netflix called the lawsuit without merit.

### 8. Shanghai’s first AI voice-cloning unfair-competition case: miHoYo wins ¥750,000 over Genshin character voices (IP)
**Summary:** According to The Paper, 17173, and TMTPost, Shanghai Pudong New Area People’s Court disclosed the city’s first AI voice-imitation unfair-competition case: an AI voice-changer sold packs cloning 63 Genshin Impact character voices without authorization and used character art and story footage for promotion. The court held that the voices had become core auditory identifiers of the game’s commercial source, finding copyright infringement and unfair competition, and ordered the defendant to stop and pay ¥750,000 in damages and reasonable costs. The defendant appealed then withdrew; the judgment is effective. miHoYo’s legal team publicly noted the final resolution on September 11.
