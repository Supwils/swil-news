## II. Regulation and Platforms

### 6. Norway opens a consultation on gambling-like game mechanics, with replies due January 12, 2027 (regulation)
**Summary:** On October 12, Norway's Ministry of Culture and Equality sent a report out for public consultation. The Norwegian Gambling and Foundation Authority prepared it with the Media Authority, the Norwegian Film Institute, and the Consumer Authority, and delivered it on October 1. The report proposes changing gambling rules so that more gambling-like elements in or attached to video games fall under the Gambling Act. The stated example is a loot box paid for with real money, directly or through virtual currency, when the player does not know the item in advance. If adopted, offering and marketing such mechanics without permission would be prohibited, and the authority could use enforcement tools including DNS blocking under section 32a. The report sets out three alternative models. All of them would define in regulation which digital values count as a stake and a prize, and which games and mechanics fall outside the act. The consultation deadline is January 12, 2027.

**Links:**

- [Norwegian government — Consultation on gambling-like mechanics](https://www.regjeringen.no/no/dokumenter/horing-av-lotteritilsynets-utredning-om-regulering-av-pengespill-lignende-elementer-i-dataspill/id3176704/)

**Commentary:** What opened today is a consultation, not a ban already in force.

### 9. France brings games with monetisable digital objects into its gambling-ad agreement (regulation)
**Summary:** EEGaming reported on October 12 that France's gambling regulator, the ANJ, and the advertising self-regulator ARPP renewed their cooperation agreement on October 7, extending the 2023 text to games with monetisable digital objects, known as JONUM. ANJ president Pascal Chevremont and ARPP president Christine Albanel signed it. The piece says JONUM comes from the May 2024 SREN law, whose implementing decree took effect on February 7, 2026: a player who spends money can obtain monetisable digital objects such as NFTs or game tokens through a chance-based mechanism, and operators must declare in advance to the ANJ. The declared list includes Cylimit, Sorare, and Meta Football League. Commercial communications must carry a problem-gambling warning, though the wording and format still await a later order, and they must not be sent to minors or to self-excluded accounts.

**Links:**

- [EEGaming — ANJ and ARPP renew their agreement](https://eegaming.org/latest-news/2026/10/12/53085/france-adds-new-obligations-to-sign-off-gambling-ad-campaigns/)
