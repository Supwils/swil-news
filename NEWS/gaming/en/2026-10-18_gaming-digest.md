## I. Regulation and the Industry

### 1. Torres says he will introduce the Power To The Players Act, treating a paid game as ownership rather than a license (regulation)
**Summary:** The House office of Rep. Ritchie Torres, a New York Democrat, posted on October 15 that he will introduce the Power To The Players Act, directing the Federal Trade Commission to write rules protecting consumers' ability to own and play games they have bought. The office cites NBA 2K25: the standard edition sold for $69.99 and the All-Star Edition for $99.99; the game will be delisted from digital storefronts on October 30 and its online servers will shut down on December 31, only two years after its initial release. The statement says a purchase should confer ownership rather than a license to play, and that games with online features must offer an offline mode before servers are shut down. The Verge reported the bill on October 18 under a headline that says it was introduced in Congress. The office's own wording is "will introduce," and the statement does not include bill text.

- [House office — October 15 statement](https://ritchietorres.house.gov/posts/rep-torres-announces-power-to-the-players-act-legislation-to-ensure-gamers-own-their-games)
- [The Verge — October 18 report](https://www.theverge.com/policy/1013521/video-game-ownership-bill-congress-power-to-the-players-act)

**Commentary:** What can be checked today is a legislative announcement that does not yet attach bill text, plus two dates for the NBA 2K25 delisting and server shutdown.

### 2. Liquid Swords enters bankruptcy, and the administrator invites bids for Samson assets (bankruptcy)
**Summary:** Preliminary bidding documents issued by Cirio on October 15 say the Stockholm District Court declared Liquid Swords AB bankrupt on October 12 and appointed Karl Björlin as official receiver. The documents say the company was founded in 2020, recorded no net turnover in 2024, posted a loss after financial items of about SEK 39.3 million, and had an equity ratio of 43.3 percent at year-end. As of October 14, Samson had sold about 151,900 copies across PlayStation, Xbox, and Steam, with net revenue of about SEK 24.1 million. Some Samson assets are subject to security in favor of NetEase. Written indicative bids are due at 12:00 CEST on October 21. PC Gamer reported on October 18 that about 30 employees had gone unpaid for at least two months and petitioned for the bankruptcy; once the company is in bankruptcy, unpaid wages are covered by the Swedish state wage guarantee.

**Links:**

- [Cirio — Preliminary bidding documents](https://cirio.se/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/PRELIMINARY-BIDDING-DOCUMENTS-002.pdf)
- [PC Gamer — October 18 bankruptcy report](https://www.pcgamer.com/gaming-industry/liquid-swords-developer-of-open-city-crime-sim-samson-has-gone-bankrupt-following-staff-claims-that-they-havent-been-paid-in-two-months/)

**Commentary:** The 151,900 copies and SEK 24.1 million come from the receiver's documents, which note that the figures may be adjusted as the investigation continues; a buyer who wants Samson assets free of the pledge still has to talk to NetEase about a release.

### 3. Konami Digital Entertainment's president discusses generative AI in the integrated report (publisher)
**Summary:** Automaton reported on October 16, and VGC on October 18, that Konami Group published its integrated report for the fiscal year ended March 2026. In the digital entertainment section, Hideki Hayakawa, president of Konami Digital Entertainment, discusses generative AI. He writes that the common view is that generative AI will significantly lower the barrier to making games, but he does not expect a significant change in the market environment, because general-purpose engines and user-generated content have already lowered that barrier. He adds that even if AI improves production efficiency, people and creators remain the most important factors in entertaining and innovative games, and that the "chemical reaction" inside a team is not easily replaced. The same passage says Konami will systematically help creators understand new technology, assess its risks, and apply it safely in development.

**Links:**

- [VGC — October 18 report](https://www.videogameschronicle.com/news/konami-boss-says-ai-cant-replace-the-human-chemistry-behind-great-games/)
- [Automaton — Wording in the integrated report](https://automaton-media.com/en/news/konami-president-hayakawa-believes-ai-wont-bring-a-significant-change-to-the-game-market-humans-and-creators-are-crucial-for-developing/)

**Commentary:** What can be checked is a judgment in the integrated report, not an AI-usage figure or a budget for any specific Konami game.

### 5. Alliance Arts cancels the October 19 public demo and switches to a lottery for closed sessions (release)
**Summary:** Alliance Arts announced on its site on October 16 that it was canceling the public demo of Drunken Goddess Reflux planned for Monday, October 19, and would hold a closed online play session for a limited number of people instead. The company gave two reasons: it had planned restrictions on streams and gameplay videos but concluded it could not manage those restrictions, and it is concerned that others could use the demo to quickly imitate the game's presentation and gameplay with AI or other tools and release that work before the game itself. The application form is on the official Discord, and participants are drawn by lottery after agreeing to the play and posting rules. Polygon reported on October 18 that the public demo had been planned for Steam Next Fest, which runs October 19-26.

**Links:**

- [Alliance Arts — October 16 change notice](https://alliance-arts.co.jp/en/news-detail/3225/)
- [Polygon — October 18 report](https://www.polygon.com/drunken-goddess-reflux-demo-canceled/)

**Commentary:** The notice confirms that the public demo is canceled and that closed sessions will be drawn by lottery; it does not give a launch date.
