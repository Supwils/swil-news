### 2. Activision apologizes for Itaewon crowd-crush footage in Modern Warfare 4; Nov. 12 update will remove it (controversy)
**Summary:** NBC News, VGC, and Korean outlets reported that Activision apologized to South Korean media and confirmed it will fully remove roughly 10 seconds of real news footage from Call of Duty: Modern Warfare 4’s opening montage in a November 12 update. Players identified the clip as coverage of the Oct. 29, 2022 Itaewon crowd crush in Seoul that killed about 159 people; the game uses archival news imagery to sell a fictional peninsula-wide war. Bereaved-family groups demanded removal. Activision said it never intended to evoke or trivialize the disaster and apologized to victims’ families and those distressed by the material. The game launched globally about a week earlier.

**Links:**

- [NBC News — Outcry prompts removal of Seoul tragedy footage](https://www.nbcnews.com/world/asia/outcry-prompts-call-duty-cut-seoul-tragedy-footage-rcna606496)

**Commentary:** When “real news immersion” collides with a still-grieving public tragedy, patch speed and apology tone matter more than creative intent.

### 3. Rockstar UK staff to vote on union recognition during the GTA VI launch window; unit could top 1,600 workers (labor)
**Summary:** The Independent Workers’ Union of Great Britain (IWGB), Game File, The Verge, and PC Gamer confirmed that Rockstar’s UK employees will hold an anonymous online recognition vote from November 12–26, bracketing Grand Theft Auto VI’s November 19 console launch. A yes vote would create a bargaining unit of more than 1,600 workers below director level across Rockstar’s UK studios—potentially the largest recognised union in the UK games sector—covering pay and bonuses, overtime, redundancies, flexible working, and generative AI policies. Closing arguments in a Scottish employment tribunal over 2025 dismissals of more than 30 union-linked staff also fall inside the ballot window; Rockstar has tied those firings to misconduct, while the union calls them busting.

**Links:**

- [The Verge — Rockstar union vote overlaps GTA VI launch](https://www.theverge.com/games/1019719/rockstar-workers-will-vote-on-union-recognition-around-gta-vis-launch)

## III. Platform Policy, Esports, and VR

### 9. Valve at Steam Indie Connect: no AI-tool gatekeeping; fix discovery instead (platform)
**Summary:** At Steam Indie Connect in Busan, Steam Business Team members Tom Giardino and Marc Wilkinson told Korean developers that AI-tool use is each studio’s choice and Valve does not want to be a gatekeeper of which tools are allowed. Facing complaints about AI-heavy catalogs during Next Fest, they said a significant number of staff are working on visibility and ranking rather than bans by tool type. On reports of extortion emails threatening AI clones unless paid, Giardino said Valve would look into cases but framed extortion as closer to a law-enforcement issue, urging depth and ongoing updates over single copyable mechanics. VGC, GamesRadar+, and TechSpot carried the remarks.

**Links:**

- [VGC — Valve will not gatekeep AI games](https://www.videogameschronicle.com/news/valve-has-a-significant-number-working-on-steams-visibility-dilemma-but-says-it-wont-gatekeep-games-made-with-ai/)

### 10. Celeste and TowerFall delisted from Xbox as developer cites BDS boycott of Microsoft (platform)
**Summary:** After players noticed Celeste and TowerFall missing from the Xbox Store this week, Maddy Makes Games’ Maddy Thorson confirmed on Bluesky that the studio deliberately delisted both titles to support the BDS boycott targeting Microsoft. The games remain for sale on PlayStation, Nintendo, and non-Microsoft PC storefronts; existing Xbox owners keep library access and can still download. Polygon, GamesRadar+, and others noted it as another high-profile indie exit after House House kept Big Walk off Xbox for the same reason.
