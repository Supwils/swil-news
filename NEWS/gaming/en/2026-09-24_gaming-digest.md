## II. Regulation, Litigation, and Studio Pressure

### 4. Nintendo Wins $4.5 Million Default Judgment Against Reddit Mod Over Switch Piracy (Litigation)
**Summary:** The Verge reported on September 24 that a Washington federal judge entered a default judgment ordering Reddit moderator James Williams (online as “archbox”) to pay Nintendo about $4.5 million and shut down online stores and social accounts allegedly used to distribute pirated Switch games. Nintendo sued in 2024, claiming Williams was a leading moderator of r/SwitchPirates and solicited eShop gift cards and game copies for pirate shops. The award reflects $150,000 per work across 30 titles, including Animal Crossing: New Horizons, Luigi’s Mansion 3, and Pikmin 4—continuing Nintendo’s recent wave of copyright enforcement.

### 5. Dutch Consumer Group Targets Epic Over Fortnite “Dark Patterns,” Seeking Over €100 Million (Regulation)
**Summary:** Dualshockers, Emerce, and others reported on September 24 that Dutch foundation Stichting Massaschade & Consument (SMC) served Epic Games with a liability notice ahead of a mass claim over Fortnite marketing practices aimed at players under 21, seeking more than €100 million in refunds and damages plus further compensation for alleged collection of children’s data without parental consent. SMC cites a study of Dutch players aged 16–19 in which about 60% said V-Bucks purchases “didn’t feel like real money” and roughly half regretted buys made under perceived time pressure. Dutch regulators previously fined Epic €1.125 million over countdown timers and purchase prompts; a Rotterdam court upheld the fine in January 2026. SMC says that penalty is a fraction of Fortnite earnings in the Netherlands and will pursue settlement before court.
