## II. Regulation and Industry

### 4. PEGI starts moving existing digital games with paid random items to 16 (regulation)
**Summary:** VGC reported on October 21 that European ratings body PEGI has started raising age ratings to 16 for games with paid random items. Titles already showing the higher rating include the EA Sports FC games, Pokemon GO, Pokemon TCG Pocket, Mario Kart Tour, and HoYoverse's Zenless Zone Zero. Connor Hinde, policy manager at the Games Rating Authority, the UK administrator of PEGI, said newly submitted games have been assessed against expanded criteria since June, and that the authority is now applying those criteria to selected existing digital products. Existing digital-only games rated PEGI 3, 7, or 12 that include paid random items will be updated to PEGI 16 – Paid random items. He said the changes begin rolling out this week and through the rest of October. VGC also writes that PEGI said earlier this year a paid-random-item default of 16 means the games cannot legally be sold to children 15 or under in the UK or other European countries that have written PEGI into law.

**Links:**

- [VGC — October 21](https://www.videogameschronicle.com/news/ea-sports-fc-and-pokemon-go-age-ratings-bumped-to-16-in-pegi-loot-box-crackdown/)

**Commentary:** What landed today is a rescoring of existing digital editions, not a new statute; VGC's account of where a PEGI 16 rating is a legal sales age is the ratings system's own explanation.

### 5. A Korean civic group files a tax complaint against Google and five game companies (regulation)
**Summary:** The Asia Business Daily reported on October 21 that the Citizens' Coalition for Economic Justice held a press conference that day and filed a complaint with the Financial Supervisory Service and the National Tax Service against Google and five domestic game companies, NC, Nexon, Netmarble, Com2uS, and Pearl Abyss, over alleged tax evasion and accounting irregularities tied to Project Hug, also called GVP. The group says Google offered fee rebates and marketing support in exchange for those companies not releasing games on competing app marketplaces, and that the companies may have booked the full 30 percent in-app fee as an expense without recognizing the rebates and discounts as taxable income. The report says the Fair Trade Commission had already found Google's exclusive app-marketplace agreements unlawful and opened a review, and that in July it reportedly proposed corrective orders and fines of up to 850 billion won, without sanctioning the game companies in GVP. A representative of one named company told the paper the accounting and tax allegations are unfounded.

**Links:**

- [The Asia Business Daily — October 21](https://www.asiae.co.kr/en/article/2026102116514299495)

**Commentary:** This is a civic complaint and a set of allegations, not a finding by a regulator; the fine figure is reported as what the commission reportedly proposed.

### 6. Krafton releases a preview of its in-house language model, Raon-1 mini (industry)
**Summary:** Seoul Economic Daily reported on October 21 that Krafton published a preview of Raon-1 mini, a large language model it designed and trained itself, on Hugging Face under an Apache 2.0 license, free to download for research and commercial development. The company told the paper it is about a 30-billion-parameter model that activates about 4 billion parameters for a given task, combined with in-house linear attention to cut inference cost, and that on some metrics it beats overseas models of similar size and approaches larger domestic models. The model card lists 28.8 billion total parameters and 4.3 billion activated per token, a native context of 262,144 tokens, and a note that specs may change before the final release. Chief AI officer Lee Kang-wook said the release rests on more than five years of AI research, that the preview will be improved before the official version, and that a technical report will be published separately.
