## II. China and Asia

### 6. Kunlun Tech releases and open-sources Matrix-Game 4.0 (product)
**Summary:** China Securities Journal reported on October 15 that Kunlun Tech released Matrix-Game 4.0, a video-model game that runs on a single consumer graphics card, and open-sourced it the same day. On one NVIDIA RTX 5090, the report says it can run at 480p and 60 frames per second, with end-to-end latency from input to image under 150 milliseconds, a steady single-step denoising rate of 83 frames per second, and peak memory of 18.5 GiB. The stack is a coding agent, an engine that keeps world state and rules, and a video model that draws the frame; natural language can change scenes and play. Yicai quoted the company calling it the first video-model game to run on a single consumer GPU. Later work mentioned in the securities-journal report includes higher resolution, view consistency in multiplayer, and more game types.

## III. Regulation and Esports

### 9. The EU consumer-protection network opens coordinated action against nine game companies (regulation)
**Summary:** Spain's Ministry of Social Rights, Consumer Affairs and the 2030 Agenda said on October 14 that the EU Consumer Protection Cooperation Network started coordinated action against nine video-game companies, applying EU consumer law to paid virtual items. The titles named are Hunt: Showdown 1896, Forge of Empires, Candy Crush Saga, Minecraft, Mech Arena, Gardenscapes, Valorant, Clash of Clans, and For Honor, tied to Crytek, InnoGames, King, Mojang, Plarium, PLR Worldwide, Riot, Supercell, and Ubisoft EMEA. The notice asks for real-money prices, a ban on hiding cost behind stacked virtual currencies, a ban on making paid items necessary to progress, information before purchase, a 14-day withdrawal right for unused virtual items, and protection for vulnerable users, especially children. Lewis Silkin wrote on October 15 that the network also adopted a joint statement and is looking in particular at loot boxes aimed at children and at dark patterns. The firm said the European Commission has reportedly indicated it intends to publish a draft Digital Fairness Act in November.

### 10. Brazil proposes a lower loan floor for game companies alongside industry rules (regulation)
**Summary:** Valor reported on October 15 that President Lula, while signing regulations for the Legal Framework for the Video Game Industry on October 14, proposed easier credit for Brazilian game companies. The Ministry of Culture said the minimum loan for the sector would fall from BRL 500,000 to BRL 150,000, and annual interest from the reference rate TR plus 2% to TR plus 0.5%. The proposal is to be put forward by film agency Ancine with the management committee of the Audiovisual Sector Fund. The fund has BRL 460 million in credit earmarked for the audiovisual industry in 2026, including games. The decree also creates Game Brasil, a free public catalog and promotion platform, with a planned initial investment of BRL 1.4 million, plus Rouanet Games to help find sponsorship under the Rouanet Law. Two further programs, for labs and for making and selling independent Brazilian games, are still being built and are not expected before 2027; the ministry estimates raising BRL 50 million for each.

### 11. Brazil's Justice Ministry asks the data authority to watch six loot-box games (regulation)
**Summary:** Brazil's Ministry of Justice and Public Security said on October 15 that its digital-rights secretariat, in Technical Note 55/2026, asked the national data-protection authority ANPD to monitor loot-box games that have not put age checks in place. The note recommends priority checks on NBA 2K26, WWE 2K26, Counter-Strike 2, Call of Duty, Genshin Impact, and PUBG Mobile. It says the review of 15 high-user titles or franchises in Brazil is preliminary and not exhaustive, and cannot by itself show that any supplier complies or breaks the law. The cited TIC Kids Online Brazil 2025 survey says 70% of users aged 9 to 17 played online. Article 23 of Decree 12,880/2026 offers three paths: verify age and block the feature for children and adolescents, ship a version without loot boxes, or turn the feature off by default for users in Brazil. Warnings, published odds, and an 18 rating do not replace those steps. The note is also sent to the health ministry's mental-health department.

### 12. Mexico orders games and social platforms to verify age; a typed age is not enough (regulation)
**Summary:** ID Tech reported on October 15 that President Claudia Sheinbaum signed a decree on October 14, published the same day in the official gazette, requiring social networks, video-game services, app stores, and other platforms to verify users' ages. A user simply declaring an age is not enough. Platforms must keep minors from content that promotes cyberbullying, sexual abuse and exploitation, hate speech and violent extremism, illegal conduct, pornography, suicide, self-harm, and eating disorders, and must give parents supervision tools. New accounts default to the strictest privacy and safety settings. The decree took effect on publication. Platforms have up to 30 calendar days to document and report the measures they have put in place. Sheinbaum said that if compliance is not visible, the government would move to a law that also includes sanctions. The Digital Transformation Agency oversees compliance.

**Links:**

- [ID Tech — Effective date and the 30-day report window](https://idtechwire.com/mexico-decree-requires-social-media-and-gaming-platforms-to-verify-users-ages/)

**Commentary:** What can be checked today is that the decree is in force and that the report window is 30 days; sanctions are not yet in the current text.

### 13. Brazil recognizes esports as a sporting activity by decree, without automatic funding (esports)
**Summary:** Dot Esports reported on October 15 that Decree 13,138, signed by Lula on October 14, regulates Law 14,852/2024, the games legal framework sanctioned in May 2024. Article 20 defines esports as a competitive sporting manifestation, individual or collective, played through digital games with set rules. Article 21 lets esports organizations join the National Sports System, Sinesp, and take part in public policies on tournament support, research, athlete development, social inclusion, and integrity, including match-fixing. Cadu Albuquerque, national esports director at the Ministry of Sport, said recognition is a legal basis for sports policy, not an automatic public subsidy, and not state control of esports management. The report says Riot's Brazilian League of Legends championship, CBLOL, and Ubisoft's regional Rainbow Six Siege circuit do not have to change format, calendar, or commercial model because of the decree. The same report says the Ministry of Education will create undergraduate gaming programs at five federal universities, with 230 accredited places.

- [Dot Esports — Decree 13,138 and Sinesp](https://dotesports.com/general/news/brazil-esports-recognized-sport-decree)

**Commentary:** What is clear today is legal status and a route into the sports system; money still depends on later programs.
