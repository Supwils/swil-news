## I. M&A and Capital

### 1. Reuters: Silver Lake and Saudi PIF in Talks to Buy Unity at About $25 Billion (M&A)
**Summary:** Multiple outlets citing Reuters report that Silver Lake and Saudi Arabia’s Public Investment Fund (PIF) have approached Unity Software about a full buyout, with a valuation discussed around $25 billion. Silver Lake already holds roughly 8% of Unity; PIF is said to have initiated outreach. Unity has engaged a bank to evaluate options; no deal is certain and terms could change. The same investor pairing recently took EA private in a roughly $55 billion LBO. Unity’s engine powers a large share of mobile and console/PC titles, so a deal would reshape the tools layer of the industry.

### 2. Fox Closes About $22 Billion Roku Deal Early; DOJ Closes Probe With Open-Platform Commitments (Streaming · M&A)
**Summary:** Fox Corp. said on November 6 it has completed its acquisition of Roku at a valuation of about $22 billion, ahead of the previously expected first-half 2027 close. Roku’s platform reaches more than 100 million households; founder Anthony Wood joins the Fox board and will co-chair the new FOX Digital Platforms unit (Roku, Tubi, FOX One, and more) with Paul Cheesbrough. Fox expects about $400 million in run-rate cost synergies within two years. The U.S. Justice Department said the same day it closed its antitrust review, finding substantial competitive harm unlikely, while recording Fox commitments to keep carrying non-Fox content, avoid search discrimination, and maintain data firewalls for at least three years. Roku shares ceased Nasdaq trading.

**Links:**

- [Fox Corporation — Completes Acquisition of Roku](https://www.foxcorporation.com/news/corp-press-releases/2026/fox-corporation-completes-acquisition-of-roku/)

**Commentary:** Legacy broadcast is buying the CTV shelf; regulators traded a green light for three-year neutrality promises—the real test starts when those clocks run out.

### 3. China Clears ByteDance’s Moonton Sale to Savvy/PIF for Over $60 Billion (M&A · China)
**Summary:** China’s State Administration for Market Regulation published its unconditional merger-clearance list for October 26–November 1, including Saudi Electronic Games Holding’s acquisition of Moonton Holdings and related equity, cleared on October 29. That is the Chinese antitrust step; ByteDance had already signed a definitive deal with PIF-backed Savvy Games Group to sell Moonton for more than $60 billion, with closing still subject to other conditions. Moonton, headquartered in Shanghai and known for Mobile Legends: Bang Bang and other titles, is expected to become a wholly owned Savvy subsidiary while keeping its management structure and CEO Zhang Yunfan, according to reports.

### 7. SAG-AFTRA Rolls Out Pre-Funding Pitch Agreement for Union Talent on Game Demos (Industry)
**Summary:** SAG-AFTRA introduced the Pre-Funding Pitch Interactive Media Agreement for trailers, teasers, demos, vertical slices, sizzle reels, and playable prototypes made solely to solicit production funding. Eligibility generally requires no more than about 30% of initial production funding secured; the material must not feel like a complete game; it cannot be commercially released or opened to public play; crowdfunding-released projects are typically ineligible, with possible waivers. President Sean Astin and CEO Duncan Crabtree-Ireland said the framework helps developers hire voice and motion talent earlier to improve funding odds.

## III. Regulation and Regional Markets

### 9. Video Games Europe and 22 Groups Open Letter on EU Digital Fairness Act (Regulation · Europe)
**Summary:** GamesMarket reported on November 6 that Video Games Europe and 21 other organizations sent a joint open letter urging the European Commission to avoid excessive regulation under the forthcoming Digital Fairness Act. The industry warns of stacking new rules on existing consumer-protection frameworks, raising compliance costs and chilling innovation. The letter does not change draft law, but marks a coordinated lobbying push before final text lands.

### 10. Korea’s GRAC Warns Dokkaebi’s World Over RMT Remarks; Rating Review Possible (Regulation · Korea)
**Summary:** South Korea’s Game Rating and Administration Committee said on November 6 it will monitor whether Dokkaebi’s World (Kakao Games) remains appropriately rated after a business lead said on a livestream that moderate item selling would not be blocked. GRAC rules treat systems that allow user item trades via paid currency as leaning toward an Adults Only rating; the committee said it will also watch actual trading behavior. Remarks alone are not a direct statutory offense, but the clash with published operating policy has drawn scrutiny and possible reclassification risk.
