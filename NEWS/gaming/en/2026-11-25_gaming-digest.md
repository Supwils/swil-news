### 1. Ubisoft reportedly asks new projects to explain generative AI use—or justify skipping it; Motherbrain unit lands (AI)
**Summary:** Video Games Chronicle reported on November 25, citing Vertical Slice journalist Chris Kerr’s sources, that Ubisoft is asking teams pitching new games to outline how generative AI will be used in development—and that teams that do not plan to use it must explain why. ### 3. Capcom sets Exoprimal end of service for September 29, 2027 (shutdown)

## II. Data, Funding & Regulation

### 5. Alinea: Call of Duty DAUs fell ~42% after GTA VI, ~44% on PlayStation (data)
**Summary:** PC Gamer and others on November 25 amplified Alinea Analytics estimates from market-analysis head Rhys Elliott: after GTA VI launched, Call of Duty HQ daily active users across PlayStation, Xbox, and Steam dropped from about 7.5 million to about 4.4 million (~42%). On PlayStation, CoD’s main platform, the hit was about 44%, and four days later activity was still roughly 41% below baseline—slower to recover than Fortnite (~28% console DAU drop) or EA Sports FC 27 (~27%). The figures are analyst estimates, not Activision disclosures; commentary also flagged a possible second wave when GTA Online and the PC version arrive.

### 6. UK funding package tops £45m for creatives; eight games-related firms win Next Wave grants (funding)
**Summary:** The UK Department for Digital, Culture, Media and Sport and UKRI announced on November 25 a creative-industries package in which games studios, music-tech firms, and film/TV innovators share more than £45 million (UKRI’s broader framing is about £49 million), inside a wider £500 million Creative Industries Sector Plan. ### 7. EU Digital Fairness Act draft opens consultation: paid loot boxes and pay-to-win for minors in the crosshairs (regulation)
**Summary:** The European Commission published a draft Digital Fairness Act on November 11 and, from around November 23, has been taking comments from citizens, businesses, and associations through a window that in principle closes January 14, 2027. Draft articles on virtual currency, variable-reward systems, and pay-to-win / pay-to-progress would require loot-box odds disclosure and an easy opt-out for all players; paid loot boxes would be banned for minors with no parental override; pay-to-win and pay-to-progress would also be off-limits to minors; and virtual currency would have to show real-money value. Rules would apply to any company targeting EU consumers regardless of domicile. The text remains a draft pending Parliament and Council negotiation.

**Links:**

- [FedGamer — EU consultation to ban loot boxes and pay-to-win for minors](https://fedgamer.net/en/2026/11/25/eu-loot-boxes-minors-digital-fairness-act/)

**Commentary:** Moving from “disclose odds” to “no paid randomness for minors” would raise localization and compliance cost for every global live-service title.
