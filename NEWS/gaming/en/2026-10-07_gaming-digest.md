### 2. Google Opens Playground, an Experiment That Turns Text Prompts into Browser Games (Platform)
**Summary:** Google's blog announced on October 7 that the experimental gaming platform Playground is live the same day for users 18 and older in the United States at playground.google. People describe rules, characters, and environments in text and get a playable game without coding. A project can stay private, be shared by link, or be published to the Explore gallery. Google said selected genres support leaderboards and multiplayer, and that published games go through safety screenings aligned with its community guidelines. The post also said Unity Spark is in testing, with a closed beta coming soon, to add more complete mechanics and high-fidelity 3D. The Verge quoted Google spokesperson Nia Carter the same day: Playground runs on Gemini, Nano Banana, and Lyria with a custom harness; the platform is free to use, and Google One subscribers get higher weekly token limits by plan. Carter said a closed beta for Unity Spark is scheduled later this year and that a waitlist opens today. The official blog described tiered creation access as rolling out by Google AI subscription, which is not the same wording as the Google One account Carter gave The Verge.

**Links:**

- [Google blog — Playground: create and play custom games](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/ai/playground-experimental-gaming-platform/)
- [The Verge — Google now lets you make games with AI](https://www.theverge.com/tech/1006477/google-playground-unity-spark-ai)

### 10. Brazil Submits 13,241 Illegal Betting Addresses in 12 Days as Refunds Move to the Banks (Regulation)
**Summary:** iGaming Brazil reported on October 7 that a task force led by the Finance Ministry's Secretariat of Prizes and Betting and the National Public Security Secretariat's Ciberlab presented results on Tuesday. After a provisional measure banning gambling-sector operations took effect, federal authorities submitted 13,241 web addresses for immediate blocking in 12 days, an average of 1,103 a day. Finance Ministry official Dario Durigan said the sweep also required removal of 3,552 profiles and groups on Telegram, TikTok, WhatsApp, and Discord, pages that together reached about 17.2 million followers. The report said about R$1.3 billion in frozen funds will be returned to 26.5 million bettors, and that 1% of that group holds 80% of the money. Forty-eight accounts holding R$500,000 to R$1 million are under investigation for possible organized-crime ties. Voluntary withdrawals ended at 23:59 on Monday. Operators have until the end of Wednesday to send CPF numbers, balances, and original account details to financial institutions. Banks process automatic refunds from October 9 through 14, and amounts that fail registration move to Caixa Econômica Federal from the 14th.
