## II. Regulation and Regional Markets

### 5. India's Supreme Court dismisses gaming firms' review pleas, leaving two state bans in place (regulation)
**Summary:** The Economic Times reported on October 26 that the Supreme Court dismissed a batch of review petitions filed by gaming companies led by Junglee Games India. Justices J. B. Pardiwala and R. Mahadevan wrote that they had gone through the review petitions and the connected papers, found no merit, and dismissed them. The report looks back to May 27, when the same bench upheld Tamil Nadu and Karnataka laws banning online games played for a bet, money, or other stake, including games of skill. The judgment said that once betting and gambling enter, the nature of the game ceases to matter, and that betting and gambling are treated as outside commerce, so no fundamental right can be claimed to carry them on. The report also names Play Games24x7, Head Digital Works, the All India Gaming Federation, and the Federation of Indian Fantasy Sports among those that had supported the high-court rulings striking the bans down.

**Links:**

- [The Economic Times — October 26](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/india/supreme-court-rejects-gaming-firms-review-pleas-upholds-tamil-nadu-and-karnataka-online-gaming-bans/articleshow/135213645.cms)

**Commentary:** What is new is the dismissal of the reviews; the May 27 holding, that a game of skill can still be banned once stakes are involved, stands.

### 6. Canada's identity minister says he will consider adding gaming platforms to the digital safety bill (regulation)
**Summary:** The Globe and Mail published on October 26 an interview, given Friday, with Federal Identity Minister Marc Miller. He said the digital safety bill, which the report identifies as Bill C-34, will make social-media companies block users under 16 from having accounts, with exemptions if a company persuades a new digital safety regulator that its safeguards are sufficient. Child-protection advocates have raised Roblox and Discord, platforms where children and adults can communicate, as outside the bill. Miller said he would "absolutely consider" changing the bill through regulations to make gaming platforms safer for children, and that he would listen to suggestions in committee. He also said gaming platforms would not be covered by the under-16 social-media ban because they are not social media, calling them "a different beast – a menacing one at that." He said an Order in Council process in the bill could carve in certain aspects of gaming, and he gave proper age verification and parental controls for a given age group as examples. In last week's second-reading debate, Conservative heritage critic Rachael Thomas asked why platforms such as Roblox and Minecraft are outside the bill.

**Links:**

- [The Globe and Mail — digital safety bill and gaming platforms](https://www.theglobeandmail.com/politics/article-digital-safety-bill-gaming-platforms-marc-miller/)

**Commentary:** The minister said he is open to carving gaming in, and he also said the under-16 account ban does not cover those platforms now.

### 8. Globoplay opens the premium catalog to its ad-supported plan (streaming)
**Summary:** Valor International reported on October 26 that Globo's Globoplay, in Brazil, is changing its subscriptions on Monday. Julia Rueff, Globo's executive director of digital platforms, said the company is giving more people access to content that had been limited to premium subscribers. The ad-supported standard plan keeps what it already had and gains the premium catalog, plus more than 20 live channels, including sportv, Combate, Multishow, GNT, Globoplay Novelas, and GloboNews, and more than 2,500 live sporting events a year on sportv. Globo does not disclose a subscriber total and says 30 million people access Globoplay each month.
