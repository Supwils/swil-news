## II. Security, Results, and Funding

### 4. Capcom's second notice: e-Capcom data viewed on 19 Japanese accounts (security)
**Summary:** Capcom's second disclosure, dated October 23, says that of the 196 CAPCOM ID accounts whose login passwords were changed without authorization, 19 accounts in Japan had their e-Capcom My Page opened by a third party. The exposed fields are name, name in kana, phone number, postal code, address, gender, date of birth, purchase history, inquiry history, and the member address book. The notice says credit-card data was not among the exposed information, and that no further leak from the password changes was found on the other accounts. It says the scope review is complete and that monitoring of CAPCOM ID will continue. The English notice of October 22 says 11,774,247 records worldwide had an email address plus account-creation time, last-login time, login count, and last-login IP exposed; 43 of those also included date of birth, gender, country or region, and nickname. Capcom says it has not confirmed that passwords originally set by customers were themselves taken, and that the 196 changed passwords were reset. Games and CAPCOM ID remain available.

- [Capcom English IR — first notice, October 22](https://www.capcom.co.jp/ir/english/news/html/e261022.html)

## III. Asia, Platforms, and Esports

### 8. TechNode reports GameScaling as open source; the repo lists eight checkpoints (technology)
**Summary:** TechNode reported on October 23 that miHoYo's Honkai AI research team and university researchers had open-sourced GameScaling. The article says the models watch gameplay and output keyboard and mouse actions, with eight weights from 800 million to 27 billion parameters. A model uses the current frame, recent frames, and earlier actions to produce about the next 200 milliseconds of input. The README for THUSI-Lab/GameScaling on GitHub describes the models as Qwen3.5 vision-language models continued-pretrained on keyboard-and-mouse play, with eight checkpoints from 0.8B to 27B. S1 uses Minecraft only; S3 is a mixture of which 27 percent is Minecraft; each checkpoint uses 1,600 hours of gameplay. The README says the weights and inference code are Apache-2.0, that the repository contains no training code, and that the authors include the miHoYo Honkai AI R&D Team and Tsinghua University among other institutions.

- [GitHub — THUSI-Lab/GameScaling](https://github.com/THUSI-Lab/GameScaling)

**Commentary:** The news date is October 23; the model sizes, the hours of gameplay, and the Apache-2.0 license are what the repository README currently states.
