## I. Studios and Products

### 1. Xbox Forms the XP Division for Film, Merchandise, and Live Experiences (Studio)
**Summary:** Xbox Wire published on October 8 a note from CEO Asha to employees worldwide and a companion post from chief strategy officer Matthew Ball. Kayleen Walters moves from president of Mojang to president of the new XP division, and Maria Angelidou-Smith becomes CEO of Mojang and Minecraft. Both report to Asha. Angelidou-Smith starts Monday, October 19. XP, short for experience points, formalizes four areas: Xbox Pictures for film, television, and other linear storytelling; Xbox Products for licensing and merchandise; Xbox Partnerships for sponsors and brand partners; and Xbox Places for broadcasts, in-person events, theme parks, and other location-based experiences. Ball wrote that under Walters, Minecraft grew from an already record 350 million units sold to more than 425 million, and that its merchandise business became one of the top 20 globally. He also wrote that A Minecraft Movie had the biggest opening weekend ever for a video game adaptation and finished number one at the domestic box office. Asha's note called that film the biggest in the United States last year and a top-five release worldwide, said Walters is a producer on the sequel, and said she grew consumer products into a multibillion-dollar business with more than 250 licensees. Angelidou-Smith spent nearly a decade at Meta, where she led Facebook Groups used by more than 1.8 billion people a month, later led product and technology at Personio, and served as chief product officer at Reddit.

### 5. Richard Garriott Says Ultima Copyright Returns to Him in 2027 (Rights)
**Summary:** VGC reported on October 8 that Richard Garriott told Jesse Petrilla he will reclaim the copyright to the RPG and MMO series Ultima from Electronic Arts under Section 203 of the U.S. Copyright Act, which lets authors terminate a transfer after 35 years. He sold Origin, and the series with it, to EA in 1992 for $30 million. He gave notice in April of this year, and EA wrote back that it knows the law and will act accordingly. Garriott said the two sides are in active talks about whether any Ultima projects can be made together, and that the rights return officially in 2027. At minimum, he said, he could re-release the earlier games or make sequels. VGC noted that the last mainline entry was 1999's Ultima IX, and that EA's most recent free-to-play revival attempt was 2013's Ultima Forever.

## II. Regulation, Streaming, and Regions

### 7. Italy's Antitrust Authority Says It Is Leading a Microsoft In-Game Purchase Review (Regulation)
**Summary:** Italy's competition authority, the AGCM, issued a press release in Rome on October 8. Within the EU Consumer Protection Cooperation Network, the action it leads with the consumer authorities of Norway and Denmark is aimed at Activision Blizzard UK Limited and other Microsoft group companies. The question is whether there has been a "widespread infringement" under Regulation (EU) 2017/2394, meaning conduct contrary to EU consumer rules that is likely to harm consumers in three or more member states. The release says the European Commission announced the wider coordinated actions on September 30, covering ten companies: Activision Blizzard UK, Crytek, InnoGames, King.com, Mojang, Plarium Europe, PLR Worldwide Sales, Riot Games, Supercell, and Ubisoft EMEA. Italy's action widens investigations the authority opened nationally last December into Diablo Immortal and Call of Duty. The stated concerns are virtual currencies such as platinum, globes, or diamonds, currency sold in bundles, dark patterns, weak parental controls, and a lack of help when accounts are blocked. Euronews the same day identified the Call of Duty title as Call of Duty Mobile; the authority's text says Call of Duty.
