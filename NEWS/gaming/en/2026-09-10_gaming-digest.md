### 1. Hideo Kojima’s Physint moves to Xbox after PlayStation ends the partnership (Publishing)
**Summary:** Per Video Games Chronicle, Game Informer, and AFP, Kojima Productions confirmed on September 10 that action-espionage title Physint will now be published by Xbox. Kojima said PlayStation Studios unexpectedly notified the studio in mid-June 2026 that it was canceling the project, prompting nearly three months of partner searches. Xbox CEO Asha Sharma called the deal “a big moment for Xbox,” with collaboration also extending into film and television; the studio’s statement stressed the relationship now expands beyond horror title OD, and development continues. No release date or exclusivity terms were announced.

### 2. Nearly 1,900 Blizzard workers ratify a Microsoft union contract covering AI, layoffs, and credits (Labor)
**Summary:** According to Kotaku and related CWA coverage, about 1,900 Blizzard Entertainment workers represented by the Communications Workers of America ratified their first broad union contract on September 9, with the story still dominating September 10 headlines days before BlizzCon. The deal consolidates smaller units into three bargaining groups (World of Warcraft/QA/Hearthstone; platform, support, localization, and story/franchise teams; Diablo and Overwatch), lasting roughly four to five years. Key terms require bargaining before AI rollouts that materially affect union work; 60 days’ layoff notice or pay in lieu; at least one week of severance per six months of tenure; 14 months of recall rights; plus crediting and remote/hybrid rules. Blizzard president Johanna Faries issued a statement welcoming the ratification.

## II. Deals, Platforms & Streaming

### 4. Saudi PIF weighs merging EA with Savvy Games to build a global gaming giant (M&A)
**Summary:** Bloomberg reported on September 10, with Startup Fortune summarizing the same sourcing, that Saudi Arabia’s Public Investment Fund is weighing whether to combine Electronic Arts—taken private on August 4, 2026 in a roughly $55 billion leveraged buyout at $210 per share, with PIF holding about 93%—with its Savvy Games Group arm to improve coordination. Savvy’s portfolio already spans ESL FACEIT Group, Scopely, Niantic’s games business including Pokémon Go, and Moonton (acquired from ByteDance for about $6 billion). No final decision has been made; reporting notes a deal is unlikely until Savvy completes the Moonton purchase among other steps. After Savvy CEO Brian Ward’s exit, PIF deputy governor Turqi Alnowaiser became interim Savvy CEO while also sitting atop EA’s board.

### 5. Amazon Prime Video rolls out AI lip-sync for dubbed shows, starting with Maxton Hall (Streaming)
**Summary:** TechSpot, citing The Verge and Amazon’s own explainer, reported that Prime Video is using AI and visual-effects tools to adjust on-screen mouth movements so they better match human-recorded English dubs. The feature launches on German teen romance Maxton Hall for seasons one and two worldwide and will ship with the third and final season due in early December. Amazon stresses the tech sits alongside human dubbing rather than replacing voice actors with synthetic speech, and says more titles will follow without naming them or detailing the underlying models. The rollout is an early high-profile test of whether post-production AI can make foreign-language hits feel more natural to English-speaking audiences.
