## II. Streaming, Regulation & Deals

### 7. DOJ issues Second Request on Fox’s acquisition of Roku (M&A)
**Summary:** Per Roku and Fox SEC Form 425 and 8-K filings, each company received a Hart-Scott-Rodino “Second Request” from the U.S. Department of Justice on September 8, 2026, extending the HSR waiting period until 30 days after both substantially comply (unless DOJ terminates earlier or the parties agree to extend). The companies say they will keep cooperating and still expect the mergers to close in the first half of 2027, subject to HSR clearance, stockholder approvals, and other customary conditions. TechTimes and others framed regulatory concern around a major content owner controlling a streaming gateway already carrying roughly one in five hours of U.S. TV viewing; coverage also cites a deal size on the order of $22 billion.

### 8. Florida AG sues Netflix, alleging children’s viewing data was tracked and monetized (Regulation)
**Summary:** CBS12 reported on September 9 that Florida Attorney General James Uthmeier sued Netflix, alleging the company recorded devices, locations, and behavioral events—what users watch, pause, rewind, search, skip, or stop—then used that data to train algorithms and power advertising despite privacy-focused messaging and claims that kids’ profiles avoid behavioral ads. The state says Netflix failed to obtain parental consent required under Florida law before commercializing sensitive data from known children and shared data with brokers and ad platforms. The suit seeks a permanent injunction, deletion of allegedly deceptively collected data, an end to “dark-pattern” designs that keep children watching, civil penalties, and other relief under Florida consumer and digital-rights statutes. Netflix had not publicly responded as of publication.

### 9. Malaysia opens 45-day consultation on a Gaming Sub-Code covering loot boxes, spending, and child safety (Regulation)
**Summary:** SAYS and WORLD OF BUZZ reported on September 9 that the Communications and Multimedia Content Forum of Malaysia (CMCF) launched a 45-day nationwide public consultation on a proposed Gaming Sub-Code, open through October 24, 2026. The draft addresses age classification and age assurance, parental controls, how virtual currencies and in-game purchases are presented, loot-box/gacha probability transparency, and responses to bullying, harassment, UGC, plus youth esports safeguards. A working group included representatives linked to Mobile Legends, PUBG Mobile, Honor of Kings, Roblox, Xbox, PlayStation, Google Play, and Minecraft, alongside child-safety bodies. The Sub-Code is intended to operate under CMCF’s self-regulatory framework and will be finalized after feedback is consolidated.
