## I. Studios and Flagship Releases

### 1. Capcom's REX Project Upgrades RE Engine in Stages, With a Goal of Making Games Together With AI (Engine)
**Summary:** IGN updated its report on October 3: at the Capcom Open Conference RE:2026 on October 2, programmer Satoshi Ishida presented the REX project, framed as evolving RE Engine for the next generation. He named three pressures: games are now so large that simple processes take extreme amounts of time, a bigger staff needs an engine more people can use, and "successfully integrating AI technology into development workflows has become an essential condition for streamlining future development." GameBiz's account says Capcom will not rewrite the engine from scratch and will add improvements to the current RE Engine in stages. At least some components will be open-sourced so they can be used to train AI. The system is unified under what the report calls the world's most common programming language, so both people and AI can read it. Capcom describes the long-term target as an "AI-generation game engine" and a future in which it "creates games together with AI." The same report quotes the company's March 2026 shareholders meeting: Capcom will not put AI-generated assets into its games, but it plans to use AI to improve development efficiency, including in graphics, sound, and programming.

- [Polygon — Capcom plans to embrace AI in development](https://www.polygon.com/capcom-ai-re-engine-statement-2026/)

**Commentary:** The public line is still workflow and testing, not generative assets inside Resident Evil; the phrase "make games together with AI" leaves that line wider than the asset ban.

### 10. India's First Final GST Orders Sharply Cut Demands on Real-Money Gaming Firms (Regulation)
**Summary:** Business Today reported on October 3 that Sanjit Bangar, founder of tax service Tax Buddy, said some online real-money gaming companies have received the first final GST orders, with sharply lower demands and no personal penalties on directors in the cases covered so far. On his account, the GST demand on Delta Corp fell from about 16,000 crore rupees to 117 crore rupees (about 160 billion rupees to 1.17 billion rupees), and the demand on Pacific Gaming fell from 721 crore rupees to 100.8 crore rupees (about 7.21 billion rupees to 1.008 billion rupees). The backdrop is India's 28% GST on online money gaming from October 1, 2023, charged on the face value of bets rather than on platform revenue. The report says final orders for Dream11 and Gameskraft are expected by October 14, 2026. These figures come from Bangar's public account, not from a tax-department release that day.

### 11. European Game Groups Answer the Virtual-Currency Probe With Smaller Packs and a 48-Hour Refund (Regulation)
**Summary:** EU Perspectives reported on October 3 that Video Games Europe and the European Game Developers Federation responded on Wednesday to the EU Consumer Protection Cooperation Network's coordinated action on in-game virtual currency. The groups reject treating that currency as bitcoin or cryptocurrency, and they warn that extensive redesigns could make some games uneconomic to offer in Europe, especially for smaller studios. Their proposals include tailored top-ups or smaller currency bundles, indicative real-money prices, and conditional refunds within 48 hours for bundles that are wholly unused. Federation managing director Jari-Pekka Kaleva said in a Thursday commentary that it was unfortunate the CPC network was not ready to develop the industry proposals further. Lawyer Andreas Lober wrote the same day that the network says it is applying existing law but appears to be extending it, potentially beyond permissible limits. The report says regulators consider earlier talks insufficient, have opened discussions with the individual companies, and may see national enforcement if concerns remain. Games named in the piece include Minecraft, Candy Crush Saga, Clash of Clans, and Valorant.

**Links:**

- [EU Perspectives — Industry response on in-game currency](https://euperspectives.eu/2026/10/eu-targets-minecraft-and-candy-crush-over-hidden-costs-industry-warns-games-could-leave-europe/)

**Commentary:** The industry answer to price labels and withdrawal rights is smaller packs and a 48-hour refund on unused currency, and the open question is whether national authorities enforce.
