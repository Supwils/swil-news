## II. Platforms and Regulation

### 5. An alpha launcher signs jailbroken PS5s into Steam (platform)
**Summary:** Tom's Hardware reported on October 25 that Playden for PS5, a GitHub port of a macOS launcher, can sign a Steam account in on a jailbroken PlayStation 5 and install games from that library onto the console. Launching a title sets up a private Windows environment through prospero-win, a PS5 port of Wine, then builds a separate PS5 app so the game appears as its own home-screen tile; files, settings, and crashes stay isolated from the launcher and from other games. The creator says the project is still in alpha. The report quotes one user whose game crashed after launch and another who played Portal without a mouse cursor and without Esc opening the menu. The creator's compatibility list includes American Truck Simulator, Cyberpunk 2077, Doom Eternal, GTA: Vice City, Left 4 Dead 2, and Team Fortress 2, among others. The project describes itself as an unofficial Steam client that Valve does not support, with the risk left on the user.

**Links:**

### 6. Illinois moves to put 50,141 video-gaming terminals under self-exclusion (regulation)
**Summary:** CDC Gaming reported on October 25 that the Illinois Gaming Board has proposed rules bringing the state's video-gaming terminals into the existing self-exclusion program. Problem gamblers can already bar themselves from casinos, sportsbooks, and fantasy contests; the proposal would let people enroll through identity checks at VGT redemption terminals. Administrator Marcus Fruchter called the integration a gaming-integrity and public-health priority and said nothing like it has been done at this scale. The report cites IGB figures of 8,800 licensed locations, 50,141 terminals, and more than $2.9 billion wagered in September. After the board approved the proposed rule on Thursday, Fruchter said it would line video gaming's problem-gambling protections up with casinos and sports wagering and set guardrails as the business moves toward digital payments. Operators could choose to offer cashless play and rewards through patron accounts, with age and identity checks, privacy and security controls, and KYC and anti-money-laundering requirements, and every account would have to let the patron set responsible-gaming limits. After the IGB vote, the rulemaking still goes to the legislature's Joint Committee on Administrative Rules before it can take effect.

**Links:**

- [CDC Gaming — October 25](https://cdcgaming.com/illinois-regulators-to-bring-video-gaming-terminals-under-self-exclusion-program/)

**Commentary:** Sunday's story is a proposed rule the board approved on Thursday; 50,141 terminals and $2.9 billion are the installed base and September handle, and the rule is not in force yet.
