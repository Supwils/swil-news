## I. Studios and Releases

### 1. Microsoft extends the Arkane Lyon consultation by one month (studio)
**Summary:** PC Gamer reported on October 23, 2026, that a Microsoft representative said the company had extended its consultation with Arkane Lyon's works council by one more month while it explores options, including a partner to support the development work still ahead. The representative named Marvel's Blade. Microsoft called the added time a defined extension and said it would keep the works council informed. On documents that describe a full shutdown and economic dismissals, the representative said they are part of the required consultation process and do not represent a final decision. Citing Le Monde, PC Gamer writes that management told employees on July 16 that Xbox intended to close the studio, that the buyer search had been due to end on November 13, and that about 217 people work there. A Push Square update the same day quoted Bloomberg's Jason Schreier saying multiple paths were being explored that could save the studio, that he would be surprised if it shut down, and that anything can still happen in negotiations.

## II. Platforms and Rules

### 5. Steam's Personal Calendar leaves Labs and becomes a store feature (platform)
**Summary:** Valve's Steam news post titled "Steam's Personal Calendar Full Release" is dated October 23. It says that with that store update the Personal Calendar graduates from Steam Labs and is a full feature of the Steam Store, available from the store homepage or at store.steampowered.com/personalcalendar. Recommendations use the games a player spends the most time in, find people with similar playtime profiles, and then look at games those players wishlist or play. The system is retrained daily, and the calendar looks about eight weeks ahead. Valve writes that in an average 24-hour period players click on more than 2,000 different games in the Personal Calendar, and that placement is not sold and not hand-curated. The feature first shipped as a Steam Labs experiment in October 2025. Polygon reported the wider rollout on October 24.

**Links:**

- [Steam News — October 23](https://store.steampowered.com/news/posts/4149575031735702629)
- [Polygon — October 24](https://www.polygon.com/steam-personal-calendar-wide-release/)

**Commentary:** The rollout confirms a discovery slot that cannot be bought; the 2,000-game figure is Valve's average click count over 24 hours, not a sales total.

### 6. SteamOS 3.9.5 is released for all users (system)
**Summary:** The Steam Deck news page posted SteamOS 3.9.5 on October 23 and says the build has been released for all users. The notes cover changes since the previous SteamOS 3.8 stable release: Desktop Mode moves from Plasma 6.4.3 to Plasma 6.7.3, the Linux kernel is rebased on Linux 7.2, and support for the Steam Frame wireless adapter is improved. One headset-related fix says the frame limiter no longer applies incorrectly to VR titles when streaming from a Steam Machine to Steam Frame. Hibernation is preliminary, is not yet exposed in the Game Mode interface, and is not yet enabled on Steam Deck because of an outstanding driver bug. The security notes say compromised eMudhra CA certificates were revoked. GamingOnLinux reported the stable release on October 24.

**Links:**

- [Steam Deck News — October 23](https://www.steamdeck.com/en/news)
- [GamingOnLinux — October 24](https://www.gamingonlinux.com/2026/10/steamos-3-9-5-released-with-plasma-6-7-linux-7-2-security-fixes-better-steam-frame-wireless-adapter-support/)

**Commentary:** The stable notes themselves list the kernel, the desktop update, and the Steam Frame adapter; hibernation is explicitly still off on Steam Deck.

### 8. Malaysia's gaming sub-code consultation reaches its published final day (regulation)
**Summary:** The Communications and Multimedia Content Forum of Malaysia lists the proposed Gaming Sub-Code consultation as open, with a window from September 9 to October 24, 2026. The page covers age checks for high-risk features, children's account settings and parental controls, transparency for in-game spending and randomised rewards, in-game advertising and creator promotions, player interaction and safety, and how responsibility is split among developers, publishers, platforms, creators, and resellers. The consultation paper uses the same start and closing dates and says the sub-code takes effect only after the Commission registers it under section 95(2) of the Communications and Multimedia Act. An October 12 note from Rahmat Lim & Partners says compliance with a registered industry code is voluntary, while section 98(2) makes that compliance a statutory defence in legal proceedings. At the time of this digest, the forum page still marked the consultation as open.

**Links:**

- [Content Forum — Gaming Sub-Code consultation](https://contentforum.my/content-code/)
- [Rahmat Lim & Partners — October 12](https://www.rahmatlim.com/perspectives/articles/34595/mykh-communications-and-multimedia-content-forum-of-malaysia-conducts-public-consultation-on-proposed-gaming-sub-code)
