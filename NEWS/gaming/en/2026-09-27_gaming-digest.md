### 1. World's Edge Loses Nearly Half Its Staff and Cancels Its Next Project Under Activision (Publisher)
**Summary:** TechSpot reported on September 27 that World's Edge, the Microsoft studio that stewards Age of Empires, lost nearly half its staff in the latest Xbox layoffs and was placed under Activision. Former senior software engineer Andrew Martz wrote on LinkedIn that the studio's next project was canceled in the reorganization and that he was laid off; former creative director Adam Isgreen, who spent 16 years on the series, said the team "had some great plans for what's next." TechSpot said the cuts are part of about 268 Xbox job reductions and count toward Microsoft's target of roughly 3,200 gaming job losses this financial year. Microsoft has not said what the canceled project was. Viking Sagas, a new expansion for Age of Empires II: Definitive Edition, has already launched with three civilizations and 15 single-player scenarios.

## II. Regulation, Platforms, and Asia

### 4. Sony Patent Describes a PlayStation Controller That Reads Cards and Phones (Platform)
**Summary:** VGC reported on September 27 that Sony's application "Video Game Controller-Driven Information Transfer," filed in March 2025 and published earlier this month, describes a controller that can read payment details from a nearby card or phone and send them to the console. The filing covers purchases of games, in-game credits, and add-ons, including a tap after a store or expo demo and reading physical gift cards. VGC notes that a patent does not mean the hardware will ship.

### 5. India's ED Attaches Another Rs 442 Crore in the Gameskraft Probe (Regulation)
**Summary:** The Hindu and Deccan Chronicle reported on September 27 that the Enforcement Directorate's Bengaluru office provisionally attached about Rs 442.35 crore in movable and immovable property on September 25 under the Prevention of Money Laundering Act in the RummyCulture case involving Gameskraft. The latest order brings assets attached or frozen in the case to about Rs 2,843 crore. The property includes fixed deposits, commercial shops, a villa, and homes held by shareholders' family members, private family trusts, and related entities. The ED says Gameskraft and RummyTime ran real-money rummy under brands including RummyCulture, RummyPrime, Playship, and RummyTime for about 30 million users, a significant share of them in Telangana, Andhra Pradesh, and Tamil Nadu, where online real-money gaming is banned, and alleges the companies deployed bots while telling users the games were fair and bot-free. The probe follows cheating FIRs in Telangana; searches ran May 7–14 and June 20–21.
