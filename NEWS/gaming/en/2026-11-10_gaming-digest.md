## I. Majors & Products

### 1. Sony cancels Haven live-service shooter Fairgame$; studio to support other teams (cancellation)
**Summary:** Bloomberg, The Verge, and Gematsu reported on November 10 that Sony Interactive Entertainment has canceled Fairgame$ (also styled Fairgames), the multiplayer shooter in development at subsidiary Haven Interactive Studios. In an internal note, executives Sean Decker and John Doyle said a recent test with thousands of players showed solid live-ops readiness but mixed feedback and retention that “fell short of the goals,” alongside “challenging market conditions in the PVP shooter genre.” A spokesperson said there will be no related layoffs and Haven will support other PlayStation studios. Announced in 2023 for PS5 and PC, the title is another cut in Sony’s scaled-back live-service slate.

**Links:**

- [The Verge — Sony cancels Fairgames](https://www.theverge.com/games/1031242/sony-playstation-fairgames-canceled)
- [Gematsu — Fairgame$ canceled](https://www.gematsu.com/2026/11/fairgame-canceled)

**Commentary:** Killing a project on soft retention metrics without cutting headcount shows Sony’s live-service posture has flipped from “ship more” to “ship only if proven.”

### 2. Bandai Namco resolves to dissolve Reflector Entertainment, expects about €21 million extraordinary loss (shutdown)
**Summary:** Bandai Namco Holdings announced on November 10 that its board resolved to dissolve and liquidate Canadian subsidiary Reflector Entertainment Ltd. in Montreal. The company said it weighed future plans, required further investment, and the chance of profitable continuation before ending the business; a concrete liquidation timetable awaits local legal procedures. Reflector was acquired in 2020 and released only Unknown 9: Awakening in 2024, which underperformed and was followed by layoffs. Bandai Namco expects to book an extraordinary loss of about €21 million (roughly ¥3.5 billion) in consolidated results for the third quarter of the fiscal year ending March 31, 2027 and thereafter.

**Links:**

- [Bandai Namco — Dissolution of Reflector Entertainment](https://www.bandainamco.co.jp/files/E_HP_Clean__E980A3E7B590E5AD90E4BC9AE7A4BEE381AEE8.pdf)

**Commentary:** One failed launch and a two-year wind-down—publishers’ patience for single-IP, high-cost studios keeps getting shorter.

### 7. More Digital Fairness Act draft details surface: paid loot boxes and P2W for minors would be barred; proposal eyed November 11 (regulation)
**Summary:** PocketGamer.biz and Mobilegamer.biz reported on November 10 that the European Commission is expected to present its Digital Fairness Act on November 11. Leaked draft language would require real-money values for virtual currencies, disable paid in-game currencies for minors by default unless a guardian approves, and ban paid variable rewards (loot boxes) plus pay-to-progress / pay-to-win mechanics for minors; adults would get tools to switch off compulsive-use features. Earlier coverage also cited dark-pattern blacklists and heavy fines. The official text may still change and must clear the EU legislative process.

**Links:**

- [PocketGamer.biz — Leaked EU proposal targets loot boxes](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/leaked-eu-proposal-targets-loot-boxes-and-addictive-game-design/)
- [Mobilegamer.biz — Digital Fairness Act draft on loot boxes](https://mobilegamer.biz/eus-draft-digital-fairness-act-takes-aim-at-loot-boxes-virtual-currencies-and-pay-to-win/)

**Commentary:** On the eve of the formal proposal, the leak already hard-codes a minors monetization red line—compliance talk is shifting from debate to countdown.
