## I. Majors & Products

### 1. GDC survey: ~40% of pros use generative AI, but a majority still see a negative industry impact (Industry)
**Summary:** PC Gamer, GamesBeat, and related coverage around November 14 report that the Game Developers Conference’s latest State of the Game Industry survey finds about 40% of game-industry respondents use generative AI at work, up from roughly 36% a year earlier, across a reported sample of more than about 2,600 people. Adoption is rising, yet about 55% say generative AI’s impact on the industry is negative, about 35% call it mixed, and only about 8% call it positive. Among adopters, the most common uses are research and brainstorming (nearly three-quarters), code generation/debugging (~56%), concept art (~22%), and only about 7% for player-facing features.

- [GDC — State of the Game Industry: November 2026](https://gdconf.com/state-of-the-game-industry-november-2026/)

**Commentary:** Tools are already in the pipeline, while sentiment is stuck between efficiency gains and fears of skill atrophy plus player-facing slop—consensus is “use it, don’t abuse it.”

### 5. CDPR uses AI to help Polish Geralt VO: original actor consents after post-stroke voice change (Industry · Ethics)
**Summary:** GamesRadar+, VGC, Game File, and others report that for The Witcher 3 expansion Songs of the Past, CD Projekt Red worked with Respeecher so Polish Geralt actor Jacek Rozenek’s new lines match his original tone. After a 2019 stroke, his voice became lower and gruffer; new lines would clash with thousands of base-game recordings. Rozenek still recorded every new line and consented to AI matching trained on his Witcher 3 archive. English Geralt Doug Cockle needed no such treatment. CDPR frames this as a health-related special case requiring contractual consent, and says the same algorithm may not apply to future projects such as The Witcher 4.

**Links:**
