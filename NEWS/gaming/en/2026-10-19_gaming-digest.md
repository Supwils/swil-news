## II. Regulation

### 3. Norway's government says the proposed age-16 limit will cover games and messaging by harm risk, and names Roblox (regulation)
**Summary:** On October 19 the Ministry of Children and Families and the Ministry of Digitalisation and Public Governance posted that the Labour government will add a harm-risk condition to its proposed social-media age law and remove a set of earlier exemptions, so harmful games and messaging services can fall inside the scope. The limit runs until January 1 of the year the person turns 16. The government says TikTok, Roblox, and Snapchat would be covered as those platforms are designed today. Minister for Children and Families Lene Vågslid said children's safety must come first while their right to participate is preserved, and that some platforms present themselves as games while carrying algorithmic recommendations and illegal advertising aimed at children. The bill is planned for parliament before the end of the year. Euronews reported the same day that platforms would handle age checks and that those systems must follow European data-protection rules.

**Links:**

- [Norwegian government — October 19 statement](https://www.regjeringen.no/no/aktuelt/malrettar-aldersgrensa-mot-skadelege-plattformer/id3178283/)
- [Euronews — October 19 report](https://www.euronews.com/2026/10/19/norway-to-extend-proposed-under-16-social-media-ban-to-online-games-and-messaging-apps)

**Commentary:** Today's news is a change in the draft's scope and a list of named platforms, not a ban already in force; whether harmless school messaging and ordinary games stay outside depends on the text sent to parliament.

### 5. Australia's ACMA publishes initial guidance for the notable-person gambling-ad ban, effective January 1, 2027 (regulation)
**Summary:** The Australian Communications and Media Authority published initial industry guidance on October 19. From January 1, 2027, gambling advertising that is broadcast or provided online must not feature a notable person, and licensed interactive wagering providers may not enter arrangements for a notable person to sponsor, take part in, or promote wagering advertising in Australia. The guidance lists television, radio, news websites, online games, and social media. A notable person includes a current or former professional athlete, celebrity, social-media influencer, or other prominent individual, plus any class the minister determines. The guidance says there is no prescribed follower or subscriber count. One example is a creator with fewer than 10,000 followers who only comments on a niche esports league and may still count as notable because of recognition inside that community. Ads that are solely or principally about racing, and that appear on dedicated racing programs, channels, or online services, are the stated exception. ACMA said formal guidance may come later.

**Links:**

- [ACMA — October 19 guidance note](https://www.acma.gov.au/articles/2026-10/acma-publishes-initial-industry-guidance-new-rules-prohibiting-notable-people-gambling-advertising)
- [ACMA — guidance PDF](https://www.acma.gov.au/sites/default/files/2026-10/Guidance%20on%20notable%20persons%20in%20wagering%20advertising%20content.pdf)

**Commentary:** The duty sits with the licensed wagering provider, not with the person on screen; the esports-creator case is an example in the guidance, not an enforcement action.
