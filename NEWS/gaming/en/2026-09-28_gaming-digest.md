### 1. Meta Opens the Horizon Create and Horizon Studio Waitlist for Prompt-Built Mobile Games (Platform)
**Summary:** Inven Global and PocketGamer.biz reported on September 28 that Meta opened an early-access waitlist at Connect for Horizon Create and Horizon Studio. Both tools use what Meta calls the agentic creation capabilities of the Meta Horizon Engine: a natural-language description can produce a 2D or 3D mobile game with progression, difficulty, art direction, and multiplayer. Create is a phone app that can run several projects at once; Studio is a browser editor for scenes, assets, and script parameters, and projects move between the two with their history intact. Published games can be distributed on Facebook, Instagram, and Horizon, with revenue from in-game purchases and the Meta Horizon Creator Fund. No date was given for wider access.

## II. Regulation and Streaming

### 7. Brazil’s Betting Ban Hits Licensees: Guidance Cuts, Shutdowns, and a Court Plan (Regulation)
**Summary:** Gaming Intelligence and Valor reported on September 28 that the financial and legal response to President Lula’s September 25 provisional ban landed together on Monday. The National Association of Games and Lotteries said about 31 million Brazilians are active on licensed platforms. The Brazilian Institute for Responsible Gaming, citing LCA, said that if 80 to 100 percent of regulated demand moves to illegal operators, fixed-odds betting revenue of R$58 billion to R$73 billion could be at risk between 2027 and 2030. Allwyn said that even if Congress rejects the measure, the shutdown could last until early March 2027. Better Collective cut its 2026 revenue-growth guidance from 7–12 percent to 3–8 percent and suspended its buyback; its Stockholm shares fell 25.05 percent to SEK 79.60 in the morning. Flutter confirmed it has stopped sports betting and iGaming in Brazil and, if it cannot operate for the rest of the year, expects about $70 million less revenue and about $20 million less adjusted EBITDA. Valor said industry groups are preparing to sue this week, with a direct constitutional challenge at the Supreme Court among the options.

### 8. Qobuz Tags AI-Generated Music and Says 60 Percent of Those Streams Look Fraudulent (Streaming)
**Summary:** Music Business Worldwide reported on September 28 that the French streaming service Qobuz has started labeling music its systems identify as AI-generated. The company says about 60 percent of streams on those tracks are currently deemed fraudulent and are excluded from royalties. Tracks identified as AI-generated account for 0.38 percent of all Qobuz streams, a figure given when the tag was announced on September 24. Qobuz has also removed from search more than a third of identified AI albums that had no listening, covering four million tracks, and it updated its AI Charter to a September 2026 edition. Deputy CEO Georges Fornay said the task has moved from detection to letting listeners see what they are hearing.

**Links:**

- [Music Business Worldwide — Qobuz tags AI-generated music](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/qobuz-starts-tagging-ai-generated-music-in-its-app-and-says-60-of-streams-on-ai-tracks-are-fraudulent/)

**Commentary:** A niche hi-fi service is pulling AI tracks out of recommendations and royalties, while admitting detection still misses mixed human-and-machine work.

### 11. NetEase Games Club Adds Local Payments in Five Latin American Markets (Asia)
**Summary:** Asia News Network on September 28 carried the announcement that NetEase Games Club and cross-border payments company EBANX will, in a first rollout, offer local payment methods beside credit and debit cards in Brazil, Mexico, Argentina, Chile, and Peru. The announcement also cited World Bank data that only about one in four adults in the region has a credit card.
