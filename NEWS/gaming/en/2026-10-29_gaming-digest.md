### 1. Xbox aims to beat PlayStation and Nintendo on margins by FY2030 as U.S. hardware sales weaken (strategy)
**Summary:** GameSpot reported on October 29 that Xbox CEO Asha Sharma set a mid-term ambition for Xbox margins to lead PlayStation and Nintendo by fiscal 2030. CNBC previously put Sony’s games and network services operating margin near 9.9% and Nintendo’s near 16%.

### 3. Unity partners with PvX on non-dilutive UA financing for growth-stage studios (platform)
**Summary:** Gaming Newsroom reported on October 29 that Unity and PvX announced a San Francisco partnership giving eligible growth-stage studios in the Unity ecosystem access to non-dilutive user-acquisition financing. Qualified games and consumer apps go through PvX-led underwriting, with funding tied to cohort performance rather than equity raises. PvX said it has committed more than $1.3 billion in UA financing across 17 markets; the deal also links financing to Unity Vector for targeting and measurement. Approved studios join Unity’s Developer Data Framework and receive ads credits and support incentives.

**Links:**

- [Gaming Newsroom — Unity and PvX partnership](https://gamingnewsroom.com/latest-news/2026/10/29/98205/unity-partners-with-pvx-on-non-dilutive-user-acquisition-financing-for-studios/)

### 13. Valve says it still believes in VR but calls out a chicken-and-egg content trap (VR)
**Summary:** Road to VR reported on October 29 that Valve’s Steam business team told developers in Korea the company still believes in virtual reality, while acknowledging Steam Frame owners currently play more flatscreen games than VR titles. Tom Giardino described a chicken-and-egg loop—developers wait for the market to grow while players wait for more VR games—plus weekly flatscreen blockbusters as the hardest hurdle for VR makers. Rising memory and component costs also made Steam Frame more expensive than hoped, he said, turning potential new-user moments into hesitation.

**Links:**

- [Road to VR — Valve on VR dilemma](https://roadtovr.com/valve-vr-still-important-content-dilemma/)

**Commentary:** Selling a headset that also plays flatscreen games is really a bet that non-VR demand can subsidize a VR content market that has not closed yet.
