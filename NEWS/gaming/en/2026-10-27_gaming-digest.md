## I. Publishers, Layoffs, and Earnings

### 1. Microsoft offers voluntary exits to about 60,000 employees, with Xbox among participating teams (workforce)
**Summary:** Microsoft chief people officer Amy Coleman announced a Voluntary Exit Offer on the company blog for eligible employees at level 70 and below in participating teams in Canada, India, the United Kingdom, and the United States. CNBC and The Verge, citing people familiar with the matter, put eligibility at about 60,000 people, or roughly 27 percent of a workforce of about 223,000. The Verge reports that Xbox, Windows, Copilot, finance, and human resources are among the teams included. Employees who accept must decide by early December and leave on December 31. The offer comes months after about 4,800 jobs were cut in July.

### 3. Capcom first-half sales rise 49 percent year on year, with 36.86 million digital units sold (earnings)
**Summary:** Capcom on October 27 reported consolidated results for the six months ended September 30, 2026: net sales of 120.887 billion yen, up 49.0 percent year on year; operating profit of 60.769 billion yen, up 54.5 percent; and profit attributable to owners of parent of 43.083 billion yen, up 56.6 percent. Digital Contents sold 36.86 million units, above 23.85 million a year earlier. Onimusha: Way of the Sword, the first new series entry in about 20 years, surpassed 1.7 million units worldwide, while the latest Resident Evil installment and catalog titles also supported growth. Full-year forecasts for the year ending March 31, 2027 remain 210 billion yen in sales and 83 billion yen in operating profit.

### 10. Save the Children warns esports is failing young players' wellbeing (esports)
**Summary:** Euronews reported on October 27 that Save the Children published Protecting Child Rights in Esports, arguing that up to 300 million children worldwide take part in competitive gaming-related activity without common protection standards. The report cites long sitting periods, inadequate breaks, training that crowds out sleep, physical activity, or schoolwork, musculoskeletal strain, sexism, anxiety, online harassment, and depression. It calls for children's rights under the UN Convention on the Rights of the Child to come before competitive aims. Psychologist Theo Toresson said children must be recognised as children first and competitors second.

**Links:**

- [Euronews — Save the Children report](https://www.euronews.com/2026/10/27/children-first-and-competitors-second-esports-industry-failing-young-players-wellbeing-rep)

**Commentary:** As esports scales, sleep, schooling, and online safety for minors are framed as systemic gaps, not isolated cases.
