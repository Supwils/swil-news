### 1. Kingdom Come studio Warhorse confirms a cyberattack (security)
**Summary:** Czech developer Warhorse Studios confirmed on October 27 that it was hit by a cyberattack involving unauthorized access to company systems and data. CEO Martin Frývaldský called it a serious incident and said the studio is working with cybersecurity experts and authorities to secure systems and assess the full impact. Warhorse said it will not comment further or give interviews until it has more verified information, and it has not disclosed what data was accessed or who was responsible. The studio is developing a new Kingdom Come title and an open-world Lord of the Rings RPG.

### 2. Rockstar UK staff to vote on union recognition Nov. 12–26, spanning the GTA VI launch (labor)
**Summary:** The IWGB Rockstar Game Workers Union said UK employees will cast anonymous online votes from November 12 to 26 on a recognition deal negotiated with management. A yes vote would create the largest recognised union in the UK games sector, with a say on pay and bonuses, overtime, redundancies, flexible working, and generative AI policies. Grand Theft Auto VI launches on consoles on November 19; closing submissions in a related 2025 dismissals tribunal are scheduled for November 17–18. Rockstar had not commented on the vote in coverage so far.

### 3. Capcom temporarily delists Street Fighter V and disables online features over PHP security work (security)
**Summary:** Capcom said on October 27 that online features in the PlayStation 4 and Steam versions of Street Fighter V are unavailable while it strengthens security related to the PHP programming language used in those builds, and that sales on the PlayStation Store and Steam will be suspended in stages until fixes are complete. Players who already own the game can still play offline content. Emergency maintenance is underway; no return date was given. The move follows the recent disclosure of major PHP vulnerabilities that forced temporary downtime across many online services.

**Links:**

- [Capcom Town Store — sales suspension notice](https://store.captown.capcom.com/en/blogs/news/0000047)
- [VGC — delisted with servers offline](https://www.videogameschronicle.com/news/capcom-has-temporarily-delisted-street-fighter-5-and-turned-off-its-servers-as-it-works-on-php-security-issue/)

**Commentary:** A language-level vulnerability can pull a veteran fighting game off storefronts and take down its online layer, making catalog tech debt visible again.

### 4. Garena launches localized Free Fire India more than four years after the ban (Asia regulation)
**Summary:** TechCrunch reported that Garena launched Free Fire India on October 28 as a stand-alone Android and iOS title. The localized version includes India-specific content, parental controls, playtime limits, and spending caps, with user data hosted on servers in India. The original Free Fire was banned in February 2022 with dozens of other apps over national security concerns; Free Fire MAX was not banned and remained available. Garena’s India head of operations said the company worked with relevant authorities and incorporated feedback. Sensor Tower data cited by TechCrunch put the franchise at about 77.4 million monthly active users in India in June, versus about 20.8 million for BGMI.

**Links:**

- [TechCrunch — Free Fire returns to India](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/28/free-fire-returns-to-india-as-garena-launches-localized-version-four-years-after-ban/)

**Commentary:** A localized stand-alone client with onshore data and parental or spending guardrails is becoming the default path for major mobile titles re-entering tightly regulated markets.
