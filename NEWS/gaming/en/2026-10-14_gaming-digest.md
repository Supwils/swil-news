## I. Publishers and Products

### 1. Ubisoft proposes closing RedLynx and affects 70 roles in Singapore (publisher)
**Summary:** Polygon reported on October 14, citing a statement seen by Eurogamer, that Ubisoft told teams it is proposing to close RedLynx in Finland, subject to the local change-negotiation process, affecting up to 52 positions. At Ubisoft Singapore, 70 positions have been impacted to align the studio with upcoming production needs, and the statement says the studio remains an important part of Ubisoft's global production organization. VGC reported the same day that RedLynx has no current project and that resizing alone would not keep it sustainable, so closure was proposed, while Singapore is being restructured for future work. RedLynx was founded in Helsinki in 2000, acquired by Ubisoft in 2011, and is known for Trials; the last mainline entry, Trials Rising, shipped in 2019.

### 2. Funcom names Scott Junior CEO and starts a restructuring (publisher)
**Summary:** Funcom said on Games Press on October 14 that Chief Product Officer Scott Junior is stepping up as CEO, succeeding Rui Casais, who led the company for 11 years. ## III. Regulation and Regional News

### 7. Pennsylvania begins enforcement against unlicensed skill games (regulation)
**Summary:** The Pennsylvania State Police said on October 14 that enforcement of the skill-games law has begun. The state Supreme Court ruled on June 15 that the machines commonly found in taverns and convenience stores are slot machines under the Gaming Act. From October 14, possessing or operating them outside businesses licensed by the Pennsylvania Gaming Control Board can bring criminal prosecution and immediate seizure. Police have agreements with six operators or distributors to render devices inoperable starting that day and remove them on a rolling basis; other distributors have asked for more time. A state-police resource page says powering a machine off or unplugging it is not compliance. The Philadelphia Inquirer reported the same day that Governor Josh Shapiro said police would enforce the law without a heavy hand, and that the attorney general's office estimates about 70,000 machines statewide. Licensed casinos and qualifying truck stops may still run approved slot machines or video gaming terminals.

**Links:**
