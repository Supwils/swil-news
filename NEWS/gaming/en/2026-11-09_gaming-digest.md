## II. Regulation & Streaming

### 5. Leaked EU Digital Fairness Act draft would ban paid loot boxes and pay-to-win for minors (regulation)
**Summary:** Euronews reported on November 9 that a draft Digital Fairness Act would blacklist ten dark-pattern designs and bar paid loot boxes and pay-to-win features for children in video games. In-game currencies would have to show real-money value and be off by default for children unless a parent approves. The proposal is expected Wednesday, with fines of up to 6% of global turnover; an impact assessment estimates €88.4 billion in consumer losses avoided between 2025 and 2035. Germany has already signaled reservations about creating a new horizontal regulation, and the text still needs Parliament and member-state agreement.

**Links:**

- [Euronews — Digital Fairness Act draft, loot boxes](https://www.euronews.com/2026/11/09/eu-to-blacklist-ten-manipulative-online-design-traps-leaked-draft-shows)

**Commentary:** Putting loot boxes and P2W on an “always unfair” list moves game monetization from platform self-policing into directly enforceable consumer law.

### 6. German games association warns EU KIDS Act could weaken USK/PEGI age ratings (regulation)
**Summary:** On November 9, game — The German Games Industry Association said it supports stronger youth protection online but warned the draft KIDS Act is modeled too heavily on social media and could let European codes of conduct override national procedures, producing inconsistent store ratings (e.g., 12+ vs 16+) that undermine trusted USK guidance. The group cited a survey in which 89% of German parents view USK ratings as reliable, and called for USK/PEGI to be the European games standard, risk-based rules suited to games, and stronger parental controls rather than blunt access bans.

**Links:**

- [Games Press — German association on EU KIDS Act](https://www.gamespress.com/en-US/EU-KIDS-Act-The-German-Games-Industry-Association-warns-of-weaker-yout)
- [BLOMSTEIN — KIDS Act overview for games](https://www.blomstein.com/en/news/are-these-kids-still-alright)

**Commentary:** Industry resistance is not to protecting kids but to importing social-network rules into games—if ratings fragment, compliance cost and parental guidance both suffer.
