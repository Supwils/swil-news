## II. Regulation and Streaming

### 7. Industry Groups Warn the Draft EU Kids Act Could Hit Old Games and Small Teams (Regulation)
**Summary:** EUobserver reported from Brussels on October 9 that the European Commission is still collecting feedback on the Kids Act, and that game-industry groups say the draft could take old games offline and squeeze small developers. The report says games would have to be child-facing by default, including an eight-hour nighttime lockout, a ban on screenshots, and guardian supervision, with age checks required for full features such as multiplayer tournaments or mystery boxes. Stop Killing Games founder Ross Scott said this could turn the EU into a pariah for videogames and push developers to rush updates or shut games down. The piece quotes Pinsent Masons partner Lauro Fava saying the impact assessment gave no cost figure for third-party age checks. Leaderboards and kill counts, described as features that increase social comparison, could also be restricted. Strictly offline physical copies and purely educational titles are written as exceptions.

**Links:**

- [EUobserver — Industry criticism of the draft EU Kids Act](https://euobserver.com/241743/video-game-industry-believes-eu-kids-act-will-drive-small-creators-and-old-games-into-extinction/)

**Commentary:** Today's story is opposition during a feedback period; the act is not passed, and there is no official compliance-cost number.
