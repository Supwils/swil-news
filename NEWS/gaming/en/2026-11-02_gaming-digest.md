## I. Hits and Big-Studio Products

### 1. Phantom Blade Zero tops 4 million sales in 72 hours as A-share gaming stocks jump nearly 9% (launch)
**Summary:** On November 2, Beijing studio S-GAME said its wuxia action RPG Phantom Blade Zero (影之刃零)—which launched October 29 on PC (Steam/Epic/WeGame) and PS5—sold more than 4 million copies across platforms in 72 hours. ## II. Studio Survival and Resets

### 4. Yakuza creator’s Nagoshi Studio formally dissolves, ending Gang of Dragon (closure)
**Summary:** Japan’s Official Gazette (Kanpo) listed Nagoshi Studio—founded by Like a Dragon / Yakuza creator Toshihiro Nagoshi with NetEase backing—as dissolved by shareholder resolution on October 31; Game Informer and VGC confirmed the news on November 2, ending development of debut title Gang of Dragon, revealed at The Game Awards last year. Bloomberg had earlier reported NetEase cutting funding and allowing Nagoshi to seek outside capital; the Tokyo studio was founded in November 2021. The shutdown fits NetEase’s broader pullback from overseas studio investments.

**Links:**

- [Game Informer — Nagoshi Studio shut down](https://gameinformer.com/2026/11/02/yakuza-creator-toshiro-nagoshis-studio-has-officially-shut-down-ending-gang-of-dragons)
- [VGC — NetEase cuts funding, studio closes](https://www.videogameschronicle.com/news/nagoshi-studio-officially-closes-after-netease-cuts-funding/)

### 5. Chronicles: Medieval studio Raw Power Games loses funding and warns of “real risk of closing” (funding)
**Summary:** Danish developer Raw Power Games posted on Steam and social channels that, due to “circumstances outside our control,” the funding behind the studio is “no longer in place,” leaving it unable to keep building medieval sandbox Chronicles: Medieval and putting the studio at “real risk of closing.” Early Access had been aimed for early 2027; the team said Early Access is “within reach,” is talking to anyone who might help, and asked players to keep the game wishlisted and discussed. IGN, PC Gamer, and others amplified the statement.

## III. M&A and Streaming

### 7. Nasdaq-listed Gamehaus signs to take a majority stake in AI micro-serial platform Anamana (M&A)
**Summary:** On November 2, Gamehaus Holdings (Nasdaq: GMHS) said it signed definitive share-purchase and subscription agreements with Techforge Co., Ltd.: about US$19.6 million in Class A shares (~24.5 million shares at US$0.80) for existing Techforge equity, plus a US$7.0 million cash subscription for new shares, expected to leave Gamehaus with roughly 56% of Techforge. Techforge holds Anamana, an AI-native micro-serial and creator platform. Gamehaus framed the deal as a shift from mobile publishing toward AI content production and distribution, targeting November 2026 closing without a shareholder vote.

**Links:**

- [Gamehaus IR — Anamana majority stake agreement](https://ir.gamehaus.com/2026-11-02-Gamehaus-Signs-Definitive-Agreement-to-Acquire-Majority-Stake-in-Anamana)

### 8. Xsolla acquires web-games platform GamePix and its 10,000+ browser catalog (platform)
**Summary:** Los Angeles commerce firm Xsolla announced on November 2 that it has acquired GamePix, a web-games publishing and distribution platform founded in 2013. GamePix brings 10,000+ browser titles and 4,000+ developers, plus tech to port PC/mobile and AI-built games to the web (claiming Unity/C++ builds up to ~90% smaller). Combined with Xsolla’s payments across 200+ geographies and 1,000+ methods, the pair pitch a path from finished (including AI-made) games to global paying players without installs.

**Links:**

- [Los Angeles Times B2B — Xsolla acquires GamePix](https://www.latimes.com/b2b/entertainment/story/2026-11-02/xsolla-acquires-gamepix-web-games)

**Commentary:** The browser remains the lowest-friction “open console”; buying a catalog platform is a race for first-mile distribution as AI game supply expands.
