## I. Space & Deep Space

### 1. Voyager 1 nears one light-day from Earth as NASA invites a global “shine bright” salute (interstellar probe)
**Summary:** NASA updates in mid-November emphasize that Voyager 1 will, around 5:16 a.m. EST on November 18, 2026, become the first human-made object one light-day from Earth—about 16.095 billion miles (25.902 billion kilometers), the distance light travels in 24 hours. Launched in 1977 and now nearly 49 years into flight in interstellar space, the craft will take a full day for radio commands to arrive. NASA is organizing “Shine Bright for Voyager’s Light-Day” after sunset on November 17 and a JPL discussion on November 19. Reaching a full light-year would still take on the order of ~18,000 years at current speed.

### 2. ESA’s Hera enters a “rectangular orbit” for early characterisation of the Didymos binary (planetary defence)
**Summary:** ESA reports that the Hera planetary-defence mission has arrived at the Didymos binary and begun early characterisation from about 30–25 km. With Didymos (~780 m) gravity ~40,000× weaker than Earth’s and Dimorphos weaker still, circular orbits are impractical. Hera flies at roughly 12 cm/s around the system barycentre and, on a three-then-four-day cycle, fires thrusters ~90° as it recedes, tracing “rectangular” paths. The scheme, inherited from Rosetta, is fail-safe: a missed burn drifts the craft away rather than into the asteroids. Next month it is due to close to ~20 km on bowtie hyperbolic arcs to document what NASA’s 2022 DART impact did to Dimorphos’s shape, crater, and mass distribution.

**Links:**

- [ESA — Hera spacecraft in ‘rectangular orbit’ around target asteroids](https://www.esa.int/articles/about-us/directorates/operations/space-safety/hera/hera-spacecraft-in-rectangular-orbit-around-target-asteroids)

**Commentary:** Turning kinetic impact into a reusable defence formula starts with measuring, in microgravity, what the asteroid looked like after the hit.

### 3. Euclid releases DR1-Foundation: more than a billion objects across ~1,900 deg² (space astronomy)
**Summary:** ESA’s Euclid mission has issued its first major data package, DR1-Foundation—about 13 months of observations covering ~1,900 square degrees (~14% of the six-year survey goal) and ~1.7 million GB of data. Contents include over 900 million galaxies, ~15,000 strong-lensing candidates, over 60 million quasar candidates, and spectra from more than 160 million sources. ESA’s science director called it the largest space-based survey of its kind, the foundation for dark-energy, dark-matter, and cosmic-web mapping; the full DR1 in mid-2027 will add weak lensing and cluster layers. Around November 14, major science outlets continued unpacking the mosaics and supercluster views.

## II. Biomedicine & Health

### 4. Single CRISPR base-edit infusion keeps monkey blood fats low for three years (gene editing)
**Summary:** In *Nature*, Verve and collaborators report a GalNAc–lipid-nanoparticle delivery of adenine base-editor mRNA and guide RNA that durably inactivates hepatic ANGPTL3 in nonhuman primates. In the durability cohort, ten high-dose monkeys followed for three years showed ~94% time-weighted mean cuts in circulating ANGPTL3 protein, ~43% lower triglycerides, and ~13% lower LDL-C; liver enzymes rose briefly then normalised within about two weeks, without evidence of cumulative hepatotoxicity. Human hepatocyte screens found no detectable off-target edits at ~3,000 candidate sites; the human candidate VERVE-201 is in a Phase 1b trial for refractory hyperlipidemia. Larger LDL drops in LDL-receptor–deficient models are separate, shorter experiments—not three-year durability proof.

**Links:**

- [Nature — In Vivo CRISPR Base Editing of ANGPTL3 for Durable Lipid Lowering](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-11233-9)
- [Medical Xpress — A single CRISPR infusion lowers blood fats in monkeys for three years](https://medicalxpress.com/news/2026-11-crispr-infusion-lowers-blood-fats.html)

**Commentary:** “One infusion instead of years of pills” still holds only for animal lipids—human trials must settle the long-term safety ledger of a lasting edit.

### 5. FDA approves pimicotinib for symptomatic TGCT when surgery risks severe harm (targeted oncology)
**Summary:** The U.S. FDA approved CSF-1R inhibitor pimicotinib (Pimjetmi) for adults with symptomatic tenosynovial giant cell tumor (TGCT) for whom resection may worsen function or cause severe morbidity. Support comes from international Phase 3 MANEUVER (NCT05804045): 94 patients randomised 2:1 to 50 mg daily versus placebo. Blinded independent ORR at week 25 was 56% vs 3.2%; median duration of response was not reached, and most responders maintained benefit on follow-up, with gains in pain, range of motion, and physical function. Common adverse reactions include pruritus, rash, and facial/periorbital edema; labelling warns on hepatotoxicity, embryo-fetal toxicity, and creatinine increases. Review ran under Project Orbis with partner agencies.

**Links:**

- [FDA — FDA approves pimicotinib for symptomatic tenosynovial giant cell tumor](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/cancer-approvals/fda-approves-pimicotinib-symptomatic-tenosynovial-giant-cell-tumor)

## III. Climate, Energy & Environment

### 8. HKUST: thermally symbiotic osmotic distillation plus alkaline electrolysis for seawater hydrogen (water–energy nexus)
**Summary:** On November 14, HKUST highlighted a *Nature Communications* study with Arizona State University and Hong Kong Polytechnic University integrating osmotic membrane distillation (OMD) with alkaline water electrolysis (AWE). Dual thermal–osmotic driving forces pull salt-free vapour from seawater for electrolysis while recycling electrolyser waste heat to run desalination and cool the stack. Major seawater constituents were rejected at >99%, with ~500 hours of stable continuous operation; modelling cites ~60 kg·m⁻²·day⁻¹ hydrogen output and ~51 kWh/kg H₂ self-sufficient energy use. Aimed at coastal water-scarce green hydrogen, the work remains lab-validated pending scale-up and durability tests.

**Links:**

- [HKUST — Unlocking Clean Fuel with Seawater and Waste Heat](https://hkust.edu.hk/boundless/article/unlocking-clean-fuel-seawater-and-waste-heat)
- [Nature Communications — Thermally symbiotic integration of osmotic membrane distillation and electrolysis](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-74854-8)

**Commentary:** Green hydrogen that keeps drinking freshwater has no coastal moral ledger—turning waste heat into the water-making drive is what a true seawater route must prove.

### 9. Transparent bamboo plus transparent PV skylights cut heat gain and cooling cost in Indian tests (building energy)
**Summary:** On November 14, *pv magazine* reported Vellore Institute of Technology work in *Scientific Reports* stacking transparent wood (delignified veneers infused with resin) with transparent solar PV (TSPV) modules of 0%/20%/40%/60% nominal transparency. Twenty-one configurations of balsa, poplar, pine, and bamboo transparent wood versus 8 mm clear glass were tested as skylights in small cardboard models under halogen irradiance. Bamboo–TSPV posted the lowest heat gain at every transparency; the 60% combo yielded ~371.78 kWh/m² seasonal cooling-related load, ~$20.32/m²/year estimated savings, and ~0.058 tCO₂/year·m² mitigation narrative. Results are model-extrapolated from benchtop setups and need cautious engineering transfer.

## IV. Fundamental Research & Physical Chemistry

### 11. 3.95-Gyr North China trondhjemitic gneiss implies Hadean mantle depletion and transient subduction (early Earth)
**Summary:** Hangqiang Xie, Yusheng Wan and colleagues at the Chinese Academy of Geological Sciences report in *Nature* a ~3,951±3-million-year-old trondhjemitic gneiss from the Shimen Complex, eastern Hebei—only the second unequivocal >3.9-Gyr terrestrial rock locality after Canada’s Acasta. Elevated zircon δ¹⁸O and positive εHf(t) point to a low-temperature water-altered basaltic source from a Hadean-depleted mantle. Thermodynamic and trace-element models favour melting at >1.6 GPa and ~740–820 °C after rapid burial of near-surface altered crust, which the authors attribute most plausibly to a transient subduction event. The outcrop is small; subduction remains model-inferred, and other burial paths are not directly ruled out.

**Links:**

- [Nature — Hadean mantle depletion and 3.95-Gyr subduction implied by North China rocks](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-11280-2)
- [Phys.org — Some of the oldest rocks ever discovered shed light on Earth's early history](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-oldest-earth-early-history.html)

**Commentary:** A second >3.9-Gyr rock matters less for “older still” than for turning early subduction from story into testable rock chemistry.

### 12. Zhejiang / UT Austin triaxial molecular weave captures 97.9% of benzene in one pass (separation chemistry)
**Summary:** Feihe Huang and Guangfeng Li (Zhejiang University) with Jonathan Sessler (UT Austin) and colleagues report in *Nature* a two-dimensional reticular triaxial woven polymer network (TWPN) built by B–N coordination polymerisation of C₃-symmetric macrocycles with biphenyl diboronate monomers. Four networks interlace; stacking opposite-chirality layers boosts elasticity and strength. From a 50/50 benzene–cyclohexane mixture, activated TWPN adsorbed benzene to ~97.9% purity in one pass (blank macrocycle ~60.5%), with similar gains for toluene and related pairs; heating regenerates the fabric. Zhejiang demonstrated larger-scale fabrication potential; a full economic analysis is still pending. Science wires amplified the story on November 14.

**Links:**

- [Nature — A two-dimensional reticular triaxial molecularly woven fabric](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-11218-8)

**Commentary:** Chemical separation is a hidden energy sink—if woven pores can skip distillation cycles, efficiency claims finally hit tonne-scale invoices.

### 13. Subcycle “slow-motion” video: graphene Landau–Zener–Majorana jumps reach 100% probability (ultrafast quantum matter)
**Summary:** Teams at Regensburg, Marburg, and Forschungszentrum Jülich report in *Nature* attosecond-precision subcycle band-structure videography across graphene’s full first Brillouin zone, directly imaging non-adiabatic Landau–Zener–Majorana tunnelling under strong light fields. Electrons accelerate within the Dirac-like bands and periodically tunnel between them, producing coherent displacement and distortion of the carrier distribution at the zone edge; transition probability across the gap can reach 100%. Extreme non-thermal distributions also help disentangle scattering channels that limit coherent electronic control. Coverage via ScienceDaily and Jülich framed the method as a microscopic key to all-optical control of quantum materials.

**Links:**

- [Nature — Subcycle videography of Landau–Zener–Majorana transitions in graphene](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-11214-y)
- [ScienceDaily — Scientists capture electrons making seemingly impossible quantum jumps](http://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/11/261112025523.htm)

**Commentary:** Filming “forbidden-gap jumps” in slow motion shows next-gen lightwave electronics will be paced by field timing, not bandgap width alone.
