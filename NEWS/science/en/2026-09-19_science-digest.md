### 1. NASA adds $946 million: SpaceX crew flights rise to 17, with performance through 2030 (Human spaceflight)
**Summary:** On September 18, NASA modified its Commercial Crew Transportation Capability (CCtCap) contract to buy three more International Space Station crew rotations from SpaceX—Crew-15, Crew-16, and Crew-17—for a firm fixed price of $946 million, with performance through 2030. The amount covers ground, launch, in-orbit, return and recovery operations, cargo on each mission, and lifeboat capability while docked. The change brings SpaceX's CCtCap total to 17 missions and about $5.92 billion. Space.com reported on September 19 that the modification follows a May notice of intent and does not preclude later purchases of transportation services.

### 2. XRISM: stellar wind seen falling onto a pulsar at about 540,000 km/h (High-energy astrophysics)
**Summary:** NASA said on September 18 that the Japan-led X-ray Imaging and Spectroscopy Mission (XRISM), using the jointly built Resolve spectrometer, directly observed stellar wind from the blue hypergiant Wray 977 being captured by the neutron-star pulsar GX 301-2 in the high-mass X-ray binary BP Crucis, about 13,000 light-years away. The star is about 40 times the Sun's mass and 60 times its size; the pulsar packs more than a solar mass into a ball about 20 kilometers across, rotates about once every 11 minutes, and orbits every 41.5 days. During roughly 16 hours on February 1, 2025, near the end of a strong flare, redshifted absorption lines from highly ionized iron showed gas racing toward the pulsar at about 335,000 mph (about 540,000 km/h). The paper appeared the same day in Science Advances. Roi Rahin of the University of Maryland, Baltimore County, and NASA Goddard said clear signs of wind plasma falling onto a compact object had not been seen before.

**Links:**

- [NASA — XRISM Sees Pulsar Gathering Companion's Wind](https://science.nasa.gov/missions/xrism/xrism-sees-pulsar-gathering-companions-wind/)
- [NASA SVS — XRISM Sees Pulsar Accreting Companion's Wind](https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/15099/)

**Commentary:** The flare's energy budget now has a velocity vector for wind falling onto the neutron star—the next test is whether the same lines can separate a turbulent disk from direct infall.

### 3. Yale: outer-Solar-System planetesimals were already 83%–92% chondrules in the first million years (Planetary science)
**Summary:** A Yale-led team with first author Damanveer Grewal reported in Nature Astronomy on September 18 that parent bodies of outer-Solar-System iron meteorites were already sorting for heat-forged chondrules over icy, volatile-rich matrix within about the first million years after the Solar System began to form. Two independent chemical tracers imply only 8%–17% matrix—about 83%–92% chondrules—lower than in any known chondrite. Earlier evidence of this sorting reached only objects that formed 2 to 4 million years after the Solar System's origin. Co-authors include Zhongtian Zhang of Princeton University and Joanna Drążkowska of the Max Planck Institute for Solar System Research.

**Links:**

- [Yale News — From the start, the solar system chose fire over ice](https://news.yale.edu/2026/09/18/start-solar-system-chose-fire-over-ice-build-its-first-bodies)
- [Nature Astronomy — Planetesimal compositions governed by aerodynamic sorting](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41550-026-02976-6)

**Commentary:** Aerodynamic sorting is pushed back to the moment planetesimals started accreting—the first solids in the outer Solar System were not a uniform mix of ice and dust.

## II. Biomedicine and Health

### 4. FDA fully approves Inluriyo plus Verzenio: 11.1 vs 5.5 months median progression-free survival in ESR1-mutated breast cancer (Oncology)
**Summary:** Eli Lilly announced on September 18 that the U.S. FDA granted full approval to the oral estrogen-receptor antagonist imlunestrant (Inluriyo) plus the CDK4/6 inhibitor abemaciclib (Verzenio) for adults with ER-positive, HER2-negative, ESR1-mutated locally advanced or metastatic breast cancer, as detected by an FDA-authorized test, after progression on at least one line of endocrine therapy. In the Phase 3 EMBER-3 ESR1-mutated population treated after an aromatase inhibitor (n=159), median progression-free survival was 11.1 months on the combination (n=67) versus 5.5 months on imlunestrant alone (n=92), with a hazard ratio of 0.53 (95% CI 0.35-0.80). Inluriyo monotherapy was approved in September 2025; the combination label rests on the benefit of switching to this pair at progression.

### 5. Nature news: first mutation-matched antisense oligonucleotide, and a man with rare motor-neuron disease still practicing a year later (Neurology)
**Summary:** Nature reported on September 18 that a man with a slowly progressing form of amyotrophic lateral sclerosis, caused by a rare mutation that leads to protein buildup, became the first person to receive an antisense oligonucleotide designed for his specific mutation. Between April 2024 and April 2025 he received three 50-milligram intrathecal doses followed by three 75-milligram doses, with no serious adverse effects and no sign of cognitive decline. Scores on a test of motor skills, breathing, and neurological function improved, other breathing and cognition scores stayed stable, and he was still working as a physician a year later. A Mayo Clinic Jacksonville co-author said sustained improvement without treatment is rare in this disease. The news said the results were published in Med this week. ClinicalTrials.gov record NCT06392126 identifies the single-participant study, run by the n-Lorem Foundation with Mayo Clinic, as nL-CHCHD-001 for a pathogenic CHCHD10 variant.

**Links:**

- [Nature — First for RNA therapy: man with rare motor-neuron disease improves after treatment](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-026-02945-z)
- [Med — N-of-1 antisense oligonucleotide study in CHCHD10-related ALS](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.medj.2026.101295)

**Commentary:** An n of 1 can turn "a drug for one mutation" into a repeatable process, but one functional improvement is not an efficacy claim for this class of ALS.

### 6. Joslin and Harvard: m6A holds pancreatic alpha-cell identity; its loss pushes cells toward immature beta-like states (Metabolism)
**Summary:** A team led by Rohit N. Kulkarni at Joslin Diabetes Center and Harvard Medical School reported in Nature Metabolism on September 18 that N6-methyladenosine (m6A) on mRNA regulates pancreatic alpha-cell function and plasticity. Metabolic cues that stimulate glucagon secretion, such as L-arginine, raise METTL3, METTL14, and m6A; loss of m6A impairs amino-acid-stimulated glucagon secretion and disrupts alpha-cell identity programs. In mice, alpha-cell-specific deletion of Mettl14 reduces alpha-cell mass, increases beta-cell mass, and promotes alpha-to-beta conversion, with late beta-like states that are incompletely mature. m6A-eCLIP identifies the transcription factor Yy1 as a direct m6A-sensitive target.

**Links:**

- [Nature Metabolism — m6A mRNA methylation regulates pancreatic α-cell plasticity](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42255-026-01591-z)

**Commentary:** Whether alpha cells can be rewritten as beta cells now has an RNA-methylation switch, but the late beta-like states in the paper are still immature—short of a functional substitute for insulin.

## III. Climate, Energy and Environment

### 7. India Meteorological Department: extreme-heat area in central and northwest India grew 20%–40% per decade, 1971–2022 (Climate extremes)
**Summary:** Scientists at the India Meteorological Department, the National Centre for Medium Range Weather Forecasting, and Atria University in Bengaluru combined seven hazards—including daytime heat, humid heat, heatwave severity, and drought—into a revised India Climate Extremes Index for 1971–2022, with trends tested by the Mann-Kendall method. The Print on September 19 and the Press Trust of India on September 18 reported that land area affected by temperature extremes expanded by about 20% to 40% per decade across central and northwest India, turning scattered 1980s hotspots into continuous zones by the 2010s. Northwest India shows the steepest rises in daytime temperature, humid heat, and heatwave severity. Southern India has the strongest drying trend; along the eastern coast, including Odisha, Andhra Pradesh, and Tamil Nadu, more than 40% of the region was in drought in several years. Northeast India shows both heavier single-day downpours and more frequent dry spells. The paper is in the International Journal of Climatology.

**Links:**

- [The Print — India's heat hotspots are spreading. Northwest is worst-hit: IMD](https://theprint.in/environment/india-extreme-climate-heat-hotspots-northwest-imd/3047233/)
- [The Times of India — Southern India drying fastest, drought hotspots along eastern coast](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/home/environment/southern-india-drying-fastest-most-persistent-drought-hotspots-along-eastern-coast-study/articleshow/134341650.cms)

**Commentary:** The risk is no longer "the national average is warmer"—it is connected heat zones, persistent eastern-coast drought, and northeast whiplash between downpours and dry spells, so adaptation has to be regional.

### 8. Washington State University: human nitrogen exceeds all natural sources along about a quarter of coasts (Marine biogeochemistry)
**Summary:** John Harrison of Washington State University and colleagues published on September 17 in Global Biogeochemical Cycles a global inventory of coastal nitrogen, phosphorus, and silicon, comparing pre-industrial and modern inputs. Marine sources such as net upwelling and onwelling still exceed land inputs in the global total, but land-based nutrients already exceed marine sources along roughly half the world's coastline. In about 25% of coastal segments, human activities add more nitrogen than all natural sources combined; the corresponding share for phosphorus is about 11%. Human nitrogen also dominates in about a fifth of large marine ecosystems. The human imprint is larger for nitrogen than for phosphorus, shifting nutrient balance toward phosphorus limitation, and nitrogen and phosphorus loads have risen more than silicon, increasing ICEP, an indicator of harmful-algal-bloom likelihood.

**Links:**

- [Washington State University — Study maps where humans are driving harmful nutrient levels along coasts](https://news.wsu.edu/press-release/2026/09/17/study-maps-where-humans-are-driving-harmful-nutrient-levels-along-coasts/)
- [Global Biogeochemical Cycles — Coastal Nitrogen, Phosphorus, and Silicon Sources](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1029/2026GB009128)

**Commentary:** The global average is still fed by the ocean; the decision-relevant fact is the quarter of coasts where human nitrogen has already overtaken every natural source—cuts belong on those hotspots, not in another average.

## IV. Fundamental Research

### 9. Boston Children's and the Broad: prime assembly integrates medium-to-large DNA in non-dividing cells (Genome engineering)
**Summary:** Daniel E. Bauer and colleagues at Boston Children's Hospital, Dana-Farber Cancer Institute, and the Broad Institute reported prime assembly in Nature on September 16. The method uses CRISPR-targeted dual-flap synthesis to assemble and integrate single- or double-stranded DNA fragments at RNA-programmed sites in human cells. Unlike homology-directed repair, it does not rely on nuclease double-strand breaks, double-stranded DNA donors, or cell-cycle progression, and it is similarly active in dividing and non-dividing cells. The team used it for exon recoding, transgene integration, and megabase-scale rearrangements in primary human CD3-positive T cells and CD34-positive hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells.

**Links:**

- [Nature — Targeted genomic integration and rearrangement using prime assembly](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-11024-2)

**Commentary:** Therapeutic editing has been stuck on large inserts, non-dividing cells, and fewer double-strand breaks; this paper puts all three in one chemistry, without yet showing clinical dose, off-target integration, or delivery.

### 10. Cambridge and UCL: reciprocal excitation between V1 and a higher visual area erases inconsistent activity faster (Neuroscience)
**Summary:** Guillaume Hennequin of the University of Cambridge, Sonja Hofer of the Sainsbury Wellcome Centre at University College London, and colleagues reported in Nature Neuroscience on September 18 how mouse primary visual cortex (V1) and the higher-order lateromedial visual area reconcile potentially conflicting activity. While mice discriminated gratings at plus or minus 45 degrees, the team recorded both areas at once and briefly, for about 150 milliseconds, activated channelrhodopsin-expressing parvalbumin interneurons to perturb one area, then fit biologically constrained latent circuit models. Reciprocal excitatory connections implement an approximate line attractor: congruent activity patterns decay more slowly, inconsistent ones more quickly. That predicted difference in timescales was verified in the recordings.

**Links:**

- [Nature Neuroscience — Reciprocal connections dynamically build consensus between neocortical areas](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41593-026-02437-3)

**Commentary:** Inter-area agreement here is not an extra decision center stacked on top—the connections themselves forget contradictory states faster, which is a mechanism of distributed computation, not a recipe ready for artificial networks.
