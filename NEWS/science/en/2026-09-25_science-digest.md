### 3. Roman Space Telescope ground stations pass high-rate downlink tests for ~1.4 TB/day (Astrophysics infrastructure)

### 4. ESA’s Juice sets up for a Sep 28 Earth gravity assist en route to Jupiter (Deep-space cruise)
**Summary:** ESA says Juice will fly within about 8,640 km of Earth on Sep 28, 2026 (closest approach over the Indian Ocean at about 11:45 UTC), deflecting its path by roughly 20° and adding about 3.5 km/s relative to the Sun ahead of a final Earth flyby in January 2029 and Jupiter arrival in July 2031. ## II. Biomedicine & Health

### 5. MSK: senescence-directed nanoparticles ease tumor scarring and restore immunotherapy response in mice (Cancer immunotherapy)
**Summary:** Memorial Sloan Kettering researchers report in Science that drug-loaded nanoparticles directed at P-selectin can selectively eliminate a subset of P-selectin–positive senescent macrophages that drive fibrosis and immune exclusion around liver and lung tumors. In mouse fibrosis and fibrotic cancer models, scar tissue eased and immune exclusion fell, allowing checkpoint inhibitors to shrink previously resistant tumors; key findings were corroborated in MSK lung-cancer samples and human datasets. Clinical efficacy is unproven, though toxicity was lower than with untargeted drugs.

**Links:**

- [Phys.org / MSK — Targeted nanoparticles reduce tumor scarring and enable immunotherapy response in mice](https://phys.org/news/2026-09-nanoparticles-tumor-scarring-enable-immunotherapy.html)
- [Science — Senescence-directed nanotherapy ameliorates fibrosis and overcomes immune exclusion (DOI: 10.1126/science.aeg4791)](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.aeg4791)

**Commentary:** Treating tumor scar as a removable microenvironment module is a concrete path to broaden immunotherapy—target specificity and long-term toxicity remain the clinical gate.

### 6. FDA clears Prime Medicine’s PM647 IND for in vivo prime editing in alpha-1 antitrypsin deficiency (Gene editing)
**Summary:** Prime Medicine announced on Sep 24 that the U.S. FDA cleared an IND for PM647, an investigational one-time intravenous in vivo prime editor for alpha-1 antitrypsin deficiency, enabling a global open-label, single-arm Phase 1/2 trial in the United States. PM647 is designed to correct the SERPINA1 E342K (PiZ) mutation carried by roughly 100,000 people with the PiZZ genotype in the U.S.; the study will first enroll adults with lung-only disease, later expanding to significant liver involvement. Safety and tolerability come first, with initial human data expected in 2027.

### 7. 103 bat genomes plus fossils rewrite the tree: powered flight may have arisen in late Paleocene Europe (Evolutionary biology)
**Summary:** Reuters and The Japan Times reported Sep 24–25 on a Nature study integrating chromosome-level long-read genomes from 103 bat species (42 newly assembled) across all 21 families with 44 pre-Quaternary fossils and hundreds of morphological characters. Analyses indicate bats—and thus powered flight—likely originated in Europe about 65 million years ago in the late Paleocene, not in North America, Asia, or Africa as often hypothesized; laryngeal echolocation appears to predate crown-bat diversification. More than 1,500 bat species live today.

**Links:**

- [Reuters — Study reveals evolutionary history of bats, dating back 65 million years](https://www.reuters.com/science/study-reveals-evolutionary-history-bats-dating-back-65-million-years-2026-09-24/)
- [Nature News & Views — Genome project opens the next chapter for studying bat evolution](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-026-02637-8)

**Commentary:** A European-origin story rewrites biogeography textbooks—the lasting asset is an all-family genomic resource for comparative immunity and longevity research.

## III. Climate, Energy & Environment

### 8. University of Toledo: lead-free 2D/3D tin perovskite cell hits 16.19% with claimed air-stability record (PV materials)
**Summary:** U.S. researchers including the University of Toledo report in Nature Materials a lead-free tin 2D/3D perovskite solar cell using the chlorinated spacer 4ClPEAI, reaching 16.19% power-conversion efficiency and lifting average efficiency across 14 devices from 10.34% (control) to 15.88%. Devices retained about 95% of initial efficiency after more than 1,600 hours in dry air and about 80% after 1,000 hours under continuous one-sun illumination at 55°C. The team calls them among the most air-stable tin perovskites of their kind to date.

### 10. Vanderbilt–Maryland: chlorine unlocks a third-electron sulfur reaction, pushing experimental Li–S beyond 1,700 Wh/kg-S (Battery chemistry)
**Summary:** Interesting Engineering reported on Sep 25 that Vanderbilt and University of Maryland researchers, publishing in Nature Energy, used chlorine to let sulfur exchange a third electron in lithium–sulfur cells. Lab tests raised sulfur charge-storage capacity by about 58% and average voltage from roughly 2.05 V to 2.54 V, enabling more than 1,700 Wh per kilogram of sulfur; a single-layer pouch cell kept about 78% capacity after 100 cycles. Stack-level specific energy is estimated near 477 Wh/kg—about 37% above the study’s conventional Li–S comparator. The work remains early-stage.

**Links:**

- [Interesting Engineering — Chlorine helps lithium-sulfur battery store 58% more energy in tests](https://interestingengineering.com/energy/new-lithium-sulfur-battery-chemistry)

**Commentary:** A third-electron voltage boost is elegant chemistry—cycle life, electrolyte compatibility, and cost will decide whether it leaves the pouch-cell prototype stage.

## IV. Fundamental Research & Quantum Science

### 11. Quantinuum H2: braiding plus fusion of non-Abelian anyons yields a universal gate set in experiment (Quantum computing)
**Summary:** Teams from the University of Chicago, Harvard, Stony Brook, and Quantinuum report in Nature creating S3-symmetry non-Abelian anyons on Quantinuum’s H2 trapped-ion processor with 54 entangled qubits, then combining braiding and fusion to demonstrate operations that in principle cover any quantum gate—capabilities braiding alone cannot provide. Authors call it the first experimental evidence that this approach can support universal quantum computing and may ease costly magic-state distillation in standard error-correction schemes.

### 12. Institut Pasteur: ArmA enzyme cracks a 50-year mystery of archaeal peptidoglycan structure (Microbiology)
**Summary:** An Institut Pasteur–led team reports in Nature the identification of ArmA from the gut methanogen Methanobrevibacter smithii as the first glycosyl hydrolase specific for archaeal peptidoglycan (pseudomurein), with dual activity that cleaves both glycosidic linkages and peptide crosslinks and is required to complete cytokinesis. Digestion reveals a glycan backbone containing the previously undescribed sugar N-acetylarmosamine with alternating β(1,4) and β(1,3) linkages, overturning a structural model dating to the 1970s; ArmA homologues appear restricted to peptidoglycan-bearing methanogens.

### 13. Nebraska team confirms intrinsic antiferroelectricity in hafnia down to the 2D limit (Condensed matter / electronics)
**Summary:** University of Nebraska–Lincoln researchers Xu, Gruverman, and Tsymbal show in Science that hafnium oxide (hafnia)—already widely used in electronics—is intrinsically antiferroelectric: neighboring polar layers cancel until an applied field aligns them, with the hallmark double hysteresis, antiparallel sublattices, and interphase boundaries, persisting down to the 2D limit. Unlike many lead-bearing antiferroelectrics, hafnia is lead-free and heat-tolerant, with potential for energy storage and information devices.

**Links:**

- [Phys.org — Discovery confirms rare, switchable electrical property in widely used electronics material](https://phys.org/news/2026-09-discovery-rare-switchable-electrical-property.html)

**Commentary:** Reclassifying a mass-production dielectric as an intrinsic antiferroelectric shortens the materials-import path—device fatigue and thin-film process windows still need engineering proof.
