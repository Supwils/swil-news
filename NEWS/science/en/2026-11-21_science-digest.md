## I. Space & Deep Space

### 1. Space | BepiColombo Enters Mercury Orbit: Europe–Japan Dual Probes Begin Close-Range Era

**Summary:** On November 21 the European Space Agency announced that the ESA–JAXA BepiColombo mission has successfully entered orbit around Mercury, ending an eight-year journey that began with the 2018 launch—only the second mission to orbit the innermost planet after NASA’s MESSENGER (2011–2015). ESA’s Mercury Planetary Orbiter (MPO) and JAXA’s Mio remain stacked for now in an initial roughly 590 × 11,640 km orbit; the pair is due to separate on December 9–10, with MPO reaching a ~400–1,500 km science orbit around March 2027 and full science operations planned for April 2027.

**Links:**

- [Nature — BepiColombo finally reaches Mercury after eight-year odyssey](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-026-03803-8)
- [Eos — BepiColombo Arrives at Mercury](https://eos.org/research-and-developments/bepicolombo-arrives-at-mercury)

**Commentary:** Dual-orbiter sampling is a new Mercury paradigm; the next test is whether the 2024 thruster derating still leaves enough trajectory margin for the science window.

### 2. Space | China’s Space Station Ultracold Atom Cabinet Cools Rubidium to 9.7 Picokelvin

**Summary:** In a November 21 People’s Daily feature, researchers at the Shanghai Institute of Optics and Fine Mechanics describe China’s space-station ultracold-atom physics cabinet cooling rubidium to an equivalent temperature of 9.7 picokelvin and achieving about seven seconds of free evolution—versus typically hundreds of milliseconds under Earth’s gravity. The setup uses two-stage crossed optical traps with evaporative and non-adiabatic expansion cooling that avoid gravity-induced “leaks” from the trap. The platform is intended to support higher-precision atom interferometry for gravity-field and dark-matter probes.

**Links:**

- [People’s Daily — Building a “cold pole” on the space station](https://data.people.com.cn/rmrb/20261121/6/66d56bb2f23f4847acb12b606ba42ae8_print.html)

**Commentary:** The record temperature matters, but the seven-second free-evolution window—sensitivity scaling with time squared—is the real scientific prize of a microgravity cold-atom lab.

## II. Biomedicine & Health

### 3. Biology | Brazil’s EVITA Trial: Wolbachia Mosquitoes Cut Child Arbovirus Infections by About 69%

**Summary:** AP and Brazilian outlets report a four-year cluster-randomized study of more than 5,700 schoolchildren in Belo Horizonte: neighborhoods releasing Aedes aegypti carrying Wolbachia saw roughly 69% fewer dengue and chikungunya infections versus matched control areas (about 68.5% for dengue and 73.3% for chikungunya). Partners include UFMG, Fiocruz, the World Mosquito Program, and Emory, Yale and Johns Hopkins universities. Results were presented November 21 at the American Society of Tropical Medicine and Hygiene meeting and are not yet peer-reviewed; follow-up spanned Brazil’s record 2024 dengue epidemic.

**Links:**

- [AP — Specially bred mosquitoes can fend off dengue](https://apnews.com/article/dengue-mosquitoes-brazil-wolbachia-f76cedcdcdf10c8834ab828d3e12ab13)
- [G1 — Wolbachia mosquitoes cut dengue by 69% in Belo Horizonte](https://g1.globo.com/saude/noticia/2026/11/21/cientistas-apresentam-evidencias-de-que-mosquitos-geneticamente-modificados-podem-combater-a-dengue.ghtml)

**Commentary:** Serology endpoints that hold through an epidemic year strengthen the case for mosquito replacement as a pesticide adjunct—scale-up still hinges on cost, sustained introgression and regulation.

### 4. Medicine | Genetically Attenuated Malaria Vaccine PfSPZ-LARC2 Shows ~87% Efficacy in Challenge Trial

**Summary:** UW Medicine and Sanaria report that the genetically attenuated whole-sporozoite vaccine PfSPZ-LARC2, in a Phase 1 randomized double-blind placebo-controlled challenge study, protected most volunteers challenged 12–16 weeks after the last dose: only 2 of 15 vaccinees versus all 7 placebo recipients became infected (~87% efficacy; 95% CI 60–99%), with a favorable safety profile. Findings were presented at ASTMH; the vaccine strain is West African and the challenge strain Brazilian, testing durable cross-strain protection. The trial was sponsored through NIAID’s Infectious Diseases Clinical Research Consortium.

**Links:**

- [UW Medicine — Malaria vaccine shows 87% efficacy in clinical trial](https://newsroom.uw.edu/news-releases/malaria-vaccine-shows-87-efficacy-in-clinical-trial)
- [Sanaria — PfSPZ-LARC2 stops infection in 87% of volunteers](https://sanaria.com/2026/11/20/genetically-engineered-malaria-vaccine-stops-infection-in-87-of-volunteers-in-first-efficacy-trial/)

**Commentary:** A small controlled challenge is not field efficacy, but late heterologous challenge is a high bar that justifies larger trials for the attenuated whole-parasite route.

## III. Climate & Environment

### 5. Climate | Science: Earth’s Cryosphere Has Breached Its Safe Operating Space

**Summary:** Teams from the Stockholm Resilience Centre, Tsinghua University, the Potsdam Institute for Climate Impact Research and others propose in Science a cryosphere planetary-boundary framework referenced to Holocene conditions of the past ~11,700 years, tracking land ice, sea ice, permafrost temperature and snow cover. They conclude the cryosphere has already left its safe operating space, with partly irreversible losses locked in by amplifying feedbacks; human-driven climate change is the main driver, worsened locally by black carbon and infrastructure in sensitive regions. Chinese media highlighted the findings on November 21, urging stronger greenhouse-gas and black-carbon cuts.

**Links:**

- [Science — The cryosphere beyond a planetary safe operating space](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.aef5636)
- [People’s Daily — Global cryosphere degradation is accelerating](https://paper.people.com.cn/rmrb/pc/content/202611/21/content_30192388.html)

**Commentary:** Making the cryosphere an explicit planetary boundary finally gives policymakers a dedicated dashboard for Earth’s frozen life-support systems.

### 6. Energy | Nature Energy: Deep Decarbonization Helps Health and Ecosystems—but Not Automatically Land, Water or Minerals

**Summary:** A November 20 Nature Energy study compares 24 climate-mitigation pathways from three integrated assessment models in a life-cycle frame spanning 2020–2100. Deep decarbonization yields large co-benefits for human health and ecosystem quality, yet does not systematically ease pressures on land, water or mineral extraction; for metals central to electrification, mitigation ambition mainly reshapes the timing of mining rather than cumulative volumes. Pathways that hit similar climate targets can impose markedly different environmental and resource burdens.

**Links:**

- [Nature Energy — Environmental and material implications of global climate mitigation pathways](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41560-026-02171-x)

**Commentary:** Choosing a net-zero pathway is itself resource politics—same temperature goal, very different footprints.

## IV. Basic Research & Earth Science

### 7. Materials | WashU: Sugar-Based Hydrogel That Strengthens Under Repeated Bending

**Summary:** Chenfeng Ke’s group at Washington University in St. Louis reports in Nature Materials “adapt-ring networks” that couple a covalent polyethylene glycol scaffold with reconfigurable α-cyclodextrin crystalline domains. Strain-directed fragmentation, translocation and alignment create kinetically trapped architectures that encode mechanical history; training can raise modulus, tensile strength, fracture energy and fatigue threshold by up to an order of magnitude, while solvent exchange resets the material for retraining. In a knee-like joint model bent about 10,000–12,000 times, reinforcement concentrated where strain was highest; the gels are also 3D-printable.

**Links:**

- [Nature Materials — Mechanical adaptation in adapt-ring networks](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41563-026-02795-0)
- [Phys.org — Repeated bending strengthens sugar-based hydrogel](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-sugar-based-hydrogel.html)

**Commentary:** Flipping “use equals wear” into “use equals strengthen” offers a bio-inspired non-equilibrium design route for soft devices and orthopedic supports.

### 8. Neuroscience | HMS & UCSD: Faster Electron Tomography Reveals Structures Connectomes Miss

**Summary:** Coverage dated November 21 highlights a Nature Methods paper from Harvard Medical School and UC San Diego on scalable electron tomography: roughly 50-fold fewer projections than standard tomography, on sections about 20 times thicker, still yielding ~15 nm or finer 3D resolution. In mouse cerebellum the team resolved electrical synapses (gap junctions) and tiny protrusions between neurons and glia that appear to mediate material exchange—neurons seemingly “nibbling” neighbors. Scaling to a whole mammalian brain will require automated sample handling.

**Links:**

- [Medical Xpress — New structures come into view in brain wiring maps](https://medicalxpress.com/news/2026-11-view-brain-wiring.html)

**Commentary:** If connectomes omit electrical synapses and glia–neuron exchange, network models will systematically underplay synchrony and metabolic coupling.

### 9. Ecology | Drone-Collected Whale Blow Yields First Whole Whale Genomes Without Invasive Sampling

**Summary:** University of St Andrews, University of Copenhagen and partners report in Molecular Ecology Resources that sterile petri dishes on a commercial drone sampled exhaled blow from 26 Northeast Pacific humpback whales (58 samples) in Gitga’at territory, British Columbia. Endogenous DNA averaged ~84%, yielding low-coverage nuclear genomes (mean ~2.3×) and high-coverage mitochondrial genomes (mean ~94×), validated against replicate blows and some tissue samples. NPR and others covered the work on November 21 as the first whole whale genomes obtained this way, avoiding dart biopsies.

**Links:**

- [University of St Andrews — Whole genome sequencing using whale snot](https://news.st-andrews.ac.uk/archive/whole-genome-sequencing-using-whale-snot/)

**Commentary:** Conservation genomics moves from “must touch the animal” to “follow a breath,” with a side door into respiratory microbiome monitoring.

### 10. Paleoanthropology | Guangxi Cave Teeth: Late Homo erectus in Rainforest, Extending the Mainland Record

**Summary:** Two Nature papers describe nine late Middle Pleistocene hominin teeth from Naxian and Baxian caves near Chongzuo, Guangxi, dated to roughly 324–184 thousand years ago. Palaeoproteomics and morphometrics assign eight teeth to Homo erectus, pushing unequivocal H. erectus fossils in the region well beyond the prior ~400 ka benchmark. Isotopes and trace elements indicate closed-canopy rainforest habitats and some long-range mobility; one proteomically distinct tooth (PA1796) also differs morphologically from H. erectus, Neanderthals and modern humans. The work challenges the idea that erectus was limited to open woodland–grassland mosaics.

**Links:**

- [Nature — Evidence for late Middle Pleistocene Homo erectus in mainland East Asia](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-11271-3)
- [Nature — Rainforest adaptation in late Homo erectus from southern China](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-11155-6)

**Commentary:** A handful of teeth rewrite timing, habitat and behavioral flexibility—East Asian erectus looks less like an early exit and more like a rainforest generalist.

### 11. Earth Science | 3.95-Billion-Year Rocks in Eastern Hebei Hint at Early Subduction and Hadean Mantle Depletion

**Summary:** Yusheng Wan’s team at the Institute of Geology, Chinese Academy of Geological Sciences, reports in Nature a ~3.95-Gyr trondhjemitic gneiss from the Shimen Complex in eastern Hebei (zircon U–Pb weighted mean ~3,951 ± 3 Ma)—Asia’s oldest known rock and the world’s second after Canada’s ~4.0-Gyr Acasta gneisses. Oxygen and hafnium isotopes plus high-pressure, relatively cool melting signatures imply surface-altered basaltic crust was rapidly buried to ~50 km and partially melted, supporting a subduction-like mechanism by 3.95 Gyr and Hadean mantle depletion by ~4.3 Gyr. China’s Geological Survey issued a November 21 briefing.

**Links:**

- [Nature — Hadean mantle depletion and 3.95-Gyr subduction implied by North China rocks](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-11280-2)
- [China Geological Survey — 3.95-billion-year-old rocks discovered in China](https://www.cgs.gov.cn/ywdt/ddyw/202611/t20261121_873066.html)

**Commentary:** An outcrop the size of a small room (~15 m²) lands hard on two early-Earth debates at once: when subduction-like burial began, and when crust and mantle first split at scale.
