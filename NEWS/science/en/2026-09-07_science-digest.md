## I. Space & Deep Space

### 1. BepiColombo completes Mercury Transfer Module separation, opening the Mercury arrival phase (Space)
**Summary:** ESA reported on Sep 7 that mission control at ESOC confirmed successful separation of BepiColombo’s Mercury Transfer Module (MTM) from the spacecraft stack at 15:49 CEST on Sep 3. After roughly eight years, 9.9 billion kilometres of travel, and nine planetary flybys, the ESA–JAXA mission’s solar-electric propulsion ferry has finished its job. MPO and Mio remain more than three million kilometres from Mercury, with orbit insertion planned for Nov 21, Mio deployment in early December, MPO’s final science orbit in March 2027, and the science phase starting around April 2027. Controllers call the six-month arrival sequence among the most complex planetary arrivals ESA has attempted—and the first designed to place two spacecraft in Mercury orbit.

### 2. China demonstrates first Earth–Moon bidirectional high-speed laser link; launches lunar-Earth Cube constellation plan (Space communications)
**Summary:** People’s Daily and CAS coverage on Sep 7 report that a team led by the Technology and Engineering Center for Space Utilization (CAS), with Zhejiang Lab and partners, established a bidirectional laser link over ~400,000 km, with preliminary uplink ~1.25 Mbps and downlink ~100 Mbps. An 8K lunar image that would take 4–5 minutes over a conventional ~5 Mbps microwave link can return in about 12 seconds via laser. The same day’s reporting from the 2026 Deep Space Exploration (Tiandu) International Conference says China has formally launched an International Lunar-Earth Cube Constellation major science program to deploy multinational satellite clusters for space-environment monitoring, gamma-ray burst detection, and lunar resource surveys, aiming to finish launches around 2030, with Phase-1 engineering already underway.

## II. Biomedicine & Life Sciences

### 3. NEJM: Single IV in vivo CAR-T (JY231) reprograms immunity in refractory neurologic autoimmunity (Cell therapy)
**Summary:** Cheng and colleagues report in a *New England Journal of Medicine* correspondence (DOI: 10.1056/nejmc2603114) that a single intravenous infusion of lentiviral vector JY231 reprograms patients’ own T cells in vivo into CD19-targeted CAR-T cells for severe neurologic and muscle autoimmune diseases resistant to standard care. All 16 patients generated CAR-T cells in vivo, peaking around day 11, with >99% of reprogrammed cells correctly specified; by six months, all four disease categories showed clinical and biomarker improvement. Coverage on Sep 7 frames the result as early proof of concept for an off-the-shelf in vivo delivery path, pending larger randomized trials and longer safety follow-up.

**Links:**

- [NEJM — Lentiviral In Vivo CD19 CAR T-Cell Therapy in Neurologic Autoimmune Disorders](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMc2603114)

**Commentary:** Moving CAR-T from an ex vivo factory into an in vivo worksite shifts the bottleneck from manufacturing capacity to regulation and long-term safety.

### 4. Novartis/Ionis: pelacarsen Phase III Lp(a)HORIZON lowers Lp(a) but misses cardiovascular-event endpoint (Cardiology)
**Summary:** Novartis announced on Sep 4 (widely covered thereafter) that antisense oligonucleotide pelacarsen in the Phase III Lp(a)HORIZON trial (NCT04023552) failed its primary endpoint of reducing major adverse cardiovascular events—cardiovascular death, non-fatal MI/stroke, and urgent coronary revascularization requiring hospitalization—in more than 8,000 patients with established CVD and elevated Lp(a). Lp(a) levels fell as intended, yet that did not translate into fewer events versus placebo on top of guideline-directed lipid-lowering and antihypertensive therapy. Elevated Lp(a) affects roughly one in five people worldwide and still lacks an approved targeted therapy; full data are slated for a future congress.

**Links:**

- [ClinicalTrials.gov — NCT04023552 Lp(a)HORIZON](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04023552)

**Commentary:** Lowering a risk marker is not the same as improving hard outcomes—biomarker success with endpoint failure is rewriting the Lp(a) drug story.

### 5. Science Translational Medicine: MLC1 autoantibodies may drive NMOSD-like astrocytopathy (Neuroimmunology)
**Summary:** A FAU Erlangen–Nürnberg team led by Simone Mader, with LMU Munich, the Medical University of Vienna, and other partners, reports in *Science Translational Medicine* (DOI: 10.1126/scitranslmed.ady0403) autoantibodies against the astrocyte membrane protein MLC1—which regulates fluid and electrolyte homeostasis—in patients with CNS inflammatory disease. Four MLC1-positive individuals were aquaporin-4 (AQP4) antibody–negative; lab studies show the antibodies bind astrocytes and can damage or destroy them. FAU’s September release argues the finding helps explain “seronegative” NMOSD-like illness and opens routes to refined classification and therapy.

### 6. NEJM: Chinese team identifies tick-borne ALTNV causing flu-like fever, worse with DBV coinfection (Infectious disease)
**Summary:** Xiao-Ai Zhang and colleagues at Beijing’s State Key Laboratory of Pathogen and Biosecurity report in *NEJM* (DOI: 10.1056/nejmc2602496) a novel orthonairovirus, Asian longhorned tick nairovirus (ALTNV). Among 3,163 patients with tick exposure, 10.4% tested positive by RNA or IgM; among Dabie bandavirus (DBV)–negative patients, positivity rose to 15.1%. ALTNV alone commonly caused fatigue (84%), GI symptoms (80%), thrombocytopenia (38%), and elevated aminotransferases (36%), with full recovery; among 38 ALTNV–DBV coinfections, respiratory and renal complications were more frequent and 7 patients (18.4%) died. ALTNV RNA was found in ~1.3% of 47,428 ticks across 15 provinces, highest in *Haemaphysalis longicornis* (~1.8%), which can transmit the virus to mice. CIDRAP and medicalxpress followed on Sep 7.

**Links:**

- [CIDRAP — Scientists in China discover new tick-borne virus](https://www.cidrap.umn.edu/tick-borne-disease/scientists-china-discover-new-tick-borne-virus-causes-flu-illness)

**Commentary:** “Negative” SFTS-like workups may hide a second tick-borne virus—diagnostic panels need to expand with the pathogen list.

## III. Climate & Environment

### 7. WMO Air Quality and Climate Bulletin No. 6: wildfires and heatwaves undercut pollution gains (Climate health)
**Summary:** On Sep 7—the UN International Day of Clean Air for blue skies—the World Meteorological Organization released its sixth Air Quality and Climate Bulletin. Climate-driven extreme wildfires and heatwaves are undermining air-quality progress: industrial and transport PM2.5 has fallen in Europe and North America, yet fire-related PM2.5 exposure is rising. One cited study finds extreme fire-smoke events have roughly tripled globally since the 1990s, contributing nearly 100,000 extra deaths per year in 2010–2018, while conventional PM2.5 models may underestimate wildfire-attributable mortality by up to 93%. Heatwaves also boost ground-level ozone, harming lungs and crops. WMO urges coordinated air-quality and climate policy plus better monitoring of fine particles, ozone, black carbon, and microplastics.

### 8. Hebei University of Technology and partners: steric-gated dual-site chelation yields 27.39% perovskite cells and ultra-stable bifacial modules (Photovoltaics)
**Summary:** Cong Chen and colleagues (Hebei University of Technology, Jiaxing Nanhu University, Tianjin University) report in *Joule* (DOI: 10.1016/j.joule.2026.102644) a steric-gated dual-site chelation (SGDC) strategy using adamantane additive ADA-DA to chelate undercoordinated Pb²⁺ while limiting interfacial crowding. Inverted p-i-n small-area cells reached a certified 27.39% efficiency (26.24% without the additive); ~151–164 cm² bifacial mini-modules exceeded 22% efficiency and retained ~99.6% power after more than 5,000 hours of continuous LED illumination. pv magazine covered the work on Sep 7, highlighting building-integrated PV potential.

## IV. Fundamental Research & Mathematics

### 9. Anthropic: Claude completes the first end-to-end machine-checked proof of Fermat’s Last Theorem in Lean in 11 days (Math/AI)
**Summary:** Anthropic announced on Sep 4 (followed by GIGAZINE and *New Scientist* through Sep 5–7) that Claude, guided by researcher Tianyi Peng on the Prove2Me platform, worked largely autonomously for about 11 days to produce the first complete, end-to-end, computer-checked Lean 4 formalization of Fermat’s Last Theorem—about 13 million lines of code and ~29,500 intermediate theorems. The proof uses only Lean’s three standard axioms, contains no `sorry` placeholders, and matches Mathlib’s FLT statement. It follows the Darmon–Diamond–Taylor exposition of the Wiles/Taylor path and is roughly five times the size of Mathlib; Kevin Buzzard called it evidence that autoformalization can now support checking large swaths of modern mathematics.

**Links:**

- [Anthropic — Formalizing Fermat's Last Theorem](https://www.anthropic.com/research/formalizing-fermats-last-theorem)
- [New Scientist — Fermat's last theorem formalised by AI agents in 11 days](https://www.newscientist.com/article/2587839-fermats-last-theorem-formalised-by-ai-agents-in-just-11-days/)

**Commentary:** This time AI did not invent a new proof—it turned a human proof into machine-checkable infrastructure, shifting where referee effort goes.

### 10. PRL: Three spin-triplet superconducting phases in K₂Cr₃As₃ point to tunable topological superconductivity (Condensed matter)
**Summary:** Guo-qing Zheng’s Okayama University group reports in *Physical Review Letters* (DOI: 10.1103/kykd-2nj4; arXiv:2606.24108) ⁷⁵As NMR evidence for three superconducting phases (A/B/C) in chromium-based K₂Cr₃As₃ (Tc ≥ 6.2 K, no magnetic order). At low field, cooling drives a helical Phase A into a chiral Phase B (d-vector rotates from in-plane to out-of-plane; point nodes); high-field Phase C hosts a line-nodal gap with the d-vector in the basal plane. Sep 7 phys.org coverage frames the result as strong evidence for spin-triplet pairing and notes that one topological phase may host Majorana excitations relevant to fault-tolerant quantum computing.

**Links:**

- [Physical Review Letters — Multiple phases in K₂Cr₃As₃ (DOI: 10.1103/kykd-2nj4)](https://doi.org/10.1103/kykd-2nj4)
- [arXiv — Multiple phases in K2Cr3As3: a playground for manipulating topological superconductivity](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.24108)

**Commentary:** Uranium-based spin-triplet candidates are experimentally punishing; a chromium platform above liquid-helium temperature finally makes “tuning topological superconductivity” look operable.

### 11. PNAS: Carbonaceous-chondrite inclusions record a 150–600 µT protoplanetary-disk field (Planet formation)
**Summary:** Cauê Borlina (Purdue) and US/China/UK collaborators measured paleomagnetism in calcium-aluminum-rich inclusions—among the oldest solar-system solids—from Antarctic carbonaceous chondrite DOM 08006. Some inclusions host nanometre-scale ferromagnetic Fe–Ni; anhysteretic remanent magnetization estimates imply fields of ~150–600 µT when the grains cooled through the Curie point (versus ~30–60 µT at Earth’s surface today). *Physics World* covered the work on Sep 7 as the first clear paleomagnetic signal from a carbonaceous chondrite, arguing accretion models can no longer ignore magnetism. The PNAS paper (DOI: 10.1073/pnas.2521660123) is based on five inclusions from one meteorite and still needs replication across other chondrite groups.

**Links:**

- [Physics World — Magnetism likely played a key role in early solar system formation](https://physicsworld.com/a/magnetism-likely-played-a-key-role-in-early-solar-system-formation/)
- [PNAS — Paleomagnetic evidence for a nebular magnetic field from CAIs (DOI: 10.1073/pnas.2521660123)](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2521660123)

**Commentary:** If planet-formation simulations only “turn up gravity,” they may be missing the magnetic driver of the early disk.
