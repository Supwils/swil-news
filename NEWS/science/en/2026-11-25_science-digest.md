## I. Space & Deep Space

### 1. Planetary defense｜ESA’s Hera finds Dimorphos flattened by a stadium-sized crater and tumbling wildly
**Summary:** On November 25, the European Space Agency reported that its Hera planetary-defence mission, arriving at the Didymos–Dimorphos binary, has for the first time imaged the aftermath of NASA’s DART impact (26 September 2022). The ~151 m Dimorphos moonlet now has one face flattened into a stadium-sized crater spanning its full width and is tumbling chaotically; hundreds of metre-scale boulders also orbit the pair. Didymos is ~730 m across and currently about 193.5 million km from Earth. Hera is in early characterisation at ~25–30 km range, plans to close to ~13 km in December, and will deploy CubeSats Juventas and Milani in January 2027.

**Links:**

- [ESA — Hera reveals stadium-sized asteroid crater from spacecraft impact](https://www.esa.int/articles/about-us/directorates/operations/space-safety/hera/hera-reveals-stadium-sized-asteroid-crater-left-after-spacecraft-impact)

**Commentary:** DART proved deflection works; Hera shows what the hit actually did—turning a one-shot demo into engineering knowledge for planetary defence.

### 2. Space｜NASA’s Europa Clipper sets Dec. 3 Earth gravity assist with all nine instruments calibrating
**Summary:** NASA said on November 25 that Europa Clipper will swing past Earth on December 3 (closest approach ~3,140–3,450 km west of Africa at 3:14 p.m. EST), using a gravity assist to save propellant and reshape its path toward Jupiter orbit in April 2030. At ~6,000 kg it is NASA’s largest deep-space probe yet. All nine science instruments will collect data during the flyby; absolute magnetometer calibration against Earth’s field is a priority, E-THEMIS will scan Earth in infrared every eight hours, and Europa-UVS will coordinate with LRO’s LAMP on Earth’s geocorona.

**Links:**

- [NASA — Europa Clipper Zooming by Earth on Way to Jupiter’s Icy Moon](https://science.nasa.gov/blogs/europa-clipper/2026/11/25/nasas-europa-clipper-zooming-by-earth-on-way-to-jupiters-icy-moon/)

**Commentary:** The flyby is both a free speed boost and the closest full-instrument dress rehearsal before Europa science begins.

## II. Biomedicine & Health

### 3. Brain delivery｜Caltech “BrainBots” light-driven nanorobots deliver ~5× more drug across the BBB in mice
**Summary:** A Caltech team led by Wei Gao reported online November 25 in *Science Robotics* ~250 nm BrainBots: ammonium-bicarbonate fuel capsules whose temperature-sensitive gates open under near-infrared light through the skull, generating gas propulsion toward blood–brain barrier (BBB) cells, with aptamers aiding uptake and payload release. In glioblastoma mouse models, light-activated bots delivered roughly five times more doxorubicin to the brain than nonactivated controls, slowing tumor growth and prolonging survival without detectable BBB disruption and with lower systemic toxicity than conventional IV chemo. Only a fraction reached the brain; long-term safety and light delivery still need work.

**Links:**

- [Caltech — Tiny Robots Deliver Treatments to the Brain](https://www.caltech.edu/about/news/tiny-robots-deliver-treatments-to-the-brain)

**Commentary:** Passive nanoparticles hoping to cross the BBB get a physical on-switch—light-driven contact before transcytosis.

### 4. Rare disease｜GSK’s Exdensur (depemokimab) Phase III OCEAN: twice-yearly shot non-inferior to monthly Nucala in EGPA
**Summary:** On November 25 GSK announced that Phase III non-inferiority trial OCEAN (NCT05263934) met its primary endpoint: in relapsing or refractory eosinophilic granulomatosis with polyangiitis (EGPA), depemokimab 200 mg every 26 weeks was non-inferior to mepolizumab (Nucala) 300 mg every 4 weeks for remission, with similar adverse-event incidence and severity. The double-blind, double-dummy study enrolled 162 participants in 19 countries over 52 weeks; remission required BVAS=0 and oral corticosteroids ≤4 mg/day at both weeks 36 and 52. Regulatory filings are planned for H1 2027; depemokimab is not yet approved for EGPA anywhere.

**Links:**

- [GSK — Positive phase III results for Exdensur in EGPA](https://www.gsk.com/en-gb/media/press-releases/gsk-announces-positive-phase-iii-results-for-exdensur/)

**Commentary:** Cutting EGPA biologic dosing from monthly to twice yearly wins on adherence first—detailed remission rates still await full publication.

## III. Climate, Environment & Energy

### 6. Climate｜MIT: drained peatlands already drive ~2.7% of human-caused warming; status quo could nearly double by 2100
**Summary:** MIT researchers including César Terrer and Jevan Yu published in *Nature* on November 25 a reconstruction of global peatland drainage radiative forcing for 1800–2020: net heating of about +73 (48–99) mW m⁻², or ~2.7% (1.8–3.6%) of industrial-era radiative forcing, from only ~0.4–0.5% of land area (~15% of peatlands). Drainage-driven CO₂ forcing (~+83 mW m⁻²) dwarfs avoided CH₄ (~−9 mW m⁻²). Even without new drainage, keeping current drained areas could nearly double peatland cumulative warming by 2100. Authors urge stopping new drainage and raising water tables on already drained peat.

**Links:**

- [MIT News — Draining peatlands leaves a long warming legacy](https://news.mit.edu/2026/draining-peatlands-leaves-long-warming-legacy-1125)
- [Nature — An enduring climatic burden from past peatland drainage](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-11339-0)

**Commentary:** Peat drainage is not a one-time deforestation pulse but a century-scale smoldering carbon store—rewetting the water table is how you snuff the ember.

### 7. Industrial decarbonization｜Microwave carbon-plasma makes cement clinker in seconds; Nature reports liquid-phase, recyclable route
**Summary:** A *Nature* paper published November 25 describes a microwave carbon-plasma method that compresses cement reactions from hours to seconds, with local temperatures up to ~2,400 °C that liquefy feedstocks and promote mixing into reactive clinker phases. In situ TEM shows surface disorder accelerates early hydration nucleation. Beyond limestone and clay, hydrated cement paste from waste concrete can be reprocessed into clinker meeting ordinary Portland cement specs, coupling circular feedstock use with ultrafast electrified processing for potentially scalable low-cost sustainable cement.

**Links:**

- [Nature — Sustainable cement production via a liquid-phase pathway](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-11313-w)

**Commentary:** Cement is among the hardest industries to decarbonize; turning kiln hours into plasma seconds opens an electrified, circular feedstock path.

### 8. Photovoltaics｜HKUST “trans-interface engineering” perovskite cells hit 27.04% (26.67% certified); 92% stable beyond 1,400 h
**Summary:** On November 25 HKUST highlighted Zhou Yuanyuan’s team’s *Nature Photonics* cover work (November 2026) on Trans-Interface Engineering (TIE): polymerizable monomers in perovskite and electron-transport layers form vertically penetrating covalent nano-link networks after thermal radical copolymerization, replacing flat weak-interaction interlayers. Devices reached 27.04% PCE (26.67% certified) with 1.220 V Voc and nearly threefold higher interfacial toughness; among >50 devices under MPP tracking, 92% of TIE cells stayed stable beyond 1,400 hours, outperforming controls under 85 °C/85% RH aging. A patent is filed with China Merchants Group support for commercialization.

**Links:**

- [HKUST — Trans-Interface Engineering for reliable perovskite solar cells](https://hkust.edu.hk/news/hkust-pioneers-trans-interface-engineering-boost-perovskite-solar-cell-efficiency-and)

**Commentary:** Perovskite scale-up stalls on interfacial peel; TIE stitches layers covalently so stability starts to look like an engineering metric, not a lab lottery.

## IV. Fundamental Research

### 9. Quantum computing｜QuTech demonstrates coherent control of a Majorana-based qubit; results in Nature
**Summary:** QuTech (Delft) announced November 25 coherent control of a Majorana-quasiparticle qubit, published in *Nature*. Researchers built two minimal Kitaev chains of two quantum dots each; four “poor man’s” Majorana modes encode one qubit. Briefly coupling the chains produced coherent oscillations proving deliberate state control. Oscillations currently decay within tens of nanoseconds; longer chains (e.g., two×three dots) are next to test whether topological protection improves with length. Equal first authors: Francesco Zatelli, Bart Roovers, and Nick van Loo.

**Links:**

- [QuTech — Demonstrates a qubit based on Majorana quasiparticles](https://qutech.nl/2026/11/25/qutech-demonstrates-a-qubit-based-on-majorana-quasiparticles/)

**Commentary:** The debate moves from “are there Majoranas?” to “can we operate them as qubits?”—coherence is still short, but the control handle is finally experimental.

### 10. Quantum metrology｜Fock-space “confocal” focusing prepares up to 500-photon probes; ~19.2 dB beyond the SQL
**Summary:** A November 25 *Nature* paper on a superconducting circuit QED platform introduces quantum confocal microscopy in Fock space: an O(1)-depth circuit maps coherent states to tightly concentrated Fock-like probes and back. Deterministic focused states with mean photon number up to N=500 cut photon-number variance by 21.5±1.1 dB versus a coherent state; displacement sensing achieved a record 19.20±0.20 dB metrological gain beyond the standard quantum limit. The protocol makes high-N non-Gaussian probes preparable and measurable, and is extensible to other bosonic platforms.

**Links:**

- [Nature — Quantum-enhanced sensing with 500 photons via Fock-space confocal focusing](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-11353-2)

**Commentary:** Quantum sensing long struggled to build large probes; importing confocal optics into Fock space is like giving high-dimensional metrology a manufacturable lens.

### 11. Animal navigation｜Bogong moths use a light-dependent geomagnetic inclination compass; Nature briefing highlights today
**Summary:** A November 25 *Nature* research briefing spotlights Chen et al. on Australian Bogong moths (*Agrotis infusa*; doi:10.1038/s41586-026-11322-9). Under a randomized starry sky with full-spectrum light, moths oriented in seasonally correct migratory directions using the geomagnetic field alone; 180° azimuth rotation or inclination inversion reversed heading, while polarity inversion alone did not. Removing violet–blue light (380–500 nm) or adding weak broadband RF noise abolished orientation, supporting a light-dependent radical-pair magnetic sense. The moths migrate up to ~1,000 km between breeding grounds and Alpine aestivation sites, now shown to back up their stellar compass with an inclination compass.

**Links:**

- [Nature — Bogong moths can migrate using an internal geomagnetic compass](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-026-03693-w)
- [Nature — Bogong moths navigate with a light-dependent geomagnetic inclination compass](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-11322-9)

**Commentary:** Night navigation in insects is dual-redundant—stars plus a quantum-tinged magnetic backup when clouds take the sky away.
