## I. Space & Deep Space

### 1. Caltech/DARPA ASTRA: First Autonomous On-Orbit Truss Assembly (In-Space Manufacturing)
**Summary:** Caltech and DARPA’s NOM4D program reported on Nov 6 that the Autonomous In-Space Tensioned Reflector Assembly (ASTRA) demo has built a structure in space without astronauts for the first time. The roughly 1 m³ payload launched March 30, 2026 on Momentus Vigoride 7 / SpaceX Transporter-16 and is assembling a ~1.4 m reflector antenna from composite tubes, aluminum joints, and a Kapton reflector; two prebuilt bays were deployed and a third bay was constructed in orbit. Time limits make completing all ~11 bays before the December mission end unlikely, but the team says the concept scales toward hundred-meter to kilometer-class apertures for telescopes, antennas, and space solar arrays.

### 2. ESA Prometheus: Methane Engine Hot-Fires and Multi-Reignition at P5 (Propulsion)
**Summary:** ESA reported on Nov 6 that the low-cost reusable liquid-oxygen/methane Prometheus engine completed several hot-fire tests on Oct 29 at the ESA/DLR P5 bench in Lampoldshausen, Germany. Supported by the Future Launchers Preparatory Programme with ArianeGroup as prime, the engine is in a power class similar to Ariane 6’s Vulcain 2.1 and demonstrated multiple reignitions without a high-pressure starter—relying on inlet pressure, a first for an engine of this class in Europe. Tests also validated a cold-gas-spray additively manufactured combustion chamber; upgraded P5 fired LOX/methane for the first time and can switch back to hydrogen for Vulcain-class work.

**Links:**

- [ESA — First Prometheus hot-fire at P5](https://www.esa.int/media/first-prometheus-hot-fire-at-p5)

**Commentary:** Europe’s reusable-launcher bottleneck is not only stages—it is a methane engine that can restart—and P5 moves that chain from drawings to the stand.

## II. Biomedicine & Health

### 4. Merck/Moderna: Personalized Melanoma mRNA Shot Reachable for 99% of Phase 3 Patients (Cancer Immunotherapy)
**Summary:** BioSpace reported on Nov 6 that in the Phase 3 INTerpath-001 melanoma trial (1,137 patients with resected stage 2B–4 disease), Merck and Moderna manufactured the personalized mRNA vaccine intismeran autogene for 99% of patients in the vaccine arm—matching earlier Phase 1/2 manufacturability. About 82% of screened patients had adequate tissue, and ~91% of those yielded the full neoantigen set. Combined with Keytruda, a previously disclosed interim analysis cut recurrence or death risk by about 42% (hazard ratio ~0.58). The study spans the Americas, Europe, Asia, Australia, the Middle East, and South Africa; the companies say global delivery workflows are already running.

### 6. Florida State University: Photochemical “Surgery” Turns Dead-End Sulfonamides into Drug Building Blocks (Medicinal Chemistry)
**Summary:** Florida State’s Bryan Kudisch and collaborators at Penn State and GSK described on Nov 6 a *Journal of the American Chemical Society* study: a light-driven reaction converts hard-to-repurpose sulfonamides into aniline building blocks by photochemically excising the sulfur atom, giving discarded intermediates a second chance in lead discovery. Picosecond laser spectroscopy mapped the pathway alongside computation and reaction optimization; GSK helped assess drug-discovery relevance. Sulfonamides underpin many antibiotics and drugs for diabetes, hypertension, HIV, and melanoma, yet few methods remake the central sulfur motif.

**Links:**

- [Florida State University — Photochemical method to resurrect dead-end compounds](https://news.fsu.edu/news/science-technology/2026/11/06/fsu-chemists-develop-light-powered-method-to-resurrect-dead-end-compounds-for-drug-discovery/)

**Commentary:** Medicinal chemistry’s scrap heap still holds structural diversity—photochemistry treats the sulfur atom as a removable screw, not a welded rivet.

## III. Climate & Environment

### 7. Copernicus: 2026 Virtually Certain to Exceed 1.5°C; October Sets New Heat Record (Climate Monitoring)
**Summary:** The Copernicus Climate Change Service October bulletin, widely reported on Nov 6, finds October 2026 global mean surface air temperature at 15.35°C—about 1.75°C above the 1850–1900 pre-industrial average—the warmest October on record and the third straight month above 1.5°C. A statistical model based on January–October data projects a full-year anomaly of about 1.56–1.61°C, with >99% probability of exceeding 1.5°C, making 2026 the second calendar year after 2024 to do so; the 2023–2026 four-year mean is also expected above 1.5°C. Extra-polar October sea-surface temperature averaged 20.95°C, and Niño 3.4 reached 29.94°C, both record highs for the month.

### 8. Nanjing University: Global Forest Cooling Strengthens and Covaries with Photosynthetic Heat Resistance (Eco-Climate)
**Summary:** Yongguang Zhang’s Nanjing University team with CREAF and partners highlighted on Nov 6 a *Nature Plants* global analysis for 2001–2020: forest surface thermal regulation strengthened, with the land-surface–air temperature slope β falling about 0.0014 °C °C⁻¹ yr⁻¹ and the hot-period surface–air difference ΔT about 0.027 °C yr⁻¹; roughly 54.6% and 62.7% of forest area improved on those metrics in 2011–2020 versus the prior decade, especially in the tropics. Species-richer and older forests cool more strongly and align with higher photosynthetic optima and heat resistance; rising tree cover is a leading correlate. The authors argue restoration must value diversity and integrity, not area alone.

### 9. Xi’an Jiaotong: Glacial AMOC “Abrupt” Shifts Averaged ~180 Years, Slower Than Ice Cores Imply (Paleoclimate)
**Summary:** An international team led by Xiyu Dong and Hai Cheng at Xi’an Jiaotong University was highlighted on Nov 6 (published in *Science* on Nov 5): annually laminated speleothem δ¹⁸O from Cherrapunji (India), Shuixi (China), and related caves shows Dansgaard–Oeschger warmings lasted more than 100 years and averaged about 180 years—far longer than the decadal shifts inferred from Greenland ice-core δ¹⁸O. Isotope modeling indicates a two-stage process: northeastern North Atlantic sea-ice retreat first triggers rapid atmospheric reorganization (the ice-core jump), then full AMOC strengthening and hemispheric thermal adjustment unfold over centuries. The work redefines glacial “abruptness” and urges separating fast atmospheric responses from slow ocean transitions when assessing future AMOC risk.

**Links:**

- [Science — Glacial abrupt transitions in ocean circulation were much slower than ice-core records imply](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.aed4270)
- [Phys.org — Ice-age abrupt climate shifts were not as fast as thought](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-ice-age-abrupt-climate-shifts.html)

**Commentary:** Ice cores catch the atmosphere changing face; speleothems catch the ocean catching up—split them, and AMOC risk stops being an overnight-collapse thriller.

## IV. Fundamental Research & Energy Materials

### 10. Rice/Hamburg: Quantum Gas Microscope Realizes First Tunable Three-Component Hubbard System (Quantum Simulation)
**Summary:** An international team including Peter Schauss (University of Hamburg) and Kaden Hazzard (Rice University) described on Nov 6 a *Science* experiment that uses ultracold atoms and a quantum gas microscope to realize the first tunable three-component (“three-flavor”) Hubbard model with single-atom site resolution. Adding a third component beyond the usual two-spin Hubbard expands accessible phases; the team observed a Mott state in which interactions suppress double occupancy. The platform is a testbed for ideas tied to high-Tc superconductivity, nuclear physics, and multicomponent quantum matter, with theory–experiment comparisons already pressing numerical methods to their limits.

**Links:**

- [Phys.org / Rice University — Quantum gas microscope reveals individual atoms in first tunable three-component Hubbard system](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-quantum-gas-microscope-reveals-individual.html)

**Commentary:** The two-component Hubbard is hard enough—a third flavor is not a flourish but a new dial on an equation theorists still cannot fully solve.

### 11. Monash/Oxford: Atomic-Scale Local Polar Boundaries Found in CsPbI₃ (Perovskites)
**Summary:** Weilun Li and colleagues at Monash University, using high-quality CsPbI₃ samples prepared at Oxford, reported on Nov 6 a *PNAS* study: low-dose electron microscopy reveals boundaries only about four atomic layers wide where lead atoms shift by ~15 picometres, creating local electrical polarization inside a crystal long treated as globally non-polar. Polar regions recur every ~30–50 nm; elsewhere the material stays non-polar, and a second boundary type does not polarize—showing atomic arrangement gates whether local polarity appears. The team suggests these boundaries could help steer charge separation, transport, and recombination in halide-perovskite solar cells.

**Links:**

- [Monash University — Hidden electrical properties in promising solar-cell material](https://www.monash.edu/science/news-events/news/2026/scientists-discover-hidden-electrical-properties-in-promising-solar-cell-material)

**Commentary:** “Bulk non-polar” is not “everywhere non-polar”—a 15-picometre atomic offset may be the signpost for which way photocarriers should go.

### 13. Max Planck: X-rays Can Break Both Water Bonds Before Electrons Relax (Ultrafast Chemistry)
**Summary:** The Fritz Haber Institute of the Max Planck Society and partners reported on Nov 6 that high-resolution electron spectroscopy at DESY’s PETRA III and MAX IV shows a single X-ray photon can rupture both O–H bonds in a water molecule within a few femtoseconds, forming neutral atomic oxygen before Auger electron relaxation finishes. Advanced calculations assign the sharp spectral fingerprints to excited neutral oxygen, not hydroxyl fragments. The result revises pathways for X-ray-driven aqueous chemistry and hints that similar ultrafast double-dissociation routes may exist in other small molecules and clusters.

**Links:**

- [Fritz Haber Institute — X-rays tear water apart before its electrons can relax](https://www.fhi.mpg.de/2337376/2026-11-06_X-rays-tear-water-apart-before-its-electrons-can-relax)

**Commentary:** Radiation chemistry’s textbook “ionize first, react later” order fails on femtosecond scales—water does not wait for its electrons to finish tidying up before it breaks.
