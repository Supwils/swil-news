## I. Space & Deep Space

### 1. Space｜SpaceX Dragon returns with science: bioprinted vessels, tumor organoids, and resistant microbes
**Summary:** NASA reported that the uncrewed SpaceX Dragon on the 35th commercial resupply mission (CRS-35) splashed down in the Pacific near Los Angeles at 2:03 p.m. PST on Nov. 21 after undocking from the International Space Station at 11:05 a.m. CST on Nov. 20. Dragon had arrived Oct. 15 with more than 6,300 pounds of science and supplies for Expedition 75.

### 2. Astro-computing｜PHEASANT: three exascale systems plus an AI supernova surrogate push Milky Way sims to star-by-star resolution
**Summary:** A University of Tokyo–RIKEN-led team presented PHEASANT at SC 2026 (Nov. 15–20, Chicago), running Milky Way N-body/hydrodynamics simulations with up to about 5.4 trillion particles—roughly 10,000× prior state-of-the-art—across Aurora, Frontier, and JUPITER. An AI surrogate from RIKEN’s Keiya Hirashima predicts supernova-driven gas evolution over ~100,000 years, replacing ~100-year timesteps and speeding that physics by about 20×. On Aurora, ~60,000 GPUs processed ~144 billion particles per second, with the gravity kernel at ~976 PFLOP/s (FP32); the team says the simulated star-formation rate matches observational estimates for the real Milky Way.

**Links:**

**Commentary:** Only star-resolving feedback can test how clustered supernovae rewrite disk-scale gas and star formation.

## II. Biomedicine & Health

### 3. Immuno-imaging｜Boston Children’s BCR-MERFISH maps plasma-cell clonal groups in intact tissue
**Summary:** Jeffrey Moffitt’s group at Boston Children’s Hospital introduced BCR-MERFISH in Nature Methods, extending MERFISH spatial transcriptomics with B-cell-receptor V-gene and heavy-chain constant-region probes so plasma-cell “clonal groups” and neighboring transcriptomes can be mapped in situ. In mouse ileum demos, more than 300,000 cells across two extended sections yielded 434 plasma-cell groups by receptor-gene usage, with unexpected clustering along the gut rather than uniform spread; germ-free mice had fewer plasma cells and lower diversity. Human IBD biopsy collaborations are underway; the method is not itself a diagnostic or therapy. Medical Xpress and others highlighted the work on Nov. 22.

**Links:**

- [Nature Methods — In situ profiling of plasma cell clonality with image-based single-cell transcriptomics](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41592-026-03296-3)
- [Medical Xpress — New imaging technology reveals previously unseen immune cell patterns in tissue](https://medicalxpress.com/news/2026-11-imaging-technology-reveals-previously-unseen.html)

**Commentary:** Adaptive immunity’s functional variance lives in V(D)J sequence—without spatial coordinates, inflammatory niches stay unexplained.

### 4. Oncology｜Daraxonrasib plus chemo in first-line metastatic PDAC: updated Phase 1/2 ORR ~60.7%
**Summary:** Revolution Medicines updated RMC-GI-102 subprotocol C at ENA 2026 in Barcelona: as of Sept. 28, 2026, 61 first-line metastatic pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma patients received oral daraxonrasib (RASONQUE, 200 mg daily) plus gemcitabine/nab-paclitaxel, with median follow-up 18.3 months. Confirmed ORR was 60.7% (complete responses 8.2%), disease-control rate 90.2%, median duration of response 11.1 months, median PFS 12.8 months, and 12-month OS ~76.6%; safety matched known profiles. The combination holds FDA Breakthrough Therapy Designation; global Phase 3 RASolute 303 is enrolling monotherapy, combination, and standard chemo arms.

### 5. Medicine｜FDA approves Livsayis (cemdisiran): first siRNA therapy for generalized myasthenia gravis
**Summary:** On Nov. 18 the FDA approved Regeneron’s Livsayis (cemdisiran) for adults with anti-AChR antibody-positive generalized myasthenia gravis (gMG). The C5-directed siRNA lowers circulating C5 by degrading hepatic C5 mRNA; the labeled dose is 567 mg subcutaneously every three months—the only approved gMG therapy dosed four times a year. In Phase 3 NIMBLE, week-24 MG-ADL improved by a placebo-adjusted 2.3 points (p=0.0005) and QMG by 2.8 points (p=0.0015). A boxed warning covers serious meningococcal infection risk under a REMS program; Nov. 22 medical roundups continued to flag the approval.

**Links:**

## III. Climate & Environment

### 7. Climate policy｜COP31 closes in Antalya without consensus after draft omits fossil fuels
**Summary:** UNFCCC COP31 ended Nov. 22 in Antalya, Türkiye, without adopting a cover political decision after roughly two days of overtime—making it the longest climate COP on record. The final draft avoided naming “fossil fuels,” citing COP28’s “transition away” language only indirectly while elevating carbon-removal technologies; the EU, Panama, and many climate-vulnerable states formally objected, while major oil exporters resisted stronger phase-down text. UN Secretary-General António Guterres called the outcome “deeply disappointing” while stressing there is “no ambiguity in the science.” Coverage notes it as the first COP since Copenhagen 2009 to fail to pass a final pact by consensus.

### 8. Marine biogeochemistry｜Science: atmospheric organic nitrogen delivers ~12.8 Mt N/year to global oceans
**Summary:** A HKUST–SUSTech-led team reports in Science that 418 marine aerosol samples from 21 research cruises (including polar legs), paired with simultaneous organic/inorganic nitrogen assays and a 3D atmospheric chemistry model, yield ~12.8 Tg N/year (~12.8 million tonnes) of atmospheric organic nitrogen (ON) deposited to oceans—about 27% of total nitrogen deposition, and ~50% in N-limited regions such as the central Atlantic and Indonesian seas. Top sources are biomass burning (~4.8 Tg), marine aerosol emissions (~3.9 Tg), and atmospherically formed imines (~2.2 Tg); terrestrial sources supply ~70% of deposited ON. Phys.org amplified the findings around Nov. 22.

**Links:**

**Commentary:** Pulling ON out of the “by subtraction” blind spot reconnects wildfires and land emissions to open-ocean fertility.

### 9. Wetlands｜Nature Communications meta-analysis of 276 studies: coastal wetland conversion flips sinks to sources
**Summary:** Ocean University of China’s Xianbiao Lin and colleagues published a multilevel meta-analysis of 276 studies in Nature Communications on Nov. 21. Pooled sustained-flux global warming potential (SGWP) was about −122.41 mg CO₂e m⁻² h⁻¹ for natural coastal wetlands versus +250.60 after land-use conversion, equating to roughly 0.17±0.06 Gt CO₂e over two decades; the contrast versus converted sites was larger for vegetated wetlands (~+309%) than tidal flats (~+165%). Restored wetlands showed negative pooled SGWP, though with wide uncertainty. Responses depended on pre-conversion type, destination use, hydrology, and soil C/N status.

**Links:**

- [Nature Communications — Greenhouse gas responses to coastal wetland land use change](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-79685-1)

**Commentary:** “Blue carbon” is a flux sign that conversion pathways can flip overnight—conservation and restoration must be type-specific.

### 10. Hydrology｜Science Advances: warming makes headwater regolith flow younger while bedrock flow ages
**Summary:** A Science Advances study at the Treeline site in Idaho’s Dry Creek Experimental Watershed uses seismic P-wave velocity to partition the critical zone into regolith, fractured bedrock, and fresh bedrock inside a 3D ParFlow-CLM model with particle tracking. Across +2.5°C and +4.0°C scenarios and multiple hydraulic realizations, warming raises evapotranspiration and shifts snow timing, cutting effective recharge; regolith-sourced streamflow generally becomes slightly younger, while fractured-bedrock-sourced flow ages and declines more. The authors warn of slower deep-water renewal, with implications for bedrock storage buffering and legacy contaminant retention. Chinese science media covered the paper on Nov. 22.

**Links:**

- [Science Advances — Contrasting regolith and bedrock responses of headwater catchments to warming](https://www.science.org/doi/full/10.1126/sciadv.aeh0536)
- [Ebiotrade — Differentiated regolith vs bedrock runoff responses under warming](https://n.ebiotrade.com/newsf/2026-11/20261122082831278.htm)

**Commentary:** Splitting “groundwater” into shallow and deep dashboards reframes warming risk as slower deep renewal, not just less water.

## IV. Basic Research & Materials

### 11. Materials｜University of Michigan: 1%/3% Sn-doped ITO hybrid metasurface tunes infrared light superlinearly
**Summary:** Delia Milliron, Thomas Truskett and colleagues at the University of Michigan report in ACS Nano that a one-crystal-thick metasurface of randomly mixed 1% and 3% tin-doped indium oxide nanocrystals can amplify or suppress infrared light beyond a linear mix of its parts. Detuned plasmons couple across ~2.5 nm gaps, boosting or canceling trapped energy by frequency; far-field IR, Berkeley Lab synchrotron infrared nanospectroscopy (SINS), and Oak Ridge EELS underpin a design model for doping levels and mix ratios. Solution assembly sidesteps costly top-down etching. Phys.org dated the coverage Nov. 22.

**Links:**

**Commentary:** Disorder becomes a design knob—random near-field coupling turns mixed nanocrystals into programmable IR metasurfaces.
