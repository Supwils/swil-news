## I. Climate & Environment

### 1. Copernicus: August 2026 ties the hottest month on record as global temperatures again exceed 1.5°C above pre-industrial (climate monitoring)
**Summary:** On Sep 10 the EU’s Copernicus Climate Change Service (C3S) reported that August 2026 had a global-average surface air temperature of 16.96°C—0.85°C above the 1991–2020 mean—making it the warmest August in ERA5 and tying July 2023 as the warmest calendar month on record. It was about 1.65°C above the 1850–1900 pre-industrial baseline, the first month above 1.5°C since November 2025. Extra-polar ocean sea-surface temperature averaged 21.07°C, a record for August and jointly the highest monthly value with March 2024; the highest daily extra-polar SST reached 21.11°C. Fossil-fuel warming plus a rapidly intensifying equatorial Pacific El Niño drove the spike, while western Europe logged its warmest summer on record.

### 2. Natural climate feedbacks may amplify this century’s warming by about 20%–30% (climate science)
**Summary:** Coverage on Sep 10 of work by Spark Climate Solutions, Stanford University, the Environmental Defense Fund, Woodwell Climate Research Center and colleagues finds that warming-induced greenhouse-gas releases from permafrost thaw, wildfires, wetlands and inland waters could amplify anthropogenic warming by roughly 20%–30% this century—adding an estimated 0.2°C–0.4°C under different scenarios. The paper, *Projected amplification of global warming by warming-induced greenhouse gas emissions*, appears in *Environmental Research Letters* (DOI: 10.1088/1748-9326/ae9e24). Authors stress these natural feedbacks remain undercounted in many projections and policies, while cutting human emissions still sharply reduces risk.

**Links:**

- [The Guardian — Climate ‘feedback loops’ could worsen global warming by 30%](https://www.theguardian.com/environment/2026/sep/10/climate-feedback-loops-global-warming-study)
- [Environmental Research Letters — DOI: 10.1088/1748-9326/ae9e24](https://doi.org/10.1088/1748-9326/ae9e24)

**Commentary:** If carbon-budget ledgers omit nature’s backlash, policy systematically overestimates remaining headroom.

## II. Space & Deep Space

### 3. NASA and IBM release an open-source lunar science foundation model trained on ~2 million LRO tiles (space/AI)
**Summary:** NASA announced on Sep 10, with IBM Research and academic partners, the NASA-IBM Lunar Foundation Model—among the first open-source AI foundation models built for lunar science. It is pre-trained mainly on ~17 years of Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter data: roughly 2 million image tiles, including more than 1 million ~1 m-resolution frames and nearly 964,000 ~100 m multispectral images, plus GRAIL, Lunar Prospector and JAXA SELENE inputs. Hosted on Hugging Face with open code and benchmarks, it can be fine-tuned with limited labels for crater mapping, irregular mare-patch detection and polar-ice stability estimates, where it showed a clear edge over several baselines.

**Links:**

- [NASA Science — NASA, IBM Launch AI Foundation Model for Lunar Science](https://science.nasa.gov/science-research/artificial-intelligence-lunar-foundation-model/)

**Commentary:** The lunar race is also a data race—whoever turns petabytes of Moon imagery into reusable science infrastructure sets the next research tempo.

### 5. ODIN survey confirms COSMOS-z3.1-A, the earliest and most distant known proto-supercluster (astronomy)
**Summary:** A Purdue-led team using the ODIN survey on the Dark Energy Camera at Cerro Tololo’s Blanco 4 m telescope identified COSMOS-z3.1-A when the Universe was about 2.1 billion years old (redshift ~z≈3.1)—a proto-supercluster roughly 5,000 times the Milky Way’s mass and the earliest, most distant such structure yet found; companion structure COSMOS-z3.1-C was also mapped. Spectroscopic 3D distances imply both will grow into systems more massive than the local Coma Cluster, with clumpy subunits anchored at cosmic-web filament intersections—direct evidence of bottom-up assembly. BBC Sky at Night Magazine detailed the result on Sep 10.

**Links:**

- [BBC Sky at Night Magazine — COSMOS-z3.1-A proto-supercluster](https://www.skyatnightmagazine.com/news/cosmos-z3-1-a-proto-super-cluster)

**Commentary:** Watching a superstructure assemble in the infant Universe is cosmologically richer than merely breaking a “most distant” record.

## III. Biomedicine & Health

### 6. Insilico doses first patient in GENESIS-IPF-3, billed as the first Phase III trial of a generative AI-discovered innovative drug (biomedicine)
**Summary:** Insilico Medicine announced on Sep 10 that generative-AI-discovered TNIK inhibitor Rentosertib (ISM001-055) has entered Phase III trial GENESIS-IPF-3 (NCT07687459), with first dosing at Peking Union Medical College Hospital and first enrollment the same day at Shanghai Pulmonary Hospital. The 52-week randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled study plans ~320 idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF) patients across 47 centres in China; the primary endpoint is the annual rate of decline in forced vital capacity (FVC). The company calls it the world’s first generative-AI-driven innovative drug to reach Phase III; the candidate remains investigational and unapproved.

### 8. AbbVie’s Phase 3 LUNA: atogepant meets primary endpoint in menstrual migraine prevention (neurology)
**Summary:** AbbVie reported positive topline Phase 3 LUNA results on Sep 10 for CGRP receptor antagonist atogepant as preventive treatment of menstrual migraine in adults. Across three menstrual cycles in the double-blind period, perimenstrual migraine days fell by 1.20 on average with atogepant versus 0.40 with placebo—0.80 fewer days versus placebo (p<0.0001). All eight ranked secondary endpoints also showed statistically significant and clinically meaningful gains; safety matched the known profile with no new signals. The study enrolled 468 adult women across Europe and Asia with pure menstrual or menstrually related migraine.

## IV. Energy, Planetary Science & Fundamental Research

### 9. Soochow University and LONGi: ZrO₂ interface yields 34.0% perovskite–silicon tandem with certified Voc over 2.01 V (photovoltaics)
**Summary:** On Sep 10 *pv magazine* detailed work from Soochow University and LONGi inserting discrete monoclinic ZrO₂ nanoparticles between the transparent conductive oxide and self-assembled monolayer to curb non-radiative recombination and leakage while aiding hole extraction. The monolithic perovskite–silicon tandem reached 34.0% efficiency under standard illumination, with Voc 1.997 V, Jsc 20.36 mA·cm⁻² and fill factor 83.62%; independently certified Voc was 2.014 V. After 2,000 hours the device retained 84% of initial efficiency. Results appear in *Science Bulletin* as *Nanoscale interfacial scaffold enables perovskite/silicon tandems with 34% efficiency and an open-circuit voltage over 2.01 V*.

### 10. Kyoto team maps multiple nitrogen species concentrated in Ryugu’s late-stage brines (planetary science)
**Summary:** Phys.org reported on Sep 10 that Toru Matsumoto and colleagues at Kyoto University, writing in *Nature Astronomy* (DOI: 10.1038/s41550-026-02962-y), used infrared and X-ray spectroscopy plus electron microscopy on Hayabusa2 Ryugu grains and found ammonium-bearing clays, C≡N-bearing species and sodium nitrate clustered with sodium carbonate—a marker of late-stage brine freeze-out or evaporation. The spatial pattern implies nitrogen was concentrated as parent-body brines dwindled, potentially enabling more complex organic chemistry, with analogies to ammonium- and salt-rich icy bodies such as Ceres.

**Links:**

### 11. *Nature Photonics*: all-organic polymer K153 reaches near-infrared refractive index ~2.53 (materials)
**Summary:** A Sep 10 *Nature Photonics* paper reports all-organic polymer K153, engineered with cyano substitutions, achieving refractive index n≈2.53 at 750 nm—higher than typical values for many chalcogenide glasses and CdS/ZnS/ZnSe materials (n>2.25)—while symmetric building blocks preserve strong infrared transparency. Demonstrations include environmental tolerance, infrared imaging, flexibility, doping-tunable n and nanoimprintability, pointing toward organic polymers for infrared optics.

### 12. *Nature Electronics*: cascaded heterointerface ion traps enable soft-matter multistate ionic neuromorphic processing (interdisciplinary)
**Summary:** On Sep 10 *Nature Electronics* described a multiphasic-gel ion-transport system with cascaded heterointerfacial ion traps, delivering ionic bipolar rectification ratios above 10³ and spike-strength- and timing-dependent dual-order plasticity. Integrating short- and long-term memory effects, the platform supports multistate ionic neuromorphic processing at energy as low as ~0.61 pJ per spike. The team also interfaced an in vivo neuro-iontronic processor with a transected rat sciatic nerve for bioneuron-driven ionic signal reconfiguration.

**Links:**

- [Nature Electronics — Soft-matter multistate ionic neuromorphic processing](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41928-026-01688-z)

**Commentary:** Writing synaptic plasticity into ion gels points toward wearable/implantable “wetware” computing—not another silicon FLOPS race.
