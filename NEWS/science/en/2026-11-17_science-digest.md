## I. Space & Deep Space

### 1. Space | ESA's Hera Flies "Rectangular Orbits" Around Didymos, Preparing to Survey the DART Aftermath

**Summary:** ESA's Hera spacecraft has reached the Didymos binary asteroid system and now sits about 25–30 km away, circling the pair's shared center of gravity at roughly 12 cm/s in "rectangular" paths, firing thrusters and turning 90 degrees every 3–4 days. Didymos is about 780 m across and its moon Dimorphos is 151 m wide; on September 26, 2022 NASA's DART hit Dimorphos at about 6.1 km/s and shortened its orbit by about 33 minutes. Next month Hera is expected to begin a detailed characterization phase on bowtie-shaped trajectories from about 20 km, and it will release the Juventas and Milani CubeSats.

**Links:**

- [ESA — Hera spacecraft in rectangular orbit around target asteroids](https://www.esa.int/articles/about-us/directorates/operations/space-safety/hera/hera-spacecraft-in-rectangular-orbit-around-target-asteroids)
- [ScienceDaily — ESA's Hera spacecraft is flying rectangles around NASA's asteroid crash site](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/11/261115085545.htm)

**Commentary:** The kinetic impact was effectively a single experiment, and Hera's answers on crater, internal structure and mass will decide whether the technique can be reliably reused.

### 2. Space | NASA's Terra Satellite to Stop Science Collection on December 1 After 27 Years

**Summary:** NASA says Terra, launched in 1999, will stop collecting science data on December 1, 2026 and will be powered off in January 2027 after using its remaining fuel to lower its orbit. Designed for six years, it has operated more than four times that long, carrying ASTER, CERES, MISR, MOPITT and MODIS; MOPITT and parts of CERES and ASTER were already shut off to save power. NASA states Terra's data records will remain free and public.

**Links:**

- [NASA Science — NASA's Terra Mission to End](https://science.nasa.gov/blogs/science-news/2026/11/17/nasas-terra-mission-to-end/)

**Commentary:** A flagship of Earth observation is retiring, and the continuity of long records for clouds, aerosols and land change now depends on successor satellites.

### 3. Space | NASA to Release the Roman Space Telescope's First Images on December 7

**Summary:** NASA will broadcast the first images from the Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope on December 7 at 3:30 p.m. EST from the AGU Annual Meeting in San Francisco. Roman launched August 30 on a Falcon Heavy and is traveling to Sun-Earth Lagrange Point 2, currently in commissioning with systems being powered on and calibrated. Its goals include dark energy, dark matter and exoplanets, plus a coronagraph demonstration for direct imaging of planets.

**Links:**

- [NASA — NASA Invites Media, Public to View Roman Telescope's First Images](https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/nasa-invites-media-public-to-view-roman-telescopes-first-images/)

## II. Biomedicine

### 4. Medicine | FDA Approves Gazyva for Primary Membranous Nephropathy, the First Therapy for the Disease

**Summary:** Genentech (Roche) announced on November 16 that the FDA approved Gazyva (obinutuzumab) to induce proteinuria remission in adults with primary membranous nephropathy (pMN) at risk of progression. In the Phase III Majesty trial, complete remission of proteinuria at week 104 was 36.1% versus 5.7% with tacrolimus, and overall remission was 50% versus 12.9%, with no new safety signals. It is Gazyva's third approval in immune-mediated kidney disease, and an FDA decision for systemic lupus erythematosus is expected by December.

**Links:**

- [Pharmaceutical Executive — FDA approves Klygefa, Gazyva, Igalmi](https://www.pharmexec.com/view/fda-approves-klygefa-gazyva--igalm)

**Commentary:** B-cell depletion replaces conventional immunosuppression and sets a new standard in a kidney disease that previously had no approved drug.

### 5. Medicine | FDA Approves Klygefa (gefurulimab) for Generalized Myasthenia Gravis

**Summary:** AstraZeneca's Alexion announced on November 16 that the FDA approved Klygefa for anti-AChR antibody-positive adults with generalized myasthenia gravis. It is the first dual-binding nanobody C5 inhibitor, given once weekly by subcutaneous autoinjector that patients can self-administer. In the Phase III Prevail trial, the week-26 MG-ADL score improved by 1.6 points versus placebo (p<0.0001), with safety consistent with eculizumab and ravulizumab; it is already approved in the EU and Japan. Separately, Teva's Igalmi sublingual film was approved for at-home use against agitation associated with schizophrenia and related conditions.

**Links:**

- [Pharmaceutical Executive — FDA approves Klygefa, Gazyva, Igalmi](https://www.pharmexec.com/view/fda-approves-klygefa-gazyva--igalm)

**Commentary:** Complement inhibition is moving from IV infusion to weekly home self-injection, making convenience the main differentiator in this class.

### 6. Biology | An Ancient Retrovirus May Help Explain Differences in Human and Chimpanzee Brain Development

**Summary:** Lund University researchers report in Science Advances that an insertion of the ancient virus PTERV1 in the chimpanzee genome is silenced by methylation, which also switches off the neighboring gene LINC00662, active in early human brain development. In brain organoids, the gene was strongly expressed in human but not at all in chimpanzee organoids, and deleting the viral DNA with CRISPR-Cas9 reactivated it in chimpanzee cells. Switching it off in human organoids reduced activity of genes tied to neuron development and connectivity.

**Links:**

- [ScienceDaily — An ancient virus may help explain what makes the human brain different](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/11/261114202230.htm)
- [Science Advances — paper DOI](http://dx.doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.aeg5791)

**Commentary:** The causal evidence comes from organoids and gene editing, so it remains a cell-model result, still some distance from explaining human brain evolution.

## III. Climate & Environment

### 7. Climate | Global Carbon Budget: Fossil CO2 Dips 0.4% in 2026, but Total Emissions Hit a Record

**Summary:** The Global Carbon Project's 21st Global Carbon Budget (preprint) projects 2026 fossil-fuel CO2 at 38.2 billion tonnes, down 0.4% from 2025. With land-use change (5.1 billion tonnes, driven by peat fires and forest degradation in Southeast Asia) the total reaches 43.3 billion tonnes, up from 42.8 billion. Oil fell 1.9%, gas 0.3% and coal rose 0.6%; China fell 0.9%, the US 1.3%, the EU 1.5%, and India rose 4.4%. The remaining 1.5°C budget is about 150 billion tonnes, under four years at current rates, and atmospheric CO2 is projected at 427.7 ppm.

### 9. Environment | NOAA/NASA: 2026 Antarctic Ozone Hole Is the Seventh Largest on Record

**Summary:** NOAA and NASA report the 2026 ozone hole averaged about 25 million sq km (September 7 to October 13), the seventh largest in 48 years of observations, with a maximum of 27.1 million sq km on September 18 and a minimum of 105 Dobson units over the South Pole on October 17. The cause was an unusually strong polar vortex, as weather systems that normally weaken it were weak in August and September. Chlorine from ozone-depleting chemicals over Antarctica is down about one-third from its 1980s peak, with recovery expected in the 2070s.

**Links:**

- [NOAA — NOAA, NASA release measurements of 2026 ozone hole](https://www.noaa.gov/news-release/noaa-nasa-release-measurements-of-2026-ozone-hole)
- [ScienceDaily — NASA Reveals Earth's Ozone Hole Has Grown to a Size Not Seen in a Decade](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/11/261110042546.htm)

**Commentary:** Year-to-year swings are driven by weather, but the long-run trend still follows the Montreal Protocol, one of the rare global environmental governance successes.

### 10. Environment | 4,139 Animals Found Inside Deep-Sea Polymetallic Nodules, Sharpening the Mining Debate

**Summary:** MARUM researchers at the University of Bremen report in Scientific Reports that 244 polymetallic nodules from 4,000–6,000 m in the Clarion-Clipperton Zone of the central Pacific held 4,139 animals (about 17 per nodule), with one nodule hosting 245. Nematodes inside nodules were about 10% of surrounding sediment levels and copepods about 25%, and communities were highly similar across sites up to 1,000 km apart but distinct from the surrounding mud. Nodules contain nickel, cobalt and copper, and the authors warn that deep-sea mining would destroy habitats that take millions of years to form.

**Links:**

- [ScienceDaily — Hidden ecosystem: Scientists discover 245 animals living inside a single deep-sea rock](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/11/261114202133.htm)
- [Scientific Reports — paper DOI](http://dx.doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-76737-w)

**Commentary:** Nodules are habitats in their own right, not just ore, and baseline data like this will feed directly into the deep-sea mining regulation fight.

### 11. Energy | Oregon State's MOF Material BVR-X Captures CO2 Even Under High Humidity

**Summary:** A team led by Kyriakos Stylianou at Oregon State University, with UC Berkeley, the University of Oregon and the ARAMCO Research and Development Center, reports in Angewandte Chemie a metal-organic framework, BVR-X, whose pores keep water and CO2 in separate regions so water does not block capture. It captured CO2 efficiently from a highly humid stream containing only 4% CO2 and kept working over dozens of cycles, so exhaust need not be dried first; a patent application has been filed. The report gives no quantitative uptake figures.

**Links:**

- [ScienceDaily — This Remarkable New Material Could Solve a Major Carbon Capture Problem](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/11/261116023405.htm)

**Commentary:** Humidity tolerance is a key hurdle for MOFs in real flue gas, but without capacity and cost data this remains laboratory-stage.

## IV. Fundamental Research

### 12. Cosmology | New Method Tests "Cosmic Birefringence" by Ruling Out Telescope Calibration Error

**Summary:** UC San Diego researchers propose in The Astrophysical Journal Letters a "differential polarization calibration" method that compares polarization maps made by different detector groups, cancelling any rotation they share and exposing only calibration differences between groups. Tested on eight Planck satellite polarization maps, it agreed with the established Minami–Komatsu analysis, giving an angle of about 0.37 ± 0.12 degrees. It cannot measure the absolute rotation on its own, so it does not independently confirm that the light has rotated.

**Links:**

- [ScienceDaily — A mysterious twist in the universe's oldest light could reveal new physics](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/11/261116023348.htm)
- [The Astrophysical Journal Letters — paper DOI](http://dx.doi.org/10.3847/2041-8213/aea401)

**Commentary:** It does not confirm new physics; its value is an independent check on instrumental systematics behind a still-unsettled signal.
