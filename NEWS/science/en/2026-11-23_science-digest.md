## I. Space & Deep Space

### 1. Space｜BepiColombo enters Mercury orbit: capture burn cut short by software glitch, craft remains safe
**Summary:** ESA reported on Nov. 23 that the joint ESA–JAXA BepiColombo mission achieved Mercury orbit insertion (MOI) at 18:24 UTC on Nov. 21. The Mercury Planetary Orbiter’s four axial thrusters ignited as planned, but the burn was autonomously interrupted after delivering about 77% of the planned velocity change because of a software issue in attitude guidance and thruster control, putting the spacecraft into safe mode. The resulting ~75-hour orbit (versus ~58 hours planned) is still a stable capture—the first planetary arrival via weak stability capture. Separation of the two science orbiters remains scheduled for Dec. 9 (Dec. 10 in Japan), with science operations still targeted for April 2027.

**Links:**

- [ESA — BepiColombo safely in Mercury orbit despite unexpected challenge](https://www.esa.int/articles/about-us/directorates/operations/bepicolombo-safely-in-mercury-orbit-despite-unexpected-challenge)

**Commentary:** Capture saved an eight-year cruise; the real test is whether the remaining burn sequence can absorb the higher orbit.

### 2. Astronomy｜Webb maps Cassiopeia A dust: shell merger may have begun hours before the supernova
**Summary:** NASA’s James Webb Space Telescope released a high-resolution mid-infrared view of Cassiopeia A (Cas A), published in The Astrophysical Journal Letters. Webb’s remnant-wide dust-composition map shows solar-system-sized clumps dominated by silica in some places and carbon dust in others, supporting a shell-merger scenario inside the progenitor shortly before explosion. Combined with Chandra and JAXA-led XRISM X-ray data, the team argues the merger may have been underway only hours before core collapse; Purdue University co-authors note that resolving clump-level dust chemistry makes reverse-engineering progenitor structure more tractable.

**Links:**

- [NASA — Webb Shows How Supernova Dust Chronicles Star Evolution](https://science.nasa.gov/missions/webb/nasas-webb-shows-how-supernova-dust-chronicles-star-evolution/)

**Commentary:** Writing “hours before death” into dust chemistry gives stellar endgames a high-resolution clock.

### 3. Space power｜NASA seeks partners for first lunar fission reactor: LR-1 aims for 20 kW, launch-ready by 2030
**Summary:** On Nov. 23 NASA released a request for proposals to develop Lunar Reactor-1 (LR-1), a small fission power system targeting about 20 kilowatts of electricity and at least five years of operation after deployment, with a launch-ready system by 2030. The reactor must meet Department of Energy nuclear-safety standards and, unlike solar arrays, can supply continuous power through the Moon’s roughly two-week nights for habitats, communications, rovers, and instruments. Proposals are due Dec. 21; the Space Reactors Division is also advancing SR-1 Freedom, a 2028 nuclear-electric propulsion demo.

**Links:**

- [NASA — Seeks Partners to Design First Nuclear Reactor for the Moon](https://www.nasa.gov/directorates/rtmd/nasa-seeks-partners-to-design-first-nuclear-reactor-for-the-moon/)

**Commentary:** Sustained lunar presence is less about landing legs than about lights that stay on through the long night.

## II. Biomedicine & Health

### 4. Oncology｜FDA approves Wilserdly (giredestrant): first new early-breast-cancer hormone therapy in 20+ years
**Summary:** On Nov. 23 the FDA approved the oral estrogen-receptor antagonist Wilserdly (giredestrant) for adults with estrogen receptor–positive, HER2-negative stage I–III breast cancer at intermediate-to-high recurrence risk after surgery—the first new hormone therapy for early-stage disease in more than two decades. In Phase 3 lidERA (NCT04961996), 4,170 patients were randomized to Wilserdly or tamoxifen/aromatase inhibitors for up to five years; invasive recurrence or death fell 30% (140 vs 196 events). At three years, 92.4% versus 89.6% were alive and free of invasive breast cancer—about 28 more patients per 1,000. Overall-survival follow-up continues under a postmarketing commitment.

**Links:**

- [FDA — Approves First New Hormone Therapy for Early-Stage Breast Cancer in More Than 20 Years](https://www.fda.gov/news-events/press-announcements/fda-approves-first-new-hormone-therapy-early-stage-breast-cancer-more-20-years)

**Commentary:** An oral SERD’s first Phase 3 win in the adjuvant setting still needs overall survival to turn fewer recurrences into longer lives.

### 5. Rare disease｜FDA approves Welgemda (ribitol): first therapy for LGMD2I/R9
**Summary:** The FDA the same day approved Welgemda (ribitol, BBP-418) for adults and pediatric patients weighing at least 30 kg with limb-girdle muscular dystrophy type 2I/R9—the first approved treatment for any LGMD form—manufactured by ML Bio Solutions and distributed by BridgeBio. In Phase 3 FORTIFY (NCT05775848), 112 patients were randomized; at the 12-month interim analysis, the 100-meter timed-test velocity improved 0.27 m/s versus placebo (p<0.0001), forced vital capacity showed ~5% treatment benefit (p=0.0071), and glycosylated alpha-dystroglycan rose significantly. Common adverse reactions included gastrointestinal effects and joint pain; the drug holds Orphan, Fast Track, and rare pediatric disease designations.

### 6. Infectious disease｜Invivyd reports positive Phase 3 DECLARATION interim data for VYD2311 COVID-19 prophylaxis
**Summary:** On Nov. 23 Invivyd announced that all interim primary and secondary endpoints were met in the Phase 3 DECLARATION trial of monoclonal antibody VYD2311 (ibramvibart). Among 2,359 participants randomized 1:1:1 to a single or monthly 250 mg intramuscular dose or volume-matched placebo, both active arms produced neutralizing titers consistent with the company’s 70–90% predicted protection from symptomatic COVID-19, with safety comparable to placebo; clinical events remain blinded for traditional approval. Invivyd plans a Q1 2027 BLA seeking accelerated approval, citing DECLARATION plus LIBERTY vaccine-comparator data, and expects roughly year-long protection from a single dose.

**Links:**

### 7. Climate policy｜COP31 fails to deliver a major deal: fossil-fuel language and adaptation finance deadlock
**Summary:** BBC and others reported that COP31 in Türkiye collapsed without a major new political agreement after deep splits over fossil-fuel wording and finance; talks stretched into Sunday night among the longest UN climate summits on record. The final draft avoided naming fossil fuels, only echoing COP28’s “transition away” language, drawing opposition from the EU, Pacific island states, and others; poorer countries also objected to the lack of clear adaptation-finance figures and baselines. UN Secretary-General António Guterres said he was “deeply disappointed”; COP32 is planned for November 2027 in Ethiopia.

### 8. Ecology｜HKU Nature study: hidden Amazon deciduousness shifts carbon-uptake estimates by 9.4%
**Summary:** Jin Wu’s HKU group (first author Guangqin Song) mapped monthly leafless-canopy fractions across the Amazon with 10-metre Sentinel-2 imagery and machine learning; the Nature paper was highlighted by HKU on Nov. 23. About 75.4% of mapped forest shows substantial seasonal leaflessness, and in ~70% of the basin leaf shedding precedes greening by at least three months; hydroclimate best predicts the leafless–greenness lag. Adding deciduousness-constrained leaf turnover and leaf-age demography improved photosynthetic-seasonality agreement across 79.9% of the basin and changed modelled annual photosynthetic carbon uptake by 9.4% versus conventional models (~1.42 billion tonnes of carbon per year as a model difference, not a directly observed stock change).

**Links:**

### 9. Emissions｜Nature Climate Change: global fossil CO₂ may dip 0.4% in 2026—likely temporary, not a lasting peak
**Summary:** Corinne Le Quéré and colleagues at the University of East Anglia project in Nature Climate Change that after ~0.8% average annual growth in global fossil CO₂ from 2019–2025, emissions may fall ~0.4% in 2026, with oil and gas down ~1.9% and ~0.3% while coal rises ~0.6%. They attribute much of the short-term dip to an energy shock raising oil prices, and stress that clean tech must accelerate further: solar/wind avoided ~4.1 GtCO₂ in 2025 (projected ~4.6 Gt in 2026) and EVs ~0.19 Gt (projected ~0.24 Gt); through 2028, EVs plus solar/wind may offset ~64–80% of demand-driven growth—still short of guaranteeing a sustained decline. Chinese science media highlighted the study on Nov. 23.

**Links:**

## IV. Basic Research & Chemistry

### 10. Chemistry｜Nature continuous N-to-C peptide synthesis: unprotected AAs, ≥95% average yield, up to ~100× less waste
**Summary:** Jingjun Yin, Ji Qi, Louis-Charles Campeau and colleagues reported on Nov. 23 in Nature a continuous N-to-C peptide synthesis (NCPS) that couples N-/C-unprotected amino acids or fragments without intermediate isolations, achieving ≥95% average yield per coupling and ≤1.0% epimerization across seven consecutive couplings and all 20 canonical amino acids plus many non-canonical ones. Versus conventional routes, isolations drop ~10-fold and waste by up to ~100-fold; a flow synthesizer enabled tirzepatide heptapeptide fragment, degarelix, a 14-mer macrocycle, and semaglutide demonstrations.

**Links:**

- [Nature — Continuous N-to-C Peptide Synthesis](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-11448-w)

**Commentary:** Moving the ribosome’s N-to-C logic into the plant may shift peptide-drug scale-up from protecting-group mazes to process engineering.

### 11. Chemistry｜ICIQ Nature: non-classical carbocations enable programmable hexa-substituted benzenes
**Summary:** Marcos G. Suero’s ICIQ team, with Robert S. Paton’s Colorado State computational group, published on Nov. 23 in Nature a highly regioselective [3+3] benzannulation of aromatic cyclopropenium cations with (trialkylsilyl)allenes that encodes all six benzene positions in the starting materials. DFT shows selectivity arises from evolution of an unusual non-classical carbocation with four-centre four-electron bonding. The method delivered previously inaccessible multi-substituted analogues of sonidegib and telmisartan plus triphenylene, and enantiopure allenes open a “memory of chirality” route to enantioenriched biaryls.

### 12. Structural biology｜ISTA Nature: 1.8-MDa paired poxvirus transcription engines and an mRNA export portal resolved in situ
**Summary:** Florian Schur’s group at ISTA used cryo-electron tomography and sub-tomogram averaging on purified vaccinia cores to resolve early-transcription machinery and a putative mRNA-export portal, published in Nature on Nov. 23. Packaged viral RNA polymerase is not monomeric but a ~1.8 MDa resting-state dimer bridged by fully ordered VETF; nearly half of dimers also bind kinase VPK2 and core protein L4, tethering the engines to the genome near the inner core wall. The portal comprises an E8 hexameric crown, E6 hexameric hub, and L3 dodecameric channel—an integrated production-and-dispatch architecture.

### 13. Quantum computing｜DeepMind: agent teams plus Lean formal verification shrink quantum circuits for fusion and drug metabolism
**Summary:** Google DeepMind reported on Nov. 23 using Gemini 4 agent teams to improve known quantum algorithms, with each result proved in the Lean theorem prover. For an inertial-confinement-fusion property, computing cost fell ~11.8× and error-corrected qubit demand from 5,650 to 4,338; Cytochrome P450 drug-metabolism simulation improved ~2.5× (481→333 qubits); a light-sensitive-molecule simulation became ~8× cheaper; a Grover implementation sped up ~2.57× and an arcsin arithmetic block ~290×. The team is open-sourcing its Lean library, specs, and verified solutions, while noting early fault-tolerant machines may still fall short.
