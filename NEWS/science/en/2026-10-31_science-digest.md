## I. Space & Deep Space

### 1. NASA CHAPEA Mission 2 ends: four-person crew completes 378 days of simulated Mars isolation (human deep-space prep)
**Summary:** On October 31, 2026, four volunteers in NASA’s Crew Health and Performance Exploration Analog (CHAPEA) Mission 2—Ross Elder, Ellen Ellis, James Spicer, and Matthew Montgomery—exited a roughly 1,700-square-foot 3D-printed habitat at Johnson Space Center after 378 days of isolation. The mission began October 19, 2025, and simulated communication delays, resource limits, EVAs, crop growth, and other deep-space stressors. Health and performance data are intended to inform Artemis and lunar-base design for food systems, crew time, Deep Space Network bandwidth, and behavioral health support. NASA livestreamed the egress ceremony at 3:00 p.m. EDT.

### 2. ESA Hera first resolves Didymos and Dimorphos as separate targets (planetary defense)
**Summary:** ESA reported that after braking maneuvers, the Hera planetary-defense spacecraft used its monochromatic Asteroid Framing Camera on the evening of October 18 to resolve the Didymos–Dimorphos binary for the first time, from about 1,280 km away; earlier views still merged the pair into one spot. The system was about 197 million km from Earth. Dimorphos is ~151 m across and Didymos ~780 m, orbiting ~1.15 km apart on average. ESA said sharper Dimorphos imagery is planned for November 25, ahead of close-up “crash site” investigation and CubeSat deployment.

**Links:**

- [ESA — First light: Hera resolves two asteroids instead of one](https://www.esa.int/Space_Safety/Hera/First_light_Hera_resolves_two_asteroids_instead_of_one)

**Commentary:** After DART’s deflection experiment, Hera moves from “can we hit it” to “what did the hit actually do.”

### 3. Swift Observatory science mission ends as reentry window nears (high-energy astrophysics)
**Summary:** NASA’s Neil Gehrels Swift Observatory ended science operations after its orbit fell to about 275 km, where drag prevented stable pointing; the final observation was October 20. Designed for a two-year prime mission, Swift operated for more than two decades. U.S. Space Force and reentry trackers expect the ~1.5-ton spacecraft to reenter around early November, with most of it burning up and a few dense pieces possibly reaching the ocean. A prior boost/rescue attempt did not proceed; a brief return to observing accelerated the orbital decay.

**Links:**

- [NASA Science — NASA’s Swift Mission Ends, Leaves Behind Scientific Bonanza](https://science.nasa.gov/missions/swift/nasas-swift-mission-ends/)
- [AP News — NASA telescope predicted to come down after failed rescue](https://apnews.com/article/swift-observatory-nasa-telescope-e890935df7f468048594286566a96357)

**Commentary:** A pioneer of rapid gamma-ray-burst response exits, leaving a deep archive and a clear need for the next generation of transient telescopes.

## II. Biomedicine & Health

### 4. FDA grants accelerated approval to Inovio’s VOCERYQ, the first approved DNA medicine (immunotherapy)
**Summary:** On October 30, 2026, Inovio announced FDA accelerated approval of VOCERYQ (formerly INO-3107), a plasmid DNA immunotherapy targeting HPV-6/11, to reduce surgery frequency in adults with recurrent respiratory papillomatosis (RRP); the company calls it the first FDA-approved DNA medicine. Approval rests on RRP-001: 32 adults enrolled; among 26 in the efficacy set (median 4.5 surgeries in the prior year), 7/26 (27%, 95% CI 12–48%) had a complete response (no surgery over 12 months). Shipping is expected in ~60 days, with full commercial launch in Q1 2027; continued approval depends on confirmatory studies.

### 5. Gladstone: type VI retrons trigger phage defense “in reverse” (bacterial immunity / gene editing)
**Summary:** Seth Shipman’s Gladstone team reports in Molecular Cell a non-canonical type VI retron mechanism (exemplified by Vibrio Retron-Vpa2): little msDNA is detected without infection; after phage entry, msDNA accumulates, engages a riboregulator on a hybrid RNA, enables RNase H cleavage, and derepresses translation of a toxic small protein that kills the infected cell. That inverts the classic “msDNA as antitoxin until phage lifts repression” logic. ScienceDaily highlighted the work on October 31; the authors argue the molecular circuit could inspire more precise DNA-editing and gene-control tools.

**Links:**

- [Gladstone — Newly Discovered Antiviral System in Bacteria Is ‘Strangest of Its Kind’](https://gladstone.org/news/newly-discovered-antiviral-system-bacteria-is-strangest-kind/)

**Commentary:** Bacterial immunity keeps supplying counterintuitive switches that gene-editing toolkits can try to engineer.

### 6. Lilly’s retatrutide beats semaglutide head-to-head on glucose and weight (metabolic disease)
**Summary:** On October 28 Lilly reported Phase 3 TRANSCEND-T2D-2 topline results: triple agonist retatrutide (9 mg / 12 mg) versus semaglutide 2 mg over 80 weeks in about 1,298 adults with type 2 diabetes inadequately controlled on metformin (±SGLT2). Both retatrutide doses cut HbA1c by 2.2 points from a ~8.3% baseline versus 1.8 for the comparator; body weight fell ~19.5% / 20.9% versus ~9.4%. Primary and key secondary endpoints were met; discontinuation for adverse events was 4.8% / 7.9% versus 2.7%. Lilly plans a Q1 2027 FDA filing.

## III. Climate & Environment

### 7. Global warming intensifies compound wind–solar “energy droughts” (renewable climate risk)
**Summary:** Amirhossein Aminimehr, Hossein Tabari and colleagues at the University of Antwerp published an open-access study in Communications Earth & Environment on October 29 using CMIP6 to assess compound renewable energy droughts—simultaneous low wind and solar output—at 1.5°C, 2°C and 3°C warming. Drought duration, frequency and intensity intensify with warming; at 3°C, statistically significant changes affect about 69.4%, 74.0% and 50.2% of global land area, respectively. Machine learning identifies solar radiation as the dominant driver, with its influence strengthening at higher warming levels.

**Links:**

- [Communications Earth & Environment — Global warming intensifies compound renewable energy droughts](https://www.nature.com/articles/s43247-026-04183-7)

**Commentary:** Energy transition planning cannot stop at installed capacity; grids and storage must price the joint “no wind, no sun” tail risk.

### 8. 2026 State of the Climate report: 7 of 16 indicators at record extremes (planetary vital signs)
**Summary:** The seventh annual international report led by Oregon State University scientists, published in BioScience and widely covered in Chinese science media around October 30, finds 7 of 16 tracked climate indicators at record extremes. Atmospheric CO₂ is about 428 ppm—higher than in ~2 million years; the warming rate rose from ~0.19°C/decade (1970–2010) to ~0.36°C/decade since; glacier thickness and Greenland ice mass hit all-time lows; 2025 fossil-fuel use still rose ~0.9%, with energy-related CO₂ topping ~41 Gt for the first time. Positives include a fourth straight year of falling Amazon deforestation in Brazil and ~18% growth in solar and wind consumption.

**Summary:** In Nature Photonics, vapor-phase trialkylamine passivation that weakens C₆₀ intermolecular coupling cut perovskite/C₆₀ interfacial non-radiative recombination, yielding inverted cells with a certified 27.2% PCE (stabilized ~26.6% on 1.02 cm²) that retained >98% of initial efficiency after ~1,000 h continuous illumination. ## IV. Basic Research & Quantum Technology

### 11. IBM links two 120-qubit processors with four meter-scale superconducting cables (modular quantum computing)
**Summary:** An IBM team demonstrated four ~80 cm superconducting aluminum coaxial L-couplers linking two 120-qubit Nighthawk R1 processors inside a dilution refrigerator. Relay-based state transfer averaged ~99.00% fidelity (best ~99.29%); distant CZ gates averaged ~94.75% (best ~96.42%); the links supported cross-module entanglement, including 4- and 6-qubit GHZ states and simultaneous Bell pairs on neighboring links. The work argues meter-scale quantum interconnects are a practical path for modular, fault-tolerant scaling; related preprint and late-October coverage detail the results.

**Links:**

### 12. Niobium–titanium alloys stay tough at 20 K via twinning and phase change (extreme-environment materials)
**Summary:** A UC Berkeley / Lawrence Berkeley National Lab / Oxford collaboration reports in Nature that two Nb–Ti-based bcc alloys remain damage-tolerant at 20 K (~−253°C) with no measurable ductile-to-brittle transition. Temperature-insensitive nanoscale twinning plus a local phase transformation create stress concentrations at grain boundaries that nucleate new dislocations, sustaining plasticity and crack-tip blunting when conventional screw-dislocation motion stalls. ScienceDaily amplified an Oxford-sourced write-up on October 31; the authors say the pathway can guide structural metals for cryogenic service.

### 13. Single-molecule spin–photon interface: carbene qubit reaches millisecond coherence (quantum networking)
**Summary:** An open-access Nature paper published October 28 shows a triplet ground-state carbene embedded in a structurally matched host crystal acting as a single-molecule-addressable spin–photon interface. The system exhibits narrow zero-phonon lines, spectral stability over more than an hour, spin-selective optical transitions and single-molecule ODMR. At 4.5 K, dynamical-decoupling coherence reaches ~2.2 ms and spin relaxation ~21 ms. The results place chemically designed molecular qubits among platforms for single-emitter quantum optics while retaining bottom-up synthetic control.

**Links:**

- [Nature — A single-molecule spin–photon interface](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-11147-6)

**Commentary:** From defect color centers to synthesizable molecules, quantum-network nodes gain another designable materials path.
