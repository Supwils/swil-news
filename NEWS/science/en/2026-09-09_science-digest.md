### 1. OpenAI releases Navier–Stokes singularity proof and Lean formalization; Clay Institute has not declared the problem solved (math/AI)
**Summary:** On Sep 8, OpenAI said an internal multi-agent system produced, in about 88 hours, a proof related to the Navier–Stokes existence and smoothness Millennium Prize Problem, arguing that an initially smooth fluid at rest, driven by a smooth external force and with finite energy throughout, can develop a singularity in finite time—framed as statements C and D in the official prize formulation. The company published a write-up, PDF, and Lean formalization (formalization and verification took about another 17 hours via GPT-6 Astra). Coverage on Sep 9 notes that the Clay Mathematics Institute still lists the problem as unsolved and says evaluation will be deliberately unhurried; OpenAI says it will not claim the $1 million prize. Experts stress the forced setting and that independent assessment is still required; related progress by Levent Alpöge and Tristan Buckmaster had been released around the same window.

## II. Space & Deep Space

### 2. Hubble and Webb together find 27 trans-Neptunian objects; fewer tiny bodies than some models predicted (astronomy)
**Summary:** NASA reported on Sep 8 (widely covered Sep 9) that teams led from the University of Victoria and Northern Arizona University combined Hubble (visible) and James Webb (infrared) on the same sky patch, discovering and characterizing 27 extremely faint trans-Neptunian objects (TNOs). The smallest is about 5 km across—comparable in faintness to spotting fireflies on the Moon from Earth. Two complementary papers appear in *The Astronomical Journal* (DOI: 10.3847/1538-3881/ae9084 and 10.3847/1538-3881/ae907f). Surprises include fewer very small TNOs than some planet-formation models expect, similar size distributions for dynamically “hot” and “cold” populations, and colors matching larger siblings—suggesting surfaces may preserve early Solar System history.

**Links:**

- [NASA Science — Hubble, Webb Find Far-out Solar System Objects ‘Remember’ Past](https://science.nasa.gov/missions/hubble/nasas-hubble-webb-find-far-out-solar-system-objects-remember-past/)
- [Sci.News — Hubble and Webb Team Up to Spy Tiny Frozen Worlds beyond Neptune](https://www.sci.news/astronomy/hubble-webb-trans-neptunian-objects-15052.html)

**Commentary:** The Solar System’s leftover planetesimal inventory looks thinner than expected—formation models may overcount small-body production and collisions.

### 4. CAS Earth–Moon two-way laser link highlighted today: ~1.25 Mbps up, ~100 Mbps down (space communications)
**Summary:** People’s Daily Online and related outlets on Sep 9 covered China’s Technology and Engineering Center for Space Utilization (CAS) establishing a two-way laser communications link across roughly 400,000 km after more than a year of in-orbit testing, with about 1.25 Mbps uplink and 100 Mbps downlink—extending Chinese space laser links from near-Earth to cislunar space. Researchers say an 8K lunar image that might take 4–5 minutes on a ~5 Mbps microwave link could take about 12 seconds at 100 Mbps. The tech is aimed at crewed lunar missions, a lunar research station, and deep-space data return; external commentary notes rates remain below NASA’s 2013 lunar laser demo (~622 Mbps downlink).

## III. Biomedicine & Life Sciences

### 5. Twin Nature papers: Denisovan radius and tools from Yunnan’s Bianfu Cave fill a Southwest China gap (paleoanthropology)
**Summary:** On Sep 9, *Nature* published two studies on Bianfu Cave in Yunnan. Fu Qiaomei and colleagues morphologically screened more than 60,000 bone fragments and used paleoproteomics to identify Denisovan remains dated ~167–134 ka, including two parietal fragments, a proximal radius (the first known Denisovan forearm long bone), and teeth (DOI: 10.1038/s41586-026-10976-9). A companion archaeology paper reports a cultural sequence ~190–70 ka, more than 1,300 stone tools, and specialized hunting of medium-to-large prey (DOI: 10.1038/s41586-026-10997-4). The site is among the richest Denisovan localities outside Siberia’s Denisova Cave and provides behavioral context for long-term occupation in eastern Asia.

**Links:**

- [Nature — Ancient proteins identify various Denisovan remains from Southwest China](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10976-9)
- [Phys.org / CAS — First Denisovan forearm bone discovered in southwest China](https://phys.org/news/2026-09-denisovan-forearm-bone-southwest-china.html)

**Commentary:** Denisovans shift from DNA ghosts to people with a radius, toolkit, and hunting grounds—Southwest China finally has a registerable home site.

### 6. Seaport’s GlyphAllo Phase 1 driving simulation meets primary endpoint: no next-morning impairment (neuropsychiatry)
**Summary:** On Sep 9, Seaport Therapeutics reported positive topline results from a Phase 1 driving simulation trial of GlyphAllo (SPT-300), an oral allopregnanolone prodrug, in 33 healthy volunteers. Evening dosing of 375 mg—the highest dose in the ongoing Phase 2b MDD trial BUOY-1—did not impair next-morning driving versus placebo on Day 5 (primary endpoint, SDLP); a single 250 mg dose likewise showed no impairment on Day 2. Zopiclone 7.5 mg as positive control confirmed assay sensitivity; no serious adverse events were reported. Results will be submitted to the FDA as part of the GlyphAllo program.

### 10. Deep-sea fungus cytosporin pathway: non-enzymatic CO₂ capture to five-membered cyclic carbonate in neutral water (green chemistry)
**Summary:** Chinese Science News / Guangming Online reported on Sep 9 that teams led by Yan Yan (SCSIO, CAS) and Wan-Shan Li (Hainan Normal University) mapped biosynthesis of cytosporin from a deep-sea *Eutypella* fungus and found the five-membered cyclic carbonate can form spontaneously via epoxide-mediated coupling with bicarbonate (aqueous CO₂) under ambient, near-neutral aqueous conditions—without transition-metal catalysts. ¹⁸O tracing showed carbonate oxygens come from bicarbonate. The work appears in *JACS* (DOI: 10.1021/jacs.6c12612); the room-temperature rate constant is about 0.15 L·mol⁻¹·h⁻¹, and related scaffolds also react, suggesting broader relevance.

### 11. Nature Physics: eight-bit cascaded random-access quantum memory with <1.5% average infidelity per mode (quantum information)
**Summary:** Li et al. reported on Sep 8 in *Nature Physics* an eight-bit cascaded random-access quantum memory: a single transmon classically addresses seven memory modes while a buffer layer isolates a multimode storage cavity from processor nonlinearities. Arbitrary random access achieves average infidelity below about 1.5% per mode, with characterization of many-body interactions that dominate the error budget. The architecture aims to cut control lines per logical qubit and support transversal operations inside the memory module as a scalable unit cell for fault-tolerant designs (DOI: 10.1038/s41567-026-03418-w).

**Links:**

- [Nature Physics — A cascaded random access quantum memory](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41567-026-03418-w)

**Commentary:** Superconducting processors need memory hierarchy, not only more qubits—this imports a DRAM-like intuition into the quantum stack.

### 12. Nature Materials: single-crystalline CoSi semimetal interconnects ~10× lower resistivity than copper at ~20 nm (materials)
**Summary:** Chen et al. on Sep 8 in *Nature Materials* demonstrate single-crystalline CoSi semimetals for scalable interconnects: resistivity falls from about 7.0 to 0.72 μΩ·cm as thickness shrinks from ~1 µm to ~20 nm via a highly conductive surface path—about one-tenth copper’s resistivity at the same ~20 nm thickness. High cohesive energy (~5.4 eV) and migration barrier (~3.7 eV) support current densities up to ~10⁸ A·cm⁻² and temperatures up to ~450 °C; RF operation reaches ~40 GHz, with integration into a 16-nm-node silicon ring oscillator (DOI: 10.1038/s41563-026-02740-1).

**Links:**

- [Nature Materials — Single-crystalline CoSi semimetals with high conductivity and reliability](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41563-026-02740-1)

**Commentary:** Facing copper’s nanoscale wall, semimetal surface conduction offers a counterintuitive “thinner, more conductive” path.
