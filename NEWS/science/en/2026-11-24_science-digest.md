## I. Space & Deep Space

### 1. Space｜Dragonfly gets its eyes: seven DragonCam cameras integrated ahead of a planned July 2028 launch to Titan
**Summary:** NASA reported on November 24 that Dragonfly, the rotorcraft bound for Saturn’s moon Titan, has completed integration of its DragonCam science imaging suite at the Johns Hopkins Applied Physics Laboratory (APL). All seven cameras built by Malin Space Science Systems were installed in insulated housings, tested, and integrated into the craft. Each views Titan through a foam window that shields against cryogenic ambient temperatures near −290°F (about −179°C); an APL-built LED array spanning infrared to ultraviolet will help infer surface composition and may flag fluorescent organics during Titan’s nearly eight-Earth-day night. Other packages—DraGNS, DraGMet, DraMS, and the DrACO sampling drill—are due for delivery and testing over coming months. Launch is planned for July 2028, with arrival in late 2034.

**Links:**

- [NASA — Titan-Bound Dragonfly Rotorcraft Gets Its Eyes](https://science.nasa.gov/missions/dragonfly/nasas-titan-bound-dragonfly-rotorcraft-gets-its-eyes/)

**Commentary:** Sampling needs a drill, but knowing where to drill—and how those cuttings map onto Titan’s landscape—depends on these cameras first reading the scene.

### 2. Space power｜NASA locks in SR-1 Freedom architecture: 2028 nuclear-electric propulsion demo to deliver Mars helicopters
**Summary:** NASA announced on November 24 that Space Reactor-1 (SR-1) Freedom completed a major design review and locked in key spacecraft architecture elements, targeting a late-2028 launch to demonstrate nuclear electric propulsion in deep space for the first time. The mission will combine nuclear and solar power to deliver SkyFall—a capsule carrying three next-generation helicopters—to Mars to study geology and climate history and search for subsurface ice. Design updates include a shorter boom between the reactor and the Power and Propulsion Element to cut mass and simplify assembly, plus shielding improvements that allow the reactor to sit closer to the spacecraft body; communications will use larger X-band antennas. NASA also awarded Johns Hopkins APL about $39.3 million to develop the nuclear power module’s heat-rejection system.

**Links:**

- [NASA — Teams Lock in Mission Architecture for SR-1 Freedom](https://www.nasa.gov/general/nasa-locks-in-sr1-freedom-mission-architecture/)

**Commentary:** Moving nuclear-electric propulsion from concept art to a buildable configuration is the first freight track for Moon-base power and deeper solar-system missions.

## II. Biomedicine & Health

### 3. Oncology｜FDA approves Ekitna (bezuclastinib) with sunitinib: first new post-imatinib GIST option in more than 20 years
**Summary:** On November 24 the U.S. FDA approved the kinase inhibitor Ekitna (bezuclastinib, Cogent Biosciences) in combination with sunitinib for adults with advanced gastrointestinal stromal tumors (GIST) previously treated with imatinib. In the global randomized Phase 3 PEAK trial (NCT05208047; n=413), median progression-free survival was 16.5 months for the combination versus 9.2 months for sunitinib alone (HR 0.50, p<0.0001), with objective response rates of 46% versus 26%; overall survival data were immature. The recommended dose is bezuclastinib 600 mg once daily with sunitinib 37.5 mg; labeling includes a boxed warning for hepatotoxicity. The combination received breakthrough therapy and orphan designations, and Cogent said the product is available by prescription in the U.S.

**Links:**

- [FDA — Approves bezuclastinib with sunitinib for advanced GIST](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/cancer-approvals/fda-approves-bezuclastinib-combination-sunitinib-advanced-gastrointestinal-stromal-tumors)
- [Cogent — FDA approval and launch of EKITNA for GIST](https://investors.cogentbio.com/news-releases/news-release-details/cogent-biosciences-announces-fda-approval-and-launch-ekitnatm)

**Commentary:** Opening roughly seven more months of progression-free survival after imatinib sets up the harder test: whether overall survival converts “holding the tumor” into longer life.

### 4. Pediatric oncology｜Penn State hits an “undruggable” Ewing sarcoma fusion via CK2: Phase 1 underway at ~27 hospitals
**Summary:** Penn State College of Medicine reported on November 24 that Ewing sarcoma depends on the EWS-FLI1 fusion protein, long considered undruggable, so the team targeted casein kinase II (CK2), which stabilizes it. In work published online in Molecular Cancer Therapeutics, the CK2 inhibitor CX-4945 (silmitasertib) reduced EWS-FLI1 abundance via K48 ubiquitination, repressed metastasis-associated programs, cut migration and invasion in vitro, slowed tumor growth and extended survival in patient-derived xenograft models, and showed synergistic cytotoxicity with irinotecan. Five-year survival for recurrent or metastatic disease remains below 30%, and standard chemotherapy has barely changed in more than 30 years. A Phase 1/II trial (NCT06541262) is evaluating the inhibitor with chemotherapy for safety and tolerability across roughly 27 hospitals.

**Links:**

- [Penn State — Molecular back door destroys undruggable Ewing sarcoma protein](https://www.psu.edu/news/research/story/molecular-back-door-destroys-undruggable-protein-driving-rare-childhood-cancer)
- [Molecular Cancer Therapeutics — CK2 inhibitor CX-4945 in metastatic Ewing sarcoma](https://aacrjournals.org/mct/article/doi/10.1158/1535-7163.MCT-26-0300/790114/CK2-inhibitor-CX-4945-targets-the-EWS-FLI1)

**Commentary:** If you cannot block the driver, dismantle its stabilizer—and rewrite an undruggable target into a testable clinical path.

### 5. AI biology｜GPT-6 shows intrinsic protein-design skills: 10.9% of 322 lab-tested minibinders showed binding
**Summary:** OpenAI reported on November 24 that GPT-6 models can design protein structures and sequences using only reasoning and general-purpose coding tools, without calling specialized biology models to generate those designs. In lab work with partner Onava, 322 minibinders based on GPT-6.2 three-dimensional designs—with specialized models supplying sequences and helping select candidates—yielded 35 binders (10.9%) across five targets including PD-L1, IL-7Rα, VEGF-A, and IL-17A; separate antibody-loop redesign screens retained binding in roughly 66%–70% of fragments. OpenAI noted that full structure prediction remains a weakness and that specialized models still matter for sequencing and triage; Nature concurrently covered general-purpose LLMs beginning to match or locally beat specialist protein tools.

**Links:**

**Commentary:** General models are starting to draft structures themselves, but wet-lab hit rates and the structure-prediction gap still gate real drug discovery.

## III. Climate & Environment

### 7. Ocean CDR｜Yale unified framework compares OAE, DOR, and enhanced rock weathering: efficiency swings with latitude and season
**Summary:** A Yale-led team published in Nature Communications on November 24 a unified efficiency framework for ocean alkalinity enhancement (OAE), direct ocean removal (DOR), and simultaneous alkalinity-plus-DIC addition (ADA, as in enhanced rock weathering), using Community Earth System Model 2 coupled to a marine biogeochemistry library over 15-year global simulations. OAE and DOR efficiencies both vary strongly by location and season, peaking in summer at subpolar latitudes; deep-water formation regions underperform because perturbed water subducts quickly and loses atmospheric contact. For ADA/enhanced-rock-weathering pathways, roughly 3%–20% of previously sequestered carbon could outgas again by year 15, depending on region and season. The authors stress that the ocean is not a uniform sink and that MRV must be time- and place-specific.

**Links:**

- [Yale News — A unified framework for carbon dioxide removal — with an ocean view](https://news.yale.edu/2026/11/24/unified-framework-carbon-dioxide-removal-ocean-view)
- [Nature Communications — Global intercomparison of ocean-based geochemical CDR efficiencies](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-78856-4)

**Commentary:** Turning “add alkalinity to the sea” into comparable efficiency maps is what lets carbon-credit accounting move from slogans to auditable numbers.

### 8. Energy systems｜MIT: at ~80% U.S. wind–solar supply, cost-optimal grids may still waste ~10% surplus power
**Summary:** MIT and Georgia Tech researchers reported in Joule on November 24 that in cost-optimal U.S. systems meeting demand with about 80% wind and solar, plants could still generate roughly 10% more power than the grid can immediately use or store—even when battery costs are very low—and the pattern holds across many national scenarios. Hourly modeling with about 40 years of weather data finds surplus most often in spring and fall, typically lasting 7–12 hours (often midday to afternoon) and sometimes spanning multiple days. The authors argue that schedulable industry (grinding, electrified steelmaking), seasonal products, and flexible data-center or AI training loads could turn curtailment into a designable resource.

**Links:**

- [MIT News — Excess renewable energy could power new industrial processes](https://news.mit.edu/2026/excess-renewable-energy-could-power-new-industrial-processes-1124)

**Commentary:** The transition bottleneck is not only “more batteries”—it may also be factories and compute that can start and stop with spring-afternoon surplus.

### 9. Climate adaptation｜Cambridge and partners release HEAT-Analysis: France, Spain, and Italy face the highest heatwave grid-bottleneck risk
**Summary:** The University of Cambridge, City University of Hong Kong, and The Chinese University of Hong Kong (Shenzhen) on November 24 highlighted the open-source HEAT-Analysis toolkit and its Nature Energy framework that jointly models heat-driven cooling demand, generator derating, and transmission thermal limits inside an AC optimal power-flow formulation. Simulations of hundreds of projected 2026–2030 heatwave scenarios across eight Western European public-grid configurations find higher bottleneck and load-shedding risk for France, Spain, and Italy, with Germany and the UK comparatively more resilient; even assuming storage starts fully charged, existing resources were insufficient to fully prevent shedding. Operators can plug in proprietary topology data to locate vulnerabilities and test storage siting or line upgrades.

**Links:**

## IV. Fundamental Research

### 10. Particle physics｜Fermilab’s FCFD “stopwatch” chip hits ~40 ps timing and 15 µm position for the Electron-Ion Collider
**Summary:** Fermilab announced on November 24 that a team led by Artur Apresyan built the Fermilab Constant Fraction Discriminator (FCFD) readout chip, which in beam tests with Caltech and Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory reached about 40-picosecond timing accuracy and 15-micrometer position precision against requirements for ePIC, the first experiment planned at Brookhaven’s Electron-Ion Collider in the mid-2030s. The chip uses constant-fraction discrimination to correct amplitude-dependent time-walk in hardware and is optimized for AC-LGAD silicon sensors; results appear in Nuclear Instruments and Methods in Physics Research. Precise time-of-flight helps distinguish protons, kaons, and pions.

**Links:**

- [Fermilab — Ultrafast ‘stopwatch’ chip for particle detectors](https://news.fnal.gov/2026/11/fermilab-led-team-builds-ultrafast-stopwatch-for-particle-detectors/)

**Commentary:** Next-generation colliders compete not only on beam energy but on who can sort trillionths-of-a-second events cleanly.

### 11. Chemistry｜Rice visible-light catalysis splits CO₂ directly: ceria “oxygen sponge” yields ~15,000 μmol·h⁻¹·g⁻¹ CO
**Summary:** Work from Rice University’s Naomi Halas and Peter Nordlander groups was widely covered on November 24 (PNAS paper DOI: 10.1073/pnas.2628340123): copper nanoparticles act as light antennas feeding ruthenium sites, while cerium oxide serves as an “oxygen sponge” that temporarily stores liberated oxygen so CO₂ can be split into CO and O₂ under visible light without external furnace heat. Peak performance under 475-nm blue light produced CO at about 15,000 μmol·h⁻¹·g⁻¹; light-driven steady production lasted about 238 minutes—nearly seven times longer than heat-driven controls that degraded in ~35 minutes—and nearly three times the yield of ceria-free controls. The antenna-reactor approach is licensed to Houston spinout Syzygy Plasmonics for industrial photoreactor scale-up.

### 12. Chemical biology｜HKUST-led single-atom editing of boracycles: O-to-S swap unlocks ferroptosis inhibition with ~20× potency gain
**Summary:** Teams led by Yangjian Quan at the Hong Kong University of Science and Technology and Billy Wai-Lung Ng at The Chinese University of Hong Kong published in Nature Chemistry on November 24 a ring-expansion method in which benzoboretenes act as a “diradical reservoir” with elemental sulfur, selenium, or sulfur dioxide, formally replacing the oxygen of the classic benzoxaborole pharmacophore with S, Se, or SO₂ to access three previously inaccessible benzochalcogenaborole skeletons. Preliminary biology showed that the oxygen-to-sulfur edit confers potent inhibition of RSL3-induced ferroptosis in HMC3 human microglia; structure–activity optimization leveraging broad functional-group tolerance improved cellular potency about 20-fold. A patent covering the method, molecules, and anti-ferroptosis uses has been filed.

**Links:**

- [Nature Chemistry — Construction of benzochalcogenaboroles via single-atom editing of boracycles](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41557-026-02301-8)

**Commentary:** Tweaking peripheral substituents is routine; editing one atom in the core skeleton is what unlocks entirely new biological function.
