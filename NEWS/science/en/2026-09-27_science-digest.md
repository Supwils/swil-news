## I. Planetary Science & Astronomy

### 1. OSIRIS-REx samples place Bennu's parent near Jupiter's water-ice line (Planetary origins)
**Summary:** Space.com on Sep 27 reported a Science Advances paper (Sep 23) from Maria Schönbächler's group at ETH Zurich. NASA's OSIRIS-REx returned about 120 grams of Bennu regolith in 2023; the lab analyzed roughly half a gram for iron, titanium, and chromium isotopes. Iron and titanium are well mixed, and the fingerprint matches Ryugu and rare CI chondrites. The team argues that the parent bodies of Bennu, Ryugu, and CI meteorites formed about 4.5 billion years ago near the water-ice line, roughly where Jupiter orbits today: fine dust from the inner and outer disk met there, and water ice acted as glue. A young, fast-growing Jupiter blocked coarser grains while letting fine dust through. Bennu therefore carries traits of both regions and looks closer to the average mix of the solar nebula than to a late, comet-like birth far from the Sun.

**Commentary:** Returned sample isotopes turn a near-Earth asteroid's birthplace into a testable snow-line mixing zone.

### 2. Mercury's radial contraction revised up to about 11.6 km as impact debris hides wrinkles (Planetary geology)
**Summary:** BBC Science Focus on Sep 27 covered a Geophysical Research Letters study led by the German Aerospace Center with Hokkaido University and the University of Tokyo. Cooling should leave shortening structures across Mercury, yet a global roughness map shows fewer visible wrinkles on rougher terrain, which the team attributes to impact ejecta burying or obscuring them. Using the global-average contractional strain of Broquet and Andrews-Hanna, estimated radial contraction rises from 8.3 km to 11.6 km, about 10% to 30% above the previous tally. More shrinkage fits a larger metal core, fewer light elements in that core, or a hotter start. The authors say the correction may still be low; ESA/JAXA BepiColombo is due to enter Mercury orbit in November 2026 and should resolve smaller structures than MESSENGER could measure reliably.

**Links:**

- [BBC Science Focus — Mercury is shrinking faster than anyone thought](https://www.sciencefocus.com/news/mercury-is-shrinking-faster-than-anyone-thought)
- [Geophysical Research Letters — Underestimation of planetary contraction due to surface roughness (DOI: 10.1029/2026GL124067)](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1029/2026GL124067)

**Commentary:** Once buried scarps are put back on the ledger, Mercury's cooling history and core composition have to be recalculated.

### 3. Indian team proposes the first quadruple-double radio galaxy, with four jet episodes (Radio astronomy)
**Summary:** Phys.org on Sep 27 reported a preprint (arXiv:2609.08506, submitted Sep 8) from IISER Pune and the National Centre for Radio Astrophysics. J023721.13−010528.5 sits in a bright cluster galaxy at redshift about 0.372. The outermost hotspots are about 1.2 megaparsecs apart in projection, making it one of the largest known S-shaped radio galaxies. Images from the upgraded GMRT (about 400 and 700 MHz), MeerKAT (1280 MHz), and the VLA (3 GHz) show four pairs of components straddling the core, each a local peak in flux and equipartition energy density. Spectral ages increase outward from roughly 4 to 21 million years. The authors argue that the inner three pairs are too symmetric about the core to be random jet knots, and present the source as the prototype of a quadruple-double radio galaxy. The paper has not yet been peer-reviewed in a journal.

**Links:**

- [Phys.org — Astronomers discover a radio galaxy with evidence of four separate jet outbursts](https://phys.org/news/2026-09-astronomers-radio-galaxy-evidence-jet.html)
- [arXiv — J023721.13−010528.5: a giant S-shaped radio galaxy with four jet episodes](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08506)

**Commentary:** If giant S-shaped sources really separate successive hotspots in space, they become archives of intermittent black-hole jets; the new class still needs journal review.

## II. Biomedicine

### 4. Two courses of blinatumomab replace intensive chemotherapy in children with high-risk B-ALL (Hematologic cancer)
**Summary:** Medscape on Sep 25 summarized a phase 3 trial in the New England Journal of Medicine (N Engl J Med. 2026;395:1075-1089). Martin Schrappe of Kiel University and colleagues enrolled 709 children under 18 with newly diagnosed high-risk B-cell acute lymphoblastic leukemia in eight countries. After consolidation and one intensive cycle, 358 received two 28-day cycles of blinatumomab (15 µg/m²/day, capped at 28 µg) and 351 continued two cycles of intensive chemotherapy. At a median 2.9 years of follow-up, estimated 4-year event-free survival was 83.0% versus 70.3% (hazard ratio 0.51). Four-year overall survival was 93.6% versus 91.0% and was not significantly different. Treatment-related infections were 23.9% versus 69.4%, life-threatening adverse events 0.5% versus 4.7%, and neurotoxic events 12.0% versus 3.2%. An accompanying editorial notes that the hazard ratio matches trials in other populations. Amgen supplied the drug free of charge and had no role in design or analysis.

**Links:**

- [Medscape — Blinatumomab boosts event-free survival in children with high-risk B-ALL](https://www.medscape.com/viewarticle/blinatumomab-improves-event-free-survival-children-high-risk-2026a1000zwc)
- [NEJM — Blinatumomab in children with high-risk B-cell ALL (DOI: 10.1056/NEJMoa2604166)](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2604166)

**Commentary:** Event-free survival and toxicity already support swapping some intensive chemotherapy for immunotherapy; whether overall survival separates still depends on longer follow-up.

## III. Life Science & Imaging

### 5. Chemosynthesis-capable proteobacteria are widespread on Caribbean reef-fish gills (Marine microbiology)
**Summary:** Phys.org on Sep 27 relayed work from the Leibniz Centre for Tropical Marine Research and the University of Oldenburg's Institute for Chemistry and Biology of the Marine Environment, published in PLOS Genetics on Aug 28, 2026. Reanalysis of 353 gill samples from 15 Hypoplectrus (hamlet) species at eight Greater Caribbean sites over 13 years recovered 70 gill-associated metagenome-assembled genomes in 17 lineages, most of them new and clearly distinct from reef-water communities. One Burkholderiaceae lineage was the most prevalent across hosts, sites, and years; its genome encodes complete carbon-fixation and sulfur-oxidation modules, which the authors describe as the first evidence they know of for sulfur-oxidizing chemosynthetic bacteria in fish gills. More than 95% of extracted gill DNA was host DNA and under 5% was microbial. Whether that potential matters for host physiology or immunity has not been shown experimentally.

**Links:**

- [PLOS Genetics — Proteobacteria with chemosynthetic potential in Hypoplectrus gills](https://journals.plos.org/plosgenetics/article?id=10.1371/journal.pgen.1012266)
- [Leibniz Centre for Tropical Marine Research — DNA analysis of the reef-fish gill microbiome](https://www.leibniz-zmt.de/en/news-at-zmt/in-focus/on-topic/dna-analysis-sheds-new-light-on-the-gill-microbiome-of-coral-reef-fish.html)

**Commentary:** A complete gene set shows the gill microbiome is specialized beyond seawater wash-in; whether the fish actually uses chemosynthesis is the next experiment.

### 6. MAX IV images a whole mantis-shrimp eye down to micrometres and finds an undescribed tube network (Structural biology)
**Summary:** Science News DK on Sep 27 reported a Journal of Structural Biology study by Anne Marie Møller Faaborg, Henrik Birkedal, and colleagues at Aarhus University. At the DANMAX beamline of MAX IV, the fourth-generation synchrotron in Lund, they scanned one peacock mantis shrimp's right eye with unstained phase-contrast X-ray tomography. The eye is about 5 by 6 millimetres; roughly six hours and 40 positions produced about 3 terabytes, resolving structures near 1 micrometre while keeping the whole eye in view. Known ommatidia and filters reappeared where expected, and muscle striations were about 8–11 micrometres apart, consistent with earlier crustacean measurements. In the midband retina, which handles color and polarized light, they found an irregular vessel-like network not previously described; its function is unknown. The same approach is already used on human bone biopsies that should not be sectioned or stained.

**Links:**

## IV. Fundamental Physics

### 7. EPFL and Weizmann independently measure e/4 quasiparticles in the ν=1/2 state (Condensed matter)
**Summary:** Interesting Engineering on Sep 27 reported a Physical Review Letters paper (Sep 22) by Mitali Banerjee (EPFL), Moty Heiblum (Weizmann Institute), Mansour Shayegan (Princeton), and colleagues. Electrons confined to a gallium-arsenide quantum well about 70 nanometres wide form the even-denominator fractional quantum Hall state ν=1/2 in the lowest Landau level. Shot noise in the weak-backscattering regime, measured on two nominally identical etched quantum point contacts in two independent setups, gave quasiparticle charges of 0.250±0.013 e and 0.249±0.018 e, both consistent with e/4. The state survives up to a few kelvin and is described as only the second known gallium-arsenide candidate that might host non-Abelian properties. Charge e/4 is expected for both Abelian and non-Abelian candidates, so the measurement does not by itself identify the topological order.

**Links:**

- [EPFL — Rare quantum state reveals particles with quarter-electron charge](https://actu.epfl.ch/news/rare-quantum-state-reveals-particles-with-quarter/)
- [Physical Review Letters — Observation of e/4 charge at ν=1/2 in GaAs (DOI: 10.1103/c73x-q4z7)](https://doi.org/10.1103/c73x-q4z7)

**Commentary:** Matching e/4 in two labs makes ν=1/2 a reproducible benchmark; braiding statistics are what would decide a topological-quantum-computing platform.

## V. Climate & Environment

### 8. Global atmospheric dust fell about 10% from 2003 to 2023, trimming a small cooling effect (Climate forcing)
**Summary:** NBC News on Sep 27 focused on a Science Advances reconstruction by Ashok Gupta, Jasper Kok, and colleagues, much of it done at UCLA. Combining satellite dust aerosol optical depth, reanalyses, and dust-dominated AERONET sites in a large-ensemble inverse model, they find that North African and Asian optical depths fell by about 10%±8% and 10%±12% from 2003 to 2023, driving a global decline of about 10%±8%. Loading, emissions, and deposition fell together. The associated effective radiative forcing is about +0.02±0.05 W/m², an uncertainty range that includes zero; the authors say less dust may have contributed modestly to warming over those two decades. Dust rose through much of the twentieth century and peaked in the 1980s. The cause of the recent drop is still open; Gupta is separating weaker surface winds from greening and other factors.

**Links:**

- [NBC News — Earth's atmosphere has gotten less dusty in a mysterious reversal](https://www.nbcnews.com/science/environment/atmosphere-less-dust-climate-change-rcna598584)
- [Science Advances — A global decline in atmospheric dust during the 21st century (DOI: 10.1126/sciadv.aef5691)](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/sciadv.aef5691)

**Commentary:** Less dust removes a slice of cooling, and the forcing uncertainty is still larger than the central estimate, so greenhouse gases remain the main warming ledger.

### 9. Monsoon convection keeps feeding biogenic organic aerosol into the lower stratosphere (Atmospheric chemistry)
**Summary:** A Nature Geoscience paper published Sep 25 combines nine airborne campaigns with a sectional aerosol climate model to constrain background stratospheric aerosol that is neither volcanic nor from wildfire. Biogenic secondary organic aerosol makes up about 40% of the global annual-mean background stratospheric aerosol, carried up through underappreciated monsoon pathways over South America, central Africa, Indonesia-Australia, and southern Asia. The estimated troposphere-to-stratosphere flux of biogenic organic aerosol is 0.42-0.72 teragrams per year, about 5% to 90% larger than the volcanic injection rate averaged over the past two decades. The authors argue that global monsoon convection is a major term in the background stratospheric aerosol budget.

**Links:**

- [Nature Geoscience — Biogenic emissions reach the lower stratosphere via monsoon convection](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41561-026-02115-w)

**Commentary:** Background stratospheric aerosol is no longer only a volcano-and-wildfire account; monsoon lofting of surface biogenic organics now sits in the radiative and chemical budget.
