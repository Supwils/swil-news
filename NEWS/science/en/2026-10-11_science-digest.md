## II. Evolution and Synthetic Biology

### 3. Nature describes the pharynx and hyoid of Middle Jurassic Megacauda sungei from Inner Mongolia (Paleontology)
**Summary:** Nature on October 7 published the paper by Y. Li, Chang-Fu Zhou, Thomas Martin, Zhe-Xi Luo, and colleagues. The holotype, PMOL-AM00026, is held at the Paleontological Museum of Liaoning in Shenyang. The paper says this Middle Jurassic mammaliaform from China preserves a therian-like pharyngeal vault and hyoid, suggesting that soft-tissue anatomy for therian-like swallowing, drinking, and suckling was already present in the common ancestors of docodonts and mammals. It calls the animal the largest known Jurassic mammaliaform, semiaquatic, with a lifestyle analogous to the platypus. An Ohio University account carried by Phys.org on October 11 adds the name Megacauda sungei, an age of nearly 165 million years, and a locality in what is now Inner Mongolia. Micro-CT shows seal-like bladed cheek teeth, a platypus-like pelvis, and an otter-like tail. The same account says therian-like bony hooks occur together with more primitive bony ridges, so the modern structures did not evolve directly from those ridges.

**Links:**

- [Nature — A Jurassic mammaliaform swimmer and transformation of the mammalian pharynx](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-11107-0)
- [Phys.org — Jurassic fossil reveals ancient origins of how mammals drink milk](https://phys.org/news/2026-10-jurassic-fossil-reveals-ancient-mammals.html)

**Commentary:** Suckling is inferred from the pharyngeal vault and hyoid; the fossil does not preserve milk or an intact soft palate.

### 4. OSAR assembles 31 nonribosomal-peptide gene clusters without custom DNA synthesis (Synthetic biology)
**Summary:** Nature Communications on October 11 published an open-access paper with corresponding authors Hao Gao of Jinan University, Jay D. Keasling of the University of California, Berkeley, and Xiaozhou Luo of the Shenzhen Institute of Advanced Technology, Chinese Academy of Sciences. OSAR combines algorithmic primer design with yeast homologous-recombination cloning to assemble and refactor nonribosomal peptide synthetase clusters without custom DNA synthesis. Applied to 34 low- to medium-GC bacterial clusters of 7–78 kb, it assembled 31. Expression in Bacillus subtilis yielded 29 peptides from 10 clusters, some from possible module skipping or premature release. The automation ran at the Shenzhen Synthetic Biology Infrastructure, InfraSynBio. Keasling and Luo declare financial interests in related companies.

**Links:**

- [Nature Communications — An automated operon refactoring workflow for NRPS gene clusters](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-78648-w)

**Commentary:** 31 of 34 is the assembly rate; only 10 clusters produced the 29 peptides, so expression, not stitching, is the present bottleneck.

## III. Biomedicine

### 5. Both tegoprazan doses raise heartburn-free days in a U.S. phase 3 trial of non-erosive reflux (Gastroenterology)
**Summary:** Braintree, part of Sebela, announced on October 11 that it was presenting phase 3 TRIUMpH results for tegoprazan at the American College of Gastroenterology 2026 meeting. TRIUMpH-NERD (NCT05587322) enrolled 783 adults with symptomatic non-erosive reflux disease and assigned once-daily 50 mg, 100 mg, or placebo for four weeks, followed by a 20-week extension. Through week 4, least-squares mean 24-hour heartburn-free days were 17.3% on placebo, 26.2% on 50 mg (p=0.0006), and 30.1% on 100 mg (p<0.0001), relative increases of 51% and 74% over placebo. Days without nighttime heartburn were 27.0%, 36.1%, and 39.9%. The company said both doses separated from placebo from day 1 and, in prespecified analyses, increased days without regurgitation. It submitted a U.S. new-drug application in January 2026 and says it anticipates approval in January 2027. The same release gives a post hoc erosive-esophagitis analysis: among patients classed as conclusive GERD under Lyon Consensus 2.0, week-2 healing was 75.4% on 100 mg versus 60.7% on lansoprazole (p<0.0001).

**Links:**

- [PR Newswire — Sebela presents Phase 3 tegoprazan NERD data at ACG 2026](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/sebela-pharmaceuticals-to-present-positive-phase-3-tegoprazan-data-in-non-erosive-reflux-disease-nerd-at-the-american-college-of-gastroenterology-acg-2026-meeting-302903109.html)

### 6. Tovinontrine shows an inverse dose response in HFrEF, with no NT-proBNP benefit at the highest dose (Cardiology)
**Summary:** On October 11, Cardurion presented phase 2 Cycle results for the oral PDE9 inhibitor tovinontrine (CRD-750) in a late-breaking plenary at the Heart Failure Society of America meeting in Phoenix. Cycle-1 REF randomized 529 patients with heart failure with reduced ejection fraction, already on guideline-directed therapy, to one of three doses or placebo. The release says the 12-week reduction in NT-proBNP was greatest at 2.5 mg twice daily, smaller at 25 mg twice daily, visible by week 2 and sustained through week 12, and that 50 mg twice daily showed no NT-proBNP benefit. Cycle-2 PEF randomized 277 patients with heart failure with preserved ejection fraction to 50 mg twice daily or placebo, and that dose likewise produced no significant NT-proBNP change. The company reads the inverse dose response as neurohormonal feedback from very high myocardial cGMP, and says safety was favorable at the doses tested. The release does not give the percent drop in NT-proBNP.

**Links:**

- [GlobeNewswire — Cardurion presents Phase 2 results for tovinontrine in heart failure](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/10/11/3378505/0/en/cardurion-pharmaceuticals-presents-phase-2-results-for-tovinontrine-crd-750-a-pde9-inhibitor-as-a-novel-treatment-for-heart-failure.html)

**Commentary:** What can be checked is the dose direction and the sample sizes; the release never states how large the NT-proBNP drop was, and the lack of benefit at 50 mg remains in the result.

## IV. Climate and Environment

### 7. Atlantic surface near-inertial currents strengthened 3.8% per decade from 2003 to 2022 (Ocean)
**Summary:** Nature Communications on October 11 published an open-access paper by Yan Du and Yuhang Zheng of the South China Sea Institute of Oceanology, Chinese Academy of Sciences, with Xiaoming Zhai and David P. Stevens of the University of East Anglia and coauthors. Using measured currents from surface drifters, they report observational evidence that Atlantic surface near-inertial currents intensified over 2003–2022. Basin-mean strength rises 3.8 ± 1.7% per decade, with strengthening in the South Atlantic and the high-latitude North Atlantic and weakening in the low-latitude North Atlantic. The paper attributes the trends mainly to changes in near-inertial wind forcing, especially mesoscale weather, with mixed-layer depth playing a minor role, and says the intensification needs to be represented in current climate models. Funding is listed from the National Natural Science Foundation of China, the Guangdong Basic and Applied Basic Research Foundation, and the Chinese Academy of Sciences.

**Links:**

- [Nature Communications — Intensification of near-inertial currents in the Atlantic Ocean](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-78555-0)

**Commentary:** The 3.8 ± 1.7% per decade figure is a basin-mean surface-drifter trend; the same paper finds weakening in the low-latitude North Atlantic.

### 8. NYU Abu Dhabi offers a seasonal sea-ice benchmark from the historical record, with September hindcast error no larger than 0.6 million km² (Sea ice)
**Summary:** Scientific Reports on October 5 published an open-access paper by Faiq Raees and Francesco Paparella, and New York University's account appeared via Phys.org on October 11. The random analog predictor (RAP) makes ensemble forecasts from the historical Arctic sea-ice extent record alone and attaches its own uncertainty. The paper says that once band-depth, a centrality measure for functional data, picks the most representative forecast in the ensemble, bias against observations is negligible and RMSE is no larger than 0.6×10^6 km². Hindcasts of September mean extent show skill comparable to models used by the Sea Ice Prediction Network. The Phys.org account says forecasts can run up to nine months ahead and that the September error was comparable to that of 34 seasonal-forecast models. Paparella said that if a much more complex model cannot beat this simple baseline, the extra complexity is not adding matching predictive information.

**Links:**

- [Scientific Reports — Random analog prediction for seasonal Arctic sea ice extent](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-72959-0)
- [Phys.org — Algorithm could predict Arctic sea ice up to 9 months ahead](https://phys.org/news/2026-10-algorithm-arctic-sea-ice-months.html)

**Commentary:** "No larger than 0.6 million km²" applies to the single most representative forecast in the ensemble, not to every member.

### 9. Photovoltaic generation in four Swedish cities is projected to rise about 5% by 2100, mostly from less spring snow (Energy and climate)
**Summary:** Communications Earth & Environment on October 11 published an open-access paper by Tianqi Ruan, Fuxing Wang, Björn Laumert, Aitor Aldama-Campino, and Wujun Wang. Using an installation model that includes air temperature, radiation, and snow, they assess photovoltaics in Stockholm, Gothenburg, Malmo, and Kiruna under SSP1-2.6 and SSP3-7.0 and three EURO-CORDEX-CMIP6 downscaled simulations, with 1951–2014 as the historical reference. At the optimal tilt, generation by 2100 is about 5% higher than in the historical period under both scenarios, and the paper says this comes mainly from weaker snow effects in spring. Optimized tilts add another 3–6% relative to the 15-degree commercial angle common in the Swedish market. The same paper says solar now accounts for 1.9% of Swedish electricity and has grown more than 55% a year over the past five years, while under SSP3-7.0 annual global horizontal irradiance falls by about 30–40 W/m² in all four cities.

**Links:**

- [Communications Earth & Environment — Climate change and photovoltaic power potential in Sweden](https://www.nature.com/articles/s43247-026-04091-w)

**Commentary:** The approximate 5% is a projection to 2100 that includes snow, not a generation increase already measured this year.

### 10. Natural Resources Institute Finland uses liquid chromatography to quantify fungal melanin in soil (Carbon cycle)
**Summary:** Nature Communications on October 11 published an open-access paper by Sylwia Adamczyk and Bartosz Adamczyk of Natural Resources Institute Finland. They note that DNA methods estimate only the genetic potential to make melanin, and that spectroscopic and spectrophotometric assays were not specific enough because related compounds interfere. They instead use liquid chromatography to quantify major fungal melanin types directly in soil. With that method, soil melanin concentration is strongly correlated with total soil carbon, which they take as support for a central role of melanized fungal necromass in long-term soil-carbon stabilization. Funding is from the Research Council of Finland. The abstract does not give a correlation coefficient or a sample size.

**Links:**

- [Nature Communications — A method quantifying soil melanin and soil carbon stability](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-78616-4)

**Commentary:** "Strongly correlated" means melanin and total carbon move together; it is not yet a share of the soil carbon stock.

## V. Energy Materials

### 11. A Tsinghua nickel/nickel-molybdate sub-nanometer heterostructure needs 14.4 mV overpotential at 10 mA/cm² (Hydrogen)
**Summary:** Nature Communications on October 11 published an open-access paper by Yuan Huang, Zhenyu Ren, Hai Xiao, Qingda Liu, Xun Wang, and colleagues at Tsinghua University. Oleylamine ligands on NiMoO4 sub-nanowires act as a mild reductant, reducing part of the Ni²⁺ to spatially confined metallic nickel and forming coherent Ni/NiMoO4 interfaces. In 1 M KOH the catalyst needs an overpotential of 14.4 ± 2.9 mV to reach 10 mA/cm². An anion-exchange-membrane electrolyzer delivers 2 A/cm² at 1.62 V and 80°C, and the paper reports stable operation for more than 2200 hours at 25°C. The authors declare no competing interests. Computation used Tsinghua's high-performance computing center, and characterization was assisted by Shanghai Synchrotron Radiation Facility beamline BL16U1.

**Links:**

- [Nature Communications — Sub-nanometer alternating heterostructures by in situ reduction](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-78575-w)

**Commentary:** 14.4 mV is the overpotential at 10 mA/cm² in alkaline three-electrode tests; 2 A/cm² is from an electrolyzer at 80°C, and the 2200-hour stability is a separate run at 25°C.
