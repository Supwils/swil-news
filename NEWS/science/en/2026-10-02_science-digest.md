## I. Spaceflight and Deep Space

### 1. Crew-13 docks and sets the fastest U.S. launch-to-docking (Spaceflight)
**Summary:** NASA said on the evening of Oct 1 that the SpaceX Crew Dragon docked at 7:05 p.m. EDT to the forward port of the International Space Station's Harmony module. The trip took 7 hours and 55 minutes from the 11:10 a.m. EDT liftoff at Cape Canaveral, the fastest launch-to-docking by a U.S. spacecraft in the station's history. The crew were NASA's Jessica Watkins and Luke Delaney, CSA astronaut Joshua Kutryk, and Roscosmos cosmonaut Sergey Teteryatnikov. The hatch opened at 9:19 p.m. EDT and the four entered the station, joining Expedition 75. Already aboard were NASA astronauts Jessica Meir, Jack Hathaway, and Anil Menon, ESA astronaut Sophie Adenot, and Roscosmos cosmonauts Pyotr Dubrov, Anna Kikina, and Andrey Fedyaev.

### 2. MeerKAT preprint reports auroral radio from Beta Pictoris b; the paper is not yet reviewed (Exoplanets)
**Summary:** Kevin N. Ortiz Ceballos, Edo Berger, and Yvette Cendes submitted a preprint on Sept 15 (arXiv:2609.16720) reporting radio emission from the gas giant Beta Pictoris b, detected with South Africa's MeerKAT array. The signal includes rapid, recurring, highly circularly polarized bursts plus persistent emission from 0.85 to 3.5 GHz. The authors identify it as electron-cyclotron maser radiation and infer a magnetic field of at least about 1.25 kilogauss at the emission site, which they call the first direct field-strength measurement for an exoplanet. CNN reported on Oct 2 that the planet is about 63 light-years away and about 12 Jupiter masses, and that the signal traces a magnetic field and aurora. ScienceAlert attributes the team to the Center for Astrophysics | Harvard & Smithsonian and the University of Oregon. The manuscript has not been peer-reviewed.

**Links:**

- [arXiv — Discovery of radio emission from the exoplanet β Pictoris b](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16720)
- [CNN / KESQ — Radio signal detected for the first time from a planet outside our solar system](https://kesq.com/cnn-other/2026/10/02/radio-signal-detected-from-exoplanet-for-the-first-time/)

**Commentary:** A radio detection counts as a planetary magnetic-field measurement only if it is localized to the planet; 1.25 kilogauss is the field implied by the top of the observed band, and the claim is still a preprint.

## II. Biomedicine

### 3. FDA approves Jaypirca for previously untreated CLL/SLL without a 17p deletion (Hematology)
**Summary:** Eli Lilly said on Oct 2 that the U.S. Food and Drug Administration approved an added indication for Jaypirca (pirtobrutinib, 100 mg and 50 mg tablets), a non-covalent BTK inhibitor, for adults with previously untreated chronic lymphocytic leukemia or small lymphocytic lymphoma and no known 17p deletion, allowing first-line use. The basis is phase 3 BRUIN CLL-313, which randomized 282 patients 1:1 to pirtobrutinib 200 mg once daily or bendamustine plus rituximab. At a median follow-up of 28 months, independent-review progression-free survival had a hazard ratio of 0.20 (95% CI 0.11-0.37, p<0.0001); median PFS was not reached with pirtobrutinib and was 33.5 months with the chemoimmunotherapy. Independent-review overall response rates were 94% (95% CI 89-98) and 81% (95% CI 73-87). Among patients on Jaypirca, serious adverse reactions occurred in 28%, permanent discontinuation for adverse reactions in 4.3%, and dose reductions in 3.6%. The label still warns of infections, hemorrhage, cytopenias, cardiac arrhythmias, second primary malignancies, hepatotoxicity, and embryo-fetal toxicity.

**Links:**

- [PR Newswire / Eli Lilly — FDA expands Jaypirca to certain previously untreated CLL/SLL](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/lillys-jaypirca-pirtobrutinib-the-first-and-only-approved-non-covalent-btk-inhibitor-receives-expanded-indication-from-us-fda-for-certain-patients-with-previously-untreated-cllsll-302897416.html)

**Commentary:** The control arm was chemoimmunotherapy, so the hazard ratio of 0.20 is against bendamustine plus rituximab, and overall survival was not the endpoint behind this approval.

### 4. Single-allele nanoscale maps: a new CTCF motif blocks the SESN3 super-enhancer (Genetics)
**Summary:** Nature Genetics published an open-access paper on Oct 2 by Joseph C. Hamley, Weijiao Zhang, James O. J. Davies, and colleagues (DOI: 10.1038/s41588-026-02776-8). They built MCCv, a Micro Capture-C variant-to-function platform that reads single-allele chromatin structure, links variants in cis-regulatory elements to target genes, and reads out interaction changes after genome editing. In CD4+ T cells they examined 405 cis-regulatory elements tied to immune-mediated inflammatory disease. The paper reports a previously undescribed gain-of-function mechanism: a variant creates a neo-CTCF motif that blocks super-enhancer contacts with the SESN3 promoter and raises autoimmune risk. They also report that SESN3 regulates mTOR by sensing tryptophan, and they show a role in autoimmunity in mouse models. The text says immune-mediated inflammatory diseases affect about 5% to 7% of the population.

**Links:**

- [Nature Genetics — Single-allele nanoscale mapping of regulatory variants](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41588-026-02776-8)

**Commentary:** The platform moves a statistical association to an allele-specific contact change; the SESN3 mechanism is one locus in mice and primary cells, and it is not yet a list of drugs to start.

### 5. KEAP1 loss makes lung-cancer cells depend on pyruvate carboxylase under KRAS G12C blockade (Metabolism)
**Summary:** Cell Death & Disease published an open-access paper on Oct 2 by Susumu Kohno and Chiaki Takahashi at Kanazawa University, Shunsuke Kitajima at the Japanese Foundation for Cancer Research, and colleagues at Kyoto University, the University of Osaka, and Keio University. In non-small cell lung cancer treated with KRAS G12C inhibitors, co-occurring STK11 and KEAP1 mutations are linked to earlier progression, and the mechanism of that primary resistance had been unclear. Loss of KEAP1 constitutively activates NRF2, which promotes pyruvate carboxylase, channels glucose-derived carbon into the TCA cycle, and raises oxaloacetate for aspartate and de novo pyrimidine synthesis, so nucleotide pools and proliferation hold up during drug treatment. In models that carry both STK11 and KEAP1 mutations, genetic ablation of pyruvate carboxylase restored sensitivity to KRAS G12C inhibition and suppressed tumor growth. The paper says the study had no human participants; animal work was approved at Kanazawa University (AP25-012).

**Links:**

- [Cell Death & Disease — Pyruvate carboxylase drives therapeutic resistance to KRAS G12C inhibitors in KEAP1-mutant lung cancer](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41419-026-09299-4)

**Commentary:** Primary resistance is written here as a metabolic dependency that can be removed in models; combining a pyruvate-carboxylase block with a KRAS inhibitor still has no human dose or efficacy data.

## III. Climate and Environment

### 6. The contiguous U.S. just had its hottest January-August since at least 1895; a full-year record is still a probability (Climate)
**Summary:** Scientific American reported on Oct 2, citing climatologist Zeke Hausfather, that the contiguous United States averaged 57.98 degrees F (14.43 degrees C) for January through August 2026, 0.08 degrees F (about 0.04 degrees C) above the previous record of 57.90 degrees F (14.39 degrees C) for that span, set in 2012, and the hottest such period since at least 1895. The hottest full year on record for the contiguous U.S. remains 2024, at 55.48 degrees F (about 13 degrees C). Hausfather said a September-December average above about 50.5 degrees F (about 10.3 degrees C) would make 2026 the hottest year, and he put the odds at about 80 percent. A NOAA spokesperson shared an early look at September and an outlook for above-normal temperatures across much of the continental U.S. from October through December, while physical scientist Russell Vose said that does not determine whether the full year sets a record. Kim Cobb of Brown University called a record "more than likely" and said a developing super El Nino in the Pacific makes the 2027 global-heat outlook more severe.

**Links:**

- [Scientific American — 2026 has been a record hot year for the U.S.—it can only get worse](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/2026-has-been-a-record-hot-year-for-the-u-s-it-can-only-get-worse/)

**Commentary:** The eight-month record is already on the thermometer; the 80 percent figure is one author's estimate for the remaining four months, and NOAA has not turned it into an official annual forecast.

### 7. The Pacific, Atlantic, and Indian Oceans each took about a third of the extra heat, with double-digit basin gaps versus models (Ocean)
**Summary:** The University of Bonn on Oct 2 described a paper in Earth System Science Data (DOI: 10.5194/essd-18-6945-2026) by Bernd Uebbing, Jurgen Kusche, and colleagues, funded by Deutsche Forschungsgemeinschaft research group FOR5361. Earth is absorbing more heat than it radiates to space, and about 90 percent of that extra heat enters the ocean. The team jointly inverted Argo floats with satellite sea level and GRACE gravity, and for the first time calculated the thermal-expansion coefficient by region instead of assuming one global value. The global total largely matches climate models; individual basins can differ from the simulations by double-digit percentages. The Indian Ocean took up an unusually large amount of heat from 2005 to 2015. Over the past 20 years the Pacific, Atlantic, and Indian Oceans each absorbed about one-third of the additional heat, which points to heat transfer among basins. The university stressed that ocean heat uptake and redistribution can change sharply within a few years, and that sea level fluctuates less than average land temperature as a thermometer of the Earth system.

**Links:**

- [University of Bonn — How Much Are the World's Oceans Warming?](https://www.uni-bonn.de/en/news/200-2026)
- [Earth System Science Data — Improving global and regional ocean heat content](https://doi.org/10.5194/essd-18-6945-2026)

**Commentary:** A global total that matches the models can still hide basin ledgers that do not; the Indian Ocean's extra decade shows heat moving between oceans.

### 8. Broccoli under magenta semi-transparent CdTe panels used sunlight 4.5 times more efficiently and matured 25 days later (Agrivoltaics)
**Summary:** The Cell Press journal Cell Reports Physical Science published on Oct 2 a paper by Silvia Ma Lu of Malardalen University and colleagues. At Karrbo Prastgard farm in Sweden they grew broccoli under two 20-by-20-meter systems of magenta semi-transparent cadmium-telluride thin-film panels with different transparency, compared with full sun, during the 2024 season. Plants under the panels used sunlight 4.5 times more efficiently than those in the open and reached a similar final size, but they took 25 days longer to mature. Broccoli performed similarly under the two transparencies, while the denser photovoltaic cells can generate more electricity. The authors say the systems are research prototypes, the findings are specific to that season and climate, and they have started lab tests of magenta, red, and blue filters that exclude unfiltered stray light. Support came from the J. Gust. Richert Foundation, the Swedish Energy Agency, the Knowledge Foundation, and Carbon2Food.

**Links:**

- [EurekAlert! / Cell Press — Broccoli grows well under magenta solar panels](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/1145257)
- [Cell Reports Physical Science — Semi-transparent colored CdTe PV and broccoli](https://www.cell.com/cell-reports-physical-science/fulltext/S2666-3864%2826%2900461-3)

**Commentary:** Splitting sunlight between electricity and the red and blue light the crop uses can hold yield, at the cost of 25 extra days, and only one growing season has been reported.

## IV. Basic Research

### 9. The first uranium-carbon Fischer-type triple bond stable enough to isolate (Actinide chemistry)
**Summary:** Nature Chemistry published an open-access paper on Oct 2 in which Stephen T. Liddle, John A. Seed, and colleagues at the University of Manchester, with collaborators in Germany, synthesized and characterized the uranium Fischer-type carbyne [N″3U(μ-CNN)UN″3] (N″ = N(SiMe3)2). Single-crystal X-ray diffraction gives a uranium-carbyne distance of 2.379(15) angstroms. Density-functional theory and quantum-crystallography Hirshfeld atom refinement show two-electron carbon-to-uranium sigma donation, weaker two-electron pi donation, and two orthogonal one-electron uranium-to-carbon pi back-bonds, for an overall sigma-2 pi-1 pi-1 Fischer-type carbyne interaction. The University of Manchester notes that earlier uranium-carbon triple bonds had been seen only in cryogenic matrices or inside fullerene cages, not as isolable molecules. The bonding is related to transition-metal Fischer carbynes and is distinct from them.

**Links:**

- [Nature Chemistry — A crystalline uranium Fischer-type carbyne](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41557-026-02260-0)
- [University of Manchester — First isolable uranium-carbon triple bond](https://www.manchester.ac.uk/about/news/first-isolable-uranium-carbon-triple-bond/)

**Commentary:** An electron-density map on a bottleable compound moves uranium-carbon multiple bonding out of matrix isolation; the bond order is still a polar sigma-2 pi-1 pi-1 interaction, a different template from a shorter covalent transition-metal triple bond.

### 10. A cavity raises semiconductor biexciton-photon indistinguishability from about 60% to about 90% (Quantum optics)
**Summary:** The Quantum Insider reported on Oct 2 that groups at Paderborn University, the University of Basel, and Ruhr University Bochum published a Physical Review Letters paper (DOI: 10.1103/t8sk-b2w4). The report says semiconductor nanostructures, controlled by a microcavity, produce single photons and photon pairs from biexciton decay that are nearly identical: about 90% indistinguishable, versus about 60% without the cavity effect, in agreement with theory. Warburton at Basel said the result depends on controlling the system with a cavity. Klaus Jons at Paderborn said photon purity can also be improved with the resonator and is limited by lattice vibrations (phonons) through cavity feeding, which later designs will need to reduce further.

**Links:**

- [The Quantum Insider — New Method Generates Photons That Are Virtually Indistinguishable](https://thequantuminsider.com/2026/10/02/new-method-generates-photons-that-are-virtually-indistinguishable/)
- [Physical Review Letters — DOI 10.1103/t8sk-b2w4](https://doi.org/10.1103/t8sk-b2w4)

**Commentary:** The jump from about 60% to about 90% shows the cavity pulling biexciton photons toward the sameness interference needs; phonons still set the purity ceiling.
