### 2. Astronomy｜ALMA detects TiO for the first time in a star-forming region around Orion Source I
**Summary:** On Nov 26 the ALMA Observatory announced that a team led by Ziwei E. Zhang and Nami Sakai (RIKEN) detected three rotational emission lines of titanium monoxide (TiO) around Orion Source I, a young massive protostar about 1,350 light-years away—the first TiO detection in a star-forming region. Emission is concentrated near the base of a rotating disk wind over roughly 50 AU and resembles previously mapped AlO. Because titanium is usually locked in cold dust grains, gaseous TiO is interpreted as high-temperature release of refractory minerals, with relevance to calcium-aluminum-rich inclusion (CAI) formation; the estimated TiO column density is about 3×10¹⁵ molecules cm⁻², with TiO/SiO ≈ 1.3/100 (approximate upper limit). The work appears in *The Astrophysical Journal Letters*.

**Links:**

- [ALMA — ALMA traces vaporized minerals around a young massive star](https://www.almaobservatory.org/en/audiences/alma-traces-vaporized-minerals-around-a-young-massive-star/)

**Commentary:** High-temperature solid processing moves from meteorite inference onto a spatially resolved disk-wind chemical map.

## II. Biomedicine & Health

### 3. Reproductive medicine｜Max Planck “TALhesin” engineered glue nearly restores young-egg cohesion in aged mouse oocytes
**Summary:** Melina Schuh’s team at the Max Planck Institute for Multidisciplinary Sciences reported in *Science* on Nov 26 an engineered TAL-effector cohesion system (TALhesin) that can substitute for age-lost cohesin near centromeres. In aged mouse eggs, the share without premature chromatid separation rose from 46.7% to 74.1%, near the 74.7% level in young mice. A built-in separase cleavage site allows timely release in anaphase. Adapted to a human chromosomal target, TALhesin bound donated unfertilized human eggs and separated normally when the cleavage site was present. The authors stress this is proof of principle; safety testing and whether it reduces aneuploidy in eggs from older women remain ahead of any fertility use.

**Links:**

- [Max Planck — An engineered “glue” for aged eggs](https://www.mpg.de/27061728/an-engineered-glue-for-aged-eggs)
- [Science — An engineered cohesion system reduces errors in aged mammalian eggs](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.aei5852)

**Commentary:** Age-worn molecular glue is, for the first time, an installable and removable engineering part—clinical distance remains large, but the experimental handle is real.

### 4. Genome engineering｜St. Jude redesign of CAST TnsA boosts RNA-guided DNA insertion ~100-fold without losing on-target fidelity
**Summary:** Elizabeth Kellogg’s group at St. Jude Children’s Research Hospital reports in *Nature* the licensing mechanisms of Vibrio cholerae type I-F3 CRISPR-associated transposase (VchCAST). Zero-shot computational redesign of hard-to-purify TnsA (AlphaFold + LigandMPNN), after screening ~500 designs, yielded a top variant (~27 mutations) that raised in vivo integration activity by nearly two orders of magnitude while keeping ~98% of insertions on target at the expected ~82 bp from the PAM. Cryo-EM of the holo integration complex shows conformational licensing among Cascade–TniQ, TnsC, and TnsA–TnsB. A companion *Nature* structure paper from Martin Jinek and colleagues maps stepwise type I CAST assembly. Institutional and media coverage clustered Nov 25–26.

**Links:**

- [St. Jude — Structural redesign of CAST system](https://www.stjude.org/media-resources/news-releases/2026-medicine-science-news/structural-redesign-of-cast-system-reveals-mechanisms-for-targeted-dna-integration.html)
- [Nature — Licensing mechanisms during RNA-guided DNA integration by type I-F3 CAST](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-11287-9)

**Commentary:** CAST’s promise is large-cargo integration without double-strand breaks; a 100-fold activity jump is the difference between curiosity and an engineerable tool.

### 6. Vaccines｜Therapeutic HPV DNA vaccine VGX-3100 Phase 3 reported at SITC: 52.5% meet composite endpoint
**Summary:** A Phase 3 study of therapeutic HPV vaccine VGX-3100, led by the Cancer Hospital of the Chinese Academy of Medical Sciences with 22 centers, was presented as a late-breaking oral at SITC 41; Chinese science outlets highlighted the results on Nov 26. Unlike prophylactic HPV vaccines, this DNA vaccine targets patients already infected with HPV16/18 who have high-grade cervical lesions. The composite primary endpoint required both lesion regression and HPV16/18 clearance; among 207 participants, 52.5% in the treatment arm met the endpoint, with overall acceptable safety. Investigators call it the first therapeutic HPV vaccine to meet a Phase 3 primary efficacy endpoint, while noting this is the week-36 primary analysis, long-term follow-up continues, and regulatory approval is not complete.

**Links:**

- [NCSTI — Breakthrough Phase 3 results for therapeutic HPV vaccine](https://www.ncsti.gov.cn/kjdt/kjrd/yyjk_kjrd/202611/t20261126_265746.html)

**Commentary:** Prophylaxis blocks infection; a therapeutic shot aims at a non-surgical option when fertility preservation matters—and now has a Phase 3 composite-endpoint readout.

### 7. Drug delivery｜HKU: endolysosomal damage spawns luminal protein condensates that still trap antisense oligonucleotides
**Summary:** Haibo Jiang and colleagues at the University of Hong Kong highlighted on Nov 26 work in *Nature Cell Biology* showing that when endolysosomal membranes are damaged, luminal contents do not simply leak into the cytosol. Selected resident proteins rapidly form solid-like condensates; super-resolution imaging, electron microscopy, and cryo-ET show therapeutic antisense oligonucleotides (ASOs) can remain immobilized inside even after membrane permeabilization—a second delivery barrier beyond the membrane itself. The response appears across injury modes and cell types and can reverse when lysosomal homeostasis recovers. The authors suggest reducing condensate trapping or helping escape could improve productive delivery and lower required doses.

**Links:**

- [HKU Lead — Hidden barrier preventing nucleic acid medicines from reaching targets](https://lead.hku.hk/en/article/2026/11/hku-chemists-discover-a-hidden-barrier-that-prevents-nucleic-acid-medicines-from-reaching-their-targets-inside-cells/)
- [Nature Cell Biology — Endolysosomal damage promotes intraluminal protein condensate formation](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41556-026-02119-z)

**Commentary:** Breaching the endosomal membrane is not enough—the lumen can grow a solid trap; nucleic-acid delivery now has a phase-separation problem.

## III. Climate, Environment & Energy

### 8. Climate｜HKU *Nature* study: 75.4% of mapped Amazon forests show seasonal leaf loss; leaf-age models shift uptake estimates by 9.4%
**Summary:** Jin Wu, Guangqin Song and colleagues combined machine learning with 10-m Sentinel-2 imagery (2019–2021) to map monthly leafless-canopy fraction across the Amazon; HKU amplified the *Nature* findings on Nov 26. In mapped forests, 75.4% show substantial seasonal leaflessness, and in ~70% of the basin leaf shedding precedes greening by at least three months; hydroclimate is the strongest predictor of that lag. Incorporating leaf turnover and age into ecophysiological models changes estimated annual photosynthetic carbon uptake by 9.4% versus conventional models (~1.42 billion tonnes of carbon per year) and improves seasonal photosynthesis agreement across 79.9% of Amazon forests (validated with independent satellites and ground fluxes). The 9.4% figure is a model-estimate gap, not a measured change in carbon storage.

**Links:**

- [HKU Lead — Hidden Leaf Cycles in the Amazon Could Reshape Carbon Estimates](https://lead.hku.hk/en/news/2026/11/hidden-leaf-cycles-in-the-amazon-could-reshape-carbon-estimates/)
- [Nature — Widespread deciduousness reveals diverse Amazonian phenological strategies](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-11284-y)

**Commentary:** “Evergreen all year” is often a satellite illusion; unseen leaf turnover can move the world’s largest tropical carbon sink’s books by nearly a tenth.

### 9. Atmospheric chemistry｜Yunnan University–led PNAS: decaying vegetation is a new terrestrial HONO source (~1.1±0.1 Tg N yr⁻¹)
**Summary:** Jingwei Zhang (Yunnan University), Dawei Lu (Research Center for Eco-Environmental Sciences, CAS) and collaborators report in PNAS that decaying aboveground plant residues emit nitrous acid (HONO) via microbial nitrate reduction (denitrification and DNRA). Yunnan University highlighted the work on Nov 26. Field observations, dual-isotope tracing, and machine learning yield a global vegetation-decay HONO flux of about 1.1±0.1 Tg N yr⁻¹—roughly 9.6% of global soil HONO emissions—with 62.1% concentrated between 30°N and 30°S; grasslands, forests, and croplands contribute ~44.1%, 38.5%, and 17.3%. Under CMIP6 SSPs, emissions could rise to ~1.15–1.59 Tg N by 2100. The pathway extends the soil–atmosphere framing to a soil–plant–atmosphere system.

**Links:**

- [Yunnan University — Vegetation decay as a new terrestrial HONO source](https://www.ynu.edu.cn/info/1429/39852.htm)
- [PNAS — Vegetation decay enhances terrestrial nitrous acid emissions through nitrate reduction](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2607492123)

**Commentary:** Models have long undercounted HONO; litter piles were missing from the ledger—air-quality and climate codes now need a plant-decay line item.

### 10. Photovoltaics｜All-perovskite tandem with in situ redox-converted molecule reaches 30.8% certified efficiency
**Summary:** A *Nature* paper reports incorporating p-aminobenzohydrazide (ABH) into PEDOT:PSS to neutralize PSS acidity, heal Sn⁴⁺ defects, and convert in situ to p-aminobenzaldehyde (ABD), which suppresses photogenerated-hole-driven Sn²⁺ oxidation and aligns energy levels. The narrow-bandgap Sn–Pb single junction reaches 25.4% PCE (certified 25.2%) with 0.92 V Voc and 83.4% fill factor; monolithic all-perovskite tandems deliver 31.1% (certified 30.8%) with improved thermal (ISOS-D-2I, 85°C) and operational (ISOS-L-1I) stability under inert conditions. Science outlets amplified the result on Nov 26; open-air stability was not reported.

**Links:**

## IV. Fundamental Research

### 11. Quantum computing｜Magic state cultivation on a superconducting processor cuts error ~40×, fidelity at least 0.9999
**Summary:** In the Nov 26 *Science* issue, Rosenfeld et al. demonstrate magic state cultivation on a superconducting quantum processor: a protocol predicted to be far cheaper than traditional distillation reduces |T⟩ error by about a factor of 40, achieving state fidelity of at least 0.9999(1) while retaining ~8% of attempts, and grafts the cultivated state into a surface-code-compatible encoding. A companion Perspective notes magic states are among the costliest ingredients of fault-tolerant quantum computing; larger-scale demonstrations remain needed. The paper appears in *Science* Vol. 394, Issue 6827.

**Links:**

- [Science — Magic state cultivation on a superconducting quantum processor](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.aee7961)
- [Science — Growing magic for quantum computers (Perspective)](https://www.science.org/doi/abs/10.1126/science.aen1265)

**Commentary:** Non-Clifford gates are the throttle of fault-tolerant quantum computing; scalable cultivation would turn the most expensive “fuel” into a manufacturable intermediate.

### 12. Geophysics｜Near-source fiber sensing images an M2 rupture that stops and renucleates 42 ms later
**Summary:** Han et al. report in the Nov 26 *Science* that near-source distributed acoustic sensing on California’s Imperial Valley Dark Fiber resolved millisecond-scale rupture of an M2 earthquake in the Brawley Seismic Zone. Inverting fiber-derived apparent source time functions reveals two rupture episodes separated by 42 milliseconds and ~150 meters—arrest followed by dynamic renucleation. Across a three-hour swarm, adjacent, largely nonoverlapping fault patches failed in succession, forming complementary slip partitioning. The work shows microearthquakes can host fragmented rupture dynamics that bridge laboratory experiments and large earthquakes.

**Links:**

- [Science — Millisecond-scale imaging of fragmented earthquake rupture by near-source fiber-optic sensing](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.aei0507)

**Commentary:** Small quakes are not simple circular patches; fiber optics turn stop–start rupture into a quantitative mechanics lesson.

### 13. Chemistry｜Counter-ion confinement reverses ion selectivity in subnanometer channels
**Summary:** Fangmeng Sheng, Tongwen Xu and colleagues report in the Nov 26 *Science* that cationic covalent organic framework membranes shift from anion-selective to cation-selective transport as channels shrink from under ~2 nm to under 1 nm. The reversal arises from counter-ion confinement that reconfigures the electrostatic environment inside the channel—effectively inverting polarity—and helps suppress competing ions in mixed-salt systems so selectivity holds under more practical separation conditions. The finding offers a design strategy for adaptive, highly selective ion-separation membranes.

**Links:**

- [Science — Counter-ion confinement reverses ion selectivity in subnanometer channels](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.aef1323)

**Commentary:** Textbooks say positive pores favor anions; at subnanometer scale, confined counter-ions can flip the script—and give membrane design a new knob.
