## I. Space & Astronomy

### 1. NASA Starling: FALCON shows GPS-free orbit determination via optical “landmarks” (space)
**Summary:** NASA and Stanford spinout EraDrive’s FALCON (Fast Autonomous Lost-in-space Catalog-based Optical Navigation) experiment on the Starling four-satellite swarm lets a spacecraft observe other satellites and debris with star-tracker cameras, match them to an onboard catalog of about 20,000 known objects, and determine its own orbit without GPS. Over three days, FALCON refined orbits for more than 200 objects with no ground intervention, and onboard catalog updates outperformed some ground-supplied predictions. SciTechDaily amplified NASA materials on Sep 11, 2026; later tests will share tracking data across the swarm.

**Links:**

**Commentary:** Beyond Earth orbit, “see your neighbors to know where you are” is more practical than waiting for a new GNSS constellation.

### 2. FAST FASHI DR2: 156,411 HI sources map the largest cosmic gas census (astronomy)
**Summary:** Led by Peng Jiang’s team at the National Astronomical Observatories of the Chinese Academy of Sciences, FAST’s HI survey (FASHI) released Data Release 2 as a cover paper in *Science China Physics, Mechanics & Astronomy*, with Chinese media coverage on Sep 11. DR2 covers about 19,500 square degrees (nearly half the sky), detects 156,411 extragalactic HI sources—about 5× ALFALFA and nearly 4× DR1 (41,741)—with typical sensitivity near 0.57 mJy/beam. The team reports ~0.6% precision on the local cosmic HI density and extends the HI mass function reliably to about one million solar masses; the catalog is public.

**Links:**

- [Chinese Academy of Sciences — FAST maps the largest cosmic gas atlas](https://www.cas.cn/cm/202609/t20260911_5120169.shtml)
- [NAOC — FASHI DR2 release note](https://www.nao.cas.cn/news/ky/202609/t20260910_8280408.html)

**Commentary:** The survey has moved from record-breaking to becoming a reference sample that galaxy evolution and gravitational-wave host studies will lean on.

### 3. Nature: AI algorithm kine reconstructs continuous blazar-jet video, challenging shock models (astronomy/AI)
**Summary:** Foschi et al. in *Nature* introduce kine, a neural-field video reconstructor for variable VLBI sources that jointly fits multi-epoch data and spatiotemporal correlations. Applied to 116 VLBA epochs of blazar 3C 345 (MOJAVE, 1995–2022), it yields continuous polarimetric video at roughly 4× the resolution of traditional methods and measures local instantaneous plasma velocities—not just discrete knots. Bright features move at speeds similar to surrounding flow, favoring magnetic/Doppler brightening over classic traveling shocks. Science outlets covered the work on Sep 11.

**Links:**

- [Nature — Video reconstruction of variable VLBI observations with neural fields](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10988-5)
- [phys.org — Continuous video challenges shock wave theory](https://phys.org/news/2026-09-video-black-hole-radio-theory.html)

**Commentary:** Turning sparse stills into a velocity field could rewrite jet-kinematics chapters—but the shock challenge is source-specific for now.

## II. Biomedicine & Health

### 4. UChicago DANDELION: network tool prioritizes 21 core asthma driver genes (bioinformatics)
**Summary:** On Sep 11, University of Chicago and Columbia researchers described DANDELION, a computational method that finds core disease genes downstream in gene-regulatory networks rather than next to GWAS hits. Using transcriptome data and UK Biobank, it flagged 21 asthma-linked genes—19 previously missed—then validated many via CRISPR cell screens and mouse models. SLC27A3 and SCD sit in a fatty-acid metabolism/palmitoylation pathway: SLC27A3 loss protected, SCD loss worsened lung inflammation. The study appears in *Cell*.

**Links:**

- [University of Chicago News — DANDELION uncovers hidden genetic pathways](https://news.uchicago.edu/story/flowery-name-new-tool-uncovers-hidden-genetic-pathways-cause-disease)

**Commentary:** GWAS delivers signal overload; DANDELION’s bet is that the druggable hubs sit far downstream in the network.

### 5. FDA’s Zanvastro (zilganersen) approval for Alexander disease draws Sep 11 coverage (rare neurology)
**Summary:** Healio on Sep 11 detailed FDA approval of Ionis’s antisense oligonucleotide Zanvastro (zilganersen) for pediatric and adult Alexander disease—about 300 U.S. patients previously limited to supportive care. In a trial of ages 2–53, the 50 mg arm (≥5 years) showed ~33.3% LSM difference vs control in 10-Meter Walk Test gait-speed change at week 61, plus ~33.6% LSM difference in plasma GFAP (P=0.003); ages 2–4 improved on GMFM-88. Dosing is 50 mg intrathecal quarterly; U.S. list price is about $285,000 per dose.

### 6. Scripps: transthyretin can denature by a second pathway that may blunt kinetic stabilizers (proteinopathy)
**Summary:** Scripps Research and University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign scientists report in *PNAS* that transthyretin (TTR) can leave its tetramer via an alternative, more expanded transition state—directly to monomers—under acidic conditions (e.g., lysosomes) or certain mutations, besides the canonical dimer pathway. After testing more than 100 variants with cryo-EM and crystal structures, they find the route more accessible in some brain-amyloidosis–linked mutants, and kinetic stabilizers such as tafamidis suppress it less effectively than the canonical path. Coverage peaked on Sep 11.

**Links:**

- [Scripps Research — New way a disease-linked protein comes apart](https://www.scripps.edu/news-events/news/scientists-discover-a-new-way-that-a-disease-linked-protein-comes-apart/)
- [PNAS — Transthyretin can denature by an alternative pathway](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2536532123)

**Commentary:** Two unfolding routes for one target help explain why a minority of patients respond poorly to current stabilizers.

### 7. Novel tick-borne virus ALTNV identified in China; ~10% of febrile tick-exposed patients positive (infectious disease)
**Summary:** Researchers including the State Key Laboratory of Pathogen and Biosecurity in Beijing describe Asian longhorned tick nairovirus (ALTNV), a new orthonairovirus, in *The New England Journal of Medicine*. Among 3,163 febrile patients with tick exposure, about 10.4% were RNA- or IgM-positive; mono-infection typically caused fatigue, GI symptoms, thrombocytopenia, and elevated aminotransferases with full recovery, while coinfection with Dabie bandavirus was more severe. ALTNV RNA appeared in ~1.3% of 47,428 ticks across 15 provinces, highest in *Haemaphysalis longicornis*; lab work showed transmission to mice. Outlets including NTD highlighted the story on Sep 11.

## III. Climate & Environment

### 9. Reported global drought “acceleration” largely a PET artifact; ~99% of land shows no significant speed-up (hydroclimate)
**Summary:** A Sep 11 *Communications Earth & Environment* paper recomputes SPEI for 1981–2024 with multiple potential evapotranspiration (PET) schemes and finds Penman-type PET inflates drying trends by at least ~6× versus energy-constrained PET. Under either class, ~99% of quasi-global land shows no significant acceleration of drought trends, a result that holds on centennial windows (1901– or 1950–2024). Authors argue recent “acceleration” claims largely reflect unconstrained PET methods rather than a clear ecohydrological response.

**Links:**

- [Communications Earth & Environment — Global drought shows no detectable recent acceleration](https://www.nature.com/articles/s43247-026-03954-6)

**Commentary:** Drought can still be severe; how steep the trend looks depends heavily on how evaporation is calculated.

### 10. *Science*: wood-formation rate outweighs longer growing seasons for annual production (ecology)
**Summary:** An international team including WSL analyzed more than 50,000 conifer wood samples across four continents. Sep 11 coverage highlights that annual xylem-cell production depends more on formation rate—from roughly one cell per month to two per day—than on season length. Warming typically extends the formation window, but above about 6°C mean annual temperature, cell-production rates often slow with water limitation, so temperate/Mediterranean forests may not gain carbon from longer summers. Published in *Science* (DOI: 10.1126/science.aec8866).

**Links:**

- [WSL — More time for trees to grow doesn’t always mean more wood](https://www.wsl.ch/en/news/more-time-for-trees-to-grow-doesnt-always-mean-more-wood/)
- [Science — Growth rate overrides extended growing season (DOI: 10.1126/science.aec8866)](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aec8866)

**Commentary:** “Longer growing season = bigger forest carbon sink” is oversold once formation rates stall.

### 11. IOCAS: solar 11-year cycle modulates North Pacific eastern subtropical mode water (ocean climate)
**Summary:** Chinese media on Sep 11 reported that Jianing Wang and colleagues at the Institute of Oceanology, Chinese Academy of Sciences, used ~80 years of ocean reanalysis and experiments to show a robust solar-cycle modulation of North Pacific eastern subtropical mode water (NPESTMW) volume—suppressed 1–2 years after solar maxima, enhanced near minima. High solar activity strengthens the Aleutian Low via stratosphere–troposphere coupling, weakening wind stress and heat loss while boosting Ekman upwelling that cuts subduction; Atlantic Multidecadal Oscillation modulates the signal strength. Published in *Geophysical Research Letters* (DOI: 10.1029/2026GL122914).

### 12. KAIST × Samsung: entropy-stabilized aluminate catalyst durably destroys CF₄ (industrial mitigation)
**Summary:** Scientific Inquirer on Sep 11 covered KAIST’s Minkee Choi team with Samsung Electronics: embedding Al, Zn, Ga, Ni, and Co into an aluminate lattice yields an entropy-stabilized catalyst that resists sintering under hot, wet, fluorine-rich conditions while hydrolyzing CF₄—a semiconductor process gas ~6,000× more potent than CO₂ with ~50,000-year lifetime. Intrinsic activity is about 2.3× conventional alumina; in a ~800°C, 150-hour stress test, conversion fell only from 98% to 92% versus 93% to 48% for the control. Published in *Angewandte Chemie International Edition*.

## IV. Fundamental Research

### 13. CLSS protein language model: one map for sequence and structure (computational biology)
**Summary:** Teams from the University of Haifa, Tel Aviv University, and ELSI at Institute of Science Tokyo on Sep 11 described CLSS, a contrastive model that pulls sequence and 3D-structure embeddings of the same protein together into a shared “protein world map.” Without training on labels, representations recover much of expert ECOD/CATH taxonomy and can place short fragments meaningfully; overlaying biology shows cofactor-associated proteins clustering while metal-binding proteins spread more widely. Published in *PNAS* (DOI: 10.1073/pnas.2532702123).
