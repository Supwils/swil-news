## I. Space & Deep Space

### 1. NASA: Roman telescope propellant may support ~22 years of science, doubling lifetime potential (Space)
**Summary:** Reuters reported on Sep 17 that, before the Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope reaches its planned orbit, NASA says its potential operational lifetime may reach about 22 years—more than double the prior ~10-year fuel budget (five-year primary plus five-year extended mission). Savings come from filling tanks to capacity because launch mass was below the design maximum, a highly accurate Aug 31 mid-course correction that used under 10% of the budgeted propellant (~18 kg vs ~200 kg reserved), and expected savings from the second correction and L2 insertion. Roman launched Aug 30 on a Falcon Heavy and is expected to arrive at Sun–Earth L2 in early December; NASA stresses this is a propellant-capacity estimate, not a guaranteed 22-year mission.

**Commentary:** After “first light” checkouts, the harder question is how long Roman can watch—stretching the fuel clock from a decade to two decades doubles the baseline for dark-energy surveys and exoplanet statistics.

### 2. ESA: Galileo Signal Authentication Service achieves first civil authenticated fix under spoofing (Navigation)
**Summary:** ESA reported on Sep 17 that for about two hours on the afternoon of Sep 16, five operational Galileo satellites broadcast encrypted test signals over Europe. Receivers at Andøya, Norway, and ESA’s ESTEC navigation lab obtained the first real-world position fixes using the forthcoming Signal Authentication Service (SAS) under spoofing conditions. SAS was defined by the European Commission and architected by ESA and EUSPA with satellite and ground upgrades; it will complement OSNMA (operational since 2025) to detect and reject spoofing. EUSPA plans validation and accreditation in 2027 before declaring the service operational; both SAS and OSNMA are intended to be free and worldwide.

**Links:**

- [ESA — Galileo's first civil authenticated position fix in spoofing conditions](https://www.esa.int/Applications/Satellite_navigation/Galileo/Galileo_s_first_civil_authenticated_position_fix_in_spoofing_conditions)

**Commentary:** Civil GNSS is moving from “can compute a fix” to “can prove the fix was not forged”—a trust-layer upgrade for drones, timing, and critical infrastructure, not just another accuracy decimal place.

### 3. “Once-in-a-century” lunar crater McGetchin: 222 m impact hurls debris over 100 km (Deep space)
**Summary:** Mid-September coverage and *Science Advances* studies describe McGetchin crater—formed in spring 2024 and confirmed via NASA’s Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter—as about 222 m wide and 43 m deep. Impacts of this scale are estimated roughly once every 132 years, making it among the largest new craters observed during the LRO mission. Ejecta modified the surface more than 120 km away, and a surrounding cold spot reflects lofted regolith. France 24, New Scientist, and LROC notes on Sep 16–17 frame the event as a natural experiment for airless-body impact physics and for designing crewed lunar assets against distant high-velocity debris.

**Links:**

- [France 24 — NASA finds a 'once in a century' lunar crater](https://www.france24.com/en/earth/20260917-nasa-finds-a-once-in-a-century-lunar-crater-bigger-than-the-roman-colosseum)
- [LROC — Spectacular McGetchin Crater](http://lroc.im-ldi.com/images/1501)

**Commentary:** The Moon is not “quiet”—a building-scale impact can rewrite surface properties at hundred-kilometer scales, so lunar engineering must budget for far-field ejecta, not just crater-rim hazards.

## II. Biomedicine & Health

### 4. Stanford *Nature Medicine*: antiseizure drug levetiracetam severs diffuse midline glioma “hijacked” synapses (Oncology)
**Summary:** Stanford Medicine–led research published Sep 17 in *Nature Medicine* shows the approved antiseizure drug levetiracetam slows diffuse midline gliomas (DMGs) by blocking GABAergic synaptic electrical input from healthy neurons. These tumors affect about 300–400 U.S. children per year with roughly 1% five-year survival. In mouse models the drug slowed DMG growth but not hemispheric high-grade gliomas; retrospective records suggested longer average survival among patients who received the drug. Clinical trials are being planned. DOI: 10.1038/s41591-026-04646-6.

**Links:**

- [Medical Xpress — Anti-seizure drug fights aggressive childhood brain tumors](https://medicalxpress.com/news/2026-09-anti-seizure-drug-aggressive-childhood.html)
- [Nature Medicine — Levetiracetam therapeutically targets GABAergic synapses in diffuse midline glioma](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-026-04646-6)

**Commentary:** Repurposing hits neuron–tumor synaptic coupling—for a near-untreatable childhood brain cancer, a known safety profile may matter more than waiting another decade for a bespoke molecule.

### 5. FDA approves Kerendia (finerenone) for adults with CKD associated with type 1 diabetes (Regulatory)
**Summary:** On Sep 17, 2026, the U.S. FDA approved Bayer’s Kerendia (finerenone) to reduce urinary albumin-to-creatinine ratio (UACR) in adults with chronic kidney disease associated with type 1 diabetes (T1D), expected to help lower risk of sustained eGFR decline and end-stage kidney disease. After Priority Review, the drug was described as a major new option in more than 30 years for this population and as the only nonsteroidal mineralocorticoid receptor antagonist indicated for adults with CKD tied to either type 1 or type 2 diabetes. Phase III Fine-One (NCT05901831, n=242) showed placebo-adjusted UACR reductions of about 22% at month 3 and 28% at month 6.

**Links:**

- [PharmExec — FDA Approves Kerendia for Adults with CKD and Type 1 Diabetes](https://www.pharmexec.com/view/fda-approves-kerendia-adults-chronic-kidney-disease-type-diabetes)

**Commentary:** T1D nephropathy long borrowed evidence from type 2 disease; a dedicated Phase III surrogate approval finally recognizes this overlooked chronic front line.

### 6. Roche’s Enspryng wins FDA Priority Review: Phase 3 shows 68% lower MOGAD relapse risk (Neuroimmunology)
**Summary:** Roche’s Enspryng (satralizumab) application for myelin oligodendrocyte glycoprotein antibody-associated disease (MOGAD) received FDA Priority Review, with an expected action date of Jan 10, 2027; the EMA has also validated the filing, with an EU decision expected in Q3 2027. Phase 3 METEOROID data showed a 68% reduction in relapse risk versus placebo; at 48 weeks, 87% of Enspryng-treated patients remained relapse-free versus 67% on placebo, with benefits on annualized relapse rate, MRI lesion activity, and rescue therapy. Approval would create the first disease-modifying therapy market for this rare neurologic condition.

### 7. University of Konstanz: gut-microbe vaccenic acid activates LRH-1, improving mouse glucose and fatty liver (Microbiome)
**Summary:** Coverage dated Sep 17 describes University of Konstanz work by Thomas Brunner and Anna Pia Plazzo in *EMBO Molecular Medicine* identifying, at molecular resolution, a bacterial ligand that activates the nuclear receptor LRH-1: vaccenic acid, which humans do not produce endogenously. In mice, vaccenic acid lowered blood glucose and lipids and improved existing fatty liver. The authors argue an intact microbiome can systemically tune host metabolism via such released molecules, while imbalance may have body-wide effects. DOI: 10.1038/s44321-026-00516-3.

**Summary:** Xinhua reported from Kathmandu on Sep 17 that World Weather Attribution analysis links late-August mudslides near the Nepal border to climate change. ### 9. Academia Sinica: ocean acidification shrinks squid brains ~49% on average and blunt predation (Marine ecology)
**Summary:** Academia Sinica’s Yung-Che Tseng and international collaborators reported on Sep 17, in work published in *Communications Biology*, that oval squids exposed for 90 days to seawater simulating 2100 acidification showed no clear body-size difference versus controls, yet mean whole-brain volume fell ~49%, optic lobes ~52%, and optic tracts ~62%. Basic vision remained, but optic-lobe metabolic networks reorganized and predatory behavior dropped sharply. The team frames this as the first clear brain-level structural change recorded in an aquatic animal under acidification, challenging the view that acidification mainly acts by impairing vision.

**Links:**

- [CNA — Academia Sinica: ocean acidification shrinks squid brains, cuts predation](https://www.cna.com.tw/news/ahel/202609170214.aspx)

**Commentary:** Acidification is not only about shells and senses—if high-order neural integration shrinks, food-web cascades from impaired top predators may dwarf single-species mortality.

### 10. *Nature Energy*: spatially targeted demand-side policies can raise European green-hydrogen cost-effectiveness by up to ~41% (Energy policy)
**Summary:** A Sep 17 *Nature Energy* paper combines an installation-level green-hydrogen demand model with a database of >14,000 potential European offtakers. Spatial spillovers—local adoption raising nearby adoption odds—intensify more than 20-fold when green hydrogen is not cost-competitive. Spillover potential concentrates in Central–Western Europe and hard-to-abate sectors; over half of the 10% most central sites sit in five German or Belgian industrial regions. Targeting the most central offtakers improves policy cost-effectiveness by up to ~41% (€0.96 kg⁻¹) in a size-, sector-, and cost-controlled setting, and ~15% (€0.20 kg⁻¹) under a European Hydrogen Bank auction format.

**Links:**

- [Nature Energy — Spatially informed demand-side policies for green hydrogen diffusion in Europe](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41560-026-02126-2)

**Commentary:** Peppering subsidies is weaker than finding network nodes—green hydrogen scale-up is as much economic geography as electrolyzer megawatts.

### 11. Stuttgart-led team: 3 nm AlOx buffer enables >17% semi-transparent perovskite and >26% tandems (PV materials)
**Summary:** Coverage dated Sep 17 describes an international team including the University of Stuttgart and Forschungszentrum Jülich reporting in *Materials Today* that a ~3 nm ALD AlOₓ buffer can replace a conventional ~20 nm SnOₓ layer, suppressing ITO sputter damage and interfacial recombination under low-power magnetron sputtering. Semi-transparent inverted perovskite cells exceeded 17% efficiency and perovskite–silicon two-terminal tandems exceeded 26%, while cutting material use and process time; a 1.5 nm layer was insufficient against sputter damage.

## IV. Fundamental Research & Biodiversity

### 12. *Nature Astronomy*: black-hole jets share a universal critical accretion rate near 2% Eddington (Astrophysics)
**Summary:** A Sep 17 *Nature Astronomy* study uses tidal disruption events (TDEs) to watch supermassive black-hole accretion flows form and evolve, finding distinct outflows during a super-Eddington phase and again near \(L_{\rm crit}\approx 0.02\,L_{\rm Edd}\)—matching the critical rate at which stellar-mass black-hole X-ray binaries launch hard-state jets. The result argues that accretion–outflow coupling and the low-rate jet threshold are scale-invariant from stellar to supermassive masses, and offers a framework to predict which black holes launch jets and when.

**Links:**

- [Nature Astronomy — A universal critical accretion rate for black hole jet formation](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41550-026-02951-1)

**Commentary:** Pinning X-ray-binary’s ~2% threshold onto TDEs suggests jet physics may truly share one cross-mass ruler.

### 13. *Nature Chemistry* News & Views: iron salts plus light-driven Mo catalysis spotlight room-temperature seawater ammonia route (Chemistry)
**Summary:** A Sep 17 *Nature Chemistry* News & Views highlights work by Zidouhia, Bassani and colleagues: inexpensive iron(II) salts under UV generate solvated electrons that drive a molybdenum catalyst to reduce N₂ to ammonia, with water as the terminal electron donor completing a water-oxidation cycle. Reported quantum yields reach ~5.0% in water at ambient temperature and pressure and ~10.8% at elevated N₂ pressure, with ~7.6% light-to-chemical efficiency; reactivity also operates in unpurified seawater or ambient air. The approach aims to sidestep energy-intensive Haber–Bosch chemistry but remains constrained by N₂ solubility, UV sources, and scale-up engineering.

**Links:**

- [Nature Chemistry — Forging ammonia with iron and light from water and nitrogen](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41557-026-02253-z)
- [Nature Chemistry — Artificial photosynthetic reduction of nitrogen to ammonia using natural seawater](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41557-026-02218-2)

**Commentary:** “Photosynthetic nitrogen fixation” only challenges Haber–Bosch once it leaves UV lamps and high-pressure N₂ tanks; today’s value is proving water can serve as the electron source.

### 14. New living cat species *Leopardus tilcayo* named in Bolivia—first formal living cat species in over a century (Biodiversity)
**Summary:** Reuters reported on Sep 17 that a small wild cat from Bolivia’s Yungas cloud forest has been genetically confirmed and formally named *Leopardus tilcayo* (locally Tilcayo)—the first living cat species formally named and described in more than a century. It measures about 46 cm long and weighs about 1.4 kg, smaller than a typical domestic cat, with light-brown fur and irregular rosettes. The find underscores cryptic diversity among South American tiger cats and the conservation urgency of humid montane forests.
