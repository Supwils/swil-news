## I. Space & Deep Exploration

### 1. ESA’s Hera first resolves Didymos and Dimorphos as separate bodies (planetary defense)
**Summary:** Coverage on Nov. 3 from Space.com, ScienceDaily and ESA highlights a milestone for Europe’s Hera planetary-defense probe: on the evening of Oct. 18 its monochrome Asteroid Framing Camera, from about 1,280 km, first resolved the Didymos–Dimorphos binary as two objects instead of a single point of light. The pair is about 197 million km from Earth; Dimorphos is roughly 151 m across and Didymos about 780 m, orbiting ~1.15 km apart on average. Prior braking burns cut relative speed to about 20 m/s. Hera will survey the aftermath of NASA’s 2022 DART impact, which shortened Dimorphos’s orbital period by ~33 minutes—more than models predicted. ESA says the first detailed Dimorphos images are due Nov. 25.

**Links:**

- [ESA — First light: Hera resolves two asteroids instead of one](https://www.esa.int/Space_Safety/Hera/First_light_Hera_resolves_two_asteroids_instead_of_one)
- [Space.com — Dimorphos in sight: Europe's Hera probe gets its 1st look](https://www.space.com/space-exploration/asteroid-comet-missions/dimorphos-in-sight-europes-hera-probe-gets-its-1st-look-at-battered-space-rock-photo)

**Commentary:** Separating two dots is the prelude; explaining why the deflection beat the models is what turns a demo into forecastable planetary defense.

### 2. ISS Expedition 75 preps Thursday’s final roll-out solar-array spacewalk (on-orbit engineering)
**Summary:** NASA’s Nov. 3 station blog says Expedition 75 is continuing preparations for Thursday’s second—and final—power-upgrade spacewalk in the series. CSA’s Joshua Kutryk and NASA’s Anil Menon staged tools and inspected tethers in the Quest airlock and reviewed procedures for a roughly seven-hour EVA starting at 8:30 a.m. EST to install the eighth and last roll-out solar array (iROSA) on the starboard truss. Completion should raise generating capacity from about 160 kW to about 215 kW; NASA+ and other platforms plan continuous coverage from 7 a.m. Thursday. The crew is also running microgravity research and cargo transfers.

**Links:**

- [NASA — Crew Keeps Up Spacewalk Preps, Research, and Cargo Operations](https://www.nasa.gov/blogs/spacestation/2026/11/03/crew-keeps-up-spacewalk-preps-research-and-cargo-operations/)

**Commentary:** Once the last iROSA locks in, ISS power expansion shifts from construction to harvesting the capacity gain.

## II. Biomedicine & Health

### 3. FDA accelerates Voceryq: first plasmid DNA immunotherapy to cut RRP surgeries (rare disease)
**Summary:** FDA granted accelerated approval on Oct. 30 to Inovio’s Voceryq (doruxapapogene ralaplasmid and lalikinogene sifuplasmid-gxfd, formerly INO-3107) to reduce surgical papilloma excisions in adults with recurrent respiratory papillomatosis (RRP); WebMD and CheckRare continued detailed coverage on Nov. 3. It is the first U.S.-approved plasmid DNA immunotherapy, targeting disease-causing HPV-6/11 plus an IL-12 adjuvant plasmid. In RRP-001 (NCT04398433), 7 of 26 patients (27%) with ≥3 baseline surgeries needed no surgery in the year after four doses; 23 of 32 overall had complete or partial responses (≥50% fewer surgeries). Continued approval hinges on confirmatory work, with a draft protocol due Dec. 30, 2026.

**Links:**

- [FDA — Approves First Plasmid DNA-based Immunotherapy for Adults with RRP](https://www.fda.gov/news-events/press-announcements/fda-approves-first-plasmid-dna-based-immunotherapy-adults-recurrent-respiratory-papillomatosis)
- [WebMD — FDA Approves Voceryq to Cut Down Airway Surgeries in Adults With RRP](https://www.webmd.com/drugs/updates/voceryq-recurrent-respiratory-papillomatosis)

**Commentary:** Moving RRP from endless cutting to immune intervention validates the platform; confirmatory trials must reproduce that 27% surgery-free rate.

### 4. University of Portsmouth: synthetic-base Z TFOs cut cancer-linked MYC activity 50%–80% (oncology)
**Summary:** The University of Portsmouth reported on Nov. 3 that work published in *Nucleic Acids Research* (flagged as a “breakthrough,” roughly the top 1%–2% of the journal) revives triplex-forming oligonucleotides (TFOs) against hard-to-target GC-rich regulatory DNA by incorporating a commercial synthetic base, Z, for stronger duplex binding. Against the cancer driver MYC, modified TFOs reduced gene activity by about 50%–80% in lab tests, outperforming unmodified DNA and prior approaches. The strategy remains preclinical and is positioned as a precise gene-regulation tool; the authors stress substantial further work before any clinical use.

**Links:**

**Commentary:** An old idea plus a new base reopens direct regulatory-DNA targeting—drug-likeness and off-target risk are the next gates.

### 5. Brazil authorizes Vivaxin Phase I against Plasmodium vivax malaria (infectious disease)
**Summary:** MalariaWorld, citing Brazilian agencies on Nov. 3, reported that Anvisa cleared human trials of Vivaxin, a malaria vaccine candidate from the Vaccine Technology Center at the Federal University of Minas Gerais. The Phase I study will enroll 48 healthy adults aged 18–59 with no malaria history and follow them for 360 days for safety, reactogenicity, and immunogenicity. Brazil’s health ministry plans about 12 million reais (~US$2.4 million) for Phases I–III. Vivaxin targets *Plasmodium vivax*, the leading malaria parasite in Brazil and common across the Americas and Asia.

**Links:**

## III. Climate, Energy & Environment

### 8. EEA: 2026 extreme heat cost the EU at least ~1% of GDP; critical infrastructure under stress (climate adaptation)
**Summary:** On Nov. 3 the European Environment Agency released the briefing “Europe’s critical infrastructure under climate stress — lessons from 2026,” estimating that climate-amplified extreme heat cost the EU at least about 1% of GDP in 2026, while drought, wildfires, and storms disrupted transport, energy, and water—matching risks flagged in the 2024 European Climate Risk Assessment. Examples include Rhine low water hitting freight, storm damage to Portuguese road/rail/power networks, and drought-heat pressure on generation in Central and Eastern Europe; cited estimates put energy at ~23% of Europe’s drought-related economic losses (~EUR 2 billion/year). The briefing lists six adaptation lessons: design for future climate, assess cross-system risks, stress-test regularly, prepare backups and warnings, clarify cross-sector roles, and pair near-term fixes with longer-term redesign.

### 9. CAS IEE & partners: Southern Ocean deep circulation drives ~1,350-year Holocene temperature swings (paleoclimate)
**Summary:** China’s Institute of Earth Environment (CAS) reported on Nov. 3 that, with Stockholm University, Laoshan Laboratory and others, an EC-Earth Holocene transient run from ~8,000 years ago plus 304 global temperature proxies—published in *PNAS*—identifies robust millennial variability with a ~1,350-year period. Southern Hemisphere westerlies, Antarctic sea ice and bottom-water formation, and tropical Pacific ENSO-like coupling sustain a self-maintained oscillation. The authors argue this frames natural warm-period variability and warn that a positive phase under anthropogenic warming could amplify feedbacks and raise the risk of rapid climate shifts.

### 10. *Scientific Reports*: PEM electrolyzers as active power sinks turn curtailed PV into green hydrogen (energy systems)
**Summary:** A Nov. 3 *Scientific Reports* paper tests proton-exchange-membrane (PEM) electrolyzers as active power sinks in active distribution networks to recover otherwise-curtailed PV into sellable green hydrogen. A two-stage stochastic co-optimization on the IEEE 33-bus system with Benban (Egypt) irradiance and tariffs cuts daily curtailment from about 44.77 to 27.16 MWh, trims active losses ~38.8%, and yields ~127,448 kg/yr of hydrogen (~1,000 t CO₂/yr displaced). Under a fair battery comparison, voltage support comes mainly from a shared smart inverter; the electrolyzer’s distinct value is bulk curtailment recovery. Full-cost levelized storage is about $0.978/kWh and hydrogen about $2.78/kg, with hydrogen revenue offsetting ~72% of hydrogen-subsystem cost.

**Links:**

- [Scientific Reports — Repurposing PEM electrolyzers as active power sinks](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-73831-x)

**Commentary:** Curtailed solar is not wasted electrons—it is priced hydrogen feedstock, if dispatch models stop mis-crediting battery-protection benefits.

## IV. Basic Research & Cross-Cutting Tech

### 11. CERN LHCb: first single-experiment evidence of one-in-ten-billion Bd→μ⁺μ⁻ decay (particle physics)
**Summary:** CERN announced on Nov. 3 that the LHCb Collaboration reported evidence—first from a single experiment—for the extremely rare decay of a Bd meson (beauty quark + down antiquark) into a muon pair (Bd → μ⁺μ⁻), with a branching fraction on the order of one in ten billion. 2024–2025 data alone reached 3.0σ; combined with 2011–2018 data the significance is 3.2σ, past the conventional “evidence” threshold. The same analysis delivers the most precise measurement yet of the previously seen Bs → μ⁺μ⁻ decay. Results match Standard Model expectations and constrain beyond-Standard-Model particles; confirmation at 5σ would make it among the rarest beauty-hadron decays observed.

**Links:**

- [CERN — LHCb finds evidence of one-in-ten-billion particle decay](https://home.cern/lhcb-finds-evidence-of-one-in-ten-billion-particle-decay/)

**Commentary:** Rare decays are indirect telescopes—you may not see new particles, but a one-in-ten-billion window still fences off where they can hide.

### 12. Stanford & partners: fingernail-sized chip untangles partially coherent light into clean beams (photonics)
**Summary:** Stanford University and collaborators, including ISTA, reported on Nov. 3 in *Nature Photonics* the first demonstration of a fingernail-sized chip that takes mixed, partially coherent light from multiple lasers and automatically sorts photons into mutually incoherent, clean output beams—succeeding with two and then three infrared inputs. The result grounds long-theorized physics and opens routes in microscopy and astronomy; the team is exploring with NASA whether the approach could unmix apparently blended light from distant galaxies into contributions from separate stars and exoplanets.

**Links:**

- [Phys.org / Stanford — Fingernail-sized chip untangles mixed light into clean beams](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-fingernail-sized-chip-untangles.html)

**Commentary:** Unmixing “blob” light is effectively a computational-optics front end for telescopes that need to separate sources before they look like one.

### 13. *Nature Nanotechnology*: ptychographic tomography places 6,648 carbon atoms in 3D at 0.12 Å precision (nanocharacterization)
**Summary:** On Nov. 3, *Nature Nanotechnology* published work led with Miaofang Chi and Jianwei Miao showing ptychographic atomic electron tomography (pAET) on twisted bilayer graphene that localizes 6,648 carbon atoms in three dimensions with 0.12 Å precision. The map reveals stacking-dependent interlayer spacing and corrugation across the moiré supercell, plus chiral lattice distortions with meron-, anti-meron-, and anti-skyrmion-like textures. DFT on the experimental structure predicts band narrowing and a K-point gap versus a relaxed model; multislice simulations support extending the method to diamond with an NV centre and to amorphous carbon—linking 3D atomic structure to light-element properties.

**Links:**

- [Nature Nanotechnology — Three-dimensional imaging of individual carbon atoms](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41565-026-02316-9)

**Commentary:** Seeing every carbon atom’s 3D coordinate finally gives light-element electronics an atomic-scale construction drawing.
