### 2. Keck confirms Elias 2-24 b: age ≲1 Myr, the youngest known exoplanet（Deep space）
**Summary:** A study published September 16, 2026, in *The Astrophysical Journal Letters* used Keck/NIRC2 vortex coronagraph imaging of stars with gapped protoplanetary disks to confirm the candidate in Elias 2-24’s narrow gap as planet Elias 2-24 b. The object sits at 394±31 mas (~54.9±4.3 au) and position angle 298.8°±3.2°; comparison with ~1 Myr isochrones yields a mass of about 1.9–4.0 Jupiter masses (without accretion effects). With a system age ≲1 Myr, it is the youngest confirmed exoplanet to date, supporting early core-accretion formation of giants near ~50 au and linking young-disk gaps to embedded planets. NASA highlighted that confirmation required stitching multi-telescope archival observations.

**Links:**

- [NASA — Newfound ‘Baby’ Planet Smashes Record for Youngest Known World](https://science.nasa.gov/universe/newfound-baby-planet-smashes-record-for-youngest-known-world/)
- [ApJL — Confirmation of a core-accretion planet in the Elias 2-24 disk](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.3847/2041-8213/ae9bb6)

**Commentary:** “Gaps mean planets” long stayed a inference; pinning a ≲1 Myr, Jupiter-class world inside a gap rewinds the formation clock.

## II. Biomedicine & Neuroscience

### 4. Stanford *Nature* paper: human cortical organoids fill cortex-depleted mice—“xenocortical” hosts for development and injury（Neuroscience）
**Summary:** On September 16, Sergiu Pașca’s Stanford Medicine team published in *Nature* a developmental “xenocortication” platform: a genetic strategy depletes most glutamatergic neurons from the mouse neocortex and hippocampus, then neonatal human stem-cell-derived cortical organoids (hCOs) are engrafted into the cortical cavity. Grafts can occupy most cortical volume, generate diverse human cortical cell types including layer-5 extratelencephalic projection neurons, and integrate with the mouse nervous system; calcium imaging and electrophysiology show organized, development-like activity. Behaviorally, apallial mice show maze-memory deficits that are largely restored in xenocortical mice, with gait intermediate between depleted and intact animals; grafts also contain rare von Economo neurons previously hard to obtain in culture. The platform supports circuit- and behavior-level readouts for injury to developing human cortical cells. BBC and Reuters stressed independent ethics review and strict welfare rules.

**Links:**

- [Nature — Developmental xenocortication using human-derived organoids in mice](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-11032-2)
- [Stanford Medicine — Advanced model for studying brain development, disorders](https://med.stanford.edu/news/all-news/2026/09/model-to-study-brain-development.html)

**Commentary:** This is not about “half-human pets”—it puts otherwise inaccessible human neural tissue into a system that yields circuit and behavioral readouts, so scientific gain and ethical guardrails must scale together.

### 5. *Nature Chemical Biology*: small-molecule IMP-2380 conformationally traps S. aureus NorA efflux, restoring ciprofloxacin potency（Infectious disease）
**Summary:** Research published September 16 reports IMP-2380, a potent, selective probe of the clinically important *Staphylococcus aureus* efflux pump NorA. A phenotypic screen for suppression of ciprofloxacin-induced SOS DNA-damage responses yielded a chemical series optimized to IMP-2380, which restores ciprofloxacin susceptibility with low-nanomolar potentiation in vitro and efficacy in an in vivo infection model. A 2.52-Å cryo-EM structure shows the inhibitor bound to an outward-open transporter conformation, occluding the cytosolic substrate-binding cavity and blocking antibiotic efflux—providing both a chemical probe and a structural framework for efflux-targeted resistance reversal.

**Links:**

- [Nature Chemical Biology — Small-molecule inhibitors block NorA efflux by conformational trapping](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41589-026-02319-6)

**Commentary:** Fighting resistance need not always mean a brand-new antibiotic—sometimes locking old drugs inside the bug is the more practical path.

## III. Climate & Environment

### 8. IMBIE: polar ice sheets lost ~11,309 Gt from 1979–2023; 84% driven by faster glacier discharge（Climate）
**Summary:** On September 16 the Ice Sheet Mass Balance Inter-comparison Exercise (IMBIE) team published in *Scientific Data* the longest combined satellite record to date: 42 independent estimates from 27 missions, extending to 1972 for Greenland and 1979 for Antarctica. Between 1979 and 2023 the ice sheets lost 11,309±565 Gt of ice, raising global mean sea level by about 31.4±1.6 mm; glacier dynamical imbalance drove ~84% of the loss and surface mass-balance changes ~16%. ESA and collaborators note the ice sheets now contribute roughly a quarter of global sea-level rise, and ice-sheet instability remains the largest uncertainty in future projections.

**Links:**

- [Scientific Data — Mass balance of the Greenland and Antarctic ice sheets from the 1970s to 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41597-026-08088-0)
- [ESA — Faster-flowing glaciers fuel decades of polar ice loss](https://www.esa.int/Applications/Observing_the_Earth/FutureEO/Faster-flowing_glaciers_fuel_decades_of_polar_ice_loss)

**Commentary:** The story is less “ice melting on top” than ice speeding into the ocean—satellite tracking of dynamics matters more than surface melt alone.

### 9. UC Davis / *Nature Climate Change*: heat-related labor losses ~$41 per ton CO₂, second-largest SC-CO₂ component（Climate economics）
**Summary:** Frances Moore and colleagues at UC Davis published on September 16 in *Nature Climate Change* an estimate that each ton of CO₂ emitted in 2025 costs about $41 (90% CI: $1–108) in heat-related lost labor productivity, with impacts concentrated in South, East, and Southeast Asia and Africa. Updating agricultural damages alongside this labor term revises the expected 2025 social cost of carbon from about $204 to about $179 per ton, while making labor the second-largest contributor after heat-related deaths. The analysis combines wet-bulb globe temperature projections with sector-specific work intensity and general-equilibrium adaptation.

**Links:**

- [Nature Climate Change — New labour and agricultural damages improve climate cost estimates](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41558-026-02749-z)
- [UC Davis — The High Cost of Climate Change on Worker Productivity](https://www.ucdavis.edu/climate/news/high-cost-climate-change-worker-productivity)

## IV. Fundamental Physics & Chemistry

### 11. CERN BASE: first road transport of trapped antiprotons over 7.5 km without loss（Particle physics）
**Summary:** A September 16 *Nature* paper from the BASE collaboration reports road transport of 92 trapped antiprotons in the cryogenic, transportable Penning-trap system BASE-STEP along a 7.5-km route outside CERN’s Antimatter Factory, with no particle loss and no measurable degradation of trap vacuum. Magnetic noise at the accelerator complex limits online CPT tests with trapped antiprotons; relocating them to low-noise offline labs is expected to enable at least ~100-fold improved symmetry tests. The demonstration marks the start of precision antiproton measurements beyond the factory floor.

**Links:**

- [Nature — Road transport of trapped antiprotons](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-11019-z)

**Commentary:** Sometimes antimatter’s bottleneck is not making it but measuring it quietly—if it can ride in a truck, lab siting logic changes.

### 12. *Nature*: a thermodynamically favoured Scaffolded DNA Computer runs reusable ~100-bit programs（Molecular computing）
**Summary:** Also on September 16, *Nature* demonstrated a Scaffolded DNA Computer (SDC) that embeds computation in relaxation toward a thermodynamically favoured equilibrium rather than costly kinetic error correction. Across 10 programs—including Multiplication-by-3, Division-by-2, 8-bit parity detection, and addition of 25-bit numbers (~100-bit computation)—protocols are comparatively simple, reusable dozens of times, and small instances finish in under a minute. The authors argue the architecture is programmable and scalable without requiring precise kinetic control or conventional error correction, offering a new template for equilibrium computation in synthetic systems.

**Links:**

- [Nature — A thermodynamically favoured molecular computer](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10996-5)

**Commentary:** Most molecular computers burn energy to suppress errors; if the right answer is the equilibrium state, the machine cools into the solution instead of endlessly proofreading.
