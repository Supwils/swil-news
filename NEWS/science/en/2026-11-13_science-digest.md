## I. Space & Deep Space

### 1. NASA Roman: Wide Field and Coronagraph instruments achieve precise on-orbit alignment (space observatory)
**Summary:** On November 13, NASA announced that the Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope has completed a key milestone: bringing its optics and instruments into precise alignment and moving closer to science operations. Program scientists say Roman must be at least about ten times more stable than Hubble or Webb to combine a huge field of view with tiny measurement uncertainties. Teams used stellar test observations to correct small shifts from launch vibration, temperature changes, and microgravity, refining the observatory’s internal map to within a tiny fraction of a hair’s breadth. The Wide Field Instrument doubles as guider, so even during long Coronagraph stares it can lock guide stars in a neighboring patch and deliver “bonus” deep fields. Next, engineers will exercise more than 3,000 moving parts on the Coronagraph’s deformable mirrors for active starlight suppression with real starlight—described as a record-setting first in space.

**Commentary:** Alignment is not mere focus—without this stability budget, Roman’s wide-field cosmology and exoplanet direct imaging both collapse into noise accounting.

### 2. Colorado team builds the first solar optical quantum clock referenced to sunlight (astro-metrology)
**Summary:** On November 13, *New Scientist* reported that Scott Diddams and colleagues at the University of Colorado Boulder built the first optical quantum clock that uses sunlight as a core reference. The setup still employs a laser and frequency comb, but instead of locking to transitions in isolated lab atoms, it locks to spectral signatures from iron atoms in the Sun’s atmosphere excited by sunlight, fed into the clock via special fiber. Accuracy does not yet match top atomic optical clocks, yet during the 2024 partial solar eclipse the team saw signature spectral shifts from lunar occultation; feeding distant starlight could similarly hint at exoplanets, though faint light and long integrations remain limits. Metrology experts note nearer-term value for diagnosing solar eruptions, sunspots, and solar-wind effects.

**Links:**

- [New Scientist — Researchers build the first ever quantum clock that uses sunlight](https://www.newscientist.com/article/2598583-researchers-built-the-first-ever-quantum-clock-that-uses-sunlight/)

**Commentary:** Moving the clock reference from a vacuum chamber to a stellar atmosphere turns a timekeeper into a Doppler stethoscope for stars.

## II. Biomedicine & Health

### 4. FDA approves durvalumab plus enfortumab vedotin for peri-operative MIBC (immuno-oncology ADC)
**Summary:** On November 13, the U.S. FDA approved PD-L1 inhibitor durvalumab (Imfinzi) with antibody–drug conjugate enfortumab vedotin-ejfv as neoadjuvant therapy, followed by adjuvant durvalumab alone after radical cystectomy, for adults with muscle-invasive bladder cancer (MIBC) who are ineligible for or refuse cisplatin-based chemotherapy. Efficacy comes from open-label randomized Phase 3 VOLGA (NCT04960709) in 695 patients (1:1:1). Versus cystectomy alone, the combination improved event-free survival (median not reached vs 12.1 months; HR=0.51, P<.0001) and overall survival (HR=0.58, P=.0027). Review ran under Project Orbis with partner agencies, used Assessment Aid, received Priority Review, and finished about six weeks ahead of the goal date.

**Commentary:** Cisplatin-ineligible MIBC patients finally get a peri-operative immuno-ADC option with quantified survival gain—not surgery alone by default.

### 5. Hopkins / Academia Sinica: allele-specific ASO rescues GRIA1 gain-of-function mice (precision RNA therapy)
**Summary:** Johns Hopkins University School of Medicine and Academia Sinica report in *Neuron* an antisense oligonucleotide (ASO) that selectively silences mutant GRIA1 RNA carrying the GluA1 A636T variant linked to autism spectrum disorder and intellectual disability, while sparing the wild-type allele. Knock-in mice showed progressive hippocampal damage, inflammation, and deficits in learning, memory, and social behavior. A single optimized neonatal intracerebroventricular dose (with cytosine methylation to cut toxicity) cut mutant transcripts to ~26% of control, prevented hippocampal pathology, and improved behavior. Coverage circulated widely around November 13; the work remains preclinical, with human-compatible sequences and delivery still needed.

**Links:**

- [Johns Hopkins Medicine — Precision RNA Therapy for Neurodevelopmental Disorders](https://www.hopkinsmedicine.org/news/newsroom/news-releases/2026/11/precision-rna-therapy-for-neurodevelopmental-disorders-caused-by-mutations-in-brain-receptors-paves-way-for-future-research)
- [Medical Xpress — Precision RNA therapy offers hope for brain receptor disorders](https://medicalxpress.com/news/2026-11-precision-rna-therapy-brain-receptor.html)

**Commentary:** Rather than shutting the whole AMPA door, the ASO removes only the broken copy—a testable allele-specific scalpel for receptor gain-of-function disease.

## III. Climate & Environment

### 7. NASA Earth Observatory spotlight: global mangroves shift from net loss toward net gain after ~2010 (coastal carbon sinks)
**Summary:** On November 13, NASA’s Earth Observatory featured *Science* work by Zhang et al. using Landsat (1984–2023) to map mangrove extent and canopy density: nearly 3,000 km² lost between about 1984 and 2010, then an unexpected gain of nearly 2,000 km² afterward, led by Asia, Australia, and South America. Roughly two-thirds of expansion came from colonizing new mudflats and one-third from restoration/regrowth in degraded areas; many standing forests also grew denser canopies. Southeast Asia—notably Myanmar—saw major reversals after post-tsunami protection and sedimentation; the Niger Delta and cyclone-hit North America/southern Africa still show net loss or degradation. Authors stress that halting deforestation plus natural expansion—not complacency—drives recovery.

**Links:**

- [NASA Earth Observatory — A Global Rebound for Mangroves](https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/a-global-rebound-for-mangroves/)
- [Science — Unexpected expansion and regrowth in Earth’s mangrove forests](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.aec9773)

**Commentary:** Mangroves show that cutting less and leaving mudflats often buys coastal protection and carbon storage more cheaply than planting alone.

### 8. SCSIO: coral-reef subsurface marine heatwaves reach 150 m, challenging deep refugia (ocean heat stress)
**Summary:** Around November 13, Wang Chunzai’s team at the South China Sea Institute of Oceanology, Chinese Academy of Sciences, reported in *Geophysical Research Letters* an analysis of warm-season marine heatwaves across seven depth layers to 150 m over global reef regions (1993–2023 reanalyses). Annual heatwave days generally fall with depth, but mean, maximum, and cumulative intensity strengthen subsurface—especially in the western Pacific warm pool—and all layers show rising trends over 31 years. During strong El Niño and the 2020–2023 triple La Niña, severe events reached 150 m; in 2020–2023, the 90–150 m layer once affected ~75% of studied reef area with cumulative intensity peaking at 160°C·days. Mesophotic reefs (~30–150 m) are not reliable thermal refuges; monitoring must go below the surface.

**Links:**

- [MOST / China Science Daily — Subsurface reef heatwaves challenge deep-water refuge hypothesis](https://www.ncsti.gov.cn/kjdt/kjrd/qtrd_kjrd/202611/t20261113_263377.html)

**Commentary:** Sea-surface temperature alone understates reef risk—if heatwaves dive into the mesophotic zone, “go a little deeper” stops being a safe strategy.

## IV. Fundamental Research & Physical Chemistry

### 10. UC San Diego: measured dark-mode counts far below theory, lowering the bar for polariton chemistry (light–matter chemistry)
**Summary:** On November 13 in *Chem*, Harsh H. Bhakta and Wei Xiong at UC San Diego used two-dimensional infrared (2DIR) microscopy to quantify vibrational polaritons versus inactive “dark modes” in an optical cavity. Theory had estimated ~10⁶–10¹² dark modes per polariton under a single-cavity-mode approximation, seeming to make strong light–matter coupling chemically irrelevant. Tracking W(CO)₆ vibrational polariton spatial propagation in a Fabry–Perot cavity, the team extracted N_D/P ≈ 10^(3.2±0.4)—orders of magnitude lower. Active states appear prominent enough for cavity-controlled chemistry, potentially enabling energy-efficient manufacturing and pharma routes that need no extra energy input to form polaritons.

**Links:**

- [UC San Diego Today — Quantifying Dark Modes to Advance Polariton Chemistry](https://today.ucsd.edu/story/quantifying-dark-modes-to-advance-polariton-chemistry)

**Commentary:** The long “dark modes drown the actives” paradox shrinks by orders of magnitude—cavity chemistry moves from “shouldn’t work” to “can be engineered.”

### 11. Brookhaven: unpaired unsupervised generative model isolates jets in heavy-ion collisions (nuclear-physics AI)
**Summary:** On November 13, a Brookhaven National Laboratory–led team with Columbia and CU Boulder described in *Scientific Reports* a generative “super intelligence” method that separates high-energy particle jets from the huge underlying-event background in relativistic heavy-ion collisions—without paired signal–background labels—to sharpen probes of the quark–gluon plasma (QGP). On simulated sPHENIX calorimeter data, the model reconstructed jet position, energy, and substructure better than conventional background subtraction, especially for larger jets where fluctuations are worst. RHIC ended operations in early 2026; sPHENIX amassed nearly 300 PB of data. Next steps target real data and possible Electron-Ion Collider applications.

**Links:**

- [Brookhaven Lab — Super Intelligence Method Learns on Its Own to Single Out Jet Signals](https://www.bnl.gov/newsroom/news.php?a=123018)

**Commentary:** Early-universe “quark soup” hides in jet modification, and jets hide in background avalanches—if unsupervised unmixing survives real data, strong-force precision gains a key.

### 12. UTokyo / Northwestern: spin-polarized radical pairs in MOFs toward a “quantum nose” (quantum sensing)
**Summary:** An international team led by Nobuhiro Yanai (University of Tokyo) and Michael R. Wasielewski (Northwestern) created ordered, light-activated spin-polarized radical pairs inside metal–organic frameworks (MOFs) and showed spin responses to certain chemicals; results were reported around November 13 in *Nature Communications* (DOI: 10.1038/s41467-026-79262-6). Prior radical-pair work was mostly limited to isolated solid-state molecules; here ultrafast spectroscopy, EPR, and quantum chemistry guided crystalline arrays aimed at chemical quantum sensing—a “quantum nose”—with possible gains for MRI-like sensitivity. Next steps expand spin systems and chemical stimuli.
