## I. Space & Astronomy

### 1. NASA–IBM open lunar foundation model: ~2 million tiles for craters, volcanism, and polar ice stability (Space/AI)
**Summary:** NASA and IBM Research released the NASA-IBM Lunar Foundation Model, among the first open-source foundation models built for lunar science; Space.com and others followed on September 12. Pretrained mainly on ~17 years of Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter data—about 2 million image tiles, including more than 1 million ~1 m images and nearly 964,000 ~100 m multispectral frames—plus GRAIL, Lunar Prospector, and JAXA SELENE inputs, the weights and code are on Hugging Face and GitHub. Fine-tuning supports crater mapping, irregular mare-patch detection, and polar ice-stability / prospectivity estimates, with a clear edge on ice-related benchmarks versus strong baselines.

### 2. Hubble confirms a strengthening decagon wave at Saturn’s south pole (~63°S, multi-layer jet) (Planetary science)
**Summary:** Sánchez-Lavega, Amy Simon and colleagues report in *Science Advances* a ten-sided atmospheric wave near planetographic latitudes ~58°S–63°S, based on Hubble Outer Planet Atmospheres Legacy imaging; NASA Science and outlets highlighted it on September 12. The structure sits in a strong eastward jet and extends through multiple atmospheric layers; it drifts east at only ~2.5 m/s versus a jet peak near 116 m/s, with vertex longitudes oscillating on a ~32-day period. Hubble shows a nascent feature by 2023 that later gained contrast; Cassini saw no long-lived southern counterpart. Formation may involve jet disturbances or a nearby anticyclone; longevity and kinship to the northern hexagon remain open for JWST and further modeling.

**Links:**

- [NASA Science — Hubble tracks new decagon at Saturn’s south pole](https://science.nasa.gov/missions/hubble/nasas-hubble-tracks-new-decagon-encircling-saturns-south-pole/)
- [Science Advances — A decagon wave around Saturn’s south pole](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/sciadv.aee4251)

**Commentary:** The northern hexagon is no longer unique—Saturn’s jet system is still forging new geometric waves as seasons turn.

## II. Biomedicine & Methods

### 3. *Nature*: WHOLISTIC records second-scale cell “conversations” across a living vertebrate (Bioimaging)
**Summary:** Ruetten, Ahrens and colleagues at HHMI Janelia publish WHOLISTIC (WHole-Organism Live-Imaging System for recording Tissue and IntraCellular activity) in *Nature* on September 12: pancellular calcium sensors, high-speed volumetric fluorescence microscopy, and machine learning enable cellular-resolution, second-timescale activity imaging across transparent larval zebrafish, with proof of concept in adult *Danionella cerebrum*, plus whole-body expansion microscopy for molecular/ultrastructural context. Findings include unexpected chondrocyte responses to cold and meningeal responses to ketamine, nephron traveling waves, muscle–organ synergies, and brainstem-controlled body-wide blood-flow redistribution; optogenetics enables all-optical brain–body causal tests.

**Links:**

- [Nature — Imaging cellular activity across all organs](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10979-6)
- [ScienceAlert — Watch cells communicate across a living body](https://www.sciencealert.com/scientists-can-now-watch-conversations-between-cells-across-the-body-of-a-living-animal)

**Commentary:** Organ-slice physiology can now meet whole-organism circuits—cross-system couplings that were once inferred can be watched live.

### 4. FDA approves Isembyld (apitegromab): first muscle-targeted therapy for SMA muscle loss (Rare disease)
**Summary:** The U.S. FDA approved Scholar Rock’s Isembyld (apitegromab-mstn) injection for adults and children aged 2+ with spinal muscular atrophy who are already on SMN2-targeted therapy; FDA and trade coverage dated September 11–12. The anti-myostatin monoclonal antibody addresses residual muscle impairment beyond neuron-sparing drugs. In the 52-week randomized double-blind trial (NCT05156320) of 188 patients aged 2–21, the primary analysis in ages 2–12 showed significant HFMSE improvement at one year with 10 mg/kg IV every four weeks versus placebo, and clinically meaningful responders at about 34.2% versus 13.5%. Common adverse events include upper respiratory infections; fracture risk is elevated.

**Links:**

- [FDA — First therapy to target muscle loss in SMA](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/news-events-human-drugs/fda-approves-first-therapy-target-muscle-loss-spinal-muscular-atrophy)
- [Pharmacally — ISEMBYLD / SAPPHIRE highlights](https://pharmacally.com/fda-approves-isembyld-apitegromab-sma/)

**Commentary:** SMA care is moving from “save neurons” to “rebuild muscle” combinations—fracture signals show muscle targeting is not cost-free.

### 5. FDA expands Joenja (leniolisib): first approved oral therapy for children 4–11 with APDS (Immune rare disease)
**Summary:** Pharming announced on September 11 that the FDA approved Joenja (leniolisib) 40 mg and 50 mg twice daily for children aged 4–11 weighing at least 27 kg with activated PI3Kδ syndrome (APDS); Pharmaletter and others followed on September 12. The drug was already approved for patients 12 and older; the pediatric sNDA rests on a multinational open-label Phase III study showing reduced lymphadenopathy and increased naïve B cells over 12 weeks, with a safety profile consistent with prior use and no drug-related serious adverse events. U.S. pediatric doses are expected around October via specialty channels.

### 6. Wuhan University team: densely modified pegRNAs reach ~70% prime editing in mouse liver after one LNP dose (Gene editing)
**Summary:** Lei, Yin and colleagues report in *Nature Biomedical Engineering* (online September 11) that pegRNAs decorated with dense RNA-motif modifications across scaffold, spacer, reverse-transcription template, and primer-binding regions enable nearly 70% prime-editing efficiency in bulk mouse liver after a single lipid-nanoparticle co-delivery of prime-editor mRNA and modified pegRNA—implying most hepatocytes were edited. At a dose the authors call clinically translatable, editing rose ~80-fold versus conventional end-modified pegRNAs and suppressed target protein expression. Toxicity and predicted off-target checks supported tolerability; similar dense motifs (including MS2) also boosted base editing. Science outlets covered the work on September 12.

**Links:**

- [Nature Biomedical Engineering — Dense RNA motif modifications for in vivo prime editing](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41551-026-01787-4)
- [Scienmag — Nearly 70% efficiency in living mice](https://scienmag.com/densely-modified-rna-guides-push-prime-editing-to-nearly-70-efficiency-in-living-mice/)

**Commentary:** Non-viral, single-dose, high-efficiency delivery remains the clinical bottleneck for prime editing—chemically armored pegRNAs narrow that gap.

## III. Climate & Environment

### 7. *Science*: Rivers sent ~263,000 tons of microplastics to the ocean in 2022—96% from the Global South (Environment)
**Summary:** Peking University’s Liu, Wang and colleagues publish a global daily river-microplastic concentration and export framework in *Science*; PKU and EurekAlert coverage dated September 11–12. They estimate ~263,000 metric tons of microplastics reached the ocean via rivers in 2022, with ~96% originating in the Global South and more than half from Southeast and East Asia. About 70% of basins show rainfall-driven “pulse enrichment,” in which high flow raises both concentration and flux; for the top eight exporting rivers, the wet season contributes ~70% of annual flux on average, and ~80% of pulse-enrichment basins lie in developing countries. Without upstream waste control, more extreme rain could intensify these pulses.

**Links:**

- [Peking University College of Urban and Environmental Sciences — Science microplastics study](https://www.ues.pku.edu.cn/xyxw/42a66b0527c24e248333c8592ed7653c.htm)
- [EurekAlert — Heavy rainfall amplifies riverine microplastic transport](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/1143036)

**Commentary:** Ocean microplastics are not only a steady trickle—storms flush terrestrial stockpiles, so climate extremes and solid-waste policy must be tackled together.

### 8. *Climate Dynamics*: Human forcing disproportionately raises odds of China’s rarest extreme downpours (Climate)
**Summary:** Duan and colleagues combine new-generation climate models with ~60 years of high-resolution Chinese observations in *Climate Dynamics* to quantify how greenhouse gases, aerosols, and land-use change reshape extreme-precipitation probabilities; Scienmag reported on September 12. Log–log scaling shows probability ratios tend to rise with return period—anthropogenic fingerprints grow stronger toward the rare, high-impact tail, including century-scale daily extremes. The authors argue adaptation planning must stratify risk by return period, not only by mean wetting trends.

**Links:**

- [Scienmag — China’s rarest downpours more likely under human forcing](https://scienmag.com/human-caused-climate-change-is-making-chinas-rarest-downpours-even-more-likely/)

**Commentary:** If the rarest storms are amplified most, flood standards still keyed to historical return periods quietly erode their safety margins.

## IV. Fundamental Physics & Chemistry

### 9. *Nature Physics*: 104-qubit digital simulation witnesses inelastic “energy into matter” in a QFT (Quantum computing)
**Summary:** Farrell and Preskill (Caltech) with Zemlevskiy and Illa (University of Washington) report in *Nature Physics* evidence of inelastic particle production in one-dimensional Ising field theory on IBM’s ibm_marrakesh processor, using up to 104 qubits and as many as ~5,589 two-qubit gates. A W-state–based algorithm with mid-circuit measurement and feed-forward keeps wavepacket-preparation depth independent of spatial volume; energy-density skewness separates elastic from inelastic outcomes (light plus heavier final particles). The toolkit extends to scalar field theory, the Schwinger model, and 2D Ising field theory. Scienmag detailed the result on September 12.

**Links:**

### 10. USTC/Tsinghua: Switchable copper single-atom catalyst oxidizes methane to acetic acid with high selectivity (Catalysis)
**Summary:** Hong (USTC), Li (Tsinghua) and colleagues report in *Nature Nanotechnology* copper single atoms on boron nanosheets (Cu–B4 sites, Cu-SAs/B) that, with hydrogen peroxide, convert methane to acetic acid at ~97% selectivity and ~221.3 mmol·gCu⁻¹·h⁻¹ without added carbon monoxide. Isolated copper atoms can reversibly reorganize into clusters during reaction to enable C–C coupling. Scienmag covered the work on September 12; DOI: 10.1038/s41565-026-02271-5.

### 11. *Nature Communications*: Sn-doped hematite disentangles space-charge width vs built-in field in water splitting (Energy chemistry)
**Summary:** Li and colleagues, publishing September 12 in *Nature Communications*, use tin doping to tune Fe₂O₃ photoanode band bending and in-situ time-resolved spectroscopies to separate space-charge-layer width (WSCL) from built-in electric-field effects on charge dynamics. Charge-separation onset shows a ~2.4 nm WSCL threshold; above it, a strong built-in field empties electron trap states and blocks recombination between back electrons and charged intermediates, aiding surface hole accumulation for water oxidation. The result offers a quantitative structure–performance rule for photoelectrochemical hydrogen photoanodes.

**Links:**

- [Nature Communications — Space charge layer vs electric field in water splitting](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-77786-5)

**Commentary:** “Wide enough to start, strong enough to empty traps” turns a vague space-charge slogan into an engineerable threshold.
