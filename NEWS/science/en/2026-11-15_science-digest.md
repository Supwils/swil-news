## I. Space & Deep-Space Exploration

### 1. White dwarf + Sun-like companion: long-period radio pulses every ~8 minutes (radio astronomy)
**Summary:** An ICRAR Curtin University team reports in *Nature Astronomy* a new long-period radio transient (LPT), GPM J071507−114153: a low-mass white dwarf spinning every ~7.9 minutes in a binary with a G-type (Sun-like) companion about 5,000 light-years away. It is the first such pulsed system confirmed with a Sun-like companion; earlier analogs typically paired white dwarfs with cooler red dwarfs. The source was found with the MWA survey, localized with MeerKAT, and validated with more than 100 hours of radio data processed at Pawsey—clear enough, for the first time, to track the white dwarf’s orbital motion and pin the pulses near the white dwarf (orbital modulation ~1.3 days). The same radio data revealed flares and a likely stellar prominence on the companion; Keck optical spectroscopy confirmed the G-type star. ScienceDaily and others amplified the result on November 15.

**Links:**

- [ICRAR — Researchers discover rare star system that emits radio signals every eight minutes](https://www.icrar.org/binary-lpt/)
- [Nature Astronomy — A long-period transient with a Sun-like companion](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41550-026-03016-z)

**Commentary:** Orbital Doppler finally answers which star is “beeping”—and stretches the LPT puzzle beyond red-dwarf special cases.

### 2. NASA ESCAPADE: powered Earth flybys complete; twins now cruising to Mars (heliophysics)
**Summary:** NASA reports that the twin ESCAPADE spacecraft completed powered Earth flybys on November 7 and 9—closest approaches near 1,000 and 650 miles altitude, each with an ~9-minute trans-Mars injection burn that combines Earth’s gravity with the Oberth effect. It is NASA’s first use of a powered Earth flyby to send craft to Mars, executed successfully twice in two days. After a 2025 launch into an Earth–Sun L2 “loiter” orbit while waiting for favorable geometry, the probes are now on a ~10-month cruise, with Mars orbit insertions planned for September 7 and 9, 2027, and science formation operations targeted for June 2028 to study how the solar wind drives atmospheric escape at Mars. UC Berkeley’s Space Sciences Laboratory leads the SIMPLEx mission.

**Links:**

- [NASA Science — ESCAPADE Completes Powered Earth Flybys, Now En Route to Mars](https://science.nasa.gov/science-research/heliophysics/nasas-escapade-completes-powered-earth-flybys-now-en-route-to-mars/)
- [UC Berkeley SSL — ESCAPADE spacecraft say goodbye to Earth](https://www.ssl.berkeley.edu/berkeleys-escapade-spacecraft-say-goodbye-to-earth-next-stop-mars/)

**Commentary:** Loitering at L2 then borrowing Earth turns a once-every-two-years launch window into schedulable engineering—the science payoff still depends on the dual-orbiter choreography.

### 3. Voyager 1 countdown to one light-day: first human-made object at that distance on Nov. 18 (interstellar)
**Summary:** NASA continues to mark Voyager 1’s approach to one light-day from Earth—about 16.095 billion miles (25.902 billion kilometers)—expected around 5:16 a.m. EST on November 18, 2026, when a radio command will take roughly 24 hours one way. Launched in 1977 and nearly 49 years into flight, the probe remains the farthest human-made object. NASA invites a global “Shine Bright for Voyager’s Light-Day” golden-light wave after sunset on November 17 and a JPL discussion on November 19. Space media on November 15 kept the countdown in focus as an engineering milestone rather than a new physical discovery.

## II. Biomedicine & Health

### 4. FDA declines Summit/Akeso PD-1×VEGF ivonescimab in EGFR-mutated NSCLC (immuno-oncology)
**Summary:** Summit Therapeutics announced on November 15 that the U.S. FDA issued a Complete Response Letter (CRL) for its biologics license application for ivonescimab (a PD-1×VEGF bispecific) plus platinum chemotherapy in locally advanced or metastatic NSCLC after prior EGFR TKI therapy. The agency said substantial evidence of effectiveness is not established without a statistically significant overall-survival (OS) benefit, and that progression-free survival (PFS) alone does not support a positive benefit–risk profile. The global Phase 3 HARMONi trial met its PFS co-primary endpoint but missed statistical significance on the primary OS analysis (publicly cited HR ~0.79, p≈0.057); Summit says longer post-hoc follow-up showed a consistent OS trend (HR ~0.76) across regions. The drug is already approved in China via Akeso for a related indication; Summit said it will continue development in NSCLC and other cancers.

**Links:**

- [Summit Therapeutics — Update Regarding Ivonescimab BLA](https://smmttx.com/news/press-releases/news-details/2026/Summit-Therapeutics-Provides-Update-Regarding-Ivonescimab-Biologics-License-Application/default.aspx)
- [BioSpace — Summit's PD-1xVEGF rejected by FDA in lung cancer](https://www.biospace.com/drug-development/summits-pd-1xvegf-rejected-by-fda-in-lung-cancer)

**Commentary:** The U.S. debut of the PD-1×VEGF class hits an OS bar—regulators are saying a clean PFS win is not yet a settled survival ledger.

### 5. Activating ~20 motor-cortex neurons can trigger a learned mouse movement (neuroscience)
**Summary:** UC San Diego researchers led by Takaki Komiyama report in *Nature* that brief, synchronous holographic optogenetic stimulation of about 20 “early-onset” neurons in mouse primary motor cortex L2/3 can evoke the learned population activity pattern of a skilled lever press and, in many trials, the movement itself. Roughly half of light-triggered presses correlated above 0.5 with trained voluntary presses; success depended on the cortex’s pre-stimulation state and task engagement—stimulation failed in task-disengaged animals. Some trials produced the learned activity without movement, arguing the pattern is not a mere motor echo. Earth.com highlighted the work on November 15; the authors stress the result is limited to one brain region and an early learning stage in mice, far from clinical stroke or paralysis therapies.

**Links:**

- [Nature — Targeted stimulation of motor cortex ensembles drives learned movements](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-11174-3)
- [UC San Diego — Neurobiologists Trigger Learned Movements by Activating Just 20 Brain Cells](https://today.ucsd.edu/story/neurobiologists-trigger-learned-movements-by-activating-just-20-brain-cells)

**Commentary:** A read–write loop turns correlation into causation—motor cortex is an active generator, but a 20-cell switch still lives inside a mouse experiment’s time window.

### 6. Israeli team: common three-drug cocktail remits ~30% of LADA-model mice (autoimmune diabetes)
**Summary:** Bar-Ilan University’s Ron Piran and colleagues report in *MedComm* that a combination of GABA, sitagliptin, and omeprazole restored normal blood sugar in about 30% of mice modeled for latent autoimmune diabetes in adults (LADA, or “Type 1.5 diabetes”), with weeks of insulin-free remission after treatment stopped. Genomic collaborators identified cell-free RNA markers Adgrb1 and Chd5 that predicted responders. The Times of Israel covered the work around World Diabetes Day, noting human trials as the next step. LADA blends Type 1 autoimmune attack with adult Type 2–like onset and remains diagnostically messy; the data are animal-only and not a human cure claim.

**Links:**

- [The Times of Israel — Israeli scientists use common drugs to cure 30% of mice with 'Type 1.5 diabetes'](https://www.timesofisrael.com/israeli-scientists-use-common-drugs-to-cure-30-of-mice-with-type-1-5-diabetes/)

**Commentary:** Pairing drug repurposing with predictive RNA markers is closer to precision immuno-metabolism than a raw remission headline alone.

## III. Climate, Energy & Environment

### 7. *Science*: Earth’s cryosphere has breached its planetary safe operating space (planetary boundaries)
**Summary:** Led by Bo Su and colleagues at the Stockholm Resilience Centre with coauthors from Tsinghua, PIK and elsewhere, a *Science* assessment proposes a cryosphere “safe operating space” (SOS) referenced to Holocene conditions over ~11,700 years and evaluates land ice, sea ice, permafrost temperature, and snow cover. The authors conclude the cryosphere has already breached its SOS, with accelerating and partly irreversible loss amplified by feedbacks that can further warm the climate. Released amid COP31, the finding was widely covered by Xinhua on November 15, urging stronger emissions cuts and less disturbance in cryosphere-sensitive regions. The cryosphere holds roughly 70% of Earth’s freshwater and already stresses water security, ecosystems, and infrastructure.

**Links:**

- [Science — The cryosphere beyond a planetary safe operating space](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.aef5636)
- [Xinhua — Study shows global cryosphere accelerating decline](https://www.news.cn/world/20261115/29b26d8381474ca4aec80b52bf1b11c8/c.html)

**Commentary:** Putting the cryosphere on the planetary-boundary ledger means delay is no longer a negotiating style—the system has started to lock in risk.

### 8. PolyU/Zhejiang: balancing grain-boundary recombination yields 26.55% perovskite cells with T90 >2,200 h (PV materials)
**Summary:** Guang Yang (Hong Kong Polytechnic University) and Zhejiang University collaborators report in *Watt* that asymmetric defects at (100)/(111) hetero-facet grain boundaries—especially formamidinium vacancies on the (111) side—drive hole buildup that accelerates light-induced ageing of otherwise more photostable grains. Treating boundaries with m-F-PEABr equalizes recombination velocities (~300 vs ~320 cm/s), lifts champion PCE from ~25.19% to 26.55% (Voc 1.195 V, FF 84.5%), and extends unencapsulated T90 lifetime under continuous 1-sun MPP in nitrogen beyond 2,200 hours (control ~800 hours). PolyU publicized the work in early November; technical outlets continued coverage around November 15. Devices are ~0.06 cm²; scale-up remains open.

**Links:**

- [PolyU — Research uncovers hidden cause of ageing in perovskite solar cells](https://www.polyu.edu.hk/media/media-releases/2026/1104_polyu-research-uncovers-hidden-cause-of-ageing-in-perovskite-solar-cells/)
- [Watt / Springer — Stabilizing high-efficiency perovskite solar cells by homogenizing inter-grain recombination](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s44503-026-00013-1)

**Commentary:** Stability often fails where neighboring grains quarrel—equalizing recombination is closer to manufacturability than chasing another point of day-zero efficiency.

### 9. HUST-led HeD-MISSION maps China carbon neutrality via power–hydrogen–derivatives coupling (energy systems)
**Summary:** Xinyu Chen’s Huazhong University of Science and Technology team, with Harvard and Shandong University coauthors, publishes in *Nature* a national HeD-MISSION model spanning electricity, hydrogen, and derivatives (ammonia, methanol, methane, DME, Fischer–Tropsch fuels)—seven carriers in all—built from 40 years of hourly weather, 3,000+ thermal units, UHV corridors, 46,000+ transport routes, 78,000+ ship tracks, and 3,248 industrial facilities, with optimization scales exceeding 100 million decision variables. Results argue that converting curtailment into hydrogen derivatives is techno-economically attractive at high renewable shares; including derivatives can cut system transition cost versus electrification-only pathways (reported ~$10.7 trillion savings and negative abatement-cost framing), with an optimal path that could end crude-oil import dependence around 2045 and cut 2060 import outlays by an amount equivalent to ~52.9% of China’s 2023 goods-trade surplus. Chinese science media continued circulating the paper on November 15.

**Links:**

- [Nature — Hydrogen Derivatives Enable Cost-Effective Carbon Neutrality in China](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-11351-4)
- [SciToday — China’s first power–hydrogen–derivatives coupled model published in Nature](http://www.scitoday.cn/paper/info-45086.aspx)

**Commentary:** If net-zero only counts kilowatt-hours, heavy industry and shipping stay invisible—derivatives turn curtailed electrons into storable molecular fuels the system ledger still needs.

## IV. Fundamental Research & Physical Chemistry

### 10. Cambridge: first direct observation of Vinen “ultraquantum” turbulence decay in a BEC (quantum fluids)
**Summary:** Sebastian Morris, Zoran Hadzibabic and colleagues at Cambridge report in *Physical Review Letters* direct imaging of a decaying isotropic tangle of vortex lines during far-from-equilibrium Bose–Einstein condensation in a homogeneous 3D potassium gas. Using matter-wave magnification and thin-slice imaging, they track vortex-line-length density L over time; the decay matches Vinen “ultraquantum” turbulence—denser tangles decay faster, with later losses at trap walls—and is largely independent of interaction strength, resembling (after mass scaling) prior helium-superfluid inferences. Phys.org and an APS Physics Synopsis highlighted the result; the experiment supplies the missing direct 3D atomic-gas evidence after years of indirect helium or 2D-atom hints.

**Links:**

- [Physical Review Letters — Observation of Vinen Turbulence during Far-from-Equilibrium Bose-Einstein Condensation](https://journals.aps.org/prl/abstract/10.1103/lywd-gjg9)
- [Phys.org — Tangled quantum whirlpools caught unraveling in an ultracold gas](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-tangled-quantum-whirlpools-caught-unraveling.html)

**Commentary:** Order grows out of chaos through a countable tangle—photographing Vinen decay as a curve is harder evidence than simply saying “it condensed.”

### 11. CuO/ZnO heterojunction drives light-powered methane oxidative coupling with 97.8% C₂₊ selectivity (catalysis)
**Summary:** A *Nature Communications* study (online November 14) reports multi-interfacial CuO/ZnO photocatalysis for oxidative coupling of methane to C₂₊ chemicals. Interfacial charge redistribution creates electronically unbalanced sites that aid methane dissociation, ease methyl coupling, and suppress electrostatic repulsion, while Z-scheme transfer spatially separates oxygen reduction (CuO) from methane oxidation (ZnO). Reported performance includes an ethane yield of ~30.87 mmol g⁻¹ h⁻¹, C₂₊ selectivity up to 97.8%, an apparent quantum yield of ~3.9% at 420 nm, and ~124 hours of durability. The authors frame charge-symmetry breaking as a design principle; scale-up, full energy accounting, and impurity tolerance remain open.

**Links:**

- [Nature Communications — Light-driven oxidative coupling of methane via charge-symmetry-breaking](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-79430-8)

**Commentary:** Turning methane into C₂ with light is less about more sunshine than about whether the interface can steal coupling from combustion pathways.
