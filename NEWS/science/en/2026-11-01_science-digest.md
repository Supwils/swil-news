## I. Space & Deep Space

### 1. Google Project Suncatcher: first AI reply from a data-center-class TPU in orbit (space computing)
**Summary:** Google reported on October 30 that its Project Suncatcher prototype—built with Planet and launched October 1 on SpaceX Transporter-18—completed power-up and early ML tests, then uplinked a prompt to onboard Gemma 4 during a ground-station pass and downlinked the first AI response from a data-center-class TPU in orbit near Svalbard, Norway. Coverage on November 1 noted roughly 1 kW of available power, so typically one of four TPUs can run for about 20 minutes at a time. Next steps focus on thermal and radiation stress tests, with twin interconnected satellites planned for 2027.

### 2. NASA seeks commercial concepts to reboost, service, or safely deorbit Hubble by 2032 (orbital lifetime)
**Summary:** NASA’s Goddard Space Flight Center issued RFI 80GSFC27RFIHSTS on October 29 asking industry how it could capture the Hubble Space Telescope, boost it to at least 600 km, enhance on-orbit performance, or perform controlled reentry/disposal—without harming the observatory, and with action expected by 2032. Hubble has no propulsion; analyses point to reentry around 2033–2034. Respondents would supply launch vehicle, servicing spacecraft, and mission operations. Responses are due November 30 at 3:00 p.m. EST; NASA stresses this is information-gathering only, with no procurement commitment or funding allocated.

**Links:**

- [NASA Science — Explores Potential Commercial Servicing for Hubble](https://science.nasa.gov/missions/hubble/nasa-explores-potential-commercial-servicing-for-hubble-telescope/)

## II. Biomedicine & Health

### 3. FDA drafts “plug and play” path for personalized genome-editing products across mutations in one gene (regulation)
**Summary:** FDA released draft guidance titled *Individualized Human Genome Editing Products: Considerations for BLA Submission and Postapproval Management*, outlining how multiple product variants targeting different mutations in the same disease gene could sit in a single Biologics License Application. A first mutation would follow a more conventional evidence package that defines an “individualized feature space”; later variants could be added largely on laboratory data rather than a new full clinical trial. Comments are due November 27, 2026. Analysts flag near-term relevance for base-editing programs at firms such as Prime Medicine and Beam Therapeutics; the document remains nonbinding.

**Links:**

- [FDA — Individualized Human Genome Editing Products (draft guidance page)](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/individualized-human-genome-editing-products-considerations-bla-submission-and-postapproval)

### 4. Genmab’s petosemtamab plus pembrolizumab halves death risk in first-line PD-L1+ head and neck cancer (oncology)
**Summary:** On October 28 Genmab reported the first interim analysis of global Phase 3 LiGeR-HN1 (NCT06525220): the EGFR×LGR5 bispecific petosemtamab plus pembrolizumab versus pembrolizumab alone in first-line PD-L1-positive recurrent/metastatic HNSCC. An independent data monitoring committee found both co-primary endpoints met—overall survival HR 0.50 (95% CI 0.37–0.67; p<0.0001) and objective response odds ratio 3.17 (95% CI 2.06–4.89; p<0.0001)—regardless of HPV status. Safety matched known profiles of each agent with no new signals. Genmab plans regulatory engagement and a future congress presentation; additional Phase 3 trials are underway in later-line HNSCC and colorectal cancer.

### 5. MD Anderson: METTL13 methylation of eEF1A selectively boosts KRAS and other oncoproteins (cancer mechanism)
**Summary:** Researchers at The University of Texas MD Anderson Cancer Center report in *Nature Cell Biology* that METTL13-catalyzed dimethylation of translation elongation factor eEF1A at lysine 55 (eEF1AK55me2) improves decoding of selected weak-pairing codons, selectively elevating synthesis of pro-oncogenic proteins including KRAS. Reducing or removing METTL13 lowered KRAS protein and slowed tumor growth in multiple preclinical models; in sotorasib-resistant KRAS G12C lung adenocarcinoma models, METTL13 loss restored inhibitor sensitivity and extended survival. Higher inferred METTL13 activity tracked with poorer clinical responses to KRAS inhibitors. Findings remain preclinical.

**Links:**

- [MD Anderson — Newly discovered pathway fuels cancer-driving proteins](https://www.mdanderson.org/newsroom/research-newsroom/newly-discovered-pathway-fuels-production-of-cancer-driving-proteins.h00-159859290.html)
- [Nature Cell Biology — Methylated eEF1A selectively decodes mRNA](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41556-026-02085-6)

**Commentary:** Moving the lever upstream of finished KRAS protein opens a codon-level attack on targeted-therapy resistance.

### 7. IBS: snowfall and clouds may cushion East Antarctic ice loss under extreme warming (ice-sheet modeling)
**Summary:** Axel Timmermann, Kyung-Sook Yun and colleagues at Korea’s Institute for Basic Science report in *Nature Climate Change* (online October 30) 100,000-year coupled climate–ice-sheet simulations: warming above ~2°C above pre-industrial still drives millennial-scale West Antarctic disintegration, yet the East Antarctic interior remains comparatively resilient even near 10°C of warming. A moister atmosphere and poleward-shifted westerlies boost snowfall; more clouds cut shortwave heating. Under the strongest forcing, Antarctic volume loss fell from about 70% without those atmospheric responses to about 20% when they were included. Authors caution that West Antarctica and East Antarctic margins still lose substantial ice, and coarse resolution limits ice-shelf process fidelity.

**Links:**

- [Nature Climate Change — Atmospheric stabilization of the East Antarctic ice sheet](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41558-026-02791-x)
- [Phys.org — Snowfall and clouds may slow East Antarctic ice loss](https://phys.org/news/2026-10-snowfall-clouds-east-antarctic-ice.html)

**Commentary:** East Antarctic resilience is not a free pass—sea-level risk still concentrates on West Antarctica and retreating margins.

## IV. Fundamental Research (Physics / Chemistry / Materials)

### 8. USTC: all-pentaheptite carbon nanoribbon shows near-ballistic metallic conductance (quantum materials)
**Summary:** Bing Wang, Qitang Fan and colleagues at the University of Science and Technology of China report in *Nature* an on-surface hierarchical synthesis of carbon pentaheptite nanoribbons built only from five- and seven-membered rings. ~0.9-nm-wide CPHN 1 is an intrinsic metal: at low temperature and 10 mV bias, conductance reaches about 0.9 conductance quantum and stays nearly constant as the ribbon is lifted to ~4 nm—near-ballistic transport—while edge-isomer CPHN 2 is a narrow-gap semiconductor with ~100× lower conductance. The result experimentally realizes a metallic pentaheptite motif theorized in 1996; device transfer off silver remains a hurdle. Technology.org highlighted the work on November 1.

**Links:**

- [Nature — Narrow carbon pentaheptite nanoribbon as a covalent metal](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-11202-2)
- [USTC News — One-dimensional covalent carbon metal created](https://news.ustc.edu.cn/info/1048/96686.htm)

**Commentary:** Thirty years after theory, topology alone flips a carbon skeleton from semiconductor to quantum wire.

### 9. University of Würzburg: stable oxoboranes isolate a long-sought boron–oxygen triple bond (main-group chemistry)
**Summary:** Holger Braunschweig’s group, with computational support from Ashwini K. Phukan’s team at Tezpur University, reports in *Nature Chemistry* (October 27) a simple route from a diboracumulene and ketones to three oxoboranes that are isolable as room-temperature crystalline solids with exceptionally short B–O bonds (~1.208–1.217 Å). Species of this class had resisted stable isolation for more than 60 years despite matrix and trapping evidence. Doctoral researchers Lukas Lubczyk and Christian Markl independently obtained orange or yellow solids as main products. University and SciTechDaily coverage around November 1 notes efforts to extend the method across the periodic table.

**Links:**

**Commentary:** A molecule class once written off returns to the bench—and rewrites a page of main-group multiple-bond chemistry.

### 10. Augsburg / Copenhagen: current-driven molecular Archimedes screw rotates both ways (molecular machines)
**Summary:** Researchers including University of Augsburg experimentalists and Michael Pittelkow’s University of Copenhagen chemistry group anchored a spiral molecule between gold electrodes via sulfur contacts and showed that applied voltage drives rotation, with polarity reversing the direction—described as the first current-driven molecular motor fixed to two metal electrodes with controllable sense of rotation. Theory and measurements link rotation angle to conductivity swings of up to two orders of magnitude (~100×). ScienceDaily dated the summary November 1, 2026, framing it as a step toward working machines at molecular scale and molecular electronics.

**Links:**

- [ScienceDaily — Scientists shrink Archimedes’ screw to the size of a single molecule](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/10/261030002940.htm)

**Commentary:** Rotation is not just motion—it is a conformational dial written directly into conductance.

### 11. Brown University: ultrathin bismuth ferrite sheds the “dead layer” and shows altermagnetism (low-power magnetoelectrics)
**Summary:** Lucas Caretta’s lab reports in *Nature Materials* that growing bismuth ferrite on a compressively mismatched dysprosium scandate substrate with a metallic screening layer keeps ferroelectricity and magnetism intact down to only a few angstroms, eliminating the usual multiferroic “dead layer.” The engineered stack also exhibits altermagnetism—potentially combining fast dynamics, dense packing, and strong electrical readout for memory and logic. SciTechDaily covered the result on November 1; the team stresses this is a materials demonstration, not a finished computing chip.

**Links:**

- [SciTechDaily — Magnetism breakthrough for lower-energy computer chips](https://scitechdaily.com/a-breakthrough-in-magnetism-could-make-computer-chips-use-dramatically-less-energy/)

**Commentary:** Once ultrathin multiferroics stay alive, altermagnetism has a credible path into real process windows.
