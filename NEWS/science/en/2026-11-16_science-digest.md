## I. Spaceflight & Astronomy

### 1. SpaceX catches a Starship upper stage returning from orbit for the first time

**Summary:** Starship's 15th full-scale flight launched from Starbase on Nov. 13. The upper stage circled Earth about 14 times (roughly 265 km up), deployed 26 Starlink satellites, and returned Nov. 14, where the launch tower's arms caught it in midair, the first catch of a Starship coming back from space. The booster's engines shut down early and its guidance triggered a self-destruct into the Gulf of Mexico, ending the planned double catch; the cause is under investigation.

**Links:**

- [Phys.org — SpaceX's Starship returns from orbit and is captured by launch pad arms in a dramatic first](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-spacex-starship-orbit-captured-pad.html)

**Commentary:** Catching the upper stage validates heat shielding and tower capture, a key step toward full reusability, but the booster loss shows the whole sequence is not yet reliable.

### 2. ESA's Hera images a new, crater-covered side of Didymos

**Summary:** After reaching the Didymos binary asteroid system, ESA's Hera imaged a side unlike the one DART saw before impact, showing an equatorial ridge, boulders, craters and possible grooves. Didymos is about 780 m across and Dimorphos about 151 m. The team is investigating whether ejecta from the 2022 DART impact reached and reshaped Didymos; detailed images are due Nov. 25.

**Links:**

- [ScienceDaily/ESA — NASA Smashed Into an Asteroid. Now Hera Has Revealed a Strange New World](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/11/261116023351.htm)

**Commentary:** Hera turns DART's one-shot experiment into quantifiable planetary-defense data, and the real payoff comes with the Nov. 25 high-resolution release.

### 3. Euclid's first data release yields about 15,000 candidate strong gravitational lenses

**Summary:** Euclid's DR1 covers about 5% of the sky from 13 months of observations, including over 900 million galaxies, 60 million quasars and spectra of more than 160 million sources. A consortium of 2,000+ scientists found about 15,000 candidate strong lenses, versus fewer than 1,000 confirmed before Euclid; 26 papers appeared in Astronomy & Astrophysics.

**Links:**

- [Phys.org — Dark matter–hunting satellite Euclid reveals record number of cosmic 'magnifying glasses'](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-dark-matterhunting-satellite-euclid-reveals.html)

**Commentary:** An order-of-magnitude jump in candidates gives dark matter and cosmology studies a statistical sample, though follow-up must still confirm them.

### 4. "Chaotic accretion" may explain early supermassive black holes

**Summary:** Researchers at the Center for Physical Sciences and Technology in Vilnius propose that gas arriving from random directions keeps black hole spin low, cutting radiative efficiency to about 6–7.5% and shortening growth times to roughly 27–32 million years. That would let J0313-1806 (about 3 billion solar masses before the universe was 700 million years old) form without heavy seeds or super-Eddington growth. The authors call it unconfirmed; a black hole above 1 billion solar masses at z of 9.1 or higher would falsify it.

**Links:**

- [Phys.org — Early black holes may have grown through productive chaos](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-early-black-holes-grown-productive.html)

**Commentary:** Stating a clear falsification test is a strength, but this is one of several competing explanations.

## II. Biomedicine

### 5. FDA approves Bayer's asundexian, the first FXIa inhibitor, for secondary stroke prevention

**Summary:** On Nov. 9 the FDA approved asundexian (Sunharbo) for adults with a recent noncardioembolic ischemic stroke or high-risk TIA, alongside antiplatelet therapy. In the Phase 3 OCEANIC-STROKE trial, 50 mg plus antiplatelet therapy cut ischemic stroke by 26% versus antiplatelet alone. The FDA flagged higher bleeding risk in patients over 75 and in the first 90 days; atrial fibrillation stroke prevention is not covered.

**Links:**

- [Pharmacy Times — Asundexian receives FDA approval, becomes first FXIa inhibitor for secondary stroke risk reduction](https://www.pharmacytimes.com/view/asundexian-receives-fda-approval-becomes-first-fxia-inhibitor-for-secondary-stroke-risk-reduction)
- [Fierce Pharma — Bayer brings factor XIa to US stroke prevention with FDA nod for 1st-in-class asundexian](https://www.fiercepharma.com/pharma/bayer-brings-factor-xia-us-stroke-prevention-fda-nod-1st-class-asundexian)

**Commentary:** A new anticoagulation target finally reaches the market, but the narrow indication means displacing established anticoagulants depends on future atrial fibrillation data.

### 6. Vitamin B12 reverses developmental defects in a worm model of a rare genetic disorder

**Summary:** The Max Planck Institute for Biology of Ageing reported in Nature Communications that C. elegans carrying a splicing-gene mutation linked to Verheij syndrome grew slowly and had disrupted B12-dependent metabolism. Adding B12 restored those pathways and largely corrected development. Human datasets and patient blood samples showed similar metabolic changes, but benefit in patients is unknown.

**Links:**

- [ScienceDaily — Vitamin B12 May Hold the Key to Treating a Rare Genetic Disorder](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/11/261115085552.htm)

**Commentary:** It suggests a cheap candidate intervention for a rare disease, but so far only in an animal model.

### 7. Heart aging concentrates inflammation and scarring in valves and aortic root, not muscle

**Summary:** A spatial single-cell study in mice from The Jackson Laboratory, published in Aging Cell, found inflammation and fibrosis clustered around the aortic valves and aortic root. Aging macrophages showed more active inflammatory genes and fibroblasts fibrotic features, with clear male–female differences and partial overlap with published human data.

**Links:**

- [ScienceDaily — Scientists Discover That the Heart Doesn't Age the Way We Thought](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/11/261115085550.htm)

**Commentary:** It points anti-aging efforts toward valves and vessels, but the evidence is from mice with only partial human support.

### 8. Wearable ultrasound patch increases REM sleep without drugs

**Summary:** A University of Texas at Austin team reported in Nature Communications a patch (NEUSLeeP) that uses gentle ultrasound on deep brain regions. In 28 participants, REM began about 43 minutes earlier and lasted about 16 minutes longer on average, with minimal side effects and improved heart rate variability. The study is small and has not shown symptom benefits for mental health disorders.

**Links:**

- [ScienceDaily — New Wearable Ultrasound Patch Boosts REM Sleep Without Drugs or Surgery](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/11/261116014842.htm)

**Commentary:** A proof of concept for noninvasive brain modulation whose clinical value awaits larger controlled trials.

## III. Earth & Environment

### 9. A single deep-sea nodule hosted 245 animals, raising the stakes for seabed mining

**Summary:** MARUM at the University of Bremen reported in Scientific Reports that 244 polymetallic nodules from the Clarion-Clipperton Zone (4,000–6,000 m deep) held 4,139 animals, averaging 17 per nodule and up to 245 in one. Nodule communities differed from those in surrounding mud and looked similar across sites up to 1,000 km apart. The nodules contain nickel, cobalt and copper, and mining would destroy habitat that takes millions of years to form.

**Links:**

- [ScienceDaily — Hidden Ecosystem: Scientists Discover 245 Animals Living Inside a Single Deep-sea Rock](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/11/261114202133.htm)

**Commentary:** It adds evidence that nodules are habitat in their own right, which carries weight in deep-sea mining impact assessments.
