### 2. TRACERS year-one results: magnetic reconnection looks more spatial than temporal (space weather)
**Summary:** NASA on October 29 released first-year findings from TRACERS (Tandem Reconnection and Cusp Electrodynamics Reconnaissance Satellites). The twin spacecraft, launched last year, probe how the solar wind drives magnetic reconnection at Earth’s magnetopause. Using Analyzers for Cusp Ions data, a Journal of Geophysical Research analysis led with Southwest Research Institute’s Stephen Fuselier argues reconnection is largely a spatial process—the reconnection site moves—rather than a rapidly time-varying rate. After recovering one satellite post-launch, TRACERS now samples the southern cusp several times a day; NASA approved a prime-mission extension into spring 2027.

**Links:**

- [NASA Science — NASA’s TRACERS First Results Offers Peek into Violent Magnetic Process](https://science.nasa.gov/blogs/tracers/2026/10/29/nasas-tracers-first-results-offers-peek-into-violent-magnetic-process/)

**Commentary:** Recasting reconnection as location migration rather than rate flicker is a modeling assumption space-weather forecasts will have to absorb.

### 3. Hubble and Webb image the “Cat’s Head Nebula” in Carina (star formation)
**Summary:** ESA/Hubble on October 29 released complementary Hubble and JWST views of a star-forming scene nicknamed the Cat’s Head Nebula inside the Carina Nebula, about 7,500 light-years away. Hubble’s visible-light image shows a dark elongated dust pillar and hints of young stars; Webb’s near-infrared view makes the dust more transparent and reveals many embedded stars and filaments, while mid-infrared resolves complex surface structure. ESA says the paired wavelengths help decode the chaotic physics of star birth.

**Links:**

- [ESA/Hubble — Ghostly Cat’s Head Nebula lurks within sight of Hubble and Webb](https://esahubble.org/news/heic2614/)

**Commentary:** Visible light maps the silhouette; infrared maps the interior—together they turn a fleeting dust pillar into a measurable star-formation lab.

### 4. TSIS-2 clears vacuum and vibration tests ahead of early-2027 launch (solar irradiance)
**Summary:** NASA Science reported on October 29 that TSIS-2 (Total and Spectral Solar Irradiance Sensor-2) passed cold vacuum and launch-like vibration testing at General Atomics in Colorado. The spacecraft carries Total and Spectral Irradiance Monitors to extend the continuous total solar irradiance record dating to 1978, constraining how tiny solar-output changes feed climate predictions. NASA leads with CU/LASP instruments and GA-EMS spacecraft; launch is planned for early 2027 on a Rocket Lab Electron from New Zealand.

## II. Biomedicine & Health

### 5. Common gut “human” virus infects bacteria—third known RNA bacteriophage family (microbiology)
**Summary:** Washington University School of Medicine’s David Wang, Tianyu Gan and collaborators reported in Nature Microbiology on October 29 that picobirnaviruses—long assumed to infect human gut cells—actually infect gut bacteria. A germ-free mouse model, anaerobic culture growth over 20 days, antibiotic shutoff, and imaging placed the viruses inside a newly identified bacterium, *Candidatus Anaerotignum faecalis*. The work redefines Picobirnaviridae as only the third known family of bacteria-infecting RNA viruses; links to diarrhea, graft-versus-host disease, and Type 1 diabetes still need causal tests.

**Links:**

- [WashU Medicine — 40-year mystery solved: Common gut virus infects bacteria, not human cells](https://medicine.washu.edu/news/40-year-mystery-solved-common-gut-virus-infects-bacteria-not-human-cells/)

**Commentary:** Knowing the true host is the first step before blaming a gut virus for human disease or designing microbiome-targeted tools.

### 6. Nature: lupus T and B cells recognize splicing-derived neoantigens (autoimmunity)
**Summary:** UW Medicine’s Tomas Mustelin and La Jolla Institute’s Stephen P. Schoenberger co-led a Nature paper published October 29, “T and B cells recognize splicing-derived neoantigens in lupus.” Lupus patients’ neutrophils produce abnormal peptides from aberrant mRNA splicing that escape nonsense surveillance, are MHC-presented, and drive T- and B-cell responses; rheumatoid arthritis patients and healthy controls lacked the pattern. The authors argue this neoantigen-response frame may fit fluctuating lupus better than simple loss of tolerance to normal self proteins, but pathogenicity still requires clinical trials that block neoantigen production.

**Links:**

- [UW Medicine — Lupus findings open new paradigm for autoimmune disease](https://newsroom.uw.edu/news-releases/lupus-findings-open-new-paradigm-for-autoimmune-disease)

**Commentary:** Shifting the target from normal self proteins to splicing mistakes could rewrite how autoimmune drugs are screened.

### 7. Harvard-led mouse work: postnatal CO₂ loss shuts regeneration; inhaled CO₂ can restore axon growth (regenerative medicine)
**Summary:** New Scientist on October 29 covered a bioRxiv study led by Harvard Medical School’s Zhigang He: within minutes after birth, mTOR activity drops in CNS neurons and in heart and skeletal muscle. The trigger is CO₂ exhalation once breathing starts, causing intracellular alkalinization that activates AMPK-family kinase SNRK and suppresses mTOR. Adult mice inhaling ~10% CO₂ reactivated mTOR across tissues and restored retinal-ganglion-cell axon regeneration after injury. Authors and outside experts stress safe human doses and exposure times are unknown.

**Links:**

- [New Scientist — Inhaling CO2 may rescue nerves’ ability to regrow](https://www.newscientist.com/article/2596332-inhaling-co2-rescues-nerves-ability-to-regrow/)
- [bioRxiv — The Onset of Breathing at Birth Switches Off Regenerative Capacity](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.10.03.756425v1)

**Commentary:** A reversible physiological switch is tantalizing, but preprint mouse data are still far from a clinical CO₂ protocol.

### 8. MIT: millimeter magnetic beads track residual-limb muscle length and velocity better than surface EMG (neuroprosthetics)
**Summary:** MIT’s Hugh Herr, Christopher Shallal, Cameron Taylor and colleagues reported in Nature Sensors on October 29 that magnetomicrometry—pairs of ~3 mm gold- and parylene-coated neodymium beads implanted in up to four ankle-related residual muscles of three below-knee amputees—can be read wirelessly to ~6 cm depth. Compared with implanted and surface EMG in a virtual ankle-foot cursor task, magnetic length/velocity signals were more accurate and stable. Participants had agonist–antagonist myoneural interface (AMI) reconstructions; a larger prosthetic-control study is underway.

**Links:**

- [MIT News — Magnetic sensors track muscle movement, for better prosthetic limb control](https://news.mit.edu/2026/magnetic-sensors-track-muscle-movement-better-prosthetic-limb-control-1029)

**Commentary:** Measuring mechanical muscle output instead of noisy electrical proxies may be what finally makes neurally controlled walking limbs safely commercial.

### 12. Nature Energy: perovskite “lattice compliance” stabilizes bifacial tandems (photovoltaics)
**Summary:** Fuzong Xu, Jiang Liu, Stefaan De Wolf and colleagues reported in Nature Energy on October 29 that lattice compliance—the crystal’s ability to elastically accommodate stress—is a materials descriptor of photothermal stability for bromide-lean ~1.55 eV perovskites. Higher compliance suppressed thermally induced microstrain, shallowed deep defects, and reduced band-edge disorder. Bifacial monolithic perovskite/silicon tandems reached 36.2 mW cm⁻² under 0.20 albedo-equivalent illumination and retained 90% of initial efficiency after more than 1,000 hours of ISOS-L-2 (1-sun, 65 °C) testing.

**Links:**

- [Nature Energy — Perovskite lattice compliance enables efficient and stable bifacial perovskite/silicon tandem photovoltaics](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41560-026-02162-y)

**Commentary:** Turning stability into a measurable mechanical screening metric is a methodological step toward engineered tandem stacks.

## IV. Fundamental Research & Physics

### 13. Science / SLAC: chiral altermagnon in MnTe confirms a third magnetic phase (condensed matter)
**Summary:** SLAC/Stanford’s Daniel Jost and colleagues reported in Science on October 29 direct evidence of a chiral altermagnon in manganese telluride (MnTe) via circular-dichroism resonant inelastic X-ray scattering, with momentum-dependent spin polarization matching g-wave altermagnetic symmetry. Left- versus right-handed polarized beams at ESRF produced a chiral signal incompatible with conventional antiferromagnetism. Lab and journal accounts call this decisive experimental proof of altermagnetism, proposed in 2022—zero net magnetization plus usable spin-polarized currents for spintronics.

**Links:**

- [SLAC — Researchers find proof of altermagnetism, a new magnetic phase](https://www6.slac.stanford.edu/news/2026-10-29-slac-researchers-find-proof-altermagnetism-new-magnetic-phase)
- [Science — Chiral altermagnon in MnTe](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.adv3196)

**Commentary:** A third magnetic class now has a measurable excitation spectrum, giving spintronics a path that is both field-robust and readable.
