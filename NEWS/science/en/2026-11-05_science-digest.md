## I. Space & Deep Space

### 1. ESA’s Hera Captures First Close-Up of the Didymos Binary Asteroids (Planetary Defense)
**Summary:** On Nov 5, ESA released Hera’s first close-up of the Didymos binary system, taken by the monochromatic Asteroid Framing Camera from about 60 km away: the ~780-m primary Didymos is clearly imaged, while the ~151-m moonlet Dimorphos is mostly in shadow. The view shows the opposite side from what NASA’s DART camera saw before the 2022 impact—same equatorial ridge, but with additional boulders, craters, and possible grooves or ridges; mission scientists note some features may stem from ejecta that crossed the ~1 km gap after impact. The spacecraft is about 196.8 million km from Earth; detailed images of both bodies are planned for Nov 25.

**Links:**

- [ESA — Hera’s first close-up view of Didymos asteroids](https://www.esa.int/articles/about-us/directorates/operations/space-safety/hera/hera-s-first-close-up-view-of-didymos-asteroids)

**Commentary:** Kinetic-impact defense is moving from “did it work?” to “map the crash site”—repeatable deflection engineering depends on surveys like this.

### 2. ISS Spacewalk Begins to Install the Final iROSA Array (On-Orbit Engineering)
**Summary:** NASA’s station blog reported at 8:44 a.m. EST on Nov 5 that CSA astronaut Joshua Kutryk and NASA astronaut Anil Menon began a spacewalk at 8:36 a.m. EST to install and deploy the eighth and final IROSA on the station’s 3B channel, completing power-augmentation upgrades. Kutryk is crew member 1 (red stripes); Menon is crew member 2; NASA is streaming on multiple platforms. Mission management had already cleared the roughly seven-hour EVA; once finished, ISS generating capacity is expected to rise from about 160 kW to about 215 kW.

**Links:**

- [NASA — Spacewalk Begins to Install Last Roll-Out Solar Array](https://www.nasa.gov/blogs/spacestation/2026/11/05/spacewalk-begins-to-install-last-roll-out-solar-array/)

## II. Biomedicine & Health

### 3. Cambridge/DIOSynVax: AI-Designed Broad Bird-Flu Vaccine Fully Protects Animals vs Stockpile Shot (Vaccines)
**Summary:** The University of Cambridge and spinout DIOSynVax reported on Nov 5 that DVX-panH5Nx—an AI-designed multi-antigen mRNA candidate built from global surveillance sequences and cell screening—gave complete protection in mice and ferrets against diverse H5Nx variants that have caused human infections and pose pandemic risks. In the same multi-variant challenges, WHO stockpiled bird-flu vaccines protected only about half of animals. The study appears in *Cell Reports Medicine*; the team aims for human trials next and is also pursuing broader seasonal influenza antigens.

**Links:**

- [Medical Xpress / University of Cambridge — AI bird flu vaccine outperforms stockpiled shot](https://medicalxpress.com/news/2026-11-scientists-ai-bird-flu-vaccine.html)
- [GEN — Bird Flu Vaccine Developed Using AI](https://www.genengnews.com/topics/infectious-diseases/bird-flu-vaccine-developed-using-ai-outperforms-current-stockpiled-vaccines-in-preclinical-tests/)

**Commentary:** Stockpiling known strains gives way to anticipating viral sequence space—animal sterilizing protection is striking, but human efficacy and manufacturing remain the gate.

### 4. Harvard Wyss: FIND DNA-Nanoswitch Platform in *Science* Screens Drugs by Protein Interactions (Drug Discovery)
**Summary:** Wesley Wong’s team at the Wyss Institute, Boston Children’s Hospital, and Harvard Medical School published FIND (Functional Interaction–based Nanoswitch Discovery) in *Science* on Nov 5: DNA nanoswitches physically link candidate molecules to protein pairs; compounds that promote protein–protein binding loop the switch, which is separated by gel electrophoresis and identified via barcode sequencing. From a ~400-million-member DNA-encoded library, FIND recovered PPARγ agonists that enhance coactivator-peptide binding and increase lipid accumulation in cells, plus a nanobody that stabilizes SARS-CoV-2 spike–ACE2 interaction. The platform selects on functional interaction outcomes rather than binding alone.

**Links:**

- [Science — Discovering molecular modulators with nanoswitch screening](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.adt7185)
- [Wyss Institute — FINDing new drug leads faster with DNA nanotechnology](https://wyss.harvard.edu/news/finding-new-drug-leads-faster-with-dna-nanotechnology/)

**Commentary:** Swapping “does it bind?” for “does the pathway move?” gives ternary-complex and PPI-modulator discovery a scalable physical readout.

### 5. Yale: Epi-PHR Nondisruptively Profiles Epigenetic Marks with 3D Genome in Single Cells (Epigenetics)
**Summary:** Yale School of Medicine’s Siyuan Wang and colleagues published Epi-PHR (epigenetic proximity hybridization reaction) in *Nature Methods* on Nov 5: an imaging method that detects combinations of epigenetic marks at hundreds of gene loci in single cells while preserving 3D genome organization. It works in tissue (e.g., hippocampus) and, with chromatin tracing, enables parent-of-origin allele-resolved analysis, revealing unexpected links between mark enrichment and folding at an imprinted cluster. The approach targets the gap left by methods that struggle to combine multi-mark, locus-specific readout without disrupting chromatin architecture.

**Links:**

- [Nature Methods — Nondisruptive 3D profiling of combinations of epigenetic marks in single cells](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41592-026-03253-0)
- [Yale School of Medicine — How DNA Markings and Shape Affect Disease](https://medicine.yale.edu/news-article/how-dna-markings-and-shape-affect-disease/)

**Commentary:** Epigenetics and folding no longer require destroying one to measure the other—single-copy, allele-aware co-profiling can explain counterintuitive regulatory rules in development and disease.

### 6. CARB-X: GSK Invasive Nontyphoidal Salmonella–Typhoid Combo Vaccine Enters Phase 2 (Global Health)
**Summary:** CARB-X announced on Nov 5 that GSK’s global-health iNTS-TCV candidate has advanced into Phase 2—the first CARB-X-supported vaccine to reach that stage. ## III. Climate, Energy & Environment

### 7. *Nature*: Hydrogen Derivatives Could Cut China’s Carbon-Neutrality Transition Cost by ~$10.7 Trillion (Energy Systems)
**Summary:** Teams including Huazhong University of Science and Technology and Harvard published in *Nature* on Nov 5 a China-wide assessment of hydrogen and derivatives as green energy carriers, spanning high-resolution renewable siting, hourly power-system operations across 29 provinces, and more than 46,000 rail/road/marine/pipeline routing options plus end-use fuel strategies. Versus renewables-plus-electrification pathways, including hydrogen derivatives lowers overall transition cost by about $10.7 trillion, yields a negative abatement cost (about −$6.15/ton), and eliminates oil-import expenditures. Authors stress hydrogen’s bridging role for hard-to-abate sectors and variable renewable integration.

**Links:**

- [Nature — Hydrogen Derivatives Enable Cost-Effective Carbon Neutrality in China](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-11351-4)

**Commentary:** Model bounds matter, but the direction is clear—without transportable molecular carriers, hard-to-electrify industry cannot live on wires alone.

### 8. *Nature Communications*: Future Heatwaves Will Far Exceed Recent Record Breakers (Climate Extremes)
**Summary:** A Nov 5 *Nature Communications* study compares recently observed exceptional heatwaves with 21st-century projections across multiple metrics and finds that even under low- and medium-emissions scenarios, many regions can still see events that surpass recent record breakers; under high to very high emissions, some regions face several-fold more cumulative heat and durations lasting months. If Paris Agreement NDCs are not implemented, a concerning share of projected heatwaves in most regions would exceed all analyzed observed characteristics at once. Authors stress that ambitious mitigation remains essential, while noting that even ~2°C pathways offer only limited protection.

**Links:**

- [Nature Communications — Recent extreme heatwaves are no match for future events](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-76809-5)

**Commentary:** The hottest summer in living memory is not an upper bound—adaptation must be designed for distributional tails, not historical samples.

### 9. PolyU: Hidden Perovskite Photodegradation Mechanism Fixed; T90 Tops 2,200 Hours (Photovoltaics)
**Summary:** Hong Kong Polytechnic University’s Guang Yang team, with Zhejiang University, reported on Nov 5 in *Watt* that in mixed-facet perovskites, adjacent (100) and (111) grains create asymmetric charge losses; formamidinium vacancies on the (111) side pile up holes and turn otherwise photostable (111) grains into aging sources. Bulky m-F-PEABr molecules collect at boundaries to fill vacancies and anchor in place; treated cells reached 26.55% efficiency and retained over 90% of initial efficiency after more than 2,200 hours under one-sun continuous operation (T90). Perovskite pioneer Nam-Gyu Park highlighted the work in the same journal.

**Links:**

- [PolyU — Hidden cause of ageing in perovskite solar cells](https://www.polyu.edu.hk/rio/news/2026/20261104---polyu-research-uncovers-hidden-cause-of-ageing-in-perovskite/)

**Commentary:** Lifetime is not just “the most stable grain”—how neighboring facets drag each other down is the aging switch commercialization must flip off.

## IV. Fundamental Research & Physics

### 11. China Space Station: Optical-Dipole-Trap BEC at ~9.7 pK with 7-Second Free Expansion (Ultracold Atoms)
**Summary:** Teams including Peking University and SIOM/CAS reported in *Science* on Nov 5 an optical-dipole-trap Bose–Einstein condensate of about 2.0×10⁴ rubidium atoms aboard China’s space station Cold Atom Physics Rack, reaching an equivalent temperature of about 9.7 pK (uncertainty roughly +3.2/−2.7 pK) with 7.0 s of free-expansion observation. The result undercuts the prior microgravity magnetic-trap mark of ~38 pK with ~2 s expansion and is colder than prior ISS Cold Atom Lab reports; optics can trap atoms in multiple internal states. *Science* news coverage calls it among the lowest equivalent temperatures yet, with long expansion enabling spaceborne atom interferometry and equivalence-principle tests.

**Links:**

- [Science — Quantum gases below 10 picokelvin in space](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.aea5324)
- [Science News — China space experiment creates coldest temperature ever](https://www.science.org/content/article/china-space-experiment-creates-coldest-temperature-ever)

**Commentary:** 9.7 pK grabs headlines, but seven seconds of drag-free quiet is the real dividend for precision measurement in orbit.

### 12. *Science*: Shot-Noise Evidence Equates Cuprate Pseudogap Energy with Pairing Onset (Superconductivity)
**Summary:** On Nov 5, *Science* published local shot-noise spectroscopy of Bi₂Sr₂CaCu₂O₈₊δ by Niu et al.: conventional superconductors show effective charge 2e, while cuprate nodal gaps complicate interpretation, yet pairing still imprints a step in effective charge. That step appears at energies associated with the pseudogap; pseudogap energy coincides with the onset of electron pairing, is spatially heterogeneous, and reaches up to about 70 meV. Results favor a pairing scenario over a pure local-order origin (e.g., charge-density waves alone) and suggest phase coherence—not pair formation—may limit higher critical temperatures.

**Links:**

- [Science — Equivalence of pseudogap and pairing energy in a cuprate high-temperature superconductor](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.adt3444)

**Commentary:** A decades-old “what is the pseudogap?” debate now has a measurable pairing-charge fingerprint—and the Tc ceiling looks more like a coherence problem than a pairing one.
